Compare the climate of two cities

ClimaClue’s free climate comparison tool puts any two places on Earth side by side: month-by-month temperature, rain and snow, sunshine, wind, humidity and dew point on the same chart and scale, with a table of the differences.

Open the comparison tool ›

Or browse ready-made comparisons of big cities, such as London vs Paris.

How to compare two places

Open Compare and pick place A: click it on the globe, search for a town, or choose one of your saved places. Then pick place B the same way. Any spot on land works, not only big cities, so you can compare a mountain village with the coast below it, or your home town with somewhere you are thinking of moving to.

Both places then share every chart. Choose a measure (temperature, rain, sunshine, wind, humidity and dew point, or snow) and the two lines are drawn on one scale, so a gap of a few degrees looks like a few degrees. Below the charts, a table lists the year’s figures for each place and the difference between them: the yearly average temperature, the warmest month’s day high, the coolest month’s night low, yearly precipitation, sunshine hours, average wind and humidity, yearly snowfall and the record high and low from each place’s nearest long-running weather station.

When the numbers raise a question, such as why one coast is so much cooler in summer, you can open the chat with ClimaClue for a plain explanation of the difference.

Pairs like these show why a comparison helps: two towns can have nearly the same yearly average and still feel very different, because one has warm summers and the other cool ones, or one gets its rain in winter and the other all year. Quito and Singapore lie close to the equator, yet Quito, high in the Andes, is far cooler. Lisbon and Melbourne are in opposite hemispheres, so their warmest months fall half a year apart.

Where the numbers come from

Temperature and precipitation are CHELSA V2.1 monthly averages for 1981–2010, worked out for areas of about 10 km (0.1°) on the globe, and for the few kilometres around each town’s centre on town pages, in the games and in the Matchmaker. Day highs and night lows take that average with the day–night range of CRU CL 2.0, sunshine comes from CRU CL 2.0 too (about 1961–1990), and snow from ERA5-Land (1981–2010). They are modelled climate averages (normals), not weather forecasts or station records. Each place is the area of about 10 km around the point you pick, averaged over its land. Record highs and lows come from NOAA GHCN-Daily weather stations. Where a source has no value for a month, the chart shows a gap instead of an invented number.

Questions and answers

How do I compare the climate of two cities?
Open Compare on ClimaClue, pick the first place on the globe or by searching, then pick the second. Both appear on shared charts with a table of the differences.
Can I compare places that are not cities?
Yes. Any point on land works: villages, mountains, islands or the countryside, each as its own area of about 10 km.
Which climate measures can I compare?
Monthly temperature (with day highs and night lows), rain and snow, sunshine hours, wind, humidity and dew point, and snowfall, plus record highs and lows from the nearest weather station.
Is the climate comparison tool free?
Yes, comparing two places is free in any web browser, with no sign-up.
How many places can I compare at once?
Two, side by side (A and B), so every chart stays easy to read.
