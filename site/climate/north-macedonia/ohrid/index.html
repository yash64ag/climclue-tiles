Ohrid at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in North Macedonia, Ohrid is 21st warmest (12.4 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,213 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (749 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Struga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Ohrid is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.8 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 126 km from the sea.

Why Ohrid has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 126 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C. The 10 km area around Ohrid, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 10.4 °C a year, 2.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ohrid itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.117°N, 20.802°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-428-2008), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
