Prilep at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in North Macedonia, Prilep is 27th warmest (12.0 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,250 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (547 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bitola, the nearest covered town with different numbers (39 km away), Prilep is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 152 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 22.3 °C more than its coolest, January (0.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 142 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Prilep, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Prilep has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 142 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Prilep itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.346°N, 21.554°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-426-2015), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 73,814, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
