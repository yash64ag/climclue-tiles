Ruwa weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ruwa, Zimbabwe (Mashonaland East Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ruwa is warmest in November, with 26.6 °C (80 °F) days and 14.3 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 20.6 °C (69 °F) days and 5.3 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (216 mm); July is driest (2 mm).

Ruwa at a glance

Among the 29 towns ClimaClue covers in Zimbabwe, Ruwa is the 3rd coolest (17.7 °C year average). Compared with Harare, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Ruwa is 0.9 °C cooler in November and 0.7 °C cooler in July, with 73 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (20.5 °C), averages 7.6 °C more than its coolest, July (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in July. The area lies about 445 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ruwa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ruwa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 445 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ruwa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.890°S, 31.245°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1018-2112), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 94,083, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
