Hwange at a glance

Among the 29 towns ClimaClue covers in Zimbabwe, Hwange is the 2nd warmest (24.1 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,198 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (538 mm a year). Compared with Victoria Falls, the nearest covered town with different numbers (85 km away), Hwange is 1.4 °C warmer in October and 1.4 °C warmer in July, with 115 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (28.1 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, July (18.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.9 °C (48 °F), in July. The area lies about 899 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hwange, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hwange has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 899 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hwange itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.364°S, 26.499°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1023-2064), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
