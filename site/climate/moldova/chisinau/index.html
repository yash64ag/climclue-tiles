Chisinau at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Moldova, Chisinau is the 3rd warmest (10.4 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 2,088 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (516 mm a year). Compared with Bender, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Chisinau is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.4 °C), averages 24.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 166 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 96% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Chisinau

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.

Why Chisinau has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 166 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chisinau itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.009°N, 28.859°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-369-2088), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
