Denov at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Denov is 20th warmest (16.0 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 2,799 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (382 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sho‘rchi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Denov is 2.1 °C cooler in July and 1.2 °C cooler in January, with 77 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.2 °C), averages 24.9 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 1,169 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Denov has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,169 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Denov itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.267°N, 67.899°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-457-2478), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
