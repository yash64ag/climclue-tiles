Dardoq at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Dardoq is 80th warmest (13.6 °C year average), 103rd sunniest (about 2,545 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (439 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oyim, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Dardoq is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 46 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.9 °C), averages 28.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,527 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Dardoq has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,527 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dardoq itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.816°N, 72.832°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-431-2528), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
