Tashkent at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Tashkent is 46th warmest (15.2 °C year average), 55th sunniest (about 2,790 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (450 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bektemir, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Tashkent is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 32 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 25.6 °C more than its coolest, January (2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,222 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 5 km from Tashkent by up to 3.1 °C (5.6 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations at a similar height, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Tashkent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,222 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.6 °C. The 10 km area around Tashkent, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.8 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tashkent itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.265°N, 69.216°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-427-2492), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
