Tashkent at a glance

Among the 20 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uzbekistan, Tashkent is the 5th wettest (457 mm a year). Compared with Chirchiq, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Tashkent is 1.4 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 283 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.1 °C), averages 27.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,222 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 5 km from Tashkent measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.1 °C (5.5 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Tashkent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,222 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
