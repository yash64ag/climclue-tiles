Beshkent at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Beshkent is 33rd warmest (15.7 °C year average), 15th sunniest (about 2,932 hours of sunshine a year) and 71st wettest (248 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Qarshi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Beshkent is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.3 °C), averages 25.4 °C more than its coolest, January (2.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 966 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Beshkent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 966 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Beshkent itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.821°N, 65.653°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-451-2456), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
