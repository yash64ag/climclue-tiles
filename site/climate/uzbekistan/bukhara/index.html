Bukhara at a glance

Among the 20 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uzbekistan, Bukhara is the 3rd warmest (16.2 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,981 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (147 mm a year). Compared with Navoiy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (88 km away), Bukhara is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 72 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.4 °C), averages 28.5 °C more than its coolest, January (1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 841 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bukhara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bukhara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 841 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
