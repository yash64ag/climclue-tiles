Qo‘qon at a glance

Among the 20 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uzbekistan, Qo‘qon is the 2nd warmest (16.8 °C year average) and the 5th driest (151 mm a year). Compared with Chust, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Qo‘qon is 4.1 °C warmer in July and 2.1 °C warmer in January, with 195 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.1 °C), averages 29.1 °C more than its coolest, January (1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,372 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qo‘qon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Qo‘qon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,372 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
