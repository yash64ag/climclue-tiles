Sho‘rchi at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Sho‘rchi is the 2nd warmest (17.5 °C year average). Compared with Denov, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Sho‘rchi is 2.1 °C warmer in July and 1.2 °C warmer in January, with 77 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.3 °C), averages 25.8 °C more than its coolest, January (4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 1,172 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Sho‘rchi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,172 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sho‘rchi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.999°N, 67.788°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-2477), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
