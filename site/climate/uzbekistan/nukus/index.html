Nukus at a glance

Among the 20 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uzbekistan, Nukus is the 2nd coolest (12.4 °C year average) and the 3rd driest (119 mm a year). Compared with Urganch, the nearest covered town with different numbers (132 km away), Nukus is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 1.4 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 31.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 447 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nukus, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nukus has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 447 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nukus itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.459°N, 59.606°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-415-2396), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
