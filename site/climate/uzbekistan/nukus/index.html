Nukus at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Nukus is 101st warmest (12.4 °C year average), 44th sunniest (about 2,820 hours of sunshine a year) and 97th wettest (119 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Xo‘jayli Shahri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Nukus is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 31.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 447 km from the sea.

Why Nukus has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 447 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nukus itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.459°N, 59.606°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-415-2396), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
