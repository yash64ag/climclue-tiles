Chust at a glance

Among the 20 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uzbekistan, Chust is the 5th least sunny (about 2,639 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Namangan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Chust is 2.3 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 61 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 27.4 °C more than its coolest, January (0.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,391 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Chust has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,391 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chust itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.003°N, 71.238°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-429-2512), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
