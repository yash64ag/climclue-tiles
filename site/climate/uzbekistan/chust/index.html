Chust at a glance

Among the 20 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uzbekistan, Chust is the 5th least sunny (about 2,639 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Namangan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Chust is 3.5 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 117 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.9 °C), averages 27.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,391 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Chust has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,391 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
