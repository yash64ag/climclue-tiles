Bo‘ka at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Bo‘ka is 42nd warmest (15.3 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 2,789 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (401 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Piskent, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Bo‘ka is 0.9 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 44 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 26.9 °C more than its coolest, January (1.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,217 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bo‘ka, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bo‘ka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,217 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bo‘ka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.811°N, 69.194°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-431-2491), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
