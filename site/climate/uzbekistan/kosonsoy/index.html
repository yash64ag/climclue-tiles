Kosonsoy weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kosonsoy, Uzbekistan (Namangan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kosonsoy is warmest in July, with 32.4 °C (90 °F) days and 17.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.6 °C (37 °F) days and -5.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around May (55 mm); August is driest (11 mm).

Kosonsoy at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Kosonsoy is 103rd warmest (12.3 °C year average), 79th sunniest (about 2,623 hours of sunshine a year) and 26th wettest (395 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yangiqo‘rg‘on, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Kosonsoy is 1.8 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 65 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.9 °C), averages 26.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,414 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kosonsoy has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,414 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kosonsoy itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.249°N, 71.547°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-427-2515), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
