Toshloq weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Toshloq, Uzbekistan (Fergana), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Toshloq is warmest in July, with 37.9 °C (100 °F) days and 23.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 5.5 °C (42 °F) days and -2.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (27 mm); August is driest (5 mm).

Toshloq at a glance

Among the 109 towns ClimaClue covers in Uzbekistan, Toshloq is 14th warmest (16.3 °C year average), 90th sunniest (about 2,581 hours of sunshine a year) and 82nd wettest (213 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Marg‘ilon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Toshloq is 0.1 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.4 °C), averages 29.0 °C more than its coolest, January (1.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 1,440 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 12 km from Toshloq measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.9 °C (5.2 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Toshloq has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,440 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Toshloq itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.477°N, 71.768°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-435-2517), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
