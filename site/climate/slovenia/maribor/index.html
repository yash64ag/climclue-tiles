Maribor weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Maribor, Slovenia (Urban Municipality of Maribor), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Maribor is warmest in July, with 26.6 °C (80 °F) days and 15.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.9 °C (37 °F) days and -4.3 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (122 mm); January is driest (38 mm).

Maribor at a glance

Among the 9 towns ClimaClue covers in Slovenia, Maribor is the 3rd sunniest (about 1,987 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (993 mm a year). Compared with Ptuj, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Maribor is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.1 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 172 km from the sea.

Why Maribor has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 172 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Maribor itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.556°N, 15.646°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-374-1956), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
