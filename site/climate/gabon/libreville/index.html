Libreville at a glance

Among the 3 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Gabon, Libreville is the warmest (25.6 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,620 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (3,136 mm a year). Compared with Port-Gentil, the nearest covered town with different numbers (144 km away), Libreville is 0.3 °C cooler in March and 0.8 °C warmer in July, with 1,276 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.8 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, July (24.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.5 °C (73 °F), in July. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Libreville, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Libreville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
