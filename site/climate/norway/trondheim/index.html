Trondheim at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Norway, Trondheim is the coolest (5.6 °C year average), the least sunny (about 1,234 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (1,096 mm a year). Compared with Oslo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (392 km away), Trondheim is 2.3 °C cooler in July and 1.7 °C warmer in January, with 78 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (14.9 °C), averages 17.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Trondheim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Trondheim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
