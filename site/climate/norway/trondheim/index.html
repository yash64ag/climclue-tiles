Trondheim at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Trondheim is 25th warmest (6.7 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 1,234 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (930 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Stjørdalshalsen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Trondheim is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 261 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.1 °C), averages 17.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Trondheim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Trondheim

For mild weather, the best months to visit Trondheim are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (16.1 °C), August (15.4 °C) and June (13.4 °C).

Why Trondheim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Trondheim climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Trondheim?
July, with an average of 16.1 °C (61 °F), day highs around 20.1 °C (68 °F) and night lows around 12.1 °C (54 °F).
What is the coldest month in Trondheim?
January, with an average of -1.1 °C (30 °F), day highs around 1.7 °C (35 °F) and night lows around -3.8 °C (25 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Trondheim?
January, with 96 mm (3.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 930 mm (36.6 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Trondheim?
April, with 47 mm (1.9 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Trondheim?

Does it snow in Trondheim?
Yes. About 291 cm (114.4 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in January; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 7 months.

How warm is the sea in Trondheim?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Trondheim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (63.430°N, 10.395°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-205-1903), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 216,518, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
