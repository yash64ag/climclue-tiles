Trondheim at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Trondheim is 26th warmest (6.3 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 1,234 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (930 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Stjørdalshalsen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Trondheim is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 261 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.8 °C), averages 18.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Trondheim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Trondheim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Trondheim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (63.430°N, 10.395°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-205-1903), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
