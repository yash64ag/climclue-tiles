Stavanger weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Stavanger, Norway (Rogaland), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Stavanger is warmest in August, with 18.1 °C (65 °F) days and 12.0 °C nights, and coolest in February, with 4.2 °C (40 °F) days and 0.3 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 75 mm in May to 218 mm in October.

Stavanger at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Stavanger is the 5th warmest (8.2 °C year average). Compared with Sola, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Stavanger is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 138 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (15.1 °C), averages 12.8 °C more than its coolest, February (2.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 17 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 11 km from Stavanger measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 134% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Stavanger has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Stavanger itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.970°N, 5.733°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-250-1857), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
