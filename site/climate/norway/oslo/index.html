Why Oslo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 57 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Oslo climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Oslo?
July, with an average of 17.3 °C (63 °F), day highs around 20.8 °C (69 °F) and night lows around 12.8 °C (55 °F).
What is the coldest month in Oslo?
January, with an average of -4.5 °C (24 °F), day highs around -2.9 °C (27 °F) and night lows around -8.1 °C (17 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Oslo?
October, with 119 mm (4.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,019 mm (40.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Oslo?
February, with 54 mm (2.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Oslo?
June, with about 246 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Oslo?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 255 cm (100.5 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 6 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Oslo?
No month meets the definition used here (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation).
What type of climate does Oslo have?
Humid continental, warm summer (Köppen Dfb). Warm summers and cold, snowy winters, with rain or snow all year. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 5.6 × 11 km, that contains Oslo’s GeoNames location (59.913°N, 10.746°E; ClimaClue area sq-240-1907), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 1,082,575 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
