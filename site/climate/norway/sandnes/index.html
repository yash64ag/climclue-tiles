Sandnes at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Sandnes is the 5th warmest (7.9 °C year average). Compared with Sola, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Sandnes is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 220 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.1 °C), averages 13.7 °C more than its coolest, February (1.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 17 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 7 km from Sandnes measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 141% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care. Why Sandnes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Sandnes climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Sandnes?
July, with an average of 15.1 °C (59 °F), day highs around 18.1 °C (65 °F) and night lows around 12.0 °C (54 °F).
What is the coldest month in Sandnes?
February, with an average of 1.3 °C (34 °F), day highs around 3.7 °C (39 °F) and night lows around -1.0 °C (30 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Sandnes?
October, with 228 mm (9.0 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,735 mm (68.3 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Sandnes?
May, with 79 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Sandnes?

Does it snow in Sandnes?
Yes. About 29 cm (11.3 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in January; it usually melts within days.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sandnes itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.852°N, 5.735°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-251-1857), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 85,386, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
