Bergen at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Norway, Bergen is the 3rd warmest (7.2 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,239 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (2,919 mm a year). Compared with Stavanger, the nearest covered town with different numbers (160 km away), Bergen is about as warm in July and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 1,131 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (14.7 °C), averages 13.7 °C more than its coolest, January (0.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 121% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Bergen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
