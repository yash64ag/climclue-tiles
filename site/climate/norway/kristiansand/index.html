Kristiansand at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Norway, Kristiansand is the warmest (8.3 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,506 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,417 mm a year). Compared with Stavanger, the nearest covered town with different numbers (160 km away), Kristiansand is 1.8 °C warmer in July and 1.0 °C cooler in February, with 237 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.5 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, February (1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 7 km from Kristiansand measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.3 °C (4.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Kristiansand has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-258-1879), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
