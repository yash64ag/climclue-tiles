Kristiansand weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kristiansand, Norway (Agder), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kristiansand is warmest in July, with 19.7 °C (67 °F) days and 13.3 °C nights, and coolest in February, with 3.4 °C (38 °F) days and -0.9 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 69 mm in April to 184 mm in October.

Kristiansand at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Kristiansand is the 4th warmest (8.3 °C year average). Compared with Grimstad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (41 km away), Kristiansand is 0.9 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C warmer in February, with 119 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.5 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, February (1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 7 km from Kristiansand measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.3 °C (4.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Kristiansand has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kristiansand itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.147°N, 7.996°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-258-1879), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
