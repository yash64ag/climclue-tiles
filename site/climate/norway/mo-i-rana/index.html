Mo i Rana at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Mo i Rana is the 4th coolest (3.6 °C year average) and the 5th wettest (1,963 mm a year). Compared with Bodø, the nearest covered town with different numbers (108 km away), Mo i Rana is 2.6 °C warmer in July and 5.2 °C cooler in January, with 393 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.9 °C), averages 22.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-6.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 62 km from the sea.

Why Mo i Rana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 62 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mo i Rana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (66.313°N, 14.143°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 4.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-176-1941), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
