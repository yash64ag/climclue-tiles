Grimstad at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Grimstad is the 2nd warmest (8.4 °C year average). Compared with Arendal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Grimstad is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 73 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.4 °C), averages 16.6 °C more than its coolest, February (0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Grimstad measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.2 °C (4.0 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Grimstad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Grimstad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.340°N, 8.593°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-256-1885), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
