Tromsdalen at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Tromsdalen is the 2nd coolest (2.7 °C year average). Compared with Tromsø, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Tromsdalen is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in February, with 92 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (12.3 °C), averages 16.8 °C more than its coolest, February (-4.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 4 km from Tromsdalen by up to 4.8 °C (8.6 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Tromsdalen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.8 °C. The 10 km area around Tromsdalen, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 1.0 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tromsdalen itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (69.650°N, 19.017°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 3.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-143-1990), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 18,291, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
