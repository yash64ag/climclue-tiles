Tromsdalen weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Tromsdalen, Norway (Troms), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Tromsdalen at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Tromsdalen is the 2nd coolest (1.9 °C year average). Compared with Tromsø, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Tromsdalen is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 92 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (13.4 °C), averages 21.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Why Tromsdalen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.7 °C. The 10 km area around Tromsdalen, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 0.3 °C a year, 1.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tromsdalen itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (69.650°N, 19.017°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 3.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-143-1990), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 18,291, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
