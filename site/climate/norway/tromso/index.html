Tromsø weather by month and climate

Tromsø at a glance

Among the 40 towns ClimaClue covers in Norway, Tromsø is the 3rd coolest (2.3 °C year average). Compared with Tromsdalen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Tromsø is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 92 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (13.8 °C), averages 21.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 32 km from the sea.

Why Tromsø has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 32 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.6 °C. The 10 km area around Tromsø, which the globe shows when you click there, averages -0.2 °C a year, 2.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the coldest month in Tromsø?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tromsø itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (69.649°N, 18.955°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 3.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-143-1989), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 41,915, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
