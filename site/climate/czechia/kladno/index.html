Kladno at a glance

Among the 117 towns ClimaClue covers in Czechia, Kladno is 59th warmest (9.2 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 1,640 hours of sunshine a year) and 72nd wettest (565 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Slaný, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Kladno is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 46 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.8 °C), averages 19.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 401 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 23 km from Kladno by up to 2.5 °C (4.5 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Kladno has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 401 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kladno itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.147°N, 14.103°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-338-1941), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 69,728, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
