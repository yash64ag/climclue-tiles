Riyadh at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, Riyadh is 29th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 44th sunniest (about 3,360 hours of sunshine a year) and 45th wettest (103 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ad Dir‘īyah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Riyadh is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (36.3 °C), averages 22.0 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 376 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 86% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Riyadh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 376 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Riyadh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.688°N, 46.722°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-593-2267), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 4,205,961, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
