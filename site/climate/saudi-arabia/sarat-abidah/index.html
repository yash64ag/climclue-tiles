Sarāt ‘Abīdah at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, Sarāt ‘Abīdah is the 2nd coolest (17.7 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 2,928 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Khamis Mushait, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Sarāt ‘Abīdah is 1.9 °C cooler in June and 2.6 °C cooler in January, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (22.6 °C), averages 10.4 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 123 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sarāt ‘Abīdah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sarāt ‘Abīdah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 123 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sarāt ‘Abīdah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.089°N, 43.132°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-659-2231), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
