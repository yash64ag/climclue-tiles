Rābigh at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, Rābigh is 13th warmest (28.5 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 3,535 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (81 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thuwal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Rābigh is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (32.9 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.5 °C (64 °F), in February. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rābigh, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Rābigh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rābigh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.799°N, 39.035°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-612-2190), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
