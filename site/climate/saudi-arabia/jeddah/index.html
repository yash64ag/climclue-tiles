Why Jeddah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Jeddah climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Jeddah?
August, with an average of 31.9 °C (89 °F), day highs around 33.9 °C (93 °F) and night lows around 29.1 °C (84 °F).
What is the coldest month in Jeddah?
January, with an average of 23.6 °C (75 °F), day highs around 25.8 °C (78 °F) and night lows around 21.1 °C (70 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Jeddah?
November, with 27 mm (1.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 69 mm (2.7 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Jeddah?
June, with 0 mm (0.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Jeddah?
May, with about 340 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Jeddah?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Jeddah?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February and March.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Jeddah’s GeoNames location (21.490°N, 39.186°E; ClimaClue area sq-625-2191), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 4,697,000 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
