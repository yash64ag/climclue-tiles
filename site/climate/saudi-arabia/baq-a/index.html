Baq‘ā’ at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, Baq‘ā’ is 77th warmest (23.3 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 3,646 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (107 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Turubah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (66 km away), Baq‘ā’ is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (33.9 °C), averages 23.2 °C more than its coolest, January (10.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 571 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Baq‘ā’, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Baq‘ā’ has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 571 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Baq‘ā’ itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.906°N, 42.389°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-560-2223), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
