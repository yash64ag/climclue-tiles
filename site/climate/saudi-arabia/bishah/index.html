Bīshah at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, Bīshah is 46th warmest (26.0 °C year average), 79th sunniest (about 3,079 hours of sunshine a year) and 77th wettest (71 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sabt Alalayah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (82 km away), Bīshah is 9.3 °C warmer in August and 4.4 °C warmer in January, with 445 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (32.8 °C), averages 14.9 °C more than its coolest, January (17.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.2 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 199 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bīshah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bīshah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 199 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bīshah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.001°N, 42.605°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-639-2226), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
