Baljurashi at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, Baljurashi is the 3rd wettest (540 mm a year). Compared with Al Bahah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Baljurashi is 0.5 °C warmer in June and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 5 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (24.1 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, January (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.2 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 86 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Baljurashi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Baljurashi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 86 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C. The 10 km area around Baljurashi, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 21.8 °C a year, 2.8 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Baljurashi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.859°N, 41.557°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-641-2215), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 51,787, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
