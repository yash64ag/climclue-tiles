Sayhāt at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, Sayhāt is 40th warmest (26.1 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 3,298 hours of sunshine a year) and 60th wettest (90 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with ‘Inak, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Sayhāt is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.6 °C), averages 18.7 °C more than its coolest, January (15.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.3 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sayhāt, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sayhāt has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sayhāt itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.483°N, 50.048°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-575-2300), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 66,702, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
