King Khalid Military City at a glance

Among the 96 towns ClimaClue covers in Saudi Arabia, King Khalid Military City is 71st warmest (24.8 °C year average), 31st sunniest (about 3,488 hours of sunshine a year) and 33rd wettest (126 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hafar Al-Batin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (72 km away), King Khalid Military City is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (35.8 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (11.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.4 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 287 km from the sea.

Why King Khalid Military City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 287 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for King Khalid Military City itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.905°N, 45.553°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-560-2255), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
