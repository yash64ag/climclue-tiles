Ghormach at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Afghanistan, Ghormach is 32nd warmest (15.3 °C year average), 31st sunniest (about 2,877 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (373 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Maymana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (93 km away), Ghormach is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 1.4 °C warmer in January, with 45 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.5 °C), averages 25.1 °C more than its coolest, January (2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 896 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ghormach has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 896 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ghormach itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.731°N, 63.783°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-482-2437), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
