Nīlī at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Afghanistan, Nīlī is 50th warmest (9.6 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 3,132 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (691 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fayrōz Kōh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (120 km away), Nīlī is 7.1 °C warmer in July and 3.0 °C warmer in January, with 272 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.4 °C), averages 30.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-6.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 934 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nīlī, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nīlī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 934 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nīlī itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.722°N, 66.130°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-502-2461), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
