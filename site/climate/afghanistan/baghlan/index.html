Baghlān at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Baghlān is the 2nd least sunny (about 2,772 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Kunduz, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Baghlān is 3.2 °C cooler in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 100 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.9 °C), averages 26.0 °C more than its coolest, January (3.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,221 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Baghlān has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,221 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
