Barakī at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Afghanistan, Barakī is 43rd warmest (12.0 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 3,109 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (320 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baraki Barak, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Barakī is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.7 °C), averages 28.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 986 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Barakī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 986 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Barakī itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.939°N, 68.922°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-500-2489), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
