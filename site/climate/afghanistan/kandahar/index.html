Kandahār at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Kandahār is the 2nd warmest (20.4 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,299 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (229 mm a year). Compared with Ghazni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (333 km away), Kandahār is 10.5 °C warmer in July and 12.7 °C warmer in January, with 160 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.5 °C), averages 26.9 °C more than its coolest, January (6.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 701 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kandahār, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kandahār has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 701 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kandahār itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.613°N, 65.710°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-523-2457), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
