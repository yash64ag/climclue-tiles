Kandahār at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Kandahār is the 2nd warmest (20.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,299 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (244 mm a year). Compared with Ghazni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (333 km away), Kandahār is 9.9 °C warmer in July and 12.6 °C warmer in January, with 168 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.0 °C), averages 26.8 °C more than its coolest, January (6.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 701 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kandahār, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kandahār has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 701 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
