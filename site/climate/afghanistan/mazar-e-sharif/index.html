Mazār-e Sharīf at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Mazār-e Sharīf is the 3rd warmest (18.5 °C year average), the 3rd least sunny (about 2,832 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (215 mm a year). Compared with Balkh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Mazār-e Sharīf is 2.5 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.8 °C), averages 26.5 °C more than its coolest, January (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,177 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mazār-e Sharīf, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mazār-e Sharīf has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,177 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
