Jabal os Saraj weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Jabal os Saraj, Afghanistan (Parwan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Jabal os Saraj at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Afghanistan, Jabal os Saraj is 41st warmest (12.2 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 2,877 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (479 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Charikar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Jabal os Saraj is 1.8 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 24.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,122 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Jabal os Saraj has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,122 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.4 °C. The 10 km area around Jabal os Saraj, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 10.5 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jabal os Saraj itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.118°N, 69.238°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-488-2492), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
