Kunduz at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Kunduz is the least sunny (about 2,687 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Baghlān, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Kunduz is 3.2 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 100 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.1 °C), averages 28.5 °C more than its coolest, January (3.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,288 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kunduz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,288 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
