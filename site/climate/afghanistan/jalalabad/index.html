Jalālābād at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Jalālābād is the warmest (22.5 °C year average) and the 3rd wettest (399 mm a year). Compared with Kabul, the nearest covered town with different numbers (118 km away), Jalālābād is 11.8 °C warmer in June and 13.1 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.0 °C), averages 23.2 °C more than its coolest, January (9.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,083 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jalālābād, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jalālābād has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,083 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
