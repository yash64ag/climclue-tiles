Bagrām at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Bagrām is 8th warmest (14.2 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 2,977 hours of sunshine a year) and 5th wettest (388 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kabul, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Bagrām is 3.1 °C warmer in July and 3.1 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.8 °C), averages 26.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 1,101 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bagrām, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bagrām has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,101 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
