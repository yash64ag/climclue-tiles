Herāt at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Afghanistan, Herāt is 7th warmest (16.4 °C year average), 4th sunniest (about 3,043 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (349 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kandahār, the nearest covered town with different numbers (447 km away), Herāt is 4.8 °C cooler in July and 2.1 °C cooler in January, with 104 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.3 °C), averages 24.1 °C more than its coolest, January (4.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 812 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Herāt, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Herāt has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 812 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.1 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
