Fayzabad weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Fayzabad, Afghanistan (Badakhshan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Fayzabad is warmest in July, with 31.1 °C (88 °F) days and 14.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.5 °C (37 °F) days and -7.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around April (70 mm); August is driest (3 mm).

Fayzabad at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Afghanistan, Fayzabad is the 3rd least sunny (about 2,468 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Bahārak, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Fayzabad is 3.1 °C warmer in July and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.0 °C), averages 25.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,368 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Fayzabad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,368 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fayzabad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.117°N, 70.580°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-468-2505), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
