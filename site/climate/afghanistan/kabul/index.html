Why Kabul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,055 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Kabul climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Kabul?
July, with an average of 23.6 °C (75 °F), day highs around 30.0 °C (86 °F) and night lows around 15.3 °C (60 °F).
What is the coldest month in Kabul?
January, with an average of -3.3 °C (26 °F), day highs around 2.2 °C (36 °F) and night lows around -10.7 °C (13 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Kabul?
March, with 85 mm (3.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 386 mm (15.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Kabul?
August, with 6 mm (0.2 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Kabul?
July, with about 339 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Kabul?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 213 cm (83.7 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in February; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 5 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Kabul?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: May, June, July, August and September.
What type of climate does Kabul have?
Continental, hot dry summer (Köppen Dsa). Hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.2 × 11 km, that contains Kabul’s GeoNames location (34.528°N, 69.172°E; ClimaClue area sq-494-2491), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 4,434,550 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
