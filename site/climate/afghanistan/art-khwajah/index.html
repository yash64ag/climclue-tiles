Ārt Khwājah at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Afghanistan, Ārt Khwājah is 23rd warmest (17.7 °C year average), 49th sunniest (about 2,639 hours of sunshine a year) and 38th wettest (320 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dasht-e Qal‘ah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Ārt Khwājah is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.6 °C), averages 28.3 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 1,332 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ārt Khwājah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,332 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
