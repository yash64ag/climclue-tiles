Jurm at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Afghanistan, Jurm is the 2nd least sunny (about 2,431 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Bahārak, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Jurm is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 21.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,345 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Jurm has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,345 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.9 °C. The 10 km area around Jurm, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 7.5 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jurm itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.865°N, 70.834°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-471-2508), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 23,064, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
