Manama at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Bahrain, Manama is the 2nd warmest (25.9 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 3,311 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (82 mm a year). Compared with Al Muharraq, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Manama is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (33.6 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, January (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Manama, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Manama has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
