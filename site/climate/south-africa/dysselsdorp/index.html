Dysselsdorp at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Dysselsdorp is 156th warmest (17.0 °C year average), 205th sunniest (about 2,901 hours of sunshine a year) and 271st wettest (472 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oudtshoorn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Dysselsdorp is 0.4 °C cooler in February and 0.4 °C cooler in July, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.1 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, July (11.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in July. The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Why Dysselsdorp has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dysselsdorp itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.576°S, 22.444°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1175-2024), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
