Queenstown at a glance

Among the 71 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Africa, Queenstown is the 4th coolest (15.1 °C year average). Compared with Bhisho, the nearest covered town with different numbers (118 km away), Queenstown is 0.9 °C cooler in January and 4.3 °C cooler in June, with 231 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (19.9 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, June (9.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 172 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Queenstown, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Queenstown has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 172 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
