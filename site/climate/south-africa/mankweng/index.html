Mankweng at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Mankweng is 82nd warmest (18.4 °C year average), 169th sunniest (about 3,001 hours of sunshine a year) and 221st wettest (584 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Polokwane, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Mankweng is 0.1 °C cooler in January and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 119 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.6 °C), averages 10.1 °C more than its coolest, July (12.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 378 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 23 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 116% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Mankweng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 378 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mankweng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.884°S, 29.706°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1078-2097), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
