Virginia at a glance

Among the 71 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Africa, Virginia is 33rd warmest (17.1 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 3,199 hours of sunshine a year) and 61st wettest (568 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Welkom, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Virginia is 0.2 °C warmer in January and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.3 °C), averages 14.1 °C more than its coolest, July (9.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 453 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Virginia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Virginia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 453 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
