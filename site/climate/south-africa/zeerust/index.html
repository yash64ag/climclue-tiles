Zeerust at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Zeerust is 74th warmest (18.7 °C year average), 50th sunniest (about 3,286 hours of sunshine a year) and 248th wettest (543 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mafikeng, the nearest covered town with different numbers (57 km away), Zeerust is 0.2 °C cooler in January and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.5 °C), averages 11.7 °C more than its coolest, July (11.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 672 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Zeerust, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Zeerust has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 672 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zeerust itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.537°S, 26.075°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1095-2060), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 19,154, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
