Driekoppies at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Driekoppies is 14th warmest (21.5 °C year average), 210th sunniest (about 2,885 hours of sunshine a year) and 135th wettest (705 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Schoemansdal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Driekoppies is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 38 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.3 °C), averages 8.7 °C more than its coolest, July (16.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 125 km from the sea.

Why Driekoppies has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 125 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Driekoppies itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.698°S, 31.564°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1096-2115), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
