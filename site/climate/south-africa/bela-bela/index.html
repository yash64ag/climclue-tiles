Bela Bela weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bela Bela, South Africa (Limpopo), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bela Bela at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Bela Bela is 59th warmest (19.5 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 3,346 hours of sunshine a year) and 220th wettest (586 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Modimolle, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Bela Bela is 0.4 °C warmer in January and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 29 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.9 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, July (12.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 468 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bela Bela, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bela Bela has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 468 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bela Bela itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.883°S, 28.283°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1088-2082), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 90,210, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
