Ladysmith at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Ladysmith is 100th warmest (17.9 °C year average), 212th sunniest (about 2,881 hours of sunshine a year) and 96th wettest (767 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Estcourt, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Ladysmith is 1.4 °C warmer in January and 1.4 °C warmer in June, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.6 °C), averages 11.1 °C more than its coolest, June (11.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, June, averages 2.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 188 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ladysmith, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ladysmith has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 188 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ladysmith itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.559°S, 29.779°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1125-2097), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
