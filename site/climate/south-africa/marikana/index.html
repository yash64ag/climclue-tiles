Marikana at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Marikana is 68th warmest (19.1 °C year average), 48th sunniest (about 3,294 hours of sunshine a year) and 210th wettest (597 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rustenburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Marikana is 0.1 °C cooler in January and 0.1 °C cooler in June, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.8 °C), averages 11.7 °C more than its coolest, June (12.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 532 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Marikana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Marikana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 532 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Marikana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.699°S, 27.475°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1096-2074), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
