Leslie at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Leslie is 268th warmest (15.1 °C year average), 135th sunniest (about 3,104 hours of sunshine a year) and 106th wettest (755 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lebohang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Leslie is about as warm in January and about as warm in June, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (19.2 °C), averages 10.4 °C more than its coolest, June (8.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in July. The area lies about 381 km from the sea.

Why Leslie has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 381 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Leslie itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.367°S, 28.919°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1103-2089), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
