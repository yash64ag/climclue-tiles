Tekwane at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Tekwane is 42nd warmest (20.4 °C year average), 189th sunniest (about 2,952 hours of sunshine a year) and 125th wettest (720 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mbombela, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Tekwane is 1.1 °C warmer in January and 1.2 °C warmer in July, with 167 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.4 °C), averages 9.4 °C more than its coolest, July (15.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 170 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tekwane, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tekwane has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 170 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tekwane itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.478°S, 31.146°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1094-2111), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,846, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
