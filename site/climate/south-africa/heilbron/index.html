Heilbron at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Heilbron is 239th warmest (15.8 °C year average), 86th sunniest (about 3,206 hours of sunshine a year) and 138th wettest (702 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Koppies, the nearest covered town with different numbers (39 km away), Heilbron is 1.4 °C cooler in January and 0.8 °C cooler in July, with 109 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (20.6 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, July (8.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June and July. The area lies about 415 km from the sea.

Why Heilbron has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 415 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Heilbron itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.281°S, 27.971°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1112-2079), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
