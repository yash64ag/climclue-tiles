Kimberley at a glance

Among the 71 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Africa, Kimberley is the sunniest (about 3,437 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (464 mm a year). Compared with Bloemfontein, the nearest covered town with different numbers (148 km away), Kimberley is 1.8 °C warmer in January and 1.4 °C warmer in June, with 153 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.3 °C), averages 15.5 °C more than its coolest, June (9.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 561 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kimberley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 561 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
