Brits at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Brits is 62nd warmest (19.4 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 3,334 hours of sunshine a year) and 251st wettest (539 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hartbeespoort, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Brits is 1.0 °C warmer in January and 0.6 °C warmer in June, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.1 °C), averages 11.7 °C more than its coolest, June (12.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 502 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Brits, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Brits has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 502 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Brits itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.635°S, 27.780°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1096-2077), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
