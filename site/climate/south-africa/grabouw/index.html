Grabouw at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Grabouw is 255th warmest (15.5 °C year average), 214th sunniest (about 2,876 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (1,285 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Somerset West, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Grabouw is 0.7 °C cooler in February and 1.7 °C cooler in July, with 804 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (19.9 °C), averages 8.6 °C more than its coolest, July (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.7 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Why Grabouw has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Grabouw itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.152°S, 19.015°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1181-1990), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
