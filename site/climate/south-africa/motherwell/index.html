Motherwell at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Motherwell is 81st warmest (18.3 °C year average), 251st sunniest (about 2,721 hours of sunshine a year) and 264th wettest (501 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Despatch, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Motherwell is 0.2 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.2 °C), averages 7.4 °C more than its coolest, July (14.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.4 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Motherwell, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Motherwell has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Motherwell itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.794°S, 25.586°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1177-2055), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
