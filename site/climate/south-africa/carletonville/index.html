Carletonville at a glance

Among the 71 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Africa, Carletonville is 43rd warmest (16.6 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 3,253 hours of sunshine a year) and 53rd wettest (608 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Westonaria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Carletonville is 1.1 °C warmer in January and 0.7 °C warmer in July, with 94 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.1 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, July (9.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 523 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Carletonville, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Carletonville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 523 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
