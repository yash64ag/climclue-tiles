Ga-Malakhe weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ga-Malakhe, South Africa (KwaZulu-Natal), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ga-Malakhe is warmest in February, with 26.5 °C (80 °F) days and 20.3 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 21.1 °C (70 °F) days and 11.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (145 mm); May is driest (38 mm).

Ga-Malakhe at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Ga-Malakhe is 52nd warmest (19.9 °C year average), 289th sunniest (about 2,510 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (1,150 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Margate, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Ga-Malakhe is 0.7 °C cooler in February and 1.3 °C cooler in July, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.4 °C), averages 7.0 °C more than its coolest, July (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.7 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ga-Malakhe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ga-Malakhe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ga-Malakhe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.806°S, 30.335°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1148-2103), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
