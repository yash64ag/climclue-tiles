Amandelbult at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Amandelbult is 20th warmest (21.2 °C year average), 40th sunniest (about 3,321 hours of sunshine a year) and 246th wettest (545 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Northam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Amandelbult is 0.6 °C warmer in January and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (26.0 °C), averages 12.2 °C more than its coolest, July (13.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 566 km from the sea.

Why Amandelbult has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 566 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Amandelbult itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.818°S, 27.297°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1088-2072), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
