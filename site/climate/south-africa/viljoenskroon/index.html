Viljoenskroon weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Viljoenskroon, South Africa (Free State), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Viljoenskroon is warmest in January, with 29.6 °C (85 °F) days and 16.2 °C nights, and coolest in June, with 18.3 °C (65 °F) days and 0.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around December (97 mm); July is driest (4 mm).

Viljoenskroon at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Viljoenskroon is 141st warmest (17.2 °C year average), 43rd sunniest (about 3,313 hours of sunshine a year) and 218th wettest (588 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Orkney, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Viljoenskroon is 0.2 °C cooler in January and 0.5 °C cooler in June, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.9 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, June (9.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 0.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 496 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Viljoenskroon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Viljoenskroon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 496 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Viljoenskroon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.208°S, 26.949°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1112-2069), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
