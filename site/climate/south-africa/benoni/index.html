Benoni at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Benoni is 254th warmest (15.6 °C year average), 130th sunniest (about 3,123 hours of sunshine a year) and 104th wettest (758 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Boksburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Benoni is 0.1 °C cooler in January and about as warm in June, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (19.8 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, June (9.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 1.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 439 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Benoni, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Benoni has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 439 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Benoni itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.188°S, 28.321°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1101-2083), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 605,344, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
