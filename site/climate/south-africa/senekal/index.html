Senekal at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Senekal is 221st warmest (16.1 °C year average), 115th sunniest (about 3,156 hours of sunshine a year) and 206th wettest (605 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Marquard, the nearest covered town with different numbers (43 km away), Senekal is 0.3 °C warmer in January and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.8 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, July (8.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June and July. The area lies about 374 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Senekal, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Senekal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 374 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Senekal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.320°S, 27.621°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1123-2076), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
