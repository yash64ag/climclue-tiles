Pietermaritzburg at a glance

Among the 71 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Africa, Pietermaritzburg is the 5th least sunny (about 2,572 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Mpumalanga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Pietermaritzburg is 0.8 °C cooler in February and 1.6 °C cooler in June, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (21.4 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, June (12.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 78 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Pietermaritzburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 78 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
