Upington weather by month and climate

Upington at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Upington is the sunniest (about 3,655 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (183 mm a year). Compared with Postmasburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (177 km away), Upington is 4.4 °C warmer in January and 2.8 °C warmer in July, with 161 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (28.7 °C), averages 16.4 °C more than its coolest, July (12.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 436 km from the sea.

Why Upington has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 436 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Upington itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.448°S, 21.256°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1124-2012), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 71,373, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
