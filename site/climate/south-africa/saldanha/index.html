Saldanha at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Saldanha is the 5th driest (315 mm a year). Compared with Vredenburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Saldanha is about as warm in February and 0.6 °C warmer in August, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (18.4 °C), averages 4.6 °C more than its coolest, August (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.9 °C (50 °F), in August. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 22 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 119% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Saldanha has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Saldanha itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.012°S, 17.944°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1170-1979), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
