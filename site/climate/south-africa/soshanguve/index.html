Soshanguve at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Soshanguve is 58th warmest (19.5 °C year average), 41st sunniest (about 3,319 hours of sunshine a year) and 172nd wettest (654 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mabopane, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Soshanguve is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.1 °C warmer in June, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.1 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, June (12.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 474 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Soshanguve, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Soshanguve has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 474 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Soshanguve itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.473°S, 28.099°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1094-2080), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 872,309, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
