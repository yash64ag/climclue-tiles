Vredenburg at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Vredenburg is 241st warmest (15.7 °C year average), 155th sunniest (about 3,051 hours of sunshine a year) and 293rd wettest (332 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Saldanha, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Vredenburg is about as warm in February and 0.7 °C cooler in July, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (18.4 °C), averages 5.3 °C more than its coolest, July (13.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in August. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Vredenburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vredenburg itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.907°S, 17.990°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1169-1979), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
