Despatch at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Despatch is 79th warmest (18.3 °C year average), 246th sunniest (about 2,734 hours of sunshine a year) and 278th wettest (451 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kariega, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Despatch is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.3 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, July (14.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Despatch, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Despatch has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Despatch itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.802°S, 25.463°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1178-2054), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
