Calcutta at a glance

Among the 298 towns ClimaClue covers in South Africa, Calcutta is 25th warmest (21.1 °C year average), 163rd sunniest (about 3,013 hours of sunshine a year) and 68th wettest (845 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mkhuhlu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Calcutta is about as warm in January and about as warm in July, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.9 °C), averages 8.9 °C more than its coolest, July (16.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.9 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 184 km from the sea.

Why Calcutta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 184 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Calcutta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.983°S, 31.235°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1089-2112), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 35,864, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
