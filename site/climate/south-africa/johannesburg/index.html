Why Johannesburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 470 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Johannesburg climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Johannesburg?
January, with an average of 19.4 °C (67 °F), day highs around 24.3 °C (76 °F) and night lows around 14.1 °C (57 °F).
What is the coldest month in Johannesburg?
June, with an average of 8.6 °C (47 °F), day highs around 15.7 °C (60 °F) and night lows around 2.6 °C (37 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Johannesburg?
January, with 141 mm (5.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 792 mm (31.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Johannesburg?
July, with 3 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Johannesburg?
August, with about 298 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Johannesburg?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Johannesburg?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: September and October.
What type of climate does Johannesburg have?
Subtropical highland, dry winter (Köppen Cwb). Mild highland climate with wet summers and dry winters. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10.0 × 11 km, that contains Johannesburg’s GeoNames location (26.202°S, 28.044°E; ClimaClue area sq-1102-2080), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 9,418,183 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
