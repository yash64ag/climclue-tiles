Johannesburg at a glance

Among the 71 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Africa, Johannesburg is the 2nd coolest (15.1 °C year average). Compared with Alberton, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Johannesburg is 0.6 °C cooler in January and 0.6 °C cooler in June, with 52 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (19.4 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, June (8.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 470 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Johannesburg, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Johannesburg

For mild weather, the best months to visit Johannesburg are September and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): September and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): January (19.4 °C), February (19.1 °C) and December (18.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (3 mm), August (8 mm) and June (10 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): August (298 h), September (290 h) and July (283 h).

Why Johannesburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 470 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
