Ziniaré weather by month and climate

Ziniaré at a glance

Among the 74 towns ClimaClue covers in Burkina Faso, Ziniaré is 25th warmest (28.5 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 2,949 hours of sunshine a year) and 51st wettest (733 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nioko I, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Ziniaré is 0.1 °C warmer in April and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (33.2 °C), averages 8.7 °C more than its coolest, January (24.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.7 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 773 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ziniaré, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ziniaré has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 773 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ziniaré itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.582°N, 1.298°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-714-1787), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
