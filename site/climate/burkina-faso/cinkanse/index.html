Cinkansé at a glance

Among the 74 towns ClimaClue covers in Burkina Faso, Cinkansé is 49th warmest (28.2 °C year average), 68th sunniest (about 2,781 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (1,006 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bittou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Cinkansé is 0.5 °C cooler in April and 0.3 °C cooler in August, with 108 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (32.3 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, August (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.5 °C (65 °F), in December. The area lies about 580 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cinkansé, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cinkansé has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 580 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cinkansé itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.119°N, 0.010°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-728-1800), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
