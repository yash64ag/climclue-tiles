Gourcy at a glance

Among the 74 towns ClimaClue covers in Burkina Faso, Gourcy is 52nd warmest (28.2 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 3,010 hours of sunshine a year) and 59th wettest (679 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yako, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Gourcy is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 36 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.7 °C), averages 9.2 °C more than its coolest, January (23.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.8 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 892 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gourcy, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gourcy has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 892 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gourcy itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.208°N, 2.359°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-707-1776), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
