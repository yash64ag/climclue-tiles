Orodara at a glance

Among the 74 towns ClimaClue covers in Burkina Faso, Orodara is the coolest (26.1 °C year average), the 5th least sunny (about 2,754 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,174 mm a year). Compared with Karankasso, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Orodara is 1.6 °C cooler in April and 1.1 °C cooler in August, with 174 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.5 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, August (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.3 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 648 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Orodara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Orodara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 648 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Orodara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.981°N, 4.919°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-730-1750), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
