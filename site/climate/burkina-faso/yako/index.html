Yako at a glance

Among the 74 towns ClimaClue covers in Burkina Faso, Yako is 60th warmest (28.0 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,992 hours of sunshine a year) and 57th wettest (714 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gourcy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Yako is 0.2 °C cooler in April and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 36 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (32.4 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.9 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 857 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Yako, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Yako has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 857 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yako itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.959°N, 2.261°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-710-1777), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
