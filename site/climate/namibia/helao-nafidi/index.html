Helao Nafidi at a glance

Among the 18 towns ClimaClue covers in Namibia, Helao Nafidi is 5th warmest (22.9 °C year average), 15th sunniest (about 3,074 hours of sunshine a year) and 5th wettest (530 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ongwediva, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Helao Nafidi is 0.1 °C cooler in October and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 63 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (26.4 °C), averages 8.5 °C more than its coolest, July (18.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in July. The area lies about 407 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Helao Nafidi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Helao Nafidi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 407 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Helao Nafidi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.393°S, 15.891°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1013-1958), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,963, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
