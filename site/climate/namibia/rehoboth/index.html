Rehoboth at a glance

Among the 18 towns ClimaClue covers in Namibia, Rehoboth is the 2nd sunniest (about 3,626 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Windhoek, the nearest covered town with different numbers (84 km away), Rehoboth is 2.3 °C warmer in December and 1.7 °C warmer in July, with 79 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (26.1 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, July (14.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in July. The area lies about 267 km from the sea.

Why Rehoboth has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 267 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rehoboth itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.317°S, 17.090°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1073-1970), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
