Grootfontein at a glance

Among the 18 towns ClimaClue covers in Namibia, Grootfontein is 12th warmest (21.2 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 3,358 hours of sunshine a year) and 4th wettest (541 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tsumeb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (54 km away), Grootfontein is 1.3 °C cooler in November and 1.6 °C cooler in July, with 55 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (24.6 °C), averages 9.6 °C more than its coolest, July (15.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 503 km from the sea.

Why Grootfontein has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 503 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Grootfontein itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.573°S, 18.100°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1035-1981), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
