Windhoek at a glance

Among the 18 towns ClimaClue covers in Namibia, Windhoek is 15th warmest (19.1 °C year average), 4th sunniest (about 3,495 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (309 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Okahandja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (67 km away), Windhoek is 2.2 °C cooler in December and 2.7 °C cooler in July, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (23.8 °C), averages 11.3 °C more than its coolest, July (12.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in July. The area lies about 267 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 2 km from Windhoek measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 1.8 °C (3.2 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Windhoek has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 267 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Windhoek itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.559°S, 17.083°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1065-1970), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 386,219, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
