Rodriguez at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Rodriguez is 481st warmest (25.8 °C year average), 443rd sunniest (about 2,262 hours of sunshine a year) and 49th wettest (3,186 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Malanday, Calabarzon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Rodriguez is 1.6 °C cooler in May and 1.4 °C cooler in January, with 721 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.6 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.3 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 24 km from Rodriguez by up to 2.2 °C (3.9 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Rodriguez has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C. The 10 km area around Rodriguez, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 24.6 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rodriguez itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.760°N, 121.200°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-692-3012), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 134,432, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
