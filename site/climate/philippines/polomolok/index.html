Polomolok at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Polomolok is the 5th driest (1,077 mm a year). Compared with General Santos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Polomolok is 1.6 °C cooler in April and 1.4 °C cooler in July, with 37 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.1 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, July (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.2 °C (68 °F), in January. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 18 km from Polomolok by up to 2.5 °C (4.6 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Polomolok has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Polomolok itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.222°N, 125.064°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-777-3050), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 63,987, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
