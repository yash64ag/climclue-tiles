Iligan City at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Iligan City is 142nd warmest (24.4 °C year average), 140th sunniest (about 2,217 hours of sunshine a year) and 62nd wettest (2,123 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Iligan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Iligan City is 2.4 °C cooler in May and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 560 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (25.3 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.0 °C (68 °F), in February. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Iligan City, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Iligan City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
