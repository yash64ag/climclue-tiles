Bacoor at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Bacoor is 18th warmest (27.6 °C year average), 42nd sunniest (about 2,397 hours of sunshine a year) and 124th wettest (1,772 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cavite City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Bacoor is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 56 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.4 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, January (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.1 °C (74 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 21 km from Bacoor measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (yearly rain and snow: this area has 69% of the station’s). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Bacoor has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
