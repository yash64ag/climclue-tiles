Cavite City at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Cavite City is 40th warmest (27.5 °C year average), 42nd sunniest (about 2,397 hours of sunshine a year) and 108th wettest (1,827 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bacoor, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Cavite City is 0.2 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.1 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, January (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.2 °C (74 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 21 km from Cavite City measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (yearly rain and snow: this area has 71% of the station’s). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Cavite City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
