General Santos at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, General Santos is the driest (1,041 mm a year). Compared with Alabel, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), General Santos is 0.3 °C warmer in April and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 243 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.3 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, July (26.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.3 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 46 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why General Santos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 46 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for General Santos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.113°N, 125.172°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-778-3051), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 722,059, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
