Santa Cruz at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Santa Cruz is 118th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 115th sunniest (about 2,289 hours of sunshine a year) and 45th wettest (2,265 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Los Baños, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Santa Cruz is about as warm in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 154 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.1 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, January (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.0 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santa Cruz, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Santa Cruz

For mild weather, the best months to visit Santa Cruz are January and February.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and February.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (28.1 °C), June (27.8 °C) and April (27.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (57 mm), March (58 mm) and April (63 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (254 h), March (228 h) and May (227 h).

Why Santa Cruz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
