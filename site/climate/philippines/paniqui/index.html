Paniqui at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Paniqui is 150th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 31st sunniest (about 2,578 hours of sunshine a year) and 267th wettest (2,087 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ramos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Paniqui is about as warm in April and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 72 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.3 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, January (25.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.2 °C (68 °F), in January. The area lies about 64 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Paniqui, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Paniqui has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 64 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Paniqui itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.669°N, 120.581°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-683-3005), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
