Manaoag at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Manaoag is 170th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 2,645 hours of sunshine a year) and 109th wettest (2,646 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mangaldan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Manaoag is about as warm in April and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 62 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.0 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, January (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.6 °C (69 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Manaoag, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Manaoag has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Manaoag itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.044°N, 120.486°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-679-3004), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
