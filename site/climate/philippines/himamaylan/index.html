Himamaylan at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Himamaylan is 27th warmest (27.6 °C year average), 120th sunniest (about 2,275 hours of sunshine a year) and 75th wettest (2,028 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kabankalan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Himamaylan is about as warm in April and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.4 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, January (26.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.1 °C (74 °F), in January. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Himamaylan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Himamaylan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Himamaylan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.099°N, 122.871°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-739-3028), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
