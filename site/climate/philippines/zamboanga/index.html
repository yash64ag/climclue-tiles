Zamboanga at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Zamboanga is 108th warmest (27.0 °C year average), 141st sunniest (about 2,211 hours of sunshine a year) and 126th wettest (1,695 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jolo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (152 km away), Zamboanga is 0.5 °C warmer in April and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 468 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.6 °C), averages 0.9 °C more than its coolest, July (26.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 25.5 °C (78 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 1 km from Zamboanga measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 1.4 °C (2.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Zamboanga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zamboanga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.910°N, 122.074°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-770-3020), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
