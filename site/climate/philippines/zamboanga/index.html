Zamboanga at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Zamboanga is 132nd warmest (26.0 °C year average), 141st sunniest (about 2,211 hours of sunshine a year) and 89th wettest (2,000 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jolo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (152 km away), Zamboanga is 0.3 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 637 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.6 °C), averages 0.8 °C more than its coolest, January (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.5 °C (76 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 1 km from Zamboanga measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.4 °C (4.2 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Zamboanga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
