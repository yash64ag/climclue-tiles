Laoag at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Laoag is the sunniest (about 2,721 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Tabuk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (125 km away), Laoag is 1.0 °C warmer in May and 2.6 °C warmer in January, with 408 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.5 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, January (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.8 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 7 km from Laoag measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 140% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Laoag has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Laoag itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.198°N, 120.596°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-658-3005), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
