Mankayan at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Mankayan is the 3rd coolest (21.2 °C year average). Compared with Bontoc, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Mankayan is 1.3 °C cooler in June and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 579 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (22.4 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, January (19.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.2 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 48 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mankayan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mankayan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 48 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 8.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mankayan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.863°N, 120.783°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-671-3007), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
