Rosario at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Rosario is 199th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 420th sunniest (about 2,268 hours of sunshine a year) and 322nd wettest (1,989 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Batangas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Rosario is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 331 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.8 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, January (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rosario, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Rosario has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C. The 10 km area around Rosario, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 26.1 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rosario itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.632°N, 121.221°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-703-3012), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
