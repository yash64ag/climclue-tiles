Cabagan at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Cabagan is 409th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 62nd sunniest (about 2,508 hours of sunshine a year) and 465th wettest (1,609 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tuguegarao, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Cabagan is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 121 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (29.3 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, January (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.5 °C (65 °F), in February. The area lies about 54 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cabagan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Cabagan

For mild weather, the best months to visit Cabagan are January, February and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (29.3 °C), May (29.2 °C) and July (28.5 °C).

Why Cabagan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 54 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cabagan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.428°N, 121.770°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-665-3017), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 25,304, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
