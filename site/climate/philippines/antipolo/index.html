Antipolo at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Antipolo is 180th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 246th sunniest (about 2,333 hours of sunshine a year) and 226th wettest (2,189 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Marikina City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Antipolo is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 54 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.0 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, January (25.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.0 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 32 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 11 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 86% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Antipolo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 32 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Antipolo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.626°N, 121.123°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-693-3011), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 913,712, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
