Santiago at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Santiago is 127th warmest (26.3 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,508 hours of sunshine a year) and 88th wettest (2,010 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ilagan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (63 km away), Santiago is 0.7 °C cooler in June and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 193 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.8 °C), averages 5.7 °C more than its coolest, January (23.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.4 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 79 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santiago, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Santiago

For mild weather, the best months to visit Santiago are January and February.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and February.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (28.8 °C), May (28.4 °C) and July (28.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (68 mm), March (71 mm) and January (78 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (261 h), May (250 h) and March (246 h).

Why Santiago has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 79 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
