Subic at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Subic is 176th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 64th sunniest (about 2,507 hours of sunshine a year) and 77th wettest (2,840 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Castillejos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Subic is 0.1 °C warmer in April and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 129 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.9 °C), averages 2.9 °C more than its coolest, January (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.0 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 75% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Subic has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Subic itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.879°N, 120.232°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-691-3002), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
