Why San Pablo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

San Pablo climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in San Pablo?
May, with an average of 27.9 °C (82 °F), day highs around 30.9 °C (88 °F) and night lows around 24.4 °C (76 °F).
What is the coldest month in San Pablo?
January, with an average of 24.6 °C (76 °F), day highs around 26.5 °C (80 °F) and night lows around 21.8 °C (71 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in San Pablo?
July, with 283 mm (11.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 2,013 mm (79.3 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in San Pablo?
February, with 39 mm (1.5 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in San Pablo?
April, with about 252 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in San Pablo?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in San Pablo?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains San Pablo’s GeoNames location (14.068°N, 121.326°E; ClimaClue area sq-699-3013), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 300,166 (GeoNames).
