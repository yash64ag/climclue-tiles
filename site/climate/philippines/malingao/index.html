Malingao at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Malingao is the 2nd warmest (28.3 °C year average). Compared with Budta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Malingao is 0.2 °C warmer in April and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 49 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.8 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, July (27.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.1 °C (74 °F), in October. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Malingao, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Malingao

No month in Malingao is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are July (27.6 °C, 169 mm), August (27.8 °C, 154 mm) and October (27.9 °C, 162 mm).

Why Malingao has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Malingao itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.161°N, 124.475°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-768-3044), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
