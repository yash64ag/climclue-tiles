Bangued at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Bangued is 404th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 2,711 hours of sunshine a year) and 127th wettest (2,538 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bantay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Bangued is 0.3 °C warmer in April and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 160 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.1 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, January (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.3 °C (69 °F), in January. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bangued, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bangued has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bangued itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.608°N, 120.638°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-663-3006), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 48,331, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
