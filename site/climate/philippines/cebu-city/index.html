Cebu City at a glance

Among the 147 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Philippines, Cebu City is 121st warmest (26.4 °C year average), 100th sunniest (about 2,311 hours of sunshine a year) and 90th wettest (1,989 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mandaue City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Cebu City is 0.6 °C cooler in May and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 381 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.4 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, January (25.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.2 °C (74 °F), in February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cebu City, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cebu City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
