Sibulan at a glance

Among the 517 towns ClimaClue covers in the Philippines, Sibulan is 80th warmest (27.5 °C year average), 493rd sunniest (about 2,210 hours of sunshine a year) and 471st wettest (1,556 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dumaguete, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Sibulan is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 67 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.6 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, January (26.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.4 °C (74 °F), in February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sibulan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sibulan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sibulan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.358°N, 123.285°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-746-3032), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
