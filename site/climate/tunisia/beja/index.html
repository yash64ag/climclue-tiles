Béja at a glance

Among the 126 towns ClimaClue covers in Tunisia, Béja is the 2nd wettest (795 mm a year). Compared with Tabursuq, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Béja is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 257 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.8 °C), averages 16.8 °C more than its coolest, January (10.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.5 °C (42 °F), in February. The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Béja, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Béja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Béja itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.726°N, 9.182°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-472-1891), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
