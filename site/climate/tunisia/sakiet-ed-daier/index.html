Sakiet ed Daier weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Sakiet ed Daier, Tunisia (Sfax Governorate), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Sakiet ed Daier is warmest in August, with 33.3 °C (92 °F) days and 22.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 17.9 °C (64 °F) days and 7.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around October (44 mm); July is driest (1 mm).

Sakiet ed Daier at a glance

Among the 126 towns ClimaClue covers in Tunisia, Sakiet ed Daier is 31st warmest (20.0 °C year average), 26th sunniest (about 3,119 hours of sunshine a year) and 96th wettest (252 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Ain, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Sakiet ed Daier is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, January (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Sakiet ed Daier has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sakiet ed Daier itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.794°N, 10.806°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-492-1908), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
