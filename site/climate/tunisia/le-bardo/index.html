Le Bardo at a glance

Among the 126 towns ClimaClue covers in Tunisia, Le Bardo is 70th warmest (19.1 °C year average), 93rd sunniest (about 2,748 hours of sunshine a year) and 37th wettest (486 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tunis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Le Bardo is 0.2 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 15.9 °C more than its coolest, January (12.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in February. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Le Bardo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Le Bardo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Le Bardo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.811°N, 10.135°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-471-1901), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 71,961, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
