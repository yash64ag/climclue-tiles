La Mohammedia weather by month and climate

La Mohammedia at a glance

Among the 126 towns ClimaClue covers in Tunisia, La Mohammedia is 88th warmest (18.8 °C year average), 75th sunniest (about 2,781 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (471 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fouchana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), La Mohammedia is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 5 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.3 °C), averages 16.0 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in February. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of La Mohammedia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why La Mohammedia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Mohammedia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.674°N, 10.156°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-473-1901), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
