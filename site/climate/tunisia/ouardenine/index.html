Ouardenine weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ouardenine, Tunisia (Monastir Governorate), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ouardenine is warmest in August, with 33.4 °C (92 °F) days and 23.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 16.3 °C (61 °F) days and 7.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around October (55 mm); July is driest (2 mm).

Ouardenine at a glance

Among the 126 towns ClimaClue covers in Tunisia, Ouardenine is 43rd warmest (19.7 °C year average), 53rd sunniest (about 2,946 hours of sunshine a year) and 66th wettest (367 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ouerdanine, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Ouardenine is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.3 °C), averages 16.3 °C more than its coolest, January (12.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ouardenine, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ouardenine has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ouardenine itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.709°N, 10.674°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-482-1906), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
