Mahires at a glance

Among the 126 towns ClimaClue covers in Tunisia, Mahires is 20th warmest (20.2 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 3,146 hours of sunshine a year) and 106th wettest (212 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sidi Abid, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Mahires is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.4 °C), averages 15.8 °C more than its coolest, January (12.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.5 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mahires, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mahires has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mahires itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.528°N, 10.501°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-494-1905), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
