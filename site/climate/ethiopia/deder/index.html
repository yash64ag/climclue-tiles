Deder at a glance

Among the 233 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Deder is 200th warmest (15.8 °C year average), 76th sunniest (about 2,955 hours of sunshine a year) and 65th wettest (1,499 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chelenko, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Deder is 0.4 °C cooler in May and about as warm in December, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (17.0 °C), averages 2.9 °C more than its coolest, December (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.9 °C (43 °F), in December. The area lies about 298 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Deder, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Deder has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 298 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Deder itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.312°N, 41.443°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-746-2214), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 26,900, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
