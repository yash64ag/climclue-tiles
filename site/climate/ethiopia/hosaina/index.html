Why Hosa’ina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 713 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Hosa’ina climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Hosa’ina?
March, with an average of 17.6 °C (64 °F), day highs around 23.4 °C (74 °F) and night lows around 11.3 °C (52 °F).
What is the coldest month in Hosa’ina?
July, with an average of 14.4 °C (58 °F), day highs around 18.4 °C (65 °F) and night lows around 10.7 °C (51 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Hosa’ina?
August, with 223 mm (8.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,375 mm (54.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Hosa’ina?
December, with 24 mm (0.9 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Hosa’ina?
December, with about 292 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Hosa’ina?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Hosa’ina?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, October, November and December.
What type of climate does Hosa’ina have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Hosa’ina’s GeoNames location (7.550°N, 37.854°E; ClimaClue area sq-764-2178), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 188,200 (GeoNames).
