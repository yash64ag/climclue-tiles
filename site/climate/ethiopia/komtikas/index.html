Komtikas at a glance

Among the 233 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Komtikas is 80th warmest (20.2 °C year average), 192nd sunniest (about 2,840 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (2,197 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mīzan Teferī, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Komtikas is 0.4 °C warmer in March and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 83 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (21.8 °C), averages 2.7 °C more than its coolest, July (19.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in December. The area lies about 956 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Komtikas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Komtikas

For mild weather, the best months to visit Komtikas are January, February and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and December.

Why Komtikas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 956 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.7 °C. The 10 km area around Komtikas, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.1 °C a year, 1.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Komtikas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.958°N, 35.557°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-770-2155), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 29,400, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
