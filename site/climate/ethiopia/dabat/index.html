Dabat at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Dabat is 196th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 70th sunniest (about 2,963 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (1,893 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Debark’, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Dabat is 1.4 °C warmer in April and 1.3 °C warmer in August, with 1,354 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (19.2 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, August (13.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 354 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dabat, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dabat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 354 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dabat itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.984°N, 37.765°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-710-2177), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 28,300, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
