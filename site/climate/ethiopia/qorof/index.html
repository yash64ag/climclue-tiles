Qorof at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Qorof is 15th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 30th sunniest (about 3,074 hours of sunshine a year) and 229th wettest (219 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Waal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Qorof is 0.1 °C warmer in September and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (28.5 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.4 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 274 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qorof, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Qorof has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 274 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qorof itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.582°N, 46.433°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-774-2264), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
