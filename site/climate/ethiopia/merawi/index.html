Mer’āwī at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Mer’āwī is 124th warmest (18.7 °C year average), 83rd sunniest (about 2,950 hours of sunshine a year) and 72nd wettest (1,459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gurbetē, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Mer’āwī is 0.2 °C cooler in April and about as warm in July, with 116 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (21.6 °C), averages 4.9 °C more than its coolest, July (16.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.9 °C (48 °F), in December. The area lies about 524 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mer’āwī, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mer’āwī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 524 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mer’āwī itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.417°N, 37.167°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-725-2171), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
