Gambēla at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Gambēla is 11th warmest (28.3 °C year average), 117th sunniest (about 2,920 hours of sunshine a year) and 137th wettest (1,066 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dembī Dolo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (39 km away), Gambēla is 10.5 °C warmer in March and 9.1 °C warmer in July, with 745 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (31.5 °C), averages 5.6 °C more than its coolest, July (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.5 °C (69 °F), in November. The area lies about 980 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gambēla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gambēla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 980 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gambēla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.250°N, 34.583°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-757-2145), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
