Neefkuceliye at a glance

Among the 233 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Neefkuceliye is 25th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 3,083 hours of sunshine a year) and 216th wettest (255 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Bahay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Neefkuceliye is 0.1 °C cooler in March and 0.3 °C cooler in November, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.0 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, November (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.7 °C (66 °F), in January. The area lies about 372 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Neefkuceliye, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Neefkuceliye has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 372 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Neefkuceliye itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.555°N, 45.390°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-774-2253), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
