Kercha at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Kercha is 187th warmest (16.2 °C year average), 225th sunniest (about 2,805 hours of sunshine a year) and 53rd wettest (1,589 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hagere Maryam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Kercha is 1.8 °C cooler in March and 1.6 °C cooler in July, with 483 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (18.1 °C), averages 3.7 °C more than its coolest, July (14.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.1 °C (47 °F), in December. The area lies about 809 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kercha, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kercha has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 809 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kercha itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.780°N, 38.409°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-782-2184), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
