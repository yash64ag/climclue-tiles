Hadero at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Hadero is 86th warmest (20.0 °C year average), 173rd sunniest (about 2,858 hours of sunshine a year) and 89th wettest (1,364 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shinshicho, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Hadero is 1.6 °C warmer in March and 1.4 °C warmer in July, with 132 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (22.0 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, July (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in December. The area lies about 751 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hadero, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hadero has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 751 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C. The 10 km area around Hadero, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.4 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hadero itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.200°N, 37.667°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-767-2176), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
