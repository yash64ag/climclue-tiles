‘Alemaya at a glance

Among the 233 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, ‘Alemaya is 170th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 73rd sunniest (about 2,960 hours of sunshine a year) and 134th wettest (1,094 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Harar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), ‘Alemaya is 1.2 °C cooler in March and 1.4 °C cooler in December, with 136 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (18.5 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, December (15.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in December. The area lies about 265 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of ‘Alemaya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why ‘Alemaya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 265 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for ‘Alemaya itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.393°N, 42.010°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-746-2220), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 63,700, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
