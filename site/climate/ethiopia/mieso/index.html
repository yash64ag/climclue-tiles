Mī’ēso at a glance

Among the 233 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Mī’ēso is 40th warmest (22.9 °C year average), 41st sunniest (about 3,015 hours of sunshine a year) and 171st wettest (841 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Āsbe Teferī, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Mī’ēso is 4.5 °C warmer in June and 3.0 °C warmer in December, with 175 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (24.7 °C), averages 4.1 °C more than its coolest, December (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.1 °C (56 °F), in December. The area lies about 352 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mī’ēso, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mī’ēso has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 352 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mī’ēso itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.235°N, 40.756°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-747-2207), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
