Sek’ot’a at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in Ethiopia, Sek’ot’a is 165th warmest (17.6 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 2,980 hours of sunshine a year) and 157th wettest (930 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Korem, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Sek’ot’a is 1.8 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C cooler in August, with 47 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (20.3 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, August (16.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in December. The area lies about 302 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sek’ot’a, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sek’ot’a has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 302 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sek’ot’a itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.631°N, 39.035°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-713-2190), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
