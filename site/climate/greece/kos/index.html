Kos at a glance

Among the 117 towns ClimaClue covers in Greece, Kos is the sunniest (about 3,234 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Rhodes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (98 km away), Kos is 1.0 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C cooler in February, with 164 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.1 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, February (12.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kos, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C. The 10 km area around Kos, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.4 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.893°N, 27.288°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-471-2072), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
