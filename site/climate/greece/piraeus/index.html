Piraeus at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Greece, Piraeus is the 2nd warmest (18.1 °C year average). Compared with Kallithéa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Piraeus is 0.7 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 18.3 °C more than its coolest, January (9.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 117% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Piraeus has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
