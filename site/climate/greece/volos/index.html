Volos at a glance

Among the 117 towns ClimaClue covers in Greece, Volos is 70th warmest (17.1 °C year average), 86th sunniest (about 2,687 hours of sunshine a year) and 35th wettest (564 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Néa Ionía, Thessaly, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Volos is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.2 °C), averages 17.4 °C more than its coolest, January (8.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Volos, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Volos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Volos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.369°N, 22.948°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-446-2029), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
