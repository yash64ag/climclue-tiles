Irákleion at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Greece, Irákleion is the warmest (18.9 °C year average), the 3rd least sunny (about 2,767 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (629 mm a year). Compared with Kallithéa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (319 km away), Irákleion is 2.0 °C cooler in July and 2.0 °C warmer in February, with 221 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.1 °C), averages 13.9 °C more than its coolest, February (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in February. The area lies about 21 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Irákleion has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
