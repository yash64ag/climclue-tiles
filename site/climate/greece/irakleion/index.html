Irákleion at a glance

Among the 117 towns ClimaClue covers in Greece, Irákleion is 13th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 81st sunniest (about 2,767 hours of sunshine a year) and 22nd wettest (629 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rethymno, the nearest covered town with different numbers (60 km away), Irákleion is 0.5 °C cooler in July and about as warm in February, with 77 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.8 °C), averages 13.8 °C more than its coolest, February (12.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.8 °C (48 °F), in February. The area lies about 21 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Irákleion measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 136% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Irákleion has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Irákleion itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.328°N, 25.143°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-486-2051), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 137,154, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
