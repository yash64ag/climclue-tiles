Néa Mákri at a glance

Among the 117 towns ClimaClue covers in Greece, Néa Mákri is 20th warmest (18.5 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,868 hours of sunshine a year) and 63rd wettest (432 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gérakas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Néa Mákri is 1.3 °C warmer in July and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.5 °C), averages 18.9 °C more than its coolest, January (9.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Néa Mákri, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Néa Mákri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.9 °C. The 10 km area around Néa Mákri, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.2 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Néa Mákri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.087°N, 23.976°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-459-2039), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
