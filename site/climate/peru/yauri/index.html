Yauri at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Yauri is 129th warmest (6.9 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 2,730 hours of sunshine a year) and 49th wettest (930 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sicuani, the nearest covered town with different numbers (61 km away), Yauri is 1.2 °C cooler in November and 1.8 °C cooler in June, with 47 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (8.2 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, June (4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in April, May, June, July, August, September and October. The area lies about 252 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Yauri, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Yauri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Low sun and long winters keep it cold; even the warmest month stays below 10 °C, too cool for trees. Cold air holds little moisture, so precipitation is usually light. About 252 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yauri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.791°S, 71.410°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-987-1085), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
