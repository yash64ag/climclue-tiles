Tambopata at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Tambopata is 18th warmest (24.7 °C year average), 59th sunniest (about 2,297 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (2,442 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Puerto Maldonado, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Tambopata is 0.2 °C cooler in December and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 172 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (25.5 °C), averages 2.7 °C more than its coolest, July (22.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.4 °C (62 °F), in July. The area lies about 571 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tambopata, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tambopata has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 571 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tambopata itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.672°S, 69.355°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-966-1106), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
