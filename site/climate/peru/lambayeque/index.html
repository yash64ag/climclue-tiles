Lambayeque at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Lambayeque is 47th warmest (21.8 °C year average), 50th sunniest (about 2,411 hours of sunshine a year) and 95th wettest (74 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chiclayo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Lambayeque is 0.1 °C warmer in February and about as warm in August, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.6 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, August (19.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.9 °C (59 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lambayeque, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lambayeque has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lambayeque itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.701°S, 79.906°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-907-1000), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
