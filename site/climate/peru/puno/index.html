Puno at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Puno is the coolest (7.7 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,864 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,407 mm a year). Compared with Juliaca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Puno is 1.2 °C cooler in November and 0.8 °C cooler in June, with 362 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (9.4 °C), averages 3.7 °C more than its coolest, June (5.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in May, June, July and August. The area lies about 231 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Puno, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Puno has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Low sun and long winters keep it cold; even the warmest month stays below 10 °C, too cool for trees. Cold air holds little moisture, so precipitation is usually light. About 231 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
