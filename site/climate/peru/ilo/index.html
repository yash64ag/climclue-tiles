Ilo at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Ilo is the 4th driest (21 mm a year). Compared with San Antonio, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Ilo is 2.3 °C warmer in February and 5.8 °C cooler in August, with 87 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (21.9 °C), averages 8.1 °C more than its coolest, August (13.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.1 °C (48 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ilo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ilo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ilo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.632°S, 71.341°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1016-1086), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
