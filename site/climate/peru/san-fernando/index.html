San Fernando at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, San Fernando is the 2nd warmest (26.1 °C year average). Compared with Pucallpa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), San Fernando is about as warm in October and about as warm in July, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (26.6 °C), averages 1.2 °C more than its coolest, July (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.3 °C (67 °F), in July. The area lies about 445 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Fernando, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Fernando has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 445 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Fernando itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.398°S, 74.538°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-923-1054), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 67,844, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
