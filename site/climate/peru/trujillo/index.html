Trujillo at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Trujillo is 10th warmest (19.6 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 1,933 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (69 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chimbote, the nearest covered town with different numbers (117 km away), Trujillo is 0.5 °C cooler in February and about as warm in August, with 28 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.1 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, August (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.0 °C (59 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Trujillo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Trujillo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
