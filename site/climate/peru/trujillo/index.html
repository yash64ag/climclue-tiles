Trujillo at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Trujillo is 66th warmest (19.7 °C year average), 87th sunniest (about 1,933 hours of sunshine a year) and 92nd wettest (76 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Moche, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Trujillo is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in August, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.3 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, August (17.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.9 °C (57 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Trujillo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Trujillo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

What is the coldest month in Trujillo?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Trujillo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.116°S, 79.030°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-921-1009), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,067,700, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
