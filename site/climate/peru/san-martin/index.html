San Martin at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, San Martin is the 4th warmest (24.0 °C year average) and the 5th sunniest (about 2,595 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Sullana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), San Martin is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C cooler in August, with 31 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.4 °C), averages 6.2 °C more than its coolest, August (21.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.9 °C (62 °F), in August. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Martin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Martin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
