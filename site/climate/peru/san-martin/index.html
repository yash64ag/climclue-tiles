San Martin at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, San Martin is 30th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 2,595 hours of sunshine a year) and 73rd wettest (179 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Piura, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), San Martin is 0.1 °C cooler in March and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.3 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, August (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.1 °C (59 °F), in August. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Martin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Martin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Martin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.186°S, 80.669°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-891-993), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 130,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
