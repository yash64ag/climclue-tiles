Ayacucho at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Ayacucho is 19th warmest (12.3 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,408 hours of sunshine a year) and 9th wettest (757 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Huancayo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (162 km away), Ayacucho is 2.4 °C warmer in December and 1.4 °C warmer in July, with 274 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (13.4 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, July (10.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 219 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ayacucho, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ayacucho has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 219 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
