Lima weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Lima, Peru (Lima Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Lima at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Lima is the least sunny (about 1,505 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Breña, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Lima is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.2 °C warmer in August, with 5 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.6 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, August (16.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.4 °C (56 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lima, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lima has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Lima climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Lima?
February, with an average of 22.6 °C (73 °F), day highs around 26.5 °C (80 °F) and night lows around 18.6 °C (65 °F).
What is the coldest month in Lima?
August, with an average of 16.3 °C (61 °F), day highs around 19.2 °C (67 °F) and night lows around 13.4 °C (56 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Lima?
February, with 5 mm (0.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 38 mm (1.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Lima?
May, with 2 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Lima?
March, with about 212 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Lima?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time to visit Lima?
For mild weather (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November and December. Climate only, from 1981–2010 averages: crowds and prices are not considered.
How warm is the sea in Lima?
The nearby open sea averages 21.2 °C (70 °F) in February, its warmest month, and 16.3 °C (61 °F) in September. It is 20 °C (68 °F) or warmer in January, February and March. 1981–2010 averages (ERA5); beaches and shallow bays are often a little warmer in summer.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lima itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.043°S, 77.028°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-960-1029), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 7,737,002, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
