Lima at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Lima is the least sunny (about 1,505 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (35 mm a year). Compared with San Francisco De Borja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Lima is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.8 °C cooler in August, with 21 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.6 °C), averages 6.4 °C more than its coolest, August (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.8 °C (59 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lima, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lima has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
