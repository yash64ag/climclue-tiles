Why Lima has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Lima climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Lima?
February, with an average of 22.6 °C (73 °F), day highs around 24.8 °C (77 °F) and night lows around 21.1 °C (70 °F).
What is the coldest month in Lima?
August, with an average of 16.1 °C (61 °F), day highs around 18.2 °C (65 °F) and night lows around 14.8 °C (59 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Lima?
February, with 4 mm (0.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 35 mm (1.4 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Lima?
May, with 2 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Lima?
March, with about 212 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Lima?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Lima?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November and December.
What type of climate does Lima have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Lima’s GeoNames location (12.043°S, 77.028°W; ClimaClue area sq-960-1029), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 7,737,002 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
