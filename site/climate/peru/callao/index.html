Callao at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Callao is the least sunny (about 1,505 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (23 mm a year). Compared with Lima, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Callao is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.3 °C cooler in August, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.4 °C), averages 6.6 °C more than its coolest, August (15.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.8 °C (59 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Callao, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Callao has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
