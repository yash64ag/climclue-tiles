Huánuco at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Huánuco is 16th warmest (15.2 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,133 hours of sunshine a year) and 10th wettest (637 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Huaraz, the nearest covered town with different numbers (148 km away), Huánuco is 7.1 °C warmer in November and 6.5 °C warmer in July, with 302 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (15.8 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, July (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.8 °C (48 °F), in July. The area lies about 198 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Huánuco, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Huánuco has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 198 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
