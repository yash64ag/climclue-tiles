Marcavelica at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Marcavelica is 27th warmest (24.0 °C year average), 35th sunniest (about 2,512 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (218 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sullana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Marcavelica is about as warm in March and 0.2 °C cooler in August, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.4 °C), averages 6.4 °C more than its coolest, August (21.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.3 °C (60 °F), in August. The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Marcavelica, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Marcavelica has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Marcavelica itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.878°S, 80.705°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-888-992), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
