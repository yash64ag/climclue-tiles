Satipo at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Satipo is 31st warmest (23.9 °C year average), 93rd sunniest (about 1,904 hours of sunshine a year) and 15th wettest (1,762 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Martín de Pangoa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Satipo is 0.3 °C warmer in October and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 131 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (24.5 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, July (22.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in July. The area lies about 274 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Satipo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Satipo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 274 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-952-1053), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
