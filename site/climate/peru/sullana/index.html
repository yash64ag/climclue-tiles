Sullana at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Sullana is the 3rd warmest (24.3 °C year average). Compared with Piura, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Sullana is about as warm in March and 0.4 °C warmer in August, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.4 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, August (21.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.8 °C (62 °F), in August. The area lies about 66 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sullana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sullana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 66 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
