Chincha Alta at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Chincha Alta is 60th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 112th sunniest (about 1,758 hours of sunshine a year) and 115th wettest (43 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Clemente, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Chincha Alta is about as warm in February and 0.6 °C warmer in August, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.4 °C), averages 5.7 °C more than its coolest, August (17.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.6 °C (56 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chincha Alta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chincha Alta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chincha Alta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.410°S, 76.132°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-974-1038), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
