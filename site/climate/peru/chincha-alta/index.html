Chincha Alta at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Chincha Alta is the 2nd driest (32 mm a year). Compared with Ica, the nearest covered town with different numbers (86 km away), Chincha Alta is 1.6 °C cooler in February and 2.2 °C cooler in August, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.4 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, August (17.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.0 °C (59 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chincha Alta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chincha Alta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
