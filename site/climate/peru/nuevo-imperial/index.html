Nuevo Imperial at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Nuevo Imperial is 63rd warmest (19.9 °C year average), 126th sunniest (about 1,608 hours of sunshine a year) and 101st wettest (53 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Imperial, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Nuevo Imperial is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in August, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.2 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, August (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.8 °C (55 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Nuevo Imperial has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nuevo Imperial itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.075°S, 76.317°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-970-1036), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
