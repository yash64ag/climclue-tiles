Cajamarca at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Cajamarca is the 4th wettest (1,235 mm a year). Compared with Trujillo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (121 km away), Cajamarca is 4.4 °C cooler in August and 5.3 °C cooler in October, with 1,166 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (12.7 °C), averages 0.5 °C more than its coolest, October (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 133 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cajamarca, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cajamarca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 133 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
