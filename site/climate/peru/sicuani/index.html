Sicuani at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, Sicuani is 127th warmest (8.2 °C year average), 21st sunniest (about 2,623 hours of sunshine a year) and 52nd wettest (883 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yauri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (61 km away), Sicuani is 1.2 °C warmer in November and 1.6 °C warmer in July, with 47 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (9.4 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, July (6.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in May, June, July, August and September. The area lies about 311 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sicuani, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sicuani has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Low sun and long winters keep it cold; even the warmest month stays below 10 °C, too cool for trees. Cold air holds little moisture, so precipitation is usually light. About 311 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sicuani itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.269°S, 71.226°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-982-1087), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 33,575, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
