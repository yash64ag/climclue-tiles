Arequipa at a glance

Among the 24 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Peru, Arequipa is the sunniest (about 3,178 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Puno, the nearest covered town with different numbers (173 km away), Arequipa is 6.3 °C warmer in October and 7.1 °C warmer in July, with 1,199 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (15.3 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, July (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 104 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Arequipa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Arequipa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 104 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
