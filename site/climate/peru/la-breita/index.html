La Breita at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, La Breita is 22nd warmest (24.3 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 2,536 hours of sunshine a year) and 63rd wettest (493 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Talara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (56 km away), La Breita is 1.6 °C warmer in April and 2.9 °C warmer in August, with 340 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.0 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, August (22.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.8 °C (64 °F), in August. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of La Breita, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why La Breita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Breita itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.257°S, 80.886°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-882-991), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
