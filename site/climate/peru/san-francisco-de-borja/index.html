San Francisco De Borja at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Peru, San Francisco De Borja is 87th warmest (18.9 °C year average), 115th sunniest (about 1,713 hours of sunshine a year) and 114th wettest (43 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Isidro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), San Francisco De Borja is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.4 °C), averages 6.2 °C more than its coolest, August (16.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in August. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Why San Francisco De Borja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-960-1030), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
