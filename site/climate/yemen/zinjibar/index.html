Zinjibār weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Zinjibār, Yemen (Abyan Governorate), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Zinjibār is warmest in June, with 36.2 °C (97 °F) days and 27.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 27.6 °C (82 °F) days and 20.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (17 mm); November is driest (2 mm).

Zinjibār at a glance

Among the 25 towns ClimaClue covers in Yemen, Zinjibār is the 3rd sunniest (about 3,114 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (96 mm a year). Compared with Aden, the nearest covered town with different numbers (54 km away), Zinjibār is 1.5 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.0 °C), averages 7.9 °C more than its coolest, January (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.6 °C (69 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Zinjibār, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Zinjibār has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zinjibār itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.129°N, 45.381°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-708-2253), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
