Laḩij at a glance

Among the 25 towns ClimaClue covers in Yemen, Laḩij is the 4th sunniest (about 3,113 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Aden, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Laḩij is 3.2 °C warmer in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 44 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.4 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.1 °C (68 °F), in January. The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Laḩij, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Laḩij

For mild weather, the best months to visit Laḩij are January, February and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (33.4 °C), June (32.9 °C) and August (32.5 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (2 mm), December (4 mm) and June (5 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (310 h), October (293 h) and November (278 h).

Why Laḩij has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Laḩij itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.057°N, 44.882°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-709-2248), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 23,375, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
