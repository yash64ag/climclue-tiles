Aden weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Aden, Yemen, from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Aden is warmest in June, with 34.5 °C (94 °F) days and 26.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 27.4 °C (81 °F) days and 21.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (12 mm); June is driest (2 mm).

Aden at a glance

Among the 25 towns ClimaClue covers in Yemen, Aden is the 2nd sunniest (about 3,132 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (85 mm a year). Compared with Laḩij, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Aden is 2.4 °C cooler in June and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 44 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (30.5 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, January (24.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.0 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Aden, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Aden are January, February and December.

Why Aden has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Aden itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.780°N, 45.039°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-712-2250), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,079,670, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
