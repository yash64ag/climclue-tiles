Maun at a glance

Among the 25 towns ClimaClue covers in Botswana, Maun is the warmest (23.3 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 3,304 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (414 mm a year). Compared with Ghanzi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (265 km away), Maun is 1.8 °C warmer in October and 2.0 °C warmer in July, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.3 °C), averages 10.6 °C more than its coolest, July (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.4 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 979 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Maun, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Maun has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 979 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Maun itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.983°S, 23.417°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1039-2034), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 85,350, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
