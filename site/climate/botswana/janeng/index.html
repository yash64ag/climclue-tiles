Janeng at a glance

Among the 25 towns ClimaClue covers in Botswana, Janeng is the 2nd coolest (18.4 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 3,285 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (515 mm a year). Compared with Lobatse, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Janeng is 0.8 °C cooler in January and 0.8 °C cooler in July, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.5 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, July (11.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 723 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Janeng, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Janeng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 723 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Janeng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.417°S, 25.550°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1094-2055), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,853, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
