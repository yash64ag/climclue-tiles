Mahalapye at a glance

Among the 25 towns ClimaClue covers in Botswana, Mahalapye is the least sunny (about 3,098 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th wettest (482 mm a year). Compared with Palapye, the nearest covered town with different numbers (70 km away), Mahalapye is 0.4 °C cooler in January and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 90 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.8 °C), averages 11.4 °C more than its coolest, July (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.5 °C (42 °F), in July. The area lies about 673 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Mahalapye has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 673 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mahalapye itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.104°S, 26.814°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1071-2068), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
