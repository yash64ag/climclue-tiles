Araçagi at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Araçagi is 596th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 350th sunniest (about 2,577 hours of sunshine a year) and 1914th wettest (898 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guarabira, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Araçagi is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 61 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.0 °C), averages 2.7 °C more than its coolest, July (24.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.9 °C (68 °F), in August. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Araçagi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Araçagi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Araçagi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.853°S, 35.381°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-908-1446), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
