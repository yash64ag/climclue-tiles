Itaquiraí weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Itaquiraí, Brazil (Mato Grosso do Sul), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Itaquiraí is warmest in January, with 30.9 °C (88 °F) days and 20.7 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 24.2 °C (76 °F) days and 11.3 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 66 mm in July to 199 mm in January.

Itaquiraí at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Itaquiraí is 1485th warmest (22.3 °C year average), 1177th sunniest (about 2,231 hours of sunshine a year) and 598th wettest (1,719 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Naviraí, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Itaquiraí is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.3 °C cooler in July, with 192 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.8 °C), averages 8.1 °C more than its coolest, July (17.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.3 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 635 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Itaquiraí, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Itaquiraí has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 635 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Itaquiraí itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.474°S, 54.185°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1074-1258), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
