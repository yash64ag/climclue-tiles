Rio Negro at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Rio Negro is the least sunny (about 1,138 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Mafra, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Rio Negro is 0.1 °C warmer in January and about as warm in July, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.7 °C), averages 8.2 °C more than its coolest, July (13.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 120 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rio Negro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Rio Negro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 120 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rio Negro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.106°S, 49.798°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1101-1302), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 34,411, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
