Rio de Janeiro at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Rio de Janeiro is 125th warmest (23.8 °C year average), 217th sunniest (about 2,091 hours of sunshine a year) and 276th wettest (1,368 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Niterói, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Rio de Janeiro is 0.3 °C cooler in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 55 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.9 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, July (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.4 °C (63 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rio de Janeiro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Rio de Janeiro

For mild weather, the best months to visit Rio de Janeiro are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (26.9 °C), January (26.4 °C) and March (26.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (48 mm), July (55 mm) and June (60 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (197 h), March (194 h) and August (191 h).

Why Rio de Janeiro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
