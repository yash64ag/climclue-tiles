Cacoal at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Cacoal is 701st warmest (25.5 °C year average), 1882nd sunniest (about 1,967 hours of sunshine a year) and 444th wettest (1,847 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pimenta Bueno, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Cacoal is about as warm in September and 0.1 °C warmer in June, with 67 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (26.5 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, June (24.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,241 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cacoal, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Cacoal

For mild weather, the best months to visit Cacoal are May, June and July.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June and July.
  • Warmest (average temperature): September (26.5 °C), October (26.5 °C) and August (26.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (10 mm), June (16 mm) and August (26 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (235 h), June (213 h) and August (202 h).

Why Cacoal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,241 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cacoal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.439°S, 61.447°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-954-1185), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 85,893, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
