Alto Paraíso at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Alto Paraíso is 623rd warmest (25.8 °C year average), 2065th sunniest (about 1,804 hours of sunshine a year) and 169th wettest (2,209 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ariquemes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Alto Paraíso is 0.1 °C cooler in September and about as warm in June, with 193 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (26.8 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, June (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.7 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,224 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alto Paraíso, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Alto Paraíso has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 1,224 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alto Paraíso itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.723°S, 63.310°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-937-1166), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
