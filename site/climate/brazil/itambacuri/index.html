Itambacuri at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Itambacuri is 1134th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 1940th sunniest (about 1,918 hours of sunshine a year) and 1846th wettest (967 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Teófilo Otoni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Itambacuri is about as warm in February and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.4 °C), averages 5.4 °C more than its coolest, July (21.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.7 °C (58 °F), in July. The area lies about 216 km from the sea.

Why Itambacuri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 216 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Itambacuri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.031°S, 41.685°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1020-1383), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
