São Francisco do Sul at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, São Francisco do Sul is 1687th warmest (21.4 °C year average), 2158th sunniest (about 1,702 hours of sunshine a year) and 225th wettest (2,093 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Itapoá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), São Francisco do Sul is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 100 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.5 °C), averages 8.5 °C more than its coolest, July (17.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.2 °C (56 °F), in July. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of São Francisco do Sul, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why São Francisco do Sul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for São Francisco do Sul itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.243°S, 48.638°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1102-1313), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
