Sarzedo at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Sarzedo is 1667th warmest (21.5 °C year average), 1117th sunniest (about 2,251 hours of sunshine a year) and 1169th wettest (1,453 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mário Campos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Sarzedo is 0.2 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 15 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.8 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, July (18.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.4 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 334 km from the sea.

Why Sarzedo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 334 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sarzedo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.035°S, 44.145°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1040-1358), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
