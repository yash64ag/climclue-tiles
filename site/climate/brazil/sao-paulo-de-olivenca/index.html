São Paulo de Olivença at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, São Paulo de Olivença is 646th warmest (25.7 °C year average), 2064th sunniest (about 1,806 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (2,942 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santo Antônio do Içá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (108 km away), São Paulo de Olivença is 0.4 °C cooler in October and 0.3 °C cooler in July, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (26.3 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, July (24.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.2 °C (68 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,215 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why São Paulo de Olivença has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 1,215 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for São Paulo de Olivença itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.378°S, 68.872°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-873-1111), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
