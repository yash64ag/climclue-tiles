Itajuípe at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Itajuípe is 1131st warmest (23.9 °C year average), 912th sunniest (about 2,325 hours of sunshine a year) and 1232nd wettest (1,427 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Uruçuca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Itajuípe is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 98 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.6 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, July (21.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Itajuípe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Itajuípe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Itajuípe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.678°S, 39.375°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-986-1406), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
