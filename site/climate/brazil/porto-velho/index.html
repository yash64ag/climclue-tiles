Porto Velho at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Porto Velho is 48th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 310th sunniest (about 1,732 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (2,344 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ji Paraná, the nearest covered town with different numbers (319 km away), Porto Velho is about as warm in September and 0.5 °C warmer in June, with 474 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (26.9 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, June (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.9 °C (71 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,261 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Porto Velho, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Porto Velho has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 1,261 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
