Paulínia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Paulínia is 1584th warmest (21.8 °C year average), 525th sunniest (about 2,482 hours of sunshine a year) and 860th wettest (1,579 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hortolândia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Paulínia is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 49 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.5 °C), averages 6.4 °C more than its coolest, July (18.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.3 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 167 km from the sea.

Why Paulínia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 167 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Paulínia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.761°S, 47.154°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1067-1328), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
