Boa Vista at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Boa Vista is 9th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,685 hours of sunshine a year) and 157th wettest (1,565 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manaus, the nearest covered town with different numbers (662 km away), Boa Vista is 2.3 °C warmer in March and 1.2 °C cooler in July, with 841 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.8 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, July (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.4 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 528 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Boa Vista, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Boa Vista has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 528 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
