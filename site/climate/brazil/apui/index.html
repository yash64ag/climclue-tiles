Apuí at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Apuí is 676th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 2069th sunniest (about 1,800 hours of sunshine a year) and 104th wettest (2,363 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manicoré, the nearest covered town with different numbers (219 km away), Apuí is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.7 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, January (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.9 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,054 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Apuí has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 1,054 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Apuí itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.197°S, 59.891°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-911-1201), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
