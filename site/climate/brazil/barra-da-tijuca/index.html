Barra da Tijuca at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Barra da Tijuca is 133rd warmest (23.7 °C year average), 213th sunniest (about 2,096 hours of sunshine a year) and 321st wettest (1,091 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rio de Janeiro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Barra da Tijuca is 0.4 °C cooler in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 219 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.8 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, July (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.5 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Barra da Tijuca, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Barra da Tijuca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
