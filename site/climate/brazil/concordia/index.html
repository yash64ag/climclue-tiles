Concórdia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Concórdia is 2148th warmest (18.7 °C year average), 1254th sunniest (about 2,208 hours of sunshine a year) and 184th wettest (2,164 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Seara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Concórdia is about as warm in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.1 °C), averages 9.5 °C more than its coolest, July (13.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in July. The area lies about 331 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Concórdia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Concórdia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 331 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Concórdia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.234°S, 52.028°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1112-1279), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
