Imbé at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Imbé is 2065th warmest (19.4 °C year average), 1410th sunniest (about 2,163 hours of sunshine a year) and 1251st wettest (1,423 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tramandaí, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Imbé is about as warm in February and about as warm in July, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.8 °C), averages 9.0 °C more than its coolest, July (14.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.2 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Imbé, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Imbé has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Imbé itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.975°S, 50.128°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1139-1298), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 26,824, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
