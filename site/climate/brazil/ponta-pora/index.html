Ponta Porã weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ponta Porã, Brazil (Mato Grosso do Sul), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ponta Porã is warmest in January, with 29.1 °C (84 °F) days and 19.5 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 23.7 °C (75 °F) days and 11.3 °C nights. Most rain falls around November (217 mm); July is driest (47 mm).

Ponta Porã at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Ponta Porã is 1663rd warmest (21.5 °C year average), 704th sunniest (about 2,402 hours of sunshine a year) and 472nd wettest (1,827 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Amambai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (81 km away), Ponta Porã is 0.9 °C cooler in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 63 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.3 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, July (17.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.3 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 826 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ponta Porã has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 826 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ponta Porã itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.536°S, 55.726°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1065-1242), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
