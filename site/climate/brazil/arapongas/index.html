Arapongas at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Arapongas is 290th warmest (19.8 °C year average), 262nd sunniest (about 1,958 hours of sunshine a year) and 54th wettest (1,922 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Apucarana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Arapongas is 0.3 °C warmer in January and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.6 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, July (15.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.6 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 398 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Arapongas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Arapongas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 398 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1074-1285), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
