São Paulo at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, São Paulo is 273rd warmest (20.0 °C year average), 297th sunniest (about 1,780 hours of sunshine a year) and 226th wettest (1,459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with São Caetano do Sul, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), São Paulo is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 330 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.1 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of São Paulo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit São Paulo

For mild weather, the best months to visit São Paulo are April, May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April, May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (23.1 °C), January (22.8 °C) and March (22.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (33 mm), July (50 mm) and June (55 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (166 h), August (159 h) and May (158 h).

Why São Paulo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
