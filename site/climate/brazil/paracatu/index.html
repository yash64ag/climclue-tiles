Paracatu at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Paracatu is 1324th warmest (23.2 °C year average), 1239th sunniest (about 2,213 hours of sunshine a year) and 935th wettest (1,549 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vazante, the nearest covered town with different numbers (85 km away), Paracatu is about as warm in October and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 129 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (25.0 °C), averages 4.5 °C more than its coolest, July (20.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.5 °C (56 °F), in July. The area lies about 699 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Paracatu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Paracatu

For mild weather, the best months to visit Paracatu are April, May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April, May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): October (25.0 °C), February (24.4 °C) and January (24.2 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (5 mm), June (8 mm) and August (16 mm).

Why Paracatu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 699 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Paracatu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.222°S, 46.875°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1012-1331), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
