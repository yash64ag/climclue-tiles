Why Curitiba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 92 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Curitiba climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Curitiba?
February, with an average of 20.9 °C (70 °F), day highs around 24.9 °C (77 °F) and night lows around 17.8 °C (64 °F).
What is the coldest month in Curitiba?
July, with an average of 13.4 °C (56 °F), day highs around 17.8 °C (64 °F) and night lows around 9.8 °C (50 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Curitiba?
January, with 206 mm (8.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,535 mm (60.4 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Curitiba?
August, with 76 mm (3.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Curitiba?
January, with about 153 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Curitiba?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Curitiba?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: April.
What type of climate does Curitiba have?
Oceanic (Köppen Cfb). Mild all year, with rain in every season. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Curitiba’s GeoNames location (25.428°S, 49.273°W; ClimaClue area sq-1094-1307), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 1,948,626 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
