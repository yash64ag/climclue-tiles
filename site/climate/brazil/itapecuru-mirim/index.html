Itapecuru Mirim at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Itapecuru Mirim is 89th warmest (27.6 °C year average), 756th sunniest (about 2,379 hours of sunshine a year) and 568th wettest (1,743 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cantanhede, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Itapecuru Mirim is 0.3 °C cooler in November and about as warm in March, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (29.2 °C), averages 2.7 °C more than its coolest, March (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.3 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 79 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Itapecuru Mirim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Itapecuru Mirim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 79 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Itapecuru Mirim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.392°S, 44.359°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-873-1356), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
