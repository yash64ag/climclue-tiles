Esmeraldas weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Esmeraldas, Brazil (Minas Gerais), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Esmeraldas is warmest in February, with 29.7 °C (85 °F) days and 18.5 °C nights, and coolest in June, with 25.9 °C (79 °F) days and 11.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around December (327 mm); July is driest (8 mm).

Esmeraldas at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Esmeraldas is 1577th warmest (21.9 °C year average), 580th sunniest (about 2,455 hours of sunshine a year) and 1209th wettest (1,437 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Juatuba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Esmeraldas is about as warm in February and 0.1 °C warmer in June, with 98 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.1 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, June (18.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.5 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 368 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Esmeraldas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Esmeraldas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 368 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Esmeraldas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.762°S, 44.314°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1037-1356), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 85,598, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
