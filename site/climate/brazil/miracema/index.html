Miracema at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Miracema is 1210th warmest (23.7 °C year average), 1504th sunniest (about 2,141 hours of sunshine a year) and 1601st wettest (1,251 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santo Antônio de Pádua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Miracema is 0.2 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 82 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.7 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, July (20.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.7 °C (57 °F), in July. The area lies about 113 km from the sea.

Why Miracema has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 113 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Miracema itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.412°S, 42.197°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1054-1378), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
