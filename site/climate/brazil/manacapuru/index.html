Manacapuru at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Manacapuru is 25th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 298th sunniest (about 1,779 hours of sunshine a year) and 10th wettest (2,489 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manaus, the nearest covered town with different numbers (70 km away), Manacapuru is about as warm in October and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 60 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.8 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, February (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.8 °C (75 °F), in June. The area lies about 954 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Manacapuru, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Manacapuru has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 954 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Manacapuru itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.300°S, 60.621°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-872-1193), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
