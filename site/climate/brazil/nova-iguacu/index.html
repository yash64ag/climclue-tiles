Nova Iguaçu at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Nova Iguaçu is 135th warmest (23.5 °C year average), 207th sunniest (about 2,104 hours of sunshine a year) and 274th wettest (1,376 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Belford Roxo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Nova Iguaçu is 0.5 °C cooler in February and 0.5 °C cooler in July, with 81 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.9 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, July (20.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in July. The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nova Iguaçu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nova Iguaçu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
