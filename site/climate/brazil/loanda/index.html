Loanda at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Loanda is 1456th warmest (22.5 °C year average), 1732nd sunniest (about 2,057 hours of sunshine a year) and 928th wettest (1,550 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rosana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (39 km away), Loanda is 1.5 °C cooler in January and 1.3 °C cooler in July, with 240 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.4 °C), averages 7.1 °C more than its coolest, July (18.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 572 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Loanda, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Loanda has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 572 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Loanda itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.923°S, 53.137°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1069-1268), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 23,242, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
