Santana do Araguaia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Santana do Araguaia is 477th warmest (26.3 °C year average), 1713th sunniest (about 2,066 hours of sunshine a year) and 607th wettest (1,715 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Maria das Barreiras, the nearest covered town with different numbers (87 km away), Santana do Araguaia is 0.5 °C cooler in September and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 322 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (27.9 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.5 °C (65 °F), in July. The area lies about 819 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Santana do Araguaia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 819 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santana do Araguaia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.335°S, 50.350°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-933-1296), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
