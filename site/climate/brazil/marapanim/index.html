Marapanim at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Marapanim is 367th warmest (26.7 °C year average), 1288th sunniest (about 2,198 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (2,744 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Curuçá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Marapanim is 0.1 °C warmer in November and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 125 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (27.5 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, February (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Marapanim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Marapanim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Marapanim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.718°S, 47.700°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-847-1323), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
