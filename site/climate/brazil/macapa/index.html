Macapá at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Macapá is 33rd warmest (26.7 °C year average), 152nd sunniest (about 2,227 hours of sunshine a year) and 15th wettest (2,399 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Macapá is about as warm in October and about as warm in February, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (28.1 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, February (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.5 °C (74 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Macapá, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Macapá has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
