Ipojuca at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Ipojuca is 76th warmest (25.3 °C year average), 42nd sunniest (about 2,555 hours of sunshine a year) and 34th wettest (2,055 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cabo de Santo Agostinho, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Ipojuca is about as warm in February and about as warm in August, with 96 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.4 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, August (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ipojuca, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ipojuca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
