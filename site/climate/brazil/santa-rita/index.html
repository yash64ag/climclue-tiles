Santa Rita at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Santa Rita is 60th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 28th sunniest (about 2,592 hours of sunshine a year) and 254th wettest (1,411 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with João Pessoa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Santa Rita is 0.1 °C warmer in March and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 155 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.9 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, July (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.0 °C (72 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santa Rita, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santa Rita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
