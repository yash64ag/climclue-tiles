Mogi Guaçu at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Mogi Guaçu is 188th warmest (21.9 °C year average), 131st sunniest (about 2,315 hours of sunshine a year) and 160th wettest (1,554 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Araras, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Mogi Guaçu is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.4 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, July (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.6 °C (55 °F), in July. The area lies about 196 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mogi Guaçu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mogi Guaçu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 196 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
