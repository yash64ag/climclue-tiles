Americana weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Americana, Brazil (São Paulo), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Americana at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Americana is 1591st warmest (21.8 °C year average), 674th sunniest (about 2,413 hours of sunshine a year) and 905th wettest (1,559 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nova Odessa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Americana is about as warm in February and about as warm in July, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.6 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.3 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 176 km from the sea.

Why Americana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 176 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Americana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.739°S, 47.331°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1067-1326), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
