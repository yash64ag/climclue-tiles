Quatro Barras at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Quatro Barras is 2230th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 2213th sunniest (about 1,627 hours of sunshine a year) and 316th wettest (1,969 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Campina Grande do Sul, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Quatro Barras is about as warm in February and about as warm in July, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (20.9 °C), averages 7.4 °C more than its coolest, July (13.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.1 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 78 km from the sea.

Why Quatro Barras has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 78 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Quatro Barras itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.366°S, 49.077°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1093-1309), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
