Alagoa Grande at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Alagoa Grande is 720th warmest (25.5 °C year average), 521st sunniest (about 2,484 hours of sunshine a year) and 1955th wettest (860 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Areia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Alagoa Grande is 2.5 °C warmer in February and 2.3 °C warmer in July, with 157 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.7 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, July (23.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in August. The area lies about 91 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alagoa Grande, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Alagoa Grande has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 91 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alagoa Grande itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.042°S, 35.627°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-910-1443), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
