Marabá at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Marabá is 31st warmest (26.8 °C year average), 271st sunniest (about 1,887 hours of sunshine a year) and 53rd wettest (1,927 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Parauapebas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (114 km away), Marabá is 0.4 °C warmer in September and 0.5 °C warmer in February, with 363 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (28.1 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, February (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.5 °C (73 °F), in June. The area lies about 368 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Marabá, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Marabá has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 368 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Marabá itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.381°S, 49.132°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-893-1308), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
