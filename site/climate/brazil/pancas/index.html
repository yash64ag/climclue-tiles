Pancas at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Pancas is 1295th warmest (23.4 °C year average), 1402nd sunniest (about 2,165 hours of sunshine a year) and 1626th wettest (1,232 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baixo Guandu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Pancas is 1.4 °C cooler in February and 1.5 °C cooler in July, with 312 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.9 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, July (20.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.6 °C (58 °F), in July. The area lies about 116 km from the sea.

Why Pancas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 116 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pancas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.225°S, 40.851°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1032-1391), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
