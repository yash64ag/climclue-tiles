Itaporã at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Itaporã is 1313th warmest (23.2 °C year average), 690th sunniest (about 2,406 hours of sunshine a year) and 1202nd wettest (1,442 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dourados, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Itaporã is 0.4 °C warmer in March and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 156 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.3 °C), averages 7.5 °C more than its coolest, July (18.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 764 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Itaporã, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Itaporã

For mild weather, the best months to visit Itaporã are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.

Why Itaporã has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 764 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Itaporã itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.079°S, 54.789°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1060-1252), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 24,137, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
