Campo Grande at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Campo Grande is 146th warmest (23.3 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 2,478 hours of sunshine a year) and 227th wettest (1,459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dourados, the nearest covered town with different numbers (198 km away), Campo Grande is 0.8 °C cooler in December and 1.6 °C warmer in July, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (24.9 °C), averages 4.8 °C more than its coolest, July (20.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.8 °C (60 °F), in July. The area lies about 866 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Campo Grande, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Campo Grande has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 866 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
