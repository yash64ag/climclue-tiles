Guapimirim at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Guapimirim is 1138th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 1674th sunniest (about 2,082 hours of sunshine a year) and 392nd wettest (1,898 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with São Pedro, Rio de Janeiro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Guapimirim is 5.2 °C warmer in February and 5.1 °C warmer in July, with 378 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.2 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (20.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.5 °C (60 °F), in July. The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guapimirim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Guapimirim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Guapimirim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.537°S, 42.982°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1065-1370), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
