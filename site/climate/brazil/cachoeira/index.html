Cachoeira at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Cachoeira is 865th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 1654th sunniest (about 2,089 hours of sunshine a year) and 1702nd wettest (1,145 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Muritiba, Bahia (12.6°S 39.0°W), the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Cachoeira is 0.6 °C warmer in February and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 120 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.7 °C), averages 4.1 °C more than its coolest, July (22.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.1 °C (65 °F), in August. The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cachoeira, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cachoeira has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cachoeira itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.601°S, 38.966°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-966-1410), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
