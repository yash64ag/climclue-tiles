Patos at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Patos is 39th warmest (28.1 °C year average), 59th sunniest (about 2,798 hours of sunshine a year) and 2120th wettest (683 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Luzia, Paraíba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (43 km away), Patos is 0.9 °C warmer in December and 0.9 °C warmer in July, with 66 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (29.5 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, July (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.2 °C (68 °F), in August. The area lies about 240 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Patos, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Patos

No month in Patos is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are June (26.8 °C, 25 mm), July (26.3 °C, 11 mm) and August (26.7 °C, 4 mm).

Why Patos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 240 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Patos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.024°S, 37.280°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-910-1427), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 92,575, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
