Campina Grande at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Campina Grande is 105th warmest (24.0 °C year average), 103rd sunniest (about 2,357 hours of sunshine a year) and 340th wettest (825 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Cruz do Capibaribe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (88 km away), Campina Grande is 0.8 °C cooler in February and 0.5 °C cooler in July, with 290 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.3 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, July (22.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.8 °C (66 °F), in August. The area lies about 119 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Campina Grande, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Campina Grande has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 119 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
