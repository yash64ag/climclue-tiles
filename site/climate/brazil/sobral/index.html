Sobral at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Sobral is 7th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 77th sunniest (about 2,401 hours of sunshine a year) and 334th wettest (974 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Itapipoca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (88 km away), Sobral is 1.1 °C warmer in November and 0.2 °C warmer in April, with 27 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (28.8 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, April (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.4 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 100 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sobral, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sobral has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 100 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
