Tucano at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Tucano is the 3rd driest (456 mm a year). Compared with Ribeira do Pombal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Tucano is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 97 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.6 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, July (23.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.0 °C (64 °F), in August. The area lies about 167 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tucano, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Tucano

For mild weather, the best months to visit Tucano are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (27.6 °C), January (27.5 °C) and March (27.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): October (17 mm), September (17 mm) and August (24 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (227 h), November (221 h) and December (221 h).

Why Tucano has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 167 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tucano itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.958°S, 38.791°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-949-1412), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 48,736, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
