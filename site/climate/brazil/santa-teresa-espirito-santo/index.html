Santa Teresa, Espírito Santo at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Santa Teresa, Espírito Santo is 1890th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 1285th sunniest (about 2,199 hours of sunshine a year) and 1092nd wettest (1,484 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Maria de Jetibá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Santa Teresa, Espírito Santo is 0.1 °C warmer in February and about as warm in July, with 223 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.0 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, July (17.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 53 km from the sea.

Best time to visit Santa Teresa, Espírito Santo

For mild weather, the best months to visit Santa Teresa, Espírito Santo are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (23.0 °C), January (22.7 °C) and March (22.6 °C).

Why Santa Teresa, Espírito Santo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 53 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa Teresa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.936°S, 40.600°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1039-1394), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,808, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
