Piúma at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Piúma is 1151st warmest (23.9 °C year average), 868th sunniest (about 2,336 hours of sunshine a year) and 1510th wettest (1,321 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Anchieta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Piúma is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 57 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.5 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, July (21.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.7 °C (62 °F), in July. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Piúma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Piúma itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.838°S, 40.722°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1048-1392), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
