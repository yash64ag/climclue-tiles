Pitimbu at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Pitimbu is 601st warmest (25.9 °C year average), 247th sunniest (about 2,630 hours of sunshine a year) and 534th wettest (1,773 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Caaporã, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Pitimbu is about as warm in March and 0.2 °C warmer in August, with 82 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.9 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.2 °C (68 °F), in September. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pitimbu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pitimbu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pitimbu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.471°S, 34.809°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-914-1451), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
