Ceará-Mirim at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Ceará-Mirim is 555th warmest (26.0 °C year average), 137th sunniest (about 2,720 hours of sunshine a year) and 1731st wettest (1,109 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Extremoz, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Ceará-Mirim is 0.4 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in September, with 261 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.5 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, September (24.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.0 °C (70 °F), in August. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ceará-Mirim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Ceará-Mirim

For mild weather, the best months to visit Ceará-Mirim are August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (27.5 °C), January (27.4 °C) and December (26.9 °C).

Why Ceará-Mirim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ceará-Mirim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.634°S, 35.426°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-896-1445), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 79,115, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
