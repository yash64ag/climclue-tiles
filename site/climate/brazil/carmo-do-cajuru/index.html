Carmo do Cajuru at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Carmo do Cajuru is 1602nd warmest (21.8 °C year average), 846th sunniest (about 2,347 hours of sunshine a year) and 1458th wettest (1,343 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Divinópolis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Carmo do Cajuru is 0.3 °C cooler in February and 0.3 °C cooler in June, with 86 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.9 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, June (18.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.1 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 330 km from the sea.

Why Carmo do Cajuru has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 330 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1041-1352), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
