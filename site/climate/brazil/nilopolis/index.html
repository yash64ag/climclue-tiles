Nilópolis weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nilópolis, Brazil (Rio de Janeiro), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nilópolis is warmest in February, with 32.2 °C (90 °F) days and 22.4 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 25.6 °C (78 °F) days and 15.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (224 mm); August is driest (36 mm).

Nilópolis at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Nilópolis is 1143rd warmest (23.9 °C year average), 1616th sunniest (about 2,100 hours of sunshine a year) and 1461st wettest (1,343 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with São João de Meriti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Nilópolis is about as warm in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 93 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.3 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, July (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.6 °C (60 °F), in July. The area lies about 30 km from the sea.

Why Nilópolis has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 30 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nilópolis itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.808°S, 43.414°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1068-1365), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
