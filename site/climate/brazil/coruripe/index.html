Coruripe at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Coruripe is 600th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 370th sunniest (about 2,570 hours of sunshine a year) and 1439th wettest (1,351 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Teotônio Vilela, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Coruripe is 0.4 °C warmer in March and 1.1 °C warmer in August, with 51 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.2 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, August (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.6 °C (69 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Coruripe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Coruripe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.126°S, 36.176°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-941-1438), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
