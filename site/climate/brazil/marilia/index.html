Marília at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Marília is 176th warmest (22.2 °C year average), 140th sunniest (about 2,282 hours of sunshine a year) and 244th wettest (1,427 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Assis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (69 km away), Marília is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 147 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.4 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, July (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.9 °C (57 °F), in July. The area lies about 379 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Marília, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Marília has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 379 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
