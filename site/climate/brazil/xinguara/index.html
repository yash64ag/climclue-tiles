Xinguara at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Xinguara is 574th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 2024th sunniest (about 1,841 hours of sunshine a year) and 663rd wettest (1,683 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rio Maria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Xinguara is 0.6 °C cooler in September and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (27.2 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, February (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.0 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 560 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Xinguara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Xinguara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 560 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Xinguara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.098°S, 49.944°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-910-1300), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
