Rio Verde at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Rio Verde is 1320th warmest (23.3 °C year average), 713th sunniest (about 2,399 hours of sunshine a year) and 989th wettest (1,527 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Helena de Goiás, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Rio Verde is 1.2 °C cooler in October and 1.0 °C cooler in June, with 70 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (24.7 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, June (20.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.9 °C (57 °F), in July. The area lies about 844 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rio Verde, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Rio Verde

For mild weather, the best months to visit Rio Verde are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.

Why Rio Verde has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 844 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rio Verde itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.798°S, 50.928°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1017-1290), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 241,494, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
