Candeias at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Candeias is 797th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 1421st sunniest (about 2,159 hours of sunshine a year) and 699th wettest (1,665 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Madre de Deus, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Candeias is 0.2 °C cooler in March and 0.3 °C cooler in August, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.6 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, August (23.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.6 °C (67 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Candeias, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Candeias has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Candeias itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.668°S, 38.551°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-966-1414), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
