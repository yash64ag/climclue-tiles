Santa Cruz das Palmeiras at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Santa Cruz das Palmeiras is 1520th warmest (22.1 °C year average), 1503rd sunniest (about 2,142 hours of sunshine a year) and 1079th wettest (1,490 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tambaú, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Santa Cruz das Palmeiras is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.2 °C warmer in June, with 85 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.4 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, June (18.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.6 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 259 km from the sea.

Best time to visit Santa Cruz das Palmeiras

For mild weather, the best months to visit Santa Cruz das Palmeiras are April, May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April, May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (24.4 °C), January (24.1 °C) and March (23.9 °C).

Why Santa Cruz das Palmeiras has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 259 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa Cruz das Palmeiras itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.827°S, 47.249°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1058-1327), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 28,864, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
