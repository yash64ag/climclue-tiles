Brazlândia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Brazlândia is 1657th warmest (21.6 °C year average), 870th sunniest (about 2,335 hours of sunshine a year) and 988th wettest (1,527 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Águas Lindas de Goiás, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Brazlândia is 0.1 °C warmer in September and 0.1 °C warmer in June, with 131 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (23.2 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, June (19.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.3 °C (56 °F), in July. The area lies about 915 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Brazlândia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Brazlândia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 915 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Brazlândia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.679°S, 48.197°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-996-1318), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
