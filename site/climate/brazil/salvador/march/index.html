Salvador weather in March

Average March weather in Salvador, Brazil: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In March, Salvador averages 30.1 °C (86 °F) days and 22.9 °C (73 °F) nights, with 130 mm (5.1 in) of rain over the month and about 214 hours of sunshine.

How March compares in Salvador

March is the warmest month, the 5th wettest of the 12 and the 4th sunniest of the 12 in Salvador. Compared with February: 0.1 °C warmer, 49 mm more precipitation and 13 hours more sunshine. April is 0.5 °C cooler than March on average.

Salvador in March: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Salvador in March?
In March, Salvador averages a day high of 30.1 °C (86 °F) and a night low of 22.9 °C (73 °F), with 130 mm (5.1 in) of precipitation and about 214 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is March a good time to visit Salvador?
March is not one of Salvador’s mild months; those are September and October, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Salvador in March?
130 mm (5.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making March the 5th wettest of the 12. The wettest month is April (287 mm), the driest January (60 mm).
Does it snow in Salvador in March?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for March in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Salvador get in March?
About 214 hours of bright sunshine, the 4th sunniest of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Salvador in March?
About 28.2 °C (83 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, warm for swimming; its warmest month is March (28.2 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Salvador in March?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes for humid air and a rain jacket or umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
