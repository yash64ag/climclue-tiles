Salvador at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Salvador is 80th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 123rd sunniest (about 2,331 hours of sunshine a year) and 92nd wettest (1,761 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lauro de Freitas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Salvador is 0.3 °C cooler in March and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 77 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.4 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, August (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.6 °C (73 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Salvador, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Salvador has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
