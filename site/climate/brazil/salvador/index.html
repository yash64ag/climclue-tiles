Salvador at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Salvador is 787th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 892nd sunniest (about 2,331 hours of sunshine a year) and 604th wettest (1,717 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vera Cruz, Bahia (13.0°S 38.6°W), the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Salvador is 0.2 °C cooler in March and about as warm in August, with 116 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.5 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, August (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.5 °C (69 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Salvador, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Salvador

For mild weather, the best months to visit Salvador are September and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): September and October.

Why Salvador has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Salvador itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.976°S, 38.491°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-969-1415), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
