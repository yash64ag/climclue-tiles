Why Salvador has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Salvador climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Salvador?
March, with an average of 26.4 °C (80 °F), day highs around 27.6 °C (82 °F) and night lows around 25.4 °C (78 °F).
What is the coldest month in Salvador?
August, with an average of 23.6 °C (75 °F), day highs around 24.5 °C (76 °F) and night lows around 22.6 °C (73 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Salvador?
April, with 293 mm (11.5 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,761 mm (69.3 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Salvador?
January, with 62 mm (2.5 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Salvador?
January, with about 229 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Salvador?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Salvador?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: September and October.
What type of climate does Salvador have?
Tropical rainforest (Köppen Af). Hot and wet all year, with no real dry season. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Salvador’s GeoNames location (12.976°S, 38.491°W; ClimaClue area sq-969-1415), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 2,711,840 (GeoNames).
