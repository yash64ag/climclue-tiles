Palmas at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Palmas is 17th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 93rd sunniest (about 2,372 hours of sunshine a year) and 97th wettest (1,739 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Araguaína, the nearest covered town with different numbers (331 km away), Palmas is 1.1 °C warmer in September and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 70 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (29.3 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, January (26.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in July. The area lies about 907 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Palmas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Palmas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 907 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
