Palmas at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Palmas is 24th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 93rd sunniest (about 2,372 hours of sunshine a year) and 78th wettest (1,782 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Araguaína, the nearest covered town with different numbers (331 km away), Palmas is 0.9 °C warmer in September and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (29.0 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, January (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.9 °C (70 °F), in July. The area lies about 907 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Palmas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Palmas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 907 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Palmas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.167°S, 48.328°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-941-1316), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
