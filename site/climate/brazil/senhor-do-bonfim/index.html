Senhor do Bonfim at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Senhor do Bonfim is 1261st warmest (23.5 °C year average), 1351st sunniest (about 2,180 hours of sunshine a year) and 1987th wettest (832 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Campo Formoso, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Senhor do Bonfim is 0.4 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 121 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.2 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, July (20.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in August. The area lies about 319 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Senhor do Bonfim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Senhor do Bonfim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 319 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Senhor do Bonfim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.461°S, 40.189°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-944-1398), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
