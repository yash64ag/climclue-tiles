Vilhena at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Vilhena is 138th warmest (23.4 °C year average), 239th sunniest (about 2,031 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (1,996 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ji Paraná, the nearest covered town with different numbers (285 km away), Vilhena is 2.7 °C cooler in September and 2.6 °C cooler in June, with 125 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (24.3 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, June (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,272 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Vilhena, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Vilhena has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,272 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
