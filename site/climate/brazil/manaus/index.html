Manaus at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Manaus is 19th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 299th sunniest (about 1,767 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (2,406 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manacapuru, the nearest covered town with different numbers (70 km away), Manaus is 0.1 °C warmer in October and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 206 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.9 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, February (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.8 °C (75 °F), in June. The area lies about 887 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Manaus, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Manaus has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 887 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
