Petrolândia weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Petrolândia, Brazil (Pernambuco), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Petrolândia is warmest in December, with 34.7 °C (94 °F) days and 22.5 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 28.4 °C (83 °F) days and 18.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (99 mm); October is driest (8 mm).

Petrolândia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Petrolândia is 426th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 184th sunniest (about 2,672 hours of sunshine a year) and 2269th wettest (469 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tacaratu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Petrolândia is 1.9 °C warmer in December and 1.7 °C warmer in July, with 235 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (28.6 °C), averages 5.1 °C more than its coolest, July (23.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.2 °C (65 °F), in August. The area lies about 259 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Petrolândia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Petrolândia

For mild weather, the best months to visit Petrolândia are June, July and August.

Why Petrolândia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 259 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Petrolândia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.979°S, 38.219°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-929-1417), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
