Mangaratiba weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Mangaratiba, Brazil (Rio de Janeiro), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Mangaratiba is warmest in February, with 31.9 °C (89 °F) days and 21.5 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 26.6 °C (80 °F) days and 15.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (204 mm); August is driest (45 mm).

Mangaratiba at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Mangaratiba is 1249th warmest (23.5 °C year average), 2062nd sunniest (about 1,807 hours of sunshine a year) and 1411th wettest (1,364 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rio Claro, Rio de Janeiro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Mangaratiba is 2.2 °C warmer in February and 2.6 °C warmer in July, with 424 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.7 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, July (20.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.0 °C (59 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Mangaratiba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mangaratiba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.960°S, 44.041°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1069-1359), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
