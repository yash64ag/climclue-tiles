Apucarana at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Apucarana is 2040th warmest (19.6 °C year average), 1904th sunniest (about 1,950 hours of sunshine a year) and 343rd wettest (1,942 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Arapongas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Apucarana is 0.3 °C cooler in January and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.3 °C), averages 6.6 °C more than its coolest, July (15.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.9 °C (50 °F), in July. The area lies about 391 km from the sea.

Why Apucarana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 391 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Apucarana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.551°S, 51.461°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1075-1285), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
