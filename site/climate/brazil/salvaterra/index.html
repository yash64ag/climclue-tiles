Salvaterra at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Salvaterra is 379th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 1029th sunniest (about 2,280 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (3,085 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Soure, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Salvaterra is 0.1 °C cooler in October and about as warm in February, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.5 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, February (25.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in February. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Salvaterra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-847-1314), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
