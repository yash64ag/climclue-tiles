Presidente Médici at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Presidente Médici is 667th warmest (25.7 °C year average), 1913th sunniest (about 1,945 hours of sunshine a year) and 513th wettest (1,794 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ji Paraná, the nearest covered town with different numbers (33 km away), Presidente Médici is 0.3 °C cooler in September and 0.3 °C cooler in June, with 46 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (26.8 °C), averages 1.9 °C more than its coolest, June (24.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.0 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,222 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Presidente Médici has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,222 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Presidente Médici itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.175°S, 61.901°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-951-1180), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
