Jundiaí at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Jundiaí is 1916th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 1685th sunniest (about 2,077 hours of sunshine a year) and 1348th wettest (1,389 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Várzea Paulista, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Jundiaí is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.2 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (16.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.9 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 115 km from the sea.

Why Jundiaí has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 115 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jundiaí itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.186°S, 46.884°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1071-1331), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
