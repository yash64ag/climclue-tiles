Quixeré at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Quixeré is 41st warmest (28.2 °C year average), 63rd sunniest (about 2,795 hours of sunshine a year) and 2016th wettest (804 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Limoeiro do Norte, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Quixeré is 0.2 °C cooler in December and 0.1 °C cooler in April, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (28.9 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, April (27.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.4 °C (72 °F), in August. The area lies about 71 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Quixeré, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Quixeré has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 71 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Quixeré itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.074°S, 37.989°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-890-1420), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
