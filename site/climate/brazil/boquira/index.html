Boquira at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Boquira is 890th warmest (24.8 °C year average), 186th sunniest (about 2,671 hours of sunshine a year) and 1940th wettest (876 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Macaúbas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Boquira is 0.6 °C warmer in October and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 97 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (26.4 °C), averages 3.7 °C more than its coolest, July (22.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.0 °C (61 °F), in July. The area lies about 425 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Boquira, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Boquira

For mild weather, the best months to visit Boquira are May, June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): October (26.4 °C), February (25.8 °C) and November (25.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (1 mm), August (2 mm) and June (4 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): August (262 h), July (241 h) and May (238 h).

Why Boquira has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 425 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.7 °C. The 10 km area around Boquira, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 23.6 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Boquira itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.823°S, 42.731°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-968-1372), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 21,497, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
