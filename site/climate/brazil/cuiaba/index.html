Cuiabá at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Cuiabá is 51st warmest (26.0 °C year average), 109th sunniest (about 2,355 hours of sunshine a year) and 146th wettest (1,593 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Várzea Grande, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Cuiabá is 0.3 °C cooler in October and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 119 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.6 °C), averages 4.2 °C more than its coolest, July (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.0 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,361 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cuiabá, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cuiabá has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,361 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
