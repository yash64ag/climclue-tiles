Holambra at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Holambra is 1592nd warmest (21.8 °C year average), 613th sunniest (about 2,441 hours of sunshine a year) and 795th wettest (1,613 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Terra Preta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Holambra is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.4 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, July (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.2 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 174 km from the sea.

Why Holambra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 174 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Holambra itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.633°S, 47.056°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1066-1329), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
