Jaguaribe at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Jaguaribe is 7th warmest (28.8 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 2,906 hours of sunshine a year) and 2025th wettest (794 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pereiro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Jaguaribe is 3.1 °C warmer in November and 2.7 °C warmer in April, with 244 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (30.1 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, April (27.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.6 °C (73 °F), in August. The area lies about 189 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jaguaribe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jaguaribe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 189 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jaguaribe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.891°S, 38.622°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-898-1413), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 33,726, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
