Indaiatuba at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Indaiatuba is 229th warmest (21.2 °C year average), 95th sunniest (about 2,370 hours of sunshine a year) and 244th wettest (1,411 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Salto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Indaiatuba is 0.3 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 164 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.0 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (17.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.5 °C (55 °F), in July. The area lies about 142 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Indaiatuba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Indaiatuba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 142 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1070-1327), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
