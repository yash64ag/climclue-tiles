Indaiatuba at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Indaiatuba is 225th warmest (21.3 °C year average), 95th sunniest (about 2,370 hours of sunshine a year) and 234th wettest (1,441 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Salto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Indaiatuba is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 136 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.1 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (17.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.6 °C (55 °F), in July. The area lies about 142 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Indaiatuba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Indaiatuba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 142 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
