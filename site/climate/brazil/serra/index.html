Serra at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Serra is 126th warmest (23.7 °C year average), 136th sunniest (about 2,304 hours of sunshine a year) and 255th wettest (1,411 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cariacica, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Serra is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 109 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.3 °C), averages 5.0 °C more than its coolest, July (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.4 °C (65 °F), in July. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Serra, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Serra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.0 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
