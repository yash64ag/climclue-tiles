Joinville weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Joinville, Brazil (Santa Catarina), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Joinville is warmest in February, with 29.4 °C (85 °F) days and 21.1 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 21.2 °C (70 °F) days and 12.3 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (289 mm); June is driest (96 mm).

Joinville at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Joinville is 1749th warmest (21.1 °C year average), 2192nd sunniest (about 1,653 hours of sunshine a year) and 271st wettest (2,032 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Araquari, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Joinville is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 60 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.2 °C), averages 8.5 °C more than its coolest, July (16.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Joinville, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Joinville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Joinville itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.304°S, 48.846°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1103-1311), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 664,500, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
