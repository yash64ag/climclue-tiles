Caruaru at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Caruaru is 152nd warmest (23.1 °C year average), 86th sunniest (about 2,385 hours of sunshine a year) and 341st wettest (780 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Cruz do Capibaribe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Caruaru is 1.5 °C cooler in March and 1.6 °C cooler in July, with 245 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (24.4 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, July (21.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.0 °C (64 °F), in August. The area lies about 115 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Caruaru, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Caruaru has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 115 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
