Fátima do Sul weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Fátima do Sul, Brazil (Mato Grosso do Sul), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Fátima do Sul is warmest in January, with 31.6 °C (89 °F) days and 21.3 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 26.2 °C (79 °F) days and 12.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (175 mm); July is driest (42 mm).

Fátima do Sul at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Fátima do Sul is 1222nd warmest (23.6 °C year average), 760th sunniest (about 2,378 hours of sunshine a year) and 1424th wettest (1,360 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dourados, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Fátima do Sul is 0.8 °C warmer in January and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 238 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (26.4 °C), averages 7.2 °C more than its coolest, July (19.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.1 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 729 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Fátima do Sul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 729 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fátima do Sul itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.374°S, 54.514°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1063-1254), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
