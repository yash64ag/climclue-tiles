Camaragibe at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Camaragibe is 56th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 2,577 hours of sunshine a year) and 72nd wettest (1,822 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with São Lourenço da Mata, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Camaragibe is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 117 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.1 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, July (24.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.9 °C (71 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Camaragibe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Camaragibe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
