Raposos weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Raposos, Brazil (Minas Gerais), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Raposos is warmest in February, with 28.8 °C (84 °F) days and 18.4 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 24.3 °C (76 °F) days and 12.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around December (312 mm); July is driest (6 mm).

Raposos at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Raposos is 1720th warmest (21.3 °C year average), 841st sunniest (about 2,349 hours of sunshine a year) and 1430th wettest (1,355 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nova Lima, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Raposos is about as warm in February and about as warm in July, with 183 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.6 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, July (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.0 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 344 km from the sea.

Why Raposos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 344 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Raposos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.967°S, 43.804°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1039-1361), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
