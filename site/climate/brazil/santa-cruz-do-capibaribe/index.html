Santa Cruz do Capibaribe at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Santa Cruz do Capibaribe is the 4th driest (535 mm a year). Compared with Caruaru, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Santa Cruz do Capibaribe is 1.7 °C warmer in February and 1.6 °C warmer in July, with 245 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.1 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, July (22.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.9 °C (66 °F), in August. The area lies about 158 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santa Cruz do Capibaribe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santa Cruz do Capibaribe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 158 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
