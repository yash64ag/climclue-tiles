Itanhaém at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Itanhaém is 169th warmest (22.3 °C year average), 308th sunniest (about 1,743 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (2,365 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Praia Grande, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Itanhaém is 0.7 °C cooler in February and 0.6 °C cooler in July, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.8 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, July (19.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.4 °C (62 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Itanhaém, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Itanhaém has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
