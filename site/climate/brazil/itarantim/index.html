Itarantim at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Itarantim is 1110th warmest (24.0 °C year average), 1637th sunniest (about 2,092 hours of sunshine a year) and 1871st wettest (940 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Macarani, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Itarantim is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.0 °C), averages 4.7 °C more than its coolest, July (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in July. The area lies about 128 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Itarantim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Itarantim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 128 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Itarantim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.660°S, 40.066°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-996-1399), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
