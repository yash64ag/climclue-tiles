Paulo Afonso at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Paulo Afonso is 416th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 176th sunniest (about 2,678 hours of sunshine a year) and 2249th wettest (529 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Glória, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Paulo Afonso is 0.2 °C warmer in January and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (28.4 °C), averages 4.8 °C more than its coolest, July (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.6 °C (65 °F), in August. The area lies about 218 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Paulo Afonso, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Paulo Afonso has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 218 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Paulo Afonso itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.406°S, 38.215°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-934-1417), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
