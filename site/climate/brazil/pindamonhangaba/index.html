Pindamonhangaba weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Pindamonhangaba, Brazil (São Paulo), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Pindamonhangaba is warmest in February, with 29.6 °C (85 °F) days and 19.7 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 24.9 °C (77 °F) days and 11.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (260 mm); August is driest (30 mm).

Pindamonhangaba at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Pindamonhangaba is 1597th warmest (21.8 °C year average), 1973rd sunniest (about 1,888 hours of sunshine a year) and 1199th wettest (1,443 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tremembé, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Pindamonhangaba is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 28 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.6 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.4 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 76 km from the sea.

Why Pindamonhangaba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 76 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pindamonhangaba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.924°S, 45.462°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1069-1345), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
