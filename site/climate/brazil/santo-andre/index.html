Santo André at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Santo André is 286th warmest (19.7 °C year average), 311th sunniest (about 1,730 hours of sunshine a year) and 81st wettest (1,789 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mauá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Santo André is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 200 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.9 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santo André, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santo André has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
