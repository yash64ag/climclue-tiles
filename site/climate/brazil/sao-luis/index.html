São Luís at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, São Luís is 35th warmest (26.7 °C year average), 98th sunniest (about 2,362 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (2,104 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Paço do Lumiar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), São Luís is about as warm in November and about as warm in March, with 51 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (27.3 °C), averages 1.2 °C more than its coolest, March (26.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.8 °C (77 °F), in March. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of São Luís, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why São Luís has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for São Luís itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.530°S, 44.303°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-865-1356), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
