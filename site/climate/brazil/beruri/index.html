Beruri at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Beruri is 459th warmest (26.4 °C year average), 2101st sunniest (about 1,766 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (2,664 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Anori, the nearest covered town with different numbers (33 km away), Beruri is 0.1 °C cooler in October and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 119 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.1 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, January (26.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.4 °C (71 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,045 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Beruri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 1,045 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Beruri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.898°S, 61.373°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-878-1186), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
