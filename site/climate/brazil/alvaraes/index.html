Alvarães at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Alvarães is 472nd warmest (26.3 °C year average), 2030th sunniest (about 1,830 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (2,875 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tefé, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Alvarães is about as warm in October and about as warm in June, with 196 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (26.9 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, June (25.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.1 °C (70 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,349 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Alvarães has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 1,349 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alvarães itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.221°S, 64.804°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-872-1151), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
