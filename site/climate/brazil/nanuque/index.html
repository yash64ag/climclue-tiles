Nanuque at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Nanuque is 976th warmest (24.5 °C year average), 1813th sunniest (about 2,019 hours of sunshine a year) and 1856th wettest (960 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pedro Canário, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Nanuque is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 148 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.9 °C), averages 5.1 °C more than its coolest, July (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.8 °C (62 °F), in July. The area lies about 96 km from the sea.

Why Nanuque has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 96 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nanuque itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.839°S, 40.354°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1018-1396), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
