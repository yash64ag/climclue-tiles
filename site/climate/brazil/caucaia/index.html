Caucaia at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Caucaia is 20th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 24th sunniest (about 2,614 hours of sunshine a year) and 313th wettest (1,236 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fortaleza, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Caucaia is 0.2 °C warmer in November and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 79 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (27.4 °C), averages 1.0 °C more than its coolest, July (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.0 °C (75 °F), in July. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Caucaia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Caucaia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
