Nova Olímpia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Nova Olímpia is 512th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 1625th sunniest (about 2,097 hours of sunshine a year) and 653rd wettest (1,688 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tangará da Serra, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Nova Olímpia is 1.2 °C warmer in October and 1.3 °C warmer in July, with 182 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.7 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, July (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.0 °C (61 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,465 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nova Olímpia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nova Olímpia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,465 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nova Olímpia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.797°S, 57.288°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-987-1227), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
