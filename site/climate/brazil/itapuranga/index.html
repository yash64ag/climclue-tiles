Itapuranga at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Itapuranga is 1040th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 1056th sunniest (about 2,273 hours of sunshine a year) and 352nd wettest (1,930 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Goiás, the nearest covered town with different numbers (46 km away), Itapuranga is 1.1 °C cooler in September and 1.1 °C cooler in June, with 158 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (26.2 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, June (22.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.4 °C (60 °F), in June. The area lies about 1,013 km from the sea (deep inland).

Best time to visit Itapuranga

For mild weather, the best months to visit Itapuranga are May, June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): September (26.2 °C), October (25.6 °C) and August (24.7 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (4 mm), June (10 mm) and August (13 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (263 h), June (247 h) and August (245 h).

Why Itapuranga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,013 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Itapuranga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.562°S, 49.949°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-995-1300), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
