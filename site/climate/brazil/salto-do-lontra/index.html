Salto do Lontra at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Salto do Lontra is 1909th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 1534th sunniest (about 2,131 hours of sunshine a year) and 239th wettest (2,067 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ampére, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Salto do Lontra is 0.2 °C warmer in January and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 242 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.4 °C), averages 9.1 °C more than its coolest, July (15.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.2 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 481 km from the sea.

Why Salto do Lontra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 481 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1097-1266), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
