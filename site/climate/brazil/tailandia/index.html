Tailândia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Tailândia is 254th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 1669th sunniest (about 2,084 hours of sunshine a year) and 93rd wettest (2,416 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mocajuba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (74 km away), Tailândia is 0.1 °C warmer in October and 0.3 °C cooler in March, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (28.4 °C), averages 2.7 °C more than its coolest, March (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.1 °C (70 °F), in July. The area lies about 109 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tailândia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tailândia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 109 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tailândia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.947°S, 48.953°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-869-1310), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
