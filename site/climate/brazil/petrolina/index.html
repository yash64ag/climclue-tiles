Petrolina at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Petrolina is 270th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 68th sunniest (about 2,792 hours of sunshine a year) and 2267th wettest (471 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Juazeiro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Petrolina is 0.1 °C cooler in November and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (28.6 °C), averages 4.1 °C more than its coolest, July (24.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.8 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 427 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Petrolina, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Petrolina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 427 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Petrolina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.399°S, 40.501°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-933-1394), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
