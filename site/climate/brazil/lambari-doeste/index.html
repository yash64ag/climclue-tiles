Lambari d'Oeste at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Lambari d'Oeste is 582nd warmest (25.9 °C year average), 1881st sunniest (about 1,969 hours of sunshine a year) and 1352nd wettest (1,388 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with São José dos Quatro Marcos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Lambari d'Oeste is about as warm in October and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.6 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, July (23.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.6 °C (60 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,363 km from the sea (deep inland).

Best time to visit Lambari d'Oeste

For mild weather, the best months to visit Lambari d'Oeste are May, June and July.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June and July.
  • Warmest (average temperature): October (27.6 °C), November (27.1 °C) and December (27.0 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (11 mm), June (19 mm) and August (25 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (235 h), June (197 h) and August (187 h).

Why Lambari d'Oeste has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,363 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lambari d'Oeste itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.323°S, 58.004°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-993-1219), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
