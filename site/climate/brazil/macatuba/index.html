Macatuba weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Macatuba, Brazil (São Paulo), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Macatuba is warmest in February, with 30.2 °C (86 °F) days and 19.4 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 25.1 °C (77 °F) days and 11.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (273 mm); July is driest (36 mm).

Macatuba at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Macatuba is 1509th warmest (22.2 °C year average), 1662nd sunniest (about 2,086 hours of sunshine a year) and 1207th wettest (1,440 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lençóis Paulista, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Macatuba is 0.4 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 66 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.8 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, July (18.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.9 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 278 km from the sea.

Why Macatuba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 278 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Macatuba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.502°S, 48.711°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1065-1312), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,829, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
