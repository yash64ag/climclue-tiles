Medicilândia at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Medicilândia is 458th warmest (26.4 °C year average), 2042nd sunniest (about 1,821 hours of sunshine a year) and 494th wettest (1,810 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Brasil Novo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Medicilândia is 0.6 °C warmer in September and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 224 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (27.9 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, February (25.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.8 °C (69 °F), in July. The area lies about 225 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Medicilândia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Medicilândia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 225 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Medicilândia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.446°S, 52.889°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-874-1271), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
