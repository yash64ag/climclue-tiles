Monte Santo at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Monte Santo is 999th warmest (24.4 °C year average), 1052nd sunniest (about 2,274 hours of sunshine a year) and 2248th wettest (532 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cansanção, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Monte Santo is 0.8 °C cooler in February and 0.8 °C cooler in July, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.1 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, July (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.1 °C (63 °F), in August. The area lies about 250 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Monte Santo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Monte Santo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 250 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Monte Santo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.438°S, 39.333°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-944-1406), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 47,780, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
