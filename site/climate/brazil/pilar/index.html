Pilar at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Pilar is 817th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 500th sunniest (about 2,496 hours of sunshine a year) and 1088th wettest (1,486 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Atalaia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Pilar is 0.1 °C cooler in March and 0.1 °C warmer in August, with 98 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.6 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, August (23.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.6 °C (67 °F), in August. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pilar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Pilar are September and October.

Why Pilar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pilar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.597°S, 35.957°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-935-1440), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
