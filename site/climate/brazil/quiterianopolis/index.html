Quiterianópolis at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Quiterianópolis is 311th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 457th sunniest (about 2,521 hours of sunshine a year) and 2161st wettest (640 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Novo Oriente, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Quiterianópolis is 0.6 °C cooler in November and 0.5 °C cooler in April, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (28.7 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, April (25.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.0 °C (68 °F), in June. The area lies about 339 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Quiterianópolis, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Quiterianópolis has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 339 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Quiterianópolis itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.844°S, 40.702°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-898-1392), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
