Buriti dos Lopes at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Buriti dos Lopes is 54th warmest (28.0 °C year average), 50th sunniest (about 2,823 hours of sunshine a year) and 1769th wettest (1,073 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Parnaíba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Buriti dos Lopes is 1.1 °C warmer in November and 0.1 °C warmer in March, with 71 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (29.3 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, March (26.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.5 °C (73 °F), in February. The area lies about 47 km from the sea.

Why Buriti dos Lopes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 47 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Buriti dos Lopes itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.175°S, 41.867°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-871-1381), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
