Jequitinhonha at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Jequitinhonha is 840th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 1520th sunniest (about 2,136 hours of sunshine a year) and 2102nd wettest (699 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Almenara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (43 km away), Jequitinhonha is 0.7 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 220 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.4 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, July (22.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.0 °C (63 °F), in July. The area lies about 216 km from the sea.

Why Jequitinhonha has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 216 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jequitinhonha itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.434°S, 41.003°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1004-1389), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
