Assaré at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Assaré is 416th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 150th sunniest (about 2,704 hours of sunshine a year) and 2052nd wettest (760 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nova Olinda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Assaré is about as warm in November and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 109 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (28.6 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, July (25.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.9 °C (68 °F), in August. The area lies about 361 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Assaré, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Assaré

No month in Assaré is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are April (25.4 °C, 150 mm), June (25.3 °C, 16 mm) and July (25.2 °C, 7 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): November (28.6 °C), October (28.4 °C) and December (27.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (2 mm), September (3 mm) and July (7 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): August (286 h), September (271 h) and October (257 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Assaré has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 361 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Assaré itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.874°S, 39.875°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-908-1401), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 23,478, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
