Tucuruí at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Tucuruí is 12th warmest (27.2 °C year average), 255th sunniest (about 1,994 hours of sunshine a year) and 9th wettest (2,568 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cametá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (170 km away), Tucuruí is 0.2 °C cooler in October and about as warm in February, with 210 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (28.3 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, February (26.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.5 °C (74 °F), in February. The area lies about 192 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tucuruí, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tucuruí has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 192 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tucuruí itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.766°S, 49.679°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-877-1303), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
