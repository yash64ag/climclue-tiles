Tucuruí at a glance

Among the 346 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Brazil, Tucuruí is 10th warmest (27.2 °C year average), 255th sunniest (about 1,994 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (2,347 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cametá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (170 km away), Tucuruí is 0.1 °C warmer in September and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 458 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (28.4 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, February (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.6 °C (74 °F), in February. The area lies about 192 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tucuruí, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tucuruí has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 192 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
