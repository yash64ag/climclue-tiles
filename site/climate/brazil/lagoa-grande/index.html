Lagoa Grande at a glance

Among the 2275 towns ClimaClue covers in Brazil, Lagoa Grande is 219th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 66th sunniest (about 2,794 hours of sunshine a year) and 2268th wettest (470 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Curaçá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Lagoa Grande is 0.1 °C cooler in November and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (29.0 °C), averages 4.2 °C more than its coolest, July (24.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in July. The area lies about 424 km from the sea.

Why Lagoa Grande has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 424 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lagoa Grande itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.997°S, 40.272°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-929-1397), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
