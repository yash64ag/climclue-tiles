El‘ad at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, El‘ad is 83rd warmest (19.5 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 3,269 hours of sunshine a year) and 63rd wettest (625 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rosh Ha‘Ayin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), El‘ad is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 29 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.5 °C), averages 14.6 °C more than its coolest, January (11.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 16 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 120% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why El‘ad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El‘ad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.051°N, 34.957°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-519-2149), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 50,846, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
