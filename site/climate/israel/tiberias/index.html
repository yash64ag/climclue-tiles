Tiberias at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, Tiberias is the 3rd warmest (21.4 °C year average). Compared with Maghār, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Tiberias is 1.4 °C warmer in August and 2.2 °C warmer in January, with 140 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.1 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.2 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 60 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 19 km from Tiberias by up to 5.7 °C (10.2 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Tiberias has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 60 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tiberias itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.794°N, 35.532°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-512-2155), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 52,123, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
