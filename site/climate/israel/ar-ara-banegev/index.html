‘Ar‘ara BaNegev at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, ‘Ar‘ara BaNegev is the 4th sunniest (about 3,442 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (220 mm a year). Compared with Dimona, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), ‘Ar‘ara BaNegev is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 0.8 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.9 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 80 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 22 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why ‘Ar‘ara BaNegev has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 80 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for ‘Ar‘ara BaNegev itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.160°N, 35.023°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-528-2150), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
