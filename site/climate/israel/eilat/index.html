Eilat at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, Eilat is the warmest (23.4 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,679 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (21 mm a year). Compared with Dimona, the nearest covered town with different numbers (168 km away), Eilat is 4.8 °C warmer in July and 4.7 °C warmer in January, with 197 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.1 °C), averages 16.7 °C more than its coolest, January (14.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Eilat measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.7 °C (4.9 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Eilat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Eilat itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.558°N, 34.948°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-544-2149), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
