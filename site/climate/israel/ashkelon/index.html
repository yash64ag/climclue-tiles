Ashkelon at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Israel, Ashkelon is the 2nd warmest (20.5 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 3,355 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (509 mm a year). Compared with Ashdod, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Ashkelon is 0.3 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 49 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.1 °C), averages 13.2 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.0 °C (52 °F), in February. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ashkelon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ashkelon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
