H̱olon at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, H̱olon is 18th warmest (20.4 °C year average), 48th sunniest (about 3,263 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (667 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bat Yam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), H̱olon is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 10 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.5 °C), averages 12.6 °C more than its coolest, January (13.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.2 °C (49 °F), in February. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 128% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why H̱olon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-519-2147), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
