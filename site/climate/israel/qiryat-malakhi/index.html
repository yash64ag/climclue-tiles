Qiryat Mal’akhi weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Qiryat Mal’akhi, Israel (Southern District), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Qiryat Mal’akhi is warmest in August, with 32.0 °C (90 °F) days and 21.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 18.3 °C (65 °F) days and 8.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (131 mm); August is driest (1 mm).

Qiryat Mal’akhi at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, Qiryat Mal’akhi is 26th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 3,330 hours of sunshine a year) and 93rd wettest (496 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gan Yavne, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Qiryat Mal’akhi is about as warm in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 72 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.6 °C), averages 13.3 °C more than its coolest, January (13.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qiryat Mal’akhi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Qiryat Mal’akhi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qiryat Mal’akhi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.729°N, 34.746°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-522-2147), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
