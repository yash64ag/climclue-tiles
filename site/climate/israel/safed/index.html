Safed at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, Safed is the coolest (17.0 °C year average) and the 4th least sunny (about 3,092 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Maghār, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Safed is 1.6 °C cooler in August and 3.0 °C cooler in January, with 161 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.0 °C), averages 17.1 °C more than its coolest, January (7.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 116% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Safed has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Safed itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.965°N, 35.496°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-510-2154), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 38,687, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
