Segev Shalom weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Segev Shalom, Israel (Southern District), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Segev Shalom is warmest in August, with 32.8 °C (91 °F) days and 20.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 16.1 °C (61 °F) days and 6.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (60 mm); June is driest (1 mm).

Segev Shalom at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, Segev Shalom is the 5th sunniest (about 3,435 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (238 mm a year). Compared with Tel Sheva‘, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Segev Shalom is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 33 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.5 °C), averages 15.2 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 65 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Segev Shalom has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 65 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Segev Shalom itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.199°N, 34.840°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-528-2148), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
