Reẖovot at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Israel, Reẖovot is 13th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 3,306 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (601 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rishon LeTsiyyon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Reẖovot is about as warm in August and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 59 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.6 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, January (13.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.7 °C (49 °F), in February. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 12 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 116% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Reẖovot has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
