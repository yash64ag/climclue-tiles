Jerusalem at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Israel, Jerusalem is the coolest (17.8 °C year average) and the 3rd sunniest (about 3,334 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Bet Shemesh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Jerusalem is 0.7 °C cooler in August and 3.0 °C cooler in January, with 56 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.9 °C), averages 16.9 °C more than its coolest, January (8.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 66 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Jerusalem has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 66 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
