Kfar Yona at a glance

Among the 110 towns ClimaClue covers in Israel, Kfar Yona is 23rd warmest (20.3 °C year average), 64th sunniest (about 3,228 hours of sunshine a year) and 66th wettest (624 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kadima Zoran, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Kfar Yona is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.0 °C), averages 14.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 19 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Kfar Yona has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kfar Yona itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.315°N, 34.936°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-516-2149), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
