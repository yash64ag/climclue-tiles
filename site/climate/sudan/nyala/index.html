Nyala at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sudan, Nyala is the 4th coolest (26.8 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 2,954 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Gereida, the nearest covered town with different numbers (91 km away), Nyala is 0.6 °C cooler in May and 1.5 °C cooler in January, with 70 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.9 °C), averages 8.5 °C more than its coolest, January (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.8 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,556 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nyala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nyala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,556 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
