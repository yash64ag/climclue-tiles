Atbara at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Atbara is 9th warmest (29.9 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 3,473 hours of sunshine a year) and 72nd wettest (37 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ad-Damir, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Atbara is about as warm in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.3 °C), averages 13.5 °C more than its coolest, January (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.0 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 397 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Atbara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Atbara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 397 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Atbara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.702°N, 33.986°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-662-2139), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 112,021, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
