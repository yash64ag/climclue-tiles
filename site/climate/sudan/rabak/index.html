Rabak at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sudan, Rabak is 10th warmest (29.3 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 3,125 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (316 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kosti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Rabak is about as warm in May and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.0 °C), averages 7.9 °C more than its coolest, January (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.2 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 775 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rabak, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Rabak has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 775 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
