Al Hasaheisa weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Al Hasaheisa, Sudan (Al Jazirah), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Al Hasaheisa at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Al Hasaheisa is the 3rd warmest (30.0 °C year average). Compared with Rufā‘a, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Al Hasaheisa is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.4 °C), averages 9.9 °C more than its coolest, January (24.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 646 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Al Hasaheisa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 646 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Hasaheisa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.753°N, 33.298°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-692-2132), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
