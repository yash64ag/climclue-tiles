Khartoum North at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sudan, Khartoum North is the sunniest (about 3,517 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (102 mm a year). Compared with Omdurman, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Khartoum North is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.1 °C), averages 12.0 °C more than its coolest, January (22.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.9 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 639 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Khartoum North has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 639 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
