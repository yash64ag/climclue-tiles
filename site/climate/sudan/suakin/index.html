Suakin at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Suakin is the 2nd least sunny (about 2,932 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Jubayt, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Suakin is 2.7 °C warmer in August and 4.7 °C warmer in February, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (33.7 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, February (22.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.8 °C (64 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Suakin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Suakin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.106°N, 37.332°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-648-2173), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
