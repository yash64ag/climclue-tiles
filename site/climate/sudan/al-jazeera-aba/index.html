Al Jazeera Aba at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Al Jazeera Aba is 30th warmest (29.3 °C year average), 57th sunniest (about 3,131 hours of sunshine a year) and 45th wettest (294 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kosti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Al Jazeera Aba is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.1 °C), averages 8.3 °C more than its coolest, January (24.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 777 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Al Jazeera Aba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 777 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Jazeera Aba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.319°N, 32.613°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-706-2126), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
