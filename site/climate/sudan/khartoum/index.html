Khartoum at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sudan, Khartoum is the 5th warmest (29.7 °C year average) and the 3rd sunniest (about 3,512 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Khartoum North, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Khartoum is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.3 °C), averages 11.8 °C more than its coolest, January (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.2 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 645 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Khartoum, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Khartoum has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 645 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
