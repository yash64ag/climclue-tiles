Kadugli at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Kadugli is the 3rd wettest (663 mm a year). Compared with Dilling, the nearest covered town with different numbers (116 km away), Kadugli is 1.6 °C warmer in April and about as warm in August, with 100 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (32.1 °C), averages 7.1 °C more than its coolest, August (25.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,172 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kadugli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,172 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kadugli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.011°N, 29.718°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-729-2097), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
