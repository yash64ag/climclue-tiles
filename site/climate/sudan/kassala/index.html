Kassala at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sudan, Kassala is 12th warmest (29.2 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 3,452 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (265 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Al Qadarif, the nearest covered town with different numbers (192 km away), Kassala is 0.7 °C warmer in May and 1.4 °C cooler in January, with 324 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.5 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, January (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.1 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 315 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kassala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kassala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 315 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
