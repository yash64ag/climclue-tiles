Wadi Halfa at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Wadi Halfa is the 2nd sunniest (about 3,691 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (1 mm a year). Compared with Dongola, the nearest covered town with different numbers (305 km away), Wadi Halfa is 1.3 °C cooler in July and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.0 °C), averages 17.5 °C more than its coolest, January (16.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.4 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 475 km from the sea.

Why Wadi Halfa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 475 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Wadi Halfa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.796°N, 31.371°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-622-2113), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
