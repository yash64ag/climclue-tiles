Port Sudan at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Port Sudan is 42nd warmest (28.4 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 3,114 hours of sunshine a year) and 70th wettest (58 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Suakin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Port Sudan is 0.6 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 38 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (34.3 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, February (22.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.3 °C (65 °F), in February. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Port Sudan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Port Sudan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Port Sudan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.617°N, 37.216°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-643-2172), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
