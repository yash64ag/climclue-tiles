Port Sudan at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sudan, Port Sudan is the 4th least sunny (about 3,114 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (56 mm a year). Compared with Atbara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (401 km away), Port Sudan is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C cooler in February, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (34.3 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, February (22.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.2 °C (67 °F), in February. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Port Sudan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Port Sudan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
