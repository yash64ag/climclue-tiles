Al Ḩawātah at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Al Ḩawātah is 34th warmest (29.0 °C year average), 38th sunniest (about 3,298 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (576 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ad Dindar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (56 km away), Al Ḩawātah is 1.1 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 57 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (32.8 °C), averages 6.4 °C more than its coolest, August (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 577 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Ḩawātah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Al Ḩawātah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 577 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C. The 10 km area around Al Ḩawātah, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 27.3 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Ḩawātah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.417°N, 34.633°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-705-2146), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
