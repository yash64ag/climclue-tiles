Al-Fashaqah at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Sudan, Al-Fashaqah is 15th warmest (29.7 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 3,368 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (513 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Khashm al Qirbah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Al-Fashaqah is 0.6 °C cooler in May and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 168 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.8 °C), averages 7.5 °C more than its coolest, January (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.8 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 416 km from the sea.

Why Al-Fashaqah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 416 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al-Fashaqah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.397°N, 35.867°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-696-2158), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
