‘Ibrī at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Oman, ‘Ibrī is the warmest (28.8 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 3,434 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (93 mm a year). Compared with Rustaq, the nearest covered town with different numbers (95 km away), ‘Ibrī is 2.3 °C warmer in July and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 81 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (36.4 °C), averages 17.1 °C more than its coolest, January (19.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 108 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of ‘Ibrī, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why ‘Ibrī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 108 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
