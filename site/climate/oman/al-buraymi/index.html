Al Buraymī weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Al Buraymī, Oman (Al Buraimi), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Al Buraymī is warmest in July, with 43.0 °C (109 °F) days and 29.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 24.3 °C (76 °F) days and 11.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around February (28 mm); November is driest (1 mm).

Al Buraymī at a glance

Among the 27 towns ClimaClue covers in Oman, Al Buraymī is the sunniest (about 3,455 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Liwá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (84 km away), Al Buraymī is 2.0 °C warmer in July and 2.4 °C cooler in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (36.0 °C), averages 18.0 °C more than its coolest, January (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.9 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 97 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Buraymī, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Buraymī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 97 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Buraymī itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.251°N, 55.793°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-597-2357), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
