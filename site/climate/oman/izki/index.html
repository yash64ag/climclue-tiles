Izkī weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Izkī, Oman (Ad Dakhiliyah), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Izkī at a glance

Among the 27 towns ClimaClue covers in Oman, Izkī is the 4th wettest (163 mm a year). Compared with Nizwá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Izkī is 0.2 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.8 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, January (20.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.8 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 100 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Izkī, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Izkī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 100 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C. The 10 km area around Izkī, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 27.5 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Izkī itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.933°N, 57.767°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-610-2377), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
