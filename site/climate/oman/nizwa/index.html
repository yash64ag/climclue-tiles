Nizwá at a glance

Among the 27 towns ClimaClue covers in Oman, Nizwá is the 4th warmest (29.0 °C year average) and the 2nd wettest (170 mm a year). Compared with Bahlā’, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Nizwá is 0.4 °C warmer in June and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (36.0 °C), averages 15.8 °C more than its coolest, January (20.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.8 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 101 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nizwá, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Nizwá

For mild weather, the best months to visit Nizwá are January, February and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (36.0 °C), July (35.5 °C) and May (34.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (3 mm), October (4 mm) and September (6 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (336 h), October (308 h) and June (304 h).

Why Nizwá has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 101 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nizwá itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.933°N, 57.533°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-610-2375), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 72,076, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
