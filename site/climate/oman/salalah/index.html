Şalālah at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Oman, Şalālah is the coolest (25.6 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,885 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (124 mm a year). Compared with ‘Ibrī, the nearest covered town with different numbers (735 km away), Şalālah is 5.1 °C cooler in May and 2.6 °C warmer in January, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.8 °C), averages 6.9 °C more than its coolest, January (21.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.1 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Şalālah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Şalālah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
