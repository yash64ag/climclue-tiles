Bahlā’ at a glance

Among the 27 towns ClimaClue covers in Oman, Bahlā’ is the 3rd wettest (164 mm a year). Compared with Nizwá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Bahlā’ is 0.4 °C cooler in June and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.7 °C), averages 15.9 °C more than its coolest, January (19.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.0 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 105 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bahlā’, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bahlā’ has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 105 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bahlā’ itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.979°N, 57.305°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-610-2373), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
