Nakonde at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Zambia, Nakonde is the coolest (19.0 °C year average) and the wettest (1,450 mm a year). Compared with Isoka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (92 km away), Nakonde is 1.7 °C cooler in October and 0.5 °C cooler in July, with 262 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (21.7 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, July (16.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 732 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nakonde, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nakonde has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 732 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nakonde itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.342°S, 32.745°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-933-2127), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 91,107, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
