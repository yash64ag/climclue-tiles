Mbala at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Zambia, Mbala is the 4th coolest (19.5 °C year average). Compared with Mpulungu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Mbala is 5.1 °C cooler in October and 6.0 °C cooler in July, with 278 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (22.0 °C), averages 4.5 °C more than its coolest, July (17.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.6 °C (51 °F), in July. The area lies about 881 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mbala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mbala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 881 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mbala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.840°S, 31.366°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-928-2113), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 42,424, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
