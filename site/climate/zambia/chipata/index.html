Why Chipata has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 678 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

When is the best time for mild weather in Chipata?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Chipata’s GeoNames location (13.633°S, 32.650°E; ClimaClue area sq-976-2126), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 327,059 (GeoNames).
