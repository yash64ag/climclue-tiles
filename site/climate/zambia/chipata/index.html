Chipata at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Zambia, Chipata is 49th warmest (20.7 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 2,724 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (1,165 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Katete, the nearest covered town with different numbers (77 km away), Chipata is 0.6 °C warmer in November and 0.9 °C warmer in July, with 133 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (24.1 °C), averages 7.3 °C more than its coolest, July (16.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.0 °C (50 °F), in July. The area lies about 678 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chipata, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chipata has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 678 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chipata itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.633°S, 32.650°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-976-2126), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
