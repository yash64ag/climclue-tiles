Mufumbwe at a glance

Among the 77 towns ClimaClue covers in Zambia, Mufumbwe is 23rd warmest (22.1 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 2,685 hours of sunshine a year) and 26th wettest (1,149 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manyinga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (79 km away), Mufumbwe is about as warm in October and about as warm in July, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (25.5 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, July (19.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,243 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Mufumbwe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,243 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mufumbwe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.137°S, 25.007°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-971-2050), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
