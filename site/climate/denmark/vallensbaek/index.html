Vallensbæk weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Vallensbæk, Denmark (Capital Region), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Vallensbæk is warmest in July, with 21.1 °C (70 °F) days and 13.5 °C nights, and coolest in February, with 3.1 °C (38 °F) days and -1.3 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 34 mm in April to 75 mm in August.

Vallensbæk at a glance

Among the 63 towns ClimaClue covers in Denmark, Vallensbæk is 19th warmest (8.6 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 1,655 hours of sunshine a year) and 52nd wettest (661 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ishøj, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Vallensbæk is about as warm in July and about as warm in February, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.3 °C), averages 16.3 °C more than its coolest, February (0.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 12 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Vallensbæk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vallensbæk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (55.622°N, 12.385°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-283-1923), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
