Copenhagen at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Denmark, Copenhagen is the 2nd warmest (8.5 °C year average), the sunniest (about 1,652 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (652 mm a year). Compared with Odense, the nearest covered town with different numbers (141 km away), Copenhagen is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C cooler in February, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.3 °C), averages 16.4 °C more than its coolest, February (0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Copenhagen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
