Horsens at a glance

Among the 63 towns ClimaClue covers in Denmark, Horsens is 48th warmest (8.5 °C year average), 46th sunniest (about 1,595 hours of sunshine a year) and 15th wettest (765 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hedensted, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Horsens is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 109 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.7 °C), averages 15.5 °C more than its coolest, February (1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Horsens, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Horsens has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Horsens itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (55.861°N, 9.850°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-281-1898), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 61,074, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
