Ban Phra at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Ban Phra is 168th warmest (27.5 °C year average), 98th sunniest (about 2,579 hours of sunshine a year) and 54th wettest (1,778 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thambon Na Mueang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Ban Phra is 0.1 °C cooler in April and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.6 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, December (25.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.2 °C (67 °F), in December. The area lies about 95 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ban Phra, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ban Phra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 95 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ban Phra itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.095°N, 101.389°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-699-2813), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
