Samut Sakhon at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Samut Sakhon is 122nd warmest (28.1 °C year average), 158th sunniest (about 2,541 hours of sunshine a year) and 201st wettest (1,333 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Krathum Baen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Samut Sakhon is 0.5 °C cooler in April and about as warm in December, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.9 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, December (26.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.9 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Samut Sakhon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Samut Sakhon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Samut Sakhon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.548°N, 100.274°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-704-2802), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
