Lom Sak at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Lom Sak is the driest (767 mm a year). Compared with Phetchabun, the nearest covered town with different numbers (41 km away), Lom Sak is 0.2 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 229 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.9 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, December (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.8 °C (62 °F), in December. The area lies about 373 km from the sea.

Why Lom Sak has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 373 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lom Sak itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.780°N, 101.242°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-672-2812), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
