Chiang Mai at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Thailand, Chiang Mai is the coolest (26.4 °C year average), the 5th sunniest (about 2,656 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,006 mm a year). Compared with Lampang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (77 km away), Chiang Mai is 0.3 °C cooler in April and 1.7 °C cooler in December, with 68 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.4 °C), averages 9.3 °C more than its coolest, December (21.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.5 °C (58 °F), in December. The area lies about 268 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chiang Mai, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Chiang Mai

For mild weather, the best months to visit Chiang Mai are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (30.4 °C), May (29.4 °C) and June (28.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (3 mm), February (6 mm) and December (13 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (287 h), January (277 h) and February (273 h).

Why Chiang Mai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 268 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
