Khao Saming at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Khao Saming is the 5th wettest (3,720 mm a year). Compared with Trat, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Khao Saming is 0.1 °C cooler in April and 0.2 °C cooler in December, with 160 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.2 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, December (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.6 °C (69 °F), in January. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Khao Saming, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Khao Saming

No month in Khao Saming is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are January (26.2 °C, 23 mm), November (26.7 °C, 59 mm) and December (25.8 °C, 10 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (28.2 °C), May (27.8 °C) and March (27.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (10 mm), January (23 mm) and February (50 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (268 h), December (267 h) and March (245 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Khao Saming has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Khao Saming itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.354°N, 102.434°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-716-2824), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 38,941, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
