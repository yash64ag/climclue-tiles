Bang Bo District at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Bang Bo District is 87th warmest (28.4 °C year average), 182nd sunniest (about 2,537 hours of sunshine a year) and 135th wettest (1,456 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ban Khlong Bang Sao Thong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Bang Bo District is about as warm in April and 0.2 °C warmer in December, with 43 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.3 °C), averages 4.2 °C more than its coolest, December (26.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.9 °C (70 °F), in December. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bang Bo District, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bang Bo District has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bang Bo District itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.583°N, 100.817°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-704-2808), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
