Huai Yot at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Huai Yot is 247th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 264th sunniest (about 2,438 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (2,005 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Trang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Huai Yot is about as warm in April and 0.4 °C cooler in December, with 261 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.2 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, December (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in February. The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Huai Yot, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Huai Yot has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Huai Yot itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.789°N, 99.635°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-762-2796), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
