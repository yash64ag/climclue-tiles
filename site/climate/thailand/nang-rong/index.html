Nang Rong weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nang Rong, Thailand (Buriram), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nang Rong at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Nang Rong is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,774 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Prakhon Chai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Nang Rong is 0.1 °C cooler in April and about as warm in December, with 126 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.0 °C), averages 6.2 °C more than its coolest, December (23.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 247 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nang Rong, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nang Rong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 247 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nang Rong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.638°N, 102.791°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-693-2827), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 21,961, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
