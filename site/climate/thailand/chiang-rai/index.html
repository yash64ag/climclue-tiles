Chiang Rai at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Chiang Rai is the 3rd coolest (25.3 °C year average). Compared with Mae Chan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Chiang Rai is about as warm in April and 0.2 °C warmer in December, with 163 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.9 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, December (20.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.7 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 429 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Chiang Rai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 429 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chiang Rai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.909°N, 99.832°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-640-2798), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
