Kalasin at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Kalasin is 222nd warmest (27.1 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 2,719 hours of sunshine a year) and 205th wettest (1,322 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kamalasai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Kalasin is about as warm in April and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.1 °C), averages 7.2 °C more than its coolest, December (22.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 345 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kalasin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kalasin

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kalasin are January and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (30.1 °C), May (29.3 °C) and June (28.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (3 mm), January (5 mm) and November (18 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (283 h), December (275 h) and March (255 h).

Why Kalasin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 345 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kalasin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.433°N, 103.507°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-675-2835), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 55,102, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
