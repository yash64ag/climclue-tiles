Ban Mai at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Thailand, Ban Mai is 180th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 224th sunniest (about 2,502 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (1,989 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Songkhla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Ban Mai is 0.1 °C warmer in June and about as warm in December, with 33 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.3 °C), averages 2.0 °C more than its coolest, December (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.2 °C (74 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 100% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ban Mai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ban Mai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.204°N, 100.545°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-767-2805), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 86,899, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
