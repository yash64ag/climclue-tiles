Arlit at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Niger, Arlit is the 2nd warmest (29.1 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,282 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (52 mm a year). Compared with Agadez, the nearest covered town with different numbers (206 km away), Arlit is 1.0 °C warmer in June and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 75 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.8 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, January (19.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.6 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,422 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Arlit, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Arlit has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,422 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
