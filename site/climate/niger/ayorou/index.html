Ayorou at a glance

Among the 42 towns ClimaClue covers in Niger, Ayorou is the warmest (30.0 °C year average). Compared with Méhana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Ayorou is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (35.2 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, January (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.6 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 954 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ayorou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 954 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ayorou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.731°N, 0.917°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-692-1809), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
