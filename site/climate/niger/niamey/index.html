Niamey at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Niger, Niamey is the warmest (29.3 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,904 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (462 mm a year). Compared with Tahoua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (373 km away), Niamey is 0.6 °C warmer in May and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 118 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.4 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.9 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 810 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Niamey, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Niamey has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 810 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
