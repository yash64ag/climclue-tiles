Illéla at a glance

Among the 42 towns ClimaClue covers in Niger, Illéla is 12th warmest (29.3 °C year average), 37th sunniest (about 2,894 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (362 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tahoua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Illéla is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.0 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.8 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 909 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Illéla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Illéla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 909 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Illéla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.460°N, 5.244°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-695-1852), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
