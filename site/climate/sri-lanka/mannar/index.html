Mannar at a glance

Among the 60 towns ClimaClue covers in Sri Lanka, Mannar is the 4th driest (1,104 mm a year). Compared with Vavuniya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (73 km away), Mannar is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 257 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.0 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, January (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.7 °C (73 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mannar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Mannar

No month in Mannar is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are January (25.6 °C, 67 mm), February (26.4 °C, 36 mm) and December (25.9 °C, 186 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (29.0 °C), April (28.7 °C) and June (28.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): June (18 mm), July (23 mm) and August (25 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (267 h), April (242 h) and January (230 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Mannar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mannar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.989°N, 79.878°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-750-2598), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 35,817, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
