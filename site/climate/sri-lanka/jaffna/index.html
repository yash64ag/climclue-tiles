Jaffna at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sri Lanka, Jaffna is the 2nd warmest (27.7 °C year average), the 3rd least sunny (about 2,514 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (1,265 mm a year). Compared with Trincomalee, the nearest covered town with different numbers (181 km away), Jaffna is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 268 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.4 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, January (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.4 °C (76 °F), in January. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jaffna, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jaffna has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
