Maharagama at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sri Lanka, Maharagama is the 2nd coolest (26.9 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 2,534 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (2,442 mm a year). Compared with Dehiwala-Mount Lavinia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Maharagama is 0.1 °C cooler in March and 0.1 °C cooler in November, with 188 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.9 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, November (26.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.6 °C (73 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Maharagama, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Maharagama has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
