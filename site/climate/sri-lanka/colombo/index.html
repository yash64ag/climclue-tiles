Colombo at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sri Lanka, Colombo is the sunniest (about 2,539 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Pita Kotte, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Colombo is 0.2 °C warmer in March and 0.2 °C warmer in November, with 235 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.1 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, November (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.2 °C (74 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Colombo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Colombo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
