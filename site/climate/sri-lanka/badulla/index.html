Badulla at a glance

Among the 60 towns ClimaClue covers in Sri Lanka, Badulla is the 3rd coolest (23.8 °C year average) and the 3rd least sunny (about 2,068 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Nuwara Eliya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Badulla is 9.7 °C warmer in June and 6.3 °C warmer in January, with 732 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (25.9 °C), averages 4.9 °C more than its coolest, January (21.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 94 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Badulla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Badulla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 94 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.9 °C. The 10 km area around Badulla, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 22.5 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Badulla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.980°N, 81.058°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-770-2610), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
