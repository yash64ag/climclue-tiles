Quatre Bornes weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Quatre Bornes, Mauritius (Plaines Wilhems), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Quatre Bornes at a glance

Among the 14 towns ClimaClue covers in Mauritius, Quatre Bornes is 11th warmest (22.2 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 2,589 hours of sunshine a year) and 4th wettest (1,803 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Beau Bassin-Rose Hill, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Quatre Bornes is 0.5 °C cooler in February and 0.7 °C cooler in August, with 94 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.8 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, August (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.5 °C (60 °F), in August. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 88% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Quatre Bornes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Quatre Bornes itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.264°S, 57.479°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1042-2374), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
