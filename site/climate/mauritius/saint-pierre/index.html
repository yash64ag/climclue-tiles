Saint Pierre at a glance

Among the 14 towns ClimaClue covers in Mauritius, Saint Pierre is the 2nd coolest (21.8 °C year average), the 3rd least sunny (about 2,404 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (1,847 mm a year). Compared with Beau Bassin-Rose Hill, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Saint Pierre is 0.9 °C cooler in February and 1.1 °C cooler in August, with 138 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.4 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, August (18.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.4 °C (60 °F), in August. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 90% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Saint Pierre has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Saint Pierre itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.222°S, 57.534°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1042-2375), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
