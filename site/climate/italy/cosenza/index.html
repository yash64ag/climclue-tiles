Cosenza at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Cosenza is 229th warmest (16.0 °C year average), 168th sunniest (about 2,404 hours of sunshine a year) and 154th wettest (999 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Quattromiglia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Cosenza is 0.5 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.1 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, February (8.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.1 °C (43 °F), in February. The area lies about 26 km from the sea.

Why Cosenza has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 26 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C. The 10 km area around Cosenza, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 14.2 °C a year, 1.8 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cosenza itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.299°N, 16.253°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-447-1962), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
