Barletta at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Barletta is 99th warmest (17.0 °C year average), 115th sunniest (about 2,450 hours of sunshine a year) and 588th wettest (483 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Andria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Barletta is 0.6 °C warmer in August and 1.0 °C warmer in February, with 34 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.7 °C), averages 16.2 °C more than its coolest, February (9.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.5 °C (42 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Barletta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Barletta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Barletta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.314°N, 16.282°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-426-1962), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
