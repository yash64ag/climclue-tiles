Livorno weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Livorno, Italy (Tuscany), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Livorno at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Livorno is 248th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 301st sunniest (about 2,219 hours of sunshine a year) and 330th wettest (829 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rosignano Marittimo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Livorno is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 132 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.3 °C), averages 15.7 °C more than its coolest, January (8.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 8 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Livorno has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Livorno itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.544°N, 10.326°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-404-1903), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
