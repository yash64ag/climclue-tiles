Vicenza weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Vicenza, Italy (Veneto), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Vicenza at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Vicenza is 453rd warmest (13.6 °C year average), 442nd sunniest (about 2,033 hours of sunshine a year) and 135th wettest (1,030 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Montecchio Maggiore-Alte Ceccato, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Vicenza is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.2 °C), averages 21.5 °C more than its coolest, January (2.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 66 km from the sea.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Vicenza are July, August and September.

Why Vicenza has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 66 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vicenza itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.547°N, 11.548°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-384-1915), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 111,980, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
