Frascati at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Frascati is 352nd warmest (14.5 °C year average), 166th sunniest (about 2,406 hours of sunshine a year) and 114th wettest (1,057 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Grottaferrata, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Frascati is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.7 °C), averages 17.4 °C more than its coolest, January (6.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Why Frascati has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.4 °C. The 10 km area around Frascati, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 15.5 °C a year, 1.1 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-421-1926), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
