Reggio Calabria at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Reggio Calabria is 8th warmest (17.2 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 2,459 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,030 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Messina, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Reggio Calabria is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 284 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.3 °C), averages 14.8 °C more than its coolest, February (10.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.9 °C (46 °F), in February. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Reggio Calabria, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Reggio Calabria has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
