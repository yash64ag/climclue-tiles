Cascina at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Cascina is 328th warmest (14.7 °C year average), 320th sunniest (about 2,186 hours of sunshine a year) and 309th wettest (856 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pontedera, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Cascina is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.0 °C), averages 18.2 °C more than its coolest, January (5.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 27 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cascina, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cascina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 27 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cascina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.676°N, 10.555°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-403-1905), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 40,512, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
