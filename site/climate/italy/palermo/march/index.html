Palermo weather in March

Average March weather in Palermo, Italy: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In March, Palermo averages 16.1 °C (61 °F) days and 10.3 °C (51 °F) nights, with 65 mm (2.6 in) of rain over the month and about 185 hours of sunshine.

How March compares in Palermo

March is the 3rd coolest of the 12, the 6th driest of the 12 and the 5th least sunny of the 12 in Palermo. Compared with February: 1.3 °C warmer, 3 mm less precipitation and 46 hours more sunshine. April is 2.1 °C warmer than March on average.

Palermo in March: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Palermo in March?
In March, Palermo averages a day high of 16.1 °C (61 °F) and a night low of 10.3 °C (51 °F), with 65 mm (2.6 in) of precipitation and about 185 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is March a good time to visit Palermo?
March is not one of Palermo’s mild months; those are April, May, June, September and October, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Palermo in March?
65 mm (2.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making March the 6th driest of the 12. The wettest month is November (101 mm), the driest July (5 mm).
Does it snow in Palermo in March?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for March in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Palermo get in March?
About 185 hours of bright sunshine, the 5th least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Palermo in March?
About 14.2 °C (58 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, cold for most swimmers; its warmest month is August (26.5 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Palermo in March?
Going by the averages: a jacket or sweater and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
