Palermo at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Palermo is the 3rd warmest (18.1 °C year average) and the 3rd sunniest (about 2,590 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Catania, the nearest covered town with different numbers (165 km away), Palermo is 1.6 °C cooler in August and about as warm in February, with 216 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 14.7 °C more than its coolest, February (11.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.9 °C (48 °F), in February. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Palermo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Palermo are May, June, September and October.

Why Palermo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
