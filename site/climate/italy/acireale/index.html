Acireale at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Acireale is 38th warmest (17.6 °C year average), 70th sunniest (about 2,525 hours of sunshine a year) and 334th wettest (827 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Aci Catena, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Acireale is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.9 °C), averages 15.0 °C more than its coolest, February (10.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.8 °C (44 °F), in February. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Acireale has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Acireale itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.609°N, 15.166°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-463-1951), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
