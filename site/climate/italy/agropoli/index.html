Agropoli at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Agropoli is 91st warmest (17.0 °C year average), 101st sunniest (about 2,455 hours of sunshine a year) and 148th wettest (1,003 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Battipaglia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Agropoli is about as warm in August and 1.1 °C warmer in February, with 120 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.0 °C), averages 14.8 °C more than its coolest, February (10.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in February. The area lies about 8 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Agropoli, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Agropoli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Agropoli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.349°N, 14.991°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-436-1949), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,335, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
