Trento at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Trento is the 2nd least sunny (about 1,922 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Bolzano, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Trento is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 58 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 21.9 °C more than its coolest, January (1.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 121 km from the sea.

Why Trento has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 121 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.9 °C. The 10 km area around Trento, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 11.4 °C a year, 2.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Trento itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.068°N, 11.121°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-379-1911), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
