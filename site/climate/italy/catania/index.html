Catania at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Catania is the warmest (18.7 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,592 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (538 mm a year). Compared with Siracusa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Catania is 1.4 °C warmer in August and 1.2 °C cooler in January, with 58 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 16.4 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in February. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 17 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Catania has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
