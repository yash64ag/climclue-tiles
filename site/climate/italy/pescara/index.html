Pescara at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Pescara is 14th warmest (15.7 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 2,188 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (744 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Terni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (128 km away), Pescara is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 2.0 °C warmer in January, with 242 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.9 °C), averages 17.5 °C more than its coolest, January (7.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pescara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pescara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
