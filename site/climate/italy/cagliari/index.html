Cagliari at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Cagliari is the 4th warmest (17.5 °C year average), the 4th sunniest (about 2,551 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (510 mm a year). Compared with Palermo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (389 km away), Cagliari is 0.5 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 244 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 14.9 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in February. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 129% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Cagliari has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
