Massa at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Massa is 297th warmest (15.3 °C year average), 395th sunniest (about 2,105 hours of sunshine a year) and 59th wettest (1,152 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Marina di Massa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Massa is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.5 °C), averages 15.2 °C more than its coolest, January (8.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Massa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Massa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.2 °C. The 10 km area around Massa, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.6 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Massa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.035°N, 10.139°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-399-1901), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
