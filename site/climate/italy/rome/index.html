Rome at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Rome is 12th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 2,424 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (836 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Acilia-Castel Fusano-Ostia Antica, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Rome is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.1 °C), averages 17.3 °C more than its coolest, January (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Rome has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

August, with an average of 25.1 °C (77 °F), day highs around 29.8 °C (86 °F) and night lows around 19.9 °C (68 °F).
What is the coldest month in Rome?
January, with an average of 7.8 °C (46 °F), day highs around 11.6 °C (53 °F) and night lows around 3.7 °C (39 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Rome?

Does it snow in Rome?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 2.4 cm (0.9 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in February; it usually melts within days.
