Rome weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Rome, Italy (Lazio), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Rome is warmest in August, with 31.9 °C (89 °F) days and 18.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 12.6 °C (55 °F) days and 2.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around November (109 mm); July is driest (23 mm).

Rome at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Rome is 233rd warmest (15.9 °C year average), 154th sunniest (about 2,424 hours of sunshine a year) and 377th wettest (770 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciampino, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Rome is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 182 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.1 °C), averages 17.5 °C more than its coolest, January (7.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 97% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Rome

For mild weather, the best months to visit Rome are May, June and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (25.1 °C), July (25.1 °C) and June (22.0 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (23 mm), August (29 mm) and June (32 mm).

Why Rome has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rome itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.892°N, 12.511°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-421-1925), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,318,895, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
