How April compares in Rome

April is the 6th coolest of the 12, the 4th wettest of the 12 and the 6th sunniest of the 12 in Rome. Compared with March: 2.7 °C warmer, 19 mm more precipitation and 34 hours more sunshine. May is 4.5 °C warmer than April on average.

Rome in April: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Rome in April?
In April, Rome averages a day high of 17.6 °C (64 °F) and a night low of 8.6 °C (47 °F), with 80 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation and about 199 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is April a good time to visit Rome?
April is not one of Rome’s mild months; those are May, June, July and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Rome in April?
80 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making April the 4th wettest of the 12. The wettest month is November (119 mm), the driest July (25 mm).
Does it snow in Rome in April?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for April in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Rome get in April?
About 199 hours of bright sunshine, the 6th sunniest of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Rome in April?
Going by the averages: a jacket or sweater and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
