How July compares in Rome

July is the 2nd warmest of the 12, the driest month and the sunniest month in Rome. Compared with June: 3.0 °C warmer, 8 mm less precipitation and 46 hours more sunshine. August is 0.1 °C warmer than July on average.

Rome in July: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Rome in July?
In July, Rome averages a day high of 32.1 °C (90 °F) and a night low of 18.1 °C (65 °F), with 23 mm (0.9 in) of precipitation and about 328 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is July a good time to visit Rome?
July is not one of Rome’s mild months; those are May, June and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Rome in July?
23 mm (0.9 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making July the driest month of the year. The wettest month is November (109 mm), the driest July (23 mm).
Does it snow in Rome in July?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for July in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Rome get in July?
About 328 hours of bright sunshine, the sunniest month of the year (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Rome in July?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes for humid air, an extra layer for cooler evenings and sunglasses. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
