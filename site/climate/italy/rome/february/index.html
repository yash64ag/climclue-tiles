How February compares in Rome

February is the 2nd coolest of the 12, the 6th wettest of the 12 and the 4th least sunny of the 12 in Rome. Compared with January: 0.7 °C warmer, 1 mm more precipitation and 8 hours more sunshine. March is 2.5 °C warmer than February on average.

Rome in February: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Rome in February?
In February, Rome averages a day high of 12.2 °C (54 °F) and a night low of 3.8 °C (39 °F), with 71 mm (2.8 in) of precipitation and about 126 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is February a good time to visit Rome?
February is not one of Rome’s mild months; those are May, June, July and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Rome in February?
71 mm (2.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making February the 6th wettest of the 12. The wettest month is November (119 mm), the driest July (25 mm).
Does it snow in Rome in February?
Yes, on average: ERA5-Land gives about 1.1 cm (0.4 in) of fresh snow in February (1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow).
How many hours of sunshine does Rome get in February?
About 126 hours of bright sunshine, the 4th least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Rome in February?
Going by the averages: a jacket or sweater and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
