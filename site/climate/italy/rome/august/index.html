How August compares in Rome

August is the warmest month, the 2nd driest of the 12 and the 2nd sunniest of the 12 in Rome. Compared with July: 0.1 °C warmer, 6 mm more precipitation and 33 hours less sunshine. September is 3.8 °C cooler than August on average.

Rome in August: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Rome in August?
In August, Rome averages a day high of 29.9 °C (86 °F) and a night low of 19.8 °C (68 °F), with 29 mm (1.2 in) of precipitation and about 294 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is August a good time to visit Rome?
August is not one of Rome’s mild months; those are May, June and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Rome in August?
29 mm (1.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making August the 2nd driest of the 12. The wettest month is November (109 mm), the driest July (23 mm).
Does it snow in Rome in August?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for August in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Rome get in August?
About 294 hours of bright sunshine, the 2nd sunniest of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Rome in August?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes for humid air, an extra layer for cooler evenings and sunglasses. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
