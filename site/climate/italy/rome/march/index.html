How March compares in Rome

March is the 4th coolest of the 12, the 5th driest of the 12 and the 5th least sunny of the 12 in Rome. Compared with February: 2.5 °C warmer, 9 mm less precipitation and 38 hours more sunshine. April is 2.7 °C warmer than March on average.

Rome in March: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Rome in March?
In March, Rome averages a day high of 16.1 °C (61 °F) and a night low of 5.2 °C (41 °F), with 56 mm (2.2 in) of precipitation and about 164 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is March a good time to visit Rome?
March is not one of Rome’s mild months; those are May, June and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Rome in March?
56 mm (2.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making March the 5th driest of the 12. The wettest month is November (109 mm), the driest July (23 mm).
Does it snow in Rome in March?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for March in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Rome get in March?
About 164 hours of bright sunshine, the 5th least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Rome in March?
Going by the averages: a jacket or sweater, an extra layer for cooler evenings and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
