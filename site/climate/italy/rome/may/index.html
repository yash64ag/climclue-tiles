How May compares in Rome

May is the 5th warmest of the 12, the 4th driest of the 12 and the 4th sunniest of the 12 in Rome. Compared with April: 4.5 °C warmer, 24 mm less precipitation and 62 hours more sunshine. June is 3.8 °C warmer than May on average.

Rome in May: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Rome in May?
In May, Rome averages a day high of 22.4 °C (72 °F) and a night low of 12.7 °C (55 °F), with 56 mm (2.2 in) of precipitation and about 261 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is May a good time to visit Rome?
For mild weather, yes: May is one of Rome’s mild months (May, June, July and September), counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Rome in May?
56 mm (2.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making May the 4th driest of the 12. The wettest month is November (119 mm), the driest July (25 mm).
Does it snow in Rome in May?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for May in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Rome get in May?
About 261 hours of bright sunshine, the 4th sunniest of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Rome in May?
Going by the averages: light layers, a compact umbrella and sunglasses. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
