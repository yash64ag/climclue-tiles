Prato at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Prato is 364th warmest (14.4 °C year average), 399th sunniest (about 2,101 hours of sunshine a year) and 245th wettest (909 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Campi Bisenzio, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Prato is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 47 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.3 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, January (4.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 72 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Prato, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Prato has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 72 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Prato itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.880°N, 11.097°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-401-1910), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
