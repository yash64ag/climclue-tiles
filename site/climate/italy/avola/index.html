Avola at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Avola is the warmest (18.6 °C year average). Compared with Noto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Avola is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.8 °C warmer in February, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Avola has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Avola itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.908°N, 15.139°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-470-1951), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
