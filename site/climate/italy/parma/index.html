Parma at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Parma is 28th warmest (14.1 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 2,141 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (703 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Reggio nell'Emilia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Parma is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 43 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.3 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (3.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 92 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Parma, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Parma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 92 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Parma itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.799°N, 10.326°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-392-1903), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
