San Salvo at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, San Salvo is 247th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 297th sunniest (about 2,255 hours of sunshine a year) and 570th wettest (524 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vasto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), San Salvo is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.8 °C), averages 16.9 °C more than its coolest, January (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why San Salvo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Salvo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.044°N, 14.733°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-419-1947), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
