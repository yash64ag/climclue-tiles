Carbonia at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Carbonia is 42nd warmest (17.5 °C year average), 49th sunniest (about 2,571 hours of sunshine a year) and 502nd wettest (626 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Iglesias, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Carbonia is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 48 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.9 °C), averages 15.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Why Carbonia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Carbonia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.165°N, 8.521°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-448-1885), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
