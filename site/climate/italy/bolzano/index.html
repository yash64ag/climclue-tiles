Bolzano at a glance

Among the 44 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Italy, Bolzano is the coolest (10.8 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 1,870 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Trento, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Bolzano is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.8 °C), averages 21.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 145 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bolzano, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bolzano has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 145 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 9.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
