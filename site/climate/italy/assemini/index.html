Assemini at a glance

Among the 598 towns ClimaClue covers in Italy, Assemini is 49th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 53rd sunniest (about 2,555 hours of sunshine a year) and 591st wettest (472 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sestu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Assemini is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 54 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, January (10.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Why Assemini has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Assemini itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.291°N, 8.999°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-447-1889), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
