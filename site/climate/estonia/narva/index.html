Narva weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Narva, Estonia (Ida-Virumaa), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Narva is warmest in July, with 22.4 °C (72 °F) days and 13.0 °C nights, and coolest in February, with -2.3 °C (28 °F) days and -8.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (100 mm); April is driest (32 mm).

Narva at a glance

Among the 7 towns ClimaClue covers in Estonia, Narva is the 2nd coolest (5.6 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 1,652 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Kohtla-Järve, the nearest covered town with different numbers (52 km away), Narva is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.7 °C), averages 23.4 °C more than its coolest, February (-5.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Narva has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Narva itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (59.377°N, 28.190°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-246-2081), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
