Tallinn at a glance

Its warmest month, July (17.6 °C), averages 22.3 °C more than its coolest, February (-4.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 12 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Tallinn

For mild weather, the best months to visit Tallinn are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (17.6 °C), August (16.4 °C) and June (14.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): April (31 mm), May (36 mm) and February (36 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): June (291 h), July (269 h) and May (266 h).

Why Tallinn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

July, with an average of 17.6 °C (64 °F), day highs around 21.0 °C (70 °F) and night lows around 14.2 °C (58 °F).
What is the coldest month in Tallinn?
February, with an average of -4.8 °C (23 °F), day highs around -2.7 °C (27 °F) and night lows around -7.4 °C (19 °F).

Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 153 cm (60.3 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 6 months.
