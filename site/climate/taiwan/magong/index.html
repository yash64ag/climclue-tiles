Magong at a glance

Among the 67 towns ClimaClue covers in Taiwan, Magong is the 3rd driest (1,294 mm a year). Compared with Budai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Magong is 0.6 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 362 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.6 °C), averages 10.2 °C more than its coolest, January (17.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.4 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Magong, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Magong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Magong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.565°N, 119.586°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-604-2995), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
