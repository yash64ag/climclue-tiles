Jincheng at a glance

Among the 67 towns ClimaClue covers in Taiwan, Jincheng is the 2nd coolest (21.1 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,041 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,237 mm a year). Compared with Jinhu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Jincheng is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.7 °C), averages 14.2 °C more than its coolest, January (13.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.1 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 24 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Jincheng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jincheng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.434°N, 118.317°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-595-2983), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
