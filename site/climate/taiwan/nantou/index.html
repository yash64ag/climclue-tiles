Nantou at a glance

Among the 67 towns ClimaClue covers in Taiwan, Nantou is 49th warmest (22.5 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 1,848 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (2,535 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zhongxing New Village, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Nantou is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 318 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.2 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, January (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.4 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 38 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nantou, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Nantou

For mild weather, the best months to visit Nantou are January, October, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, October, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (27.2 °C), August (26.9 °C) and June (26.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (33 mm), December (43 mm) and January (49 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (244 h), August (223 h) and September (187 h).

Why Nantou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 38 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nantou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.916°N, 120.664°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-600-3006), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 105,682, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
