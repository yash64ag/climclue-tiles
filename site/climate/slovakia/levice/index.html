Levice at a glance

Among the 59 towns ClimaClue covers in Slovakia, Levice is 19th warmest (10.5 °C year average), 28th sunniest (about 1,730 hours of sunshine a year) and 44th wettest (590 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Komárno, Nitra Region (48.1°N 18.3°E), the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Levice is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.3 °C), averages 22.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 462 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Levice, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Levice has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 462 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Levice itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (48.216°N, 18.607°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-357-1986), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,556, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
