Ash Shaţrah at a glance

Among the 105 towns ClimaClue covers in Iraq, Ash Shaţrah is 7th warmest (26.7 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 3,535 hours of sunshine a year) and 79th wettest (127 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nasiriyah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Ash Shaţrah is 0.1 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (39.4 °C), averages 27.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 281 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ash Shaţrah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ash Shaţrah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 281 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ash Shaţrah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.409°N, 46.173°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-525-2261), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
