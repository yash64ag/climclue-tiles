Sadr City at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Sadr City is 21st warmest (25.3 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 3,480 hours of sunshine a year) and 27th wettest (164 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Abū Ghurayb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Sadr City is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 24 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (38.1 °C), averages 27.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 543 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sadr City, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sadr City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 543 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
