Nahiyat Ghammas at a glance

Among the 105 towns ClimaClue covers in Iraq, Nahiyat Ghammas is the 2nd sunniest (about 3,634 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (102 mm a year). Compared with Al Mishkhāb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Nahiyat Ghammas is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (37.9 °C), averages 26.3 °C more than its coolest, January (11.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.0 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 406 km from the sea.

Why Nahiyat Ghammas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 406 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nahiyat Ghammas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.743°N, 44.620°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-522-2246), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
