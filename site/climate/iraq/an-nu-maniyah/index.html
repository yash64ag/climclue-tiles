An Nu‘mānīyah at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, An Nu‘mānīyah is 14th warmest (26.0 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 3,486 hours of sunshine a year) and 26th wettest (169 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Al-Kut, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), An Nu‘mānīyah is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (39.0 °C), averages 27.3 °C more than its coolest, January (11.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 414 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of An Nu‘mānīyah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why An Nu‘mānīyah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 414 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
