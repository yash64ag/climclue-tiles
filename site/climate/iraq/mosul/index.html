Mosul at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Mosul is 39th warmest (21.0 °C year average), 31st sunniest (about 3,196 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (430 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Xebat, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Mosul is 0.7 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 66 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (35.4 °C), averages 28.2 °C more than its coolest, January (7.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 549 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mosul, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mosul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 549 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
