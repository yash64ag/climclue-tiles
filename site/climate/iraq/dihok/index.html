Dihok at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Dihok is the 4th coolest (18.0 °C year average). Compared with Sīnah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Dihok is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 0.8 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.0 °C), averages 27.4 °C more than its coolest, January (4.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 518 km from the sea.

Why Dihok has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 518 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dihok itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.866°N, 42.988°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-471-2229), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
