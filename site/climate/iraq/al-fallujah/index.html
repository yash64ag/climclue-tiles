Al Fallūjah at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Al Fallūjah is 30th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 3,528 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (121 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Abū Ghurayb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Al Fallūjah is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (37.0 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (10.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.4 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 589 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Fallūjah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Fallūjah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 589 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
