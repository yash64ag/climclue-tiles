Sinjār at a glance

Among the 105 towns ClimaClue covers in Iraq, Sinjār is 78th warmest (19.6 °C year average), 77th sunniest (about 3,141 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (348 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sumayl, the nearest covered town with different numbers (105 km away), Sinjār is 0.6 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 330 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.4 °C), averages 27.3 °C more than its coolest, January (6.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 511 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sinjār, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sinjār has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 511 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.3 °C. The 10 km area around Sinjār, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.6 °C a year, 2.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-476-2218), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
