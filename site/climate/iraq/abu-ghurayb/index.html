Abū Ghurayb at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Abū Ghurayb is 27th warmest (24.9 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 3,503 hours of sunshine a year) and 36th wettest (140 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baghdad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Abū Ghurayb is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (37.6 °C), averages 26.8 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 563 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Abū Ghurayb, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Abū Ghurayb has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 563 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
