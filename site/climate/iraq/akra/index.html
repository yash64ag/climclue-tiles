'Ākra at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, 'Ākra is the coolest (16.6 °C year average) and the 3rd wettest (918 mm a year). Compared with Baḥarkah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), 'Ākra is 2.7 °C cooler in July and 3.3 °C cooler in January, with 143 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.9 °C), averages 28.6 °C more than its coolest, January (2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 476 km from the sea.

Why 'Ākra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 476 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
