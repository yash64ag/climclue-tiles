Ramadi at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Ramadi is 31st warmest (23.9 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 3,539 hours of sunshine a year) and 46th wettest (114 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Al Fallūjah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Ramadi is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (36.5 °C), averages 26.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.2 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 623 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ramadi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ramadi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 623 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
