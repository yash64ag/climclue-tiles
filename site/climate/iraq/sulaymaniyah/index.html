Sulaymaniyah at a glance

Among the 105 towns ClimaClue covers in Iraq, Sulaymaniyah is 92nd warmest (17.5 °C year average), 93rd sunniest (about 3,060 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (897 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baynjiwayn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (47 km away), Sulaymaniyah is 3.0 °C warmer in July and 3.7 °C warmer in January, with 333 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.5 °C), averages 27.8 °C more than its coolest, January (3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 410 km from the sea.

Why Sulaymaniyah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 410 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sulaymaniyah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.565°N, 45.433°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-484-2254), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
