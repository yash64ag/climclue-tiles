Shelādiz at a glance

Among the 105 towns ClimaClue covers in Iraq, Shelādiz is 97th warmest (17.1 °C year average), 88th sunniest (about 3,076 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (897 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dêrelûk Devera Nihêliya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Shelādiz is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.2 °C), averages 30.5 °C more than its coolest, January (1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 474 km from the sea.

Why Shelādiz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 474 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.5 °C. The 10 km area around Shelādiz, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.8 °C a year, 3.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shelādiz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.033°N, 43.776°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-469-2237), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
