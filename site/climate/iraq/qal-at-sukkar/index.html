Qal‘at Sukkar at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Qal‘at Sukkar is 9th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 3,503 hours of sunshine a year) and 35th wettest (141 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ash Shaţrah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Qal‘at Sukkar is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (39.4 °C), averages 27.2 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 319 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qal‘at Sukkar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Qal‘at Sukkar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 319 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
