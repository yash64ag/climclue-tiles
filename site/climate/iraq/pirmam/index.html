Pīrmām at a glance

Among the 105 towns ClimaClue covers in Iraq, Pīrmām is the 4th coolest (15.5 °C year average) and the 4th wettest (1,078 mm a year). Compared with Shaqlāwah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Pīrmām is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 39 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.5 °C), averages 27.4 °C more than its coolest, January (2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 465 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pīrmām, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pīrmām has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 465 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.4 °C. The 10 km area around Pīrmām, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.1 °C a year, 2.6 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pīrmām itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.385°N, 44.197°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-476-2241), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
