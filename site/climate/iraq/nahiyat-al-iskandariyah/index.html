Nāḩiyat al Iskandarīyah at a glance

Among the 52 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iraq, Nāḩiyat al Iskandarīyah is 18th warmest (25.4 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 3,542 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (128 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Al-Musayab, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Nāḩiyat al Iskandarīyah is 0.3 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (38.0 °C), averages 26.7 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.0 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 510 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nāḩiyat al Iskandarīyah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nāḩiyat al Iskandarīyah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 510 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
