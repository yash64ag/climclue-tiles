Malakal at a glance

Among the 5 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Sudan, Malakal is the 2nd warmest (28.0 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,780 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (821 mm a year). Compared with Wau, the nearest covered town with different numbers (452 km away), Malakal is 1.8 °C warmer in April and about as warm in August, with 236 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (31.9 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, August (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.6 °C (71 °F), in November. The area lies about 1,093 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Malakal, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Malakal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,093 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
