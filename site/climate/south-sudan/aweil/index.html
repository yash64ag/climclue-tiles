Aweil at a glance

Among the 16 towns ClimaClue covers in South Sudan, Aweil is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,911 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Winejok, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Aweil is 0.2 °C cooler in April and about as warm in August, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (31.6 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, August (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.0 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,529 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Aweil has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,529 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Aweil itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.762°N, 27.392°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-752-2073), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
