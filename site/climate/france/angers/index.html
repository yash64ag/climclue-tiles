Angers at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Angers is 13th warmest (12.1 °C year average), 15th sunniest (about 1,918 hours of sunshine a year) and 35th wettest (673 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nantes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (81 km away), Angers is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 184 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.6 °C), averages 14.3 °C more than its coolest, January (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 129 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Angers, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Angers has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 129 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
