Montpellier at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Montpellier is the 4th warmest (14.7 °C year average). Compared with Nîmes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (46 km away), Montpellier is 1.3 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 16.4 °C more than its coolest, January (7.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 128% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Montpellier has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
