Toulon at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Toulon is the 5th warmest (14.6 °C year average) and the sunniest (about 2,764 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Marseille, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Toulon is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 152 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.9 °C), averages 15.2 °C more than its coolest, January (7.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 119% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Toulon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
