Le Havre at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Le Havre is 22nd warmest (11.3 °C year average), 24th sunniest (about 1,768 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (900 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Caen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Le Havre is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 134 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (17.9 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, January (5.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 7 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Le Havre, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Le Havre has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
