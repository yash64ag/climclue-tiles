Perpignan at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Perpignan is the warmest (15.2 °C year average). Compared with Montpellier, the nearest covered town with different numbers (129 km away), Perpignan is 0.6 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 93 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.1 °C), averages 16.9 °C more than its coolest, January (7.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 127% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Perpignan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
