Perpignan at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Perpignan is the 3rd warmest (15.4 °C year average). Compared with Montpellier, the nearest covered town with different numbers (129 km away), Perpignan is 0.9 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 64 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.5 °C), averages 17.0 °C more than its coolest, January (7.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Perpignan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Perpignan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.698°N, 2.895°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-413-1828), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
