Nantes at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Nantes is 12th warmest (12.1 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 1,918 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (857 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Angers, the nearest covered town with different numbers (81 km away), Nantes is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 184 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.3 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, January (5.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 51 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Nantes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 51 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
