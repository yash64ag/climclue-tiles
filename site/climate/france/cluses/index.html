Why Cluses has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 254 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.5 °C. The 10 km area around Cluses, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 7.4 °C a year, 2.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cluses itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.063°N, 6.575°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-379-1865), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
