Bordeaux at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Bordeaux is 9th warmest (13.7 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 2,053 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (853 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Limoges, the nearest covered town with different numbers (181 km away), Bordeaux is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 2.4 °C warmer in January, with 304 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.4 °C), averages 15.0 °C more than its coolest, January (6.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 63 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bordeaux, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bordeaux has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 63 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
