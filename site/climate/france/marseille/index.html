Marseille at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in France, Marseille is the 2nd warmest (15.2 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,734 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (582 mm a year). Compared with Aix-en-Provence, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Marseille is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 3.1 °C warmer in January, with 87 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 14.7 °C more than its coolest, January (8.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 8 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Marseille has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
