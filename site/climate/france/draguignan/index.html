Draguignan at a glance

Among the 657 towns ClimaClue covers in France, Draguignan is 73rd warmest (14.8 °C year average), 57th sunniest (about 2,598 hours of sunshine a year) and 182nd wettest (841 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fréjus, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Draguignan is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 2.5 °C cooler in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.9 °C), averages 17.3 °C more than its coolest, January (6.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Why Draguignan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
