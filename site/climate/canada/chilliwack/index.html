Chilliwack weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Chilliwack, Canada (British Columbia), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Chilliwack is warmest in August, with 24.7 °C (76 °F) days and 14.2 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 4.1 °C (39 °F) days and -0.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around November (284 mm); July is driest (59 mm).

Chilliwack at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Chilliwack is 15th warmest (10.5 °C year average), 283rd sunniest (about 1,666 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (1,813 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Abbotsford, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Chilliwack is 1.4 °C warmer in August and 0.7 °C cooler in December, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (19.5 °C), averages 17.9 °C more than its coolest, December (1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 74 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Chilliwack has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chilliwack itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (49.166°N, 121.953°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-348-580), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
