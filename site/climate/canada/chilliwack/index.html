Chilliwack at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Chilliwack is the 3rd least sunny (about 1,666 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th wettest (1,849 mm a year). Compared with Abbotsford, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Chilliwack is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 0.9 °C cooler in December, with 62 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 17.8 °C more than its coolest, December (1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 74 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Chilliwack has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
