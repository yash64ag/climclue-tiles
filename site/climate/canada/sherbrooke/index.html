Why Sherbrooke has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 200 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Sherbrooke climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Sherbrooke?
July, with an average of 19.3 °C (67 °F), day highs around 23.8 °C (75 °F) and night lows around 15.1 °C (59 °F).
What is the coldest month in Sherbrooke?
January, with an average of -9.8 °C (14 °F), day highs around -4.8 °C (23 °F) and night lows around -15.0 °C (5 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Sherbrooke?
August, with 138 mm (5.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,243 mm (48.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Sherbrooke?
February, with 70 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Sherbrooke?
July, with about 255 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Sherbrooke?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 276 cm (108.7 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 6 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Sherbrooke?
No month meets the definition used here (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation).

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 7.8 × 11 km, that contains Sherbrooke’s GeoNames location (45.400°N, 71.899°W; ClimaClue area sq-385-1081), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 129,447 (GeoNames).
