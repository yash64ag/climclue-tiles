Sherbrooke at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Sherbrooke is 61st warmest (5.8 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 1,814 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (1,243 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Trois-Rivières, the nearest covered town with different numbers (116 km away), Sherbrooke is 1.5 °C cooler in July and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 159 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.3 °C), averages 29.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 200 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Sherbrooke has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 200 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
