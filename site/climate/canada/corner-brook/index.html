Corner Brook weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Corner Brook, Canada (Newfoundland and Labrador), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Corner Brook is warmest in August, with 21.4 °C (71 °F) days and 11.9 °C nights, and coolest in February, with -3.7 °C (25 °F) days and -12.4 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 73 mm in April to 120 mm in August.

Why Corner Brook has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Corner Brook itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (48.950°N, 57.952°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-350-1220), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 19,316, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
