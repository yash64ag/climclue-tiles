Alma weather by month and climate

Alma at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Alma is 267th warmest (3.7 °C year average), 265th sunniest (about 1,741 hours of sunshine a year) and 179th wettest (980 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Saguenay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Alma is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.1 °C), averages 33.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-14.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 156 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 17 km from Alma measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.0 °C (3.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Alma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 156 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alma itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (48.550°N, 71.649°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-354-1083), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 29,526, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
