Halifax at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Halifax is 129th warmest (7.1 °C year average), 230th sunniest (about 1,835 hours of sunshine a year) and 34th wettest (1,487 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dartmouth, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Halifax is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.3 °C), averages 22.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Halifax has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Halifax itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.643°N, 63.577°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-393-1164), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 471,559, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
