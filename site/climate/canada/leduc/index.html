Leduc at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Leduc is 276th warmest (3.3 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,232 hours of sunshine a year) and 269th wettest (461 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sherwood Park, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Leduc is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.7 °C), averages 27.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 811 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Leduc has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 811 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Leduc itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.267°N, 113.552°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-307-664), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
