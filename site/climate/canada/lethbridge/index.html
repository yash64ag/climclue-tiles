Lethbridge at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Lethbridge is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,283 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (399 mm a year). Compared with Calgary, the nearest covered town with different numbers (175 km away), Lethbridge is 2.8 °C warmer in July and 2.3 °C warmer in January, with 43 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 731 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 123% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Lethbridge has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 731 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
