Saanich at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Saanich is 23rd warmest (10.3 °C year average), 173rd sunniest (about 1,936 hours of sunshine a year) and 143rd wettest (1,040 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Central Saanich, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Saanich is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 0.6 °C warmer in December, with 76 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.2 °C), averages 10.9 °C more than its coolest, December (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 7 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Saanich has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-354-566), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
