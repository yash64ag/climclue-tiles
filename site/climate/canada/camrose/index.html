Camrose at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Camrose is 280th warmest (3.1 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 2,198 hours of sunshine a year) and 281st wettest (433 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Leduc, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Camrose is about as warm in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.7 °C), averages 28.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 840 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Camrose has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 840 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Camrose itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.017°N, 112.835°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-309-671), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
