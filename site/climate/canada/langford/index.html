Langford at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Langford is 33rd warmest (10.2 °C year average), 210th sunniest (about 1,887 hours of sunshine a year) and 54th wettest (1,213 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Colwood, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Langford is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 67 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.3 °C), averages 11.4 °C more than its coolest, December (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Langford has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Langford itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (48.450°N, 123.503°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-355-564), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
