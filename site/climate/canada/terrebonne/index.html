Terrebonne at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Terrebonne is 57th warmest (6.5 °C year average), 42nd sunniest (about 1,965 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (1,076 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rivière-des-Prairies–Pointe-aux-Trembles, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Terrebonne is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 16 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.1 °C), averages 31.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-10.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 272 km from the sea.

Why Terrebonne has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 272 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
