Markham at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Markham is 29th warmest (7.9 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 1,971 hours of sunshine a year) and 52nd wettest (918 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Richmond Hill, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Markham is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.8 °C), averages 25.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 565 km from the sea.

Why Markham has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 565 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
