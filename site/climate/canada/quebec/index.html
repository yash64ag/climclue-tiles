Québec at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Québec is 63rd warmest (5.1 °C year average), 61st sunniest (about 1,811 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (1,254 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lévis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Québec is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.8 °C), averages 31.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 51 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 111% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Québec has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 51 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the warmest month in Québec?
July, with an average of 19.8 °C (68 °F), day highs around 24.4 °C (76 °F) and night lows around 15.3 °C (60 °F).
What is the coldest month in Québec?
January, with an average of -11.9 °C (10 °F), day highs around -7.1 °C (19 °F) and night lows around -16.3 °C (3 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Québec?
July, with 133 mm (5.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,254 mm (49.4 in) in a typical year.

March, with 77 mm (3.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Québec?

Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 328 cm (129.0 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 7 months.
