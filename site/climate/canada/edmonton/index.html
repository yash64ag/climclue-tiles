Edmonton weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Edmonton, Canada (Alberta), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Edmonton is warmest in July, with 23.2 °C (74 °F) days and 11.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -7.0 °C (19 °F) days and -17.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (96 mm); February is driest (11 mm).

Edmonton at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Edmonton is 277th warmest (3.2 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 2,198 hours of sunshine a year) and 275th wettest (448 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sherwood Park, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Edmonton is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.1 °C), averages 29.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 835 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Edmonton, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Edmonton

For mild weather, the best months to visit Edmonton are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (17.1 °C), August (16.0 °C) and June (14.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (11 mm), December (16 mm) and March (17 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (298 h), June (277 h) and May (271 h).

Why Edmonton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 835 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Edmonton itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.550°N, 113.469°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-304-665), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,010,899, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
