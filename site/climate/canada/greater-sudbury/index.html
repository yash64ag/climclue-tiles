Greater Sudbury at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Greater Sudbury is 65th warmest (4.6 °C year average), 52nd sunniest (about 1,904 hours of sunshine a year) and 56th wettest (894 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Barrie, the nearest covered town with different numbers (254 km away), Greater Sudbury is 1.2 °C cooler in July and 4.6 °C cooler in January, with 36 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.1 °C), averages 31.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 540 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 89% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Greater Sudbury has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 540 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.0 °C.
