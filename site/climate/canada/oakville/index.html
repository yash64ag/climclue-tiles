Why Oakville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 542 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Does it snow in Oakville?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 144 cm (56.6 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 4 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Oakville?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.1 × 11 km, that contains Oakville’s GeoNames location (43.450°N, 79.683°W; ClimaClue area sq-405-1003), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 213,759 (GeoNames).
