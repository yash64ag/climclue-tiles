Vernon at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Vernon is 59th warmest (8.8 °C year average), 181st sunniest (about 1,928 hours of sunshine a year) and 284th wettest (418 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lake Country, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Vernon is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 1.0 °C cooler in December, with 10 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.6 °C), averages 23.4 °C more than its coolest, December (-2.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 303 km from the sea.

Why Vernon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 303 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vernon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.267°N, 119.273°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-337-607), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
