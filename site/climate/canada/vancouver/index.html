Vancouver at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Vancouver is the 5th warmest (10.5 °C year average) and the 3rd wettest (2,071 mm a year). Compared with Richmond, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Vancouver is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C cooler in December, with 493 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (17.9 °C), averages 13.8 °C more than its coolest, December (4.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 2.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 5 km from Vancouver measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 134% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Best time to visit Vancouver

For mild weather, the best months to visit Vancouver are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.

Why Vancouver has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vancouver itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (49.250°N, 123.119°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-347-568), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
