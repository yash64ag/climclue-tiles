Orléans at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Orléans is 56th warmest (6.5 °C year average), 26th sunniest (about 1,989 hours of sunshine a year) and 35th wettest (1,006 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gloucester, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Orléans is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 15 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.9 °C), averages 31.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-10.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 417 km from the sea.

Why Orléans has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 417 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
