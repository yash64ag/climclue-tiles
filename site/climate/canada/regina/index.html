Regina at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Regina is the 5th coolest (3.5 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,309 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (405 mm a year). Compared with Saskatoon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (235 km away), Regina is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 1.8 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.9 °C), averages 32.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-13.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 1,096 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Regina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,096 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
