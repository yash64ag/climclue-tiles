Red Deer at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Red Deer is 67th warmest (3.7 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 2,090 hours of sunshine a year) and 66th wettest (494 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Calgary, the nearest covered town with different numbers (137 km away), Red Deer is about as warm in July and 2.8 °C cooler in January, with 53 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.6 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-10.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 739 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Red Deer has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 739 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
