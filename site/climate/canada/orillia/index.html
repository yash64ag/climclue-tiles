Orillia at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Orillia is 179th warmest (6.7 °C year average), 144th sunniest (about 1,964 hours of sunshine a year) and 169th wettest (997 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Barrie, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Orillia is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 99 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.2 °C), averages 28.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 638 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Orillia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Orillia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.609°N, 79.421°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-393-1005), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
