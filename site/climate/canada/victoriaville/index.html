Victoriaville at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Victoriaville is 232nd warmest (5.8 °C year average), 240th sunniest (about 1,820 hours of sunshine a year) and 48th wettest (1,313 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Drummondville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Victoriaville is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 199 mm more precipitation a year. Why Victoriaville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 149 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.2 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Victoriaville itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.050°N, 71.966°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-379-1080), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 34,426, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
