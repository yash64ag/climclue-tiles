Saint-Hyacinthe at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Saint-Hyacinthe is 192nd warmest (6.4 °C year average), 270th sunniest (about 1,736 hours of sunshine a year) and 83rd wettest (1,101 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mont-Saint-Hilaire, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Saint-Hyacinthe is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 16 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.5 °C), averages 30.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-10.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 235 km from the sea.

Why Saint-Hyacinthe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 235 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.9 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Saint-Hyacinthe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.631°N, 72.957°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-383-1070), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
