Stratford at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Stratford is 114th warmest (7.3 °C year average), 229th sunniest (about 1,836 hours of sunshine a year) and 114th wettest (1,057 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Woodstock, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Stratford is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 40 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.9 °C), averages 26.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-6.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 596 km from the sea.

Why Stratford has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 596 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.0 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Stratford itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.367°N, 80.950°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-406-990), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 31,465, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
