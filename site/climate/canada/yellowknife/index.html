Yellowknife weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Yellowknife, Canada (Northwest Territories), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Yellowknife is warmest in July, with 21.0 °C (70 °F) days and 11.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -21.0 °C (-6 °F) days and -29.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (55 mm); April is driest (15 mm).

Yellowknife at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Yellowknife is the coolest (-4.1 °C year average). Compared with Fort McMurray, the nearest covered town with different numbers (659 km away), Yellowknife is 1.9 °C cooler in July and 8.0 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.4 °C), averages 41.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-25.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, May, October, November and December. The area lies about 590 km from the sea.

Why Yellowknife has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 590 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 41.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yellowknife itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (62.454°N, 114.372°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-215-656), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,340, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
