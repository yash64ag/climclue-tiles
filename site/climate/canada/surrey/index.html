Why Surrey has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Surrey climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Surrey?
August, with an average of 18.1 °C (64 °F), day highs around 24.0 °C (75 °F) and night lows around 13.6 °C (56 °F).
What is the coldest month in Surrey?
December, with an average of 3.1 °C (38 °F), day highs around 5.2 °C (41 °F) and night lows around 0.3 °C (33 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Surrey?
November, with 283 mm (11.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,827 mm (71.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Surrey?
July, with 57 mm (2.2 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Surrey?
July, with about 271 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Surrey?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 156 cm (61.3 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 5 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Surrey?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: June, July, August and September.
What type of climate does Surrey have?
Oceanic (Köppen Cfb). Mild all year, with rain in every season. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 7.3 × 11 km, that contains Surrey’s GeoNames location (49.106°N, 122.825°W; ClimaClue area sq-348-571), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 568,322 (GeoNames).
