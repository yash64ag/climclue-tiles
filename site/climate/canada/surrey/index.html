Surrey at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Surrey is 7th warmest (10.3 °C year average), 64th sunniest (about 1,773 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (1,827 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Langley, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Surrey is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 172 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.1 °C), averages 14.9 °C more than its coolest, December (3.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 0.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Surrey has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
