Hamilton at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Hamilton is 16th warmest (9.0 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 1,989 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (982 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Burlington, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Hamilton is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 53 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.9 °C), averages 26.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 532 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Hamilton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 532 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
