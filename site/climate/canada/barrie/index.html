Barrie at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Barrie is 41st warmest (6.9 °C year average), 45th sunniest (about 1,952 hours of sunshine a year) and 50th wettest (930 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Richmond Hill, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Barrie is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 1.7 °C cooler in January, with 67 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.3 °C), averages 27.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 638 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 95% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Barrie has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 638 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
