Coquitlam at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Coquitlam is the 4th warmest (10.5 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,653 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (2,503 mm a year). Compared with Burnaby, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Coquitlam is 0.6 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 267 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.6 °C), averages 15.5 °C more than its coolest, December (3.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 0.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 36 km from the sea.

Why Coquitlam has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 36 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
