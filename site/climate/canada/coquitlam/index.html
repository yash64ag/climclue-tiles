Coquitlam at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Coquitlam is the 2nd least sunny (about 1,653 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (2,727 mm a year). Compared with Burnaby, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Coquitlam is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 393 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.4 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, December (2.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in December. The area lies about 36 km from the sea.

Why Coquitlam has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 36 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Coquitlam itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (49.285°N, 122.782°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-347-572), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
