Calgary at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Calgary is the 2nd sunniest (about 2,298 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (441 mm a year). Compared with Red Deer, the nearest covered town with different numbers (137 km away), Calgary is about as warm in July and 2.8 °C warmer in January, with 53 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.6 °C), averages 23.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 675 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Calgary

For mild weather, the best months to visit Calgary are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (16.6 °C), August (15.7 °C) and June (13.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (11 mm), January (11 mm) and December (12 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (311 h), June (277 h) and August (275 h).

Why Calgary has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 675 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
