Kamloops at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Kamloops is the driest (301 mm a year). Compared with Salmon Arm, the nearest covered town with different numbers (74 km away), Kamloops is about as warm in July and 1.7 °C cooler in December, with 184 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.0 °C), averages 24.1 °C more than its coolest, December (-4.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 265 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Best time to visit Kamloops

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kamloops are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (20.0 °C), August (19.5 °C) and June (17.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (16 mm), March (17 mm) and April (17 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (293 h), August (265 h) and June (244 h).

Why Kamloops has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 265 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.1 °C. The 10 km area around Kamloops, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 7.0 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kamloops itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.666°N, 120.319°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-333-596), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 104,460, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
