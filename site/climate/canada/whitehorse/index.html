Whitehorse at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Whitehorse is the 2nd coolest (-0.1 °C year average) and the 2nd driest (336 mm a year). Compared with Terrace, the nearest covered town with different numbers (788 km away), Whitehorse is 2.6 °C cooler in July and 14.3 °C cooler in January, with 1,170 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (14.6 °C), averages 31.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-16.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 200 km from the sea.

Why Whitehorse has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 200 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Whitehorse itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (60.716°N, 135.054°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-232-449), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
