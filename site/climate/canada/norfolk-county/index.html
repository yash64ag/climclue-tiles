Norfolk County weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Norfolk County, Canada (Ontario), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Norfolk County is warmest in July, with 27.8 °C (82 °F) days and 16.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -0.7 °C (31 °F) days and -8.2 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 66 mm in February to 99 mm in September.

Norfolk County at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Norfolk County is 62nd warmest (8.9 °C year average), 109th sunniest (about 1,979 hours of sunshine a year) and 156th wettest (1,021 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tillsonburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Norfolk County is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.9 °C), averages 26.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 522 km from the sea.

Why Norfolk County has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 522 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Norfolk County itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.833°N, 80.383°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-411-996), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
