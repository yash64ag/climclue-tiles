St. Catharines at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, St. Catharines is 14th warmest (9.4 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 2,007 hours of sunshine a year) and 47th wettest (947 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oakville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (47 km away), St. Catharines is 0.6 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.4 °C), averages 25.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 497 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why St. Catharines has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 497 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
