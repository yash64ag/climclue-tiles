Windsor at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Windsor is 12th warmest (9.9 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 2,191 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (967 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with London, the nearest covered town with different numbers (164 km away), Windsor is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 70 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 26.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 661 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Windsor has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 661 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
