Victoria at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Victoria is 23rd warmest (10.4 °C year average), 57th sunniest (about 2,014 hours of sunshine a year) and 216th wettest (934 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oak Bay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Victoria is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.4 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, December (5.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 3.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 3 km from Victoria measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 141% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Victoria

For mild weather, the best months to visit Victoria are July and August.

Why Victoria has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Victoria climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Victoria?
August, with an average of 16.4 °C (62 °F), day highs around 21.3 °C (70 °F) and night lows around 11.6 °C (53 °F).
What is the coldest month in Victoria?
December, with an average of 5.2 °C (41 °F), day highs around 7.5 °C (46 °F) and night lows around 3.0 °C (37 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Victoria?
November, with 175 mm (6.9 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 934 mm (36.8 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Victoria?
July, with 18 mm (0.7 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Victoria?
July, with about 310 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Victoria?
Yes. About 11 cm (4.4 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in December; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time to visit Victoria?
For mild weather (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August. Climate only, from 1981–2010 averages: crowds and prices are not considered.
How warm is the sea in Victoria?
The nearby open sea averages 12.5 °C (55 °F) in August, its warmest month, and 7.6 °C (46 °F) in February. It stays below 20 °C (68 °F) all year. 1981–2010 averages (ERA5); beaches and shallow bays are often a little warmer in summer.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Victoria itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (48.436°N, 123.352°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-355-566), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 289,625, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
