Richmond at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Richmond is the warmest (10.8 °C year average). Compared with Vancouver, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Richmond is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in December, with 459 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.1 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, December (4.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 2.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 7 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Richmond has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
