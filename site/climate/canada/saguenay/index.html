Saguenay at a glance

Among the 73 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Canada, Saguenay is 68th warmest (3.5 °C year average), 67th sunniest (about 1,758 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (1,029 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Québec, the nearest covered town with different numbers (179 km away), Saguenay is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 3.1 °C cooler in January, with 225 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.8 °C), averages 33.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-15.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 111 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Saguenay has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 111 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
