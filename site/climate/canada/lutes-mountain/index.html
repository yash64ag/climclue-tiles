Lutes Mountain at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Lutes Mountain is 235th warmest (5.5 °C year average), 205th sunniest (about 1,897 hours of sunshine a year) and 37th wettest (1,408 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Moncton, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Lutes Mountain is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 208 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.5 °C), averages 27.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Why Lutes Mountain has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lutes Mountain itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.146°N, 64.908°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-378-1150), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
