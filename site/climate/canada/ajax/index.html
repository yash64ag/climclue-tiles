Ajax at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Ajax is 82nd warmest (8.2 °C year average), 127th sunniest (about 1,969 hours of sunshine a year) and 223rd wettest (917 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Whitby, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Ajax is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.2 °C), averages 26.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 559 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 13 km from Ajax measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.1 °C (3.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Ajax has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 559 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ajax itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.850°N, 79.033°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-401-1009), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 119,677, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
