Laflèche weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Laflèche, Canada (Quebec), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Laflèche is warmest in July, with 26.9 °C (80 °F) days and 15.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -5.0 °C (23 °F) days and -14.1 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 64 mm in February to 102 mm in July.

Laflèche at a glance

Among the 297 towns ClimaClue covers in Canada, Laflèche is 145th warmest (7.0 °C year average), 175th sunniest (about 1,932 hours of sunshine a year) and 122nd wettest (1,051 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Greenfield Park, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Laflèche is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.3 °C), averages 30.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 272 km from the sea.

Why Laflèche has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 272 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.9 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Laflèche itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.500°N, 73.466°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-384-1065), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,499, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
