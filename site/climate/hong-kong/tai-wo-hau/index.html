Tai Wo Hau at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Hong Kong, Tai Wo Hau is 58th warmest (23.0 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 1,963 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (2,280 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tsuen Wan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Tai Wo Hau is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 24 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 12.8 °C more than its coolest, January (15.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.4 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tai Wo Hau, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tai Wo Hau has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tai Wo Hau itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.367°N, 114.123°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-616-2941), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,848, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
