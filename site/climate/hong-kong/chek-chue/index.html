Chek Chue at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Hong Kong, Chek Chue is the sunniest (about 1,999 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Shek O, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Chek Chue is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 100 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, January (16.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.6 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Chek Chue has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chek Chue itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.218°N, 114.212°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-617-2942), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
