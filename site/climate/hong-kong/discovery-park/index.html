Discovery Park at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Hong Kong, Discovery Park is 113th warmest (23.4 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 1,963 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (2,367 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tsuen Wan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Discovery Park is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 111 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.9 °C), averages 12.5 °C more than its coolest, January (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.5 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Discovery Park, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Discovery Park has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Discovery Park itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.376°N, 114.111°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-616-2941), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,325, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
