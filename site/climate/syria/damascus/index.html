Damascus at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Syria, Damascus is 55th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 45th sunniest (about 3,075 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (283 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with al-Yarmūk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Damascus is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 24 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.2 °C), averages 20.3 °C more than its coolest, January (6.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 85 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Damascus, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Damascus has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 85 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C. The 10 km area around Damascus, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.5 °C a year, 1.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Damascus itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.510°N, 36.291°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-504-2162), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,569,394, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
