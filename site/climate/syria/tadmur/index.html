Tadmur at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Syria, Tadmur is the driest (119 mm a year). Compared with Al Qaryatayn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (103 km away), Tadmur is 3.4 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 72 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.1 °C), averages 23.5 °C more than its coolest, January (6.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 220 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 89% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tadmur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 220 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tadmur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.562°N, 38.284°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-494-2182), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
