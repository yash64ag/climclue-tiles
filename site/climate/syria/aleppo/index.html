Aleppo at a glance

Among the 13 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Syria, Aleppo is 10th warmest (17.8 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 2,999 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (400 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Al Bāb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Aleppo is 1.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 22.1 °C more than its coolest, January (6.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 100 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Aleppo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Aleppo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 100 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
