Dūmā at a glance

Among the 13 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Syria, Dūmā is the 3rd sunniest (about 3,106 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (230 mm a year). Compared with Damascus, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Dūmā is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 53 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 20.9 °C more than its coolest, January (7.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 100 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dūmā, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dūmā has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 100 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dūmā itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.572°N, 36.403°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-504-2164), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
