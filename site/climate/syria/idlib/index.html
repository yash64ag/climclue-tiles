Idlib at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Syria, Idlib is 66th warmest (17.2 °C year average), 80th sunniest (about 2,965 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (543 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Binnish, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Idlib is 0.6 °C cooler in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 89 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.1 °C), averages 20.5 °C more than its coolest, January (6.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 72 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Idlib, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Idlib has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 72 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Idlib itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.931°N, 36.634°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-480-2166), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 128,840, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
