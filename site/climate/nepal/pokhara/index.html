Pokhara at a glance

Among the 46 towns ClimaClue covers in Nepal, Pokhara is the wettest (3,069 mm a year). Compared with Bāgluṅ, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Pokhara is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 1.8 °C cooler in January, with 965 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.7 °C), averages 13.6 °C more than its coolest, January (11.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 822 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pokhara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Pokhara

For mild weather, the best months to visit Pokhara are March, April, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, April, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (24.7 °C), August (24.7 °C) and June (24.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (17 mm), December (18 mm) and January (30 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (269 h), March (235 h) and May (213 h).

Why Pokhara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 822 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pokhara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.267°N, 83.969°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-557-2639), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
