Butwāl at a glance

Among the 46 towns ClimaClue covers in Nepal, Butwāl is 15th warmest (24.4 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,309 hours of sunshine a year) and 10th wettest (2,200 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Siddharthanagar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Butwāl is 0.8 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 328 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (29.7 °C), averages 14.7 °C more than its coolest, January (15.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.1 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 784 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Butwāl, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Butwāl has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 784 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Butwāl itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.691°N, 83.453°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-563-2634), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
