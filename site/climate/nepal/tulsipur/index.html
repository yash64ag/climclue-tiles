Tulsi̇̄pur at a glance

Among the 46 towns ClimaClue covers in Nepal, Tulsi̇̄pur is 25th warmest (21.7 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,390 hours of sunshine a year) and 27th wettest (1,814 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nepalgunj, the nearest covered town with different numbers (67 km away), Tulsi̇̄pur is 3.7 °C cooler in June and 1.5 °C cooler in January, with 208 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.5 °C), averages 14.8 °C more than its coolest, January (12.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 894 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tulsi̇̄pur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Tulsi̇̄pur

For mild weather, the best months to visit Tulsi̇̄pur are February, March, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (27.5 °C), May (26.9 °C) and July (26.6 °C).

Why Tulsi̇̄pur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 894 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tulsi̇̄pur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.133°N, 82.298°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-558-2622), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
