Kathmandu at a glance

Among the 15 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nepal, Kathmandu is the coolest (17.7 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 1,990 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Pātan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Kathmandu is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 292 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.6 °C), averages 12.6 °C more than its coolest, January (10.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 717 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kathmandu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kathmandu

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kathmandu are March, April and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, April and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (22.6 °C), July (22.6 °C) and June (22.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (9 mm), December (12 mm) and January (22 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (270 h), March (233 h) and May (191 h).

Why Kathmandu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 717 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
