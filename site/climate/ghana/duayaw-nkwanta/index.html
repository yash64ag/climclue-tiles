Duayaw-Nkwanta at a glance

Among the 98 towns ClimaClue covers in Ghana, Duayaw-Nkwanta is 76th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 2,031 hours of sunshine a year) and 26th wettest (1,362 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bechem, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Duayaw-Nkwanta is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C cooler in August, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.2 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, August (24.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.3 °C (69 °F), in January. The area lies about 250 km from the sea.

Why Duayaw-Nkwanta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 250 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Duayaw-Nkwanta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.175°N, 2.100°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-768-1779), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
