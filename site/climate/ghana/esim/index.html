Esim at a glance

Among the 98 towns ClimaClue covers in Ghana, Esim is the 5th wettest (1,671 mm a year). Compared with Takoradi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (54 km away), Esim is 0.1 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 413 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.5 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Esim, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Esim

For mild weather, the best months to visit Esim are August and September.

Why Esim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Esim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.866°N, 2.242°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-791-1777), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
