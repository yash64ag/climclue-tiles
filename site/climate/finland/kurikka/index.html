Kurikka weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kurikka, Finland (South Ostrobothnia), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kurikka is warmest in July, with 21.9 °C (71 °F) days and 11.5 °C nights, and coolest in February, with -3.1 °C (26 °F) days and -10.5 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 29 mm in April to 83 mm in July.

Kurikka at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Kurikka is the 2nd least sunny (about 1,312 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Seinäjoki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Kurikka is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.7 °C), averages 23.5 °C more than its coolest, February (-6.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 65 km from the sea.

Why Kurikka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 65 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kurikka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (62.617°N, 22.417°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-213-2024), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 19,474, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
