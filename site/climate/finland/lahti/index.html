Lahti at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Finland, Lahti is the 3rd least sunny (about 1,437 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (671 mm a year). Compared with Vantaa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (84 km away), Lahti is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C cooler in February, with 41 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.4 °C), averages 24.1 °C more than its coolest, February (-6.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 80 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Lahti has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 80 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
