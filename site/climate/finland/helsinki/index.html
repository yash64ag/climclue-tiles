Helsinki at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Helsinki is 16th warmest (5.3 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 1,594 hours of sunshine a year) and 58th wettest (674 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Myyrmäki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Helsinki is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 80 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.3 °C), averages 22.9 °C more than its coolest, February (-5.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Helsinki has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Helsinki itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (60.170°N, 24.935°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-238-2049), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 658,864, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
