Varkaus at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Varkaus is 63rd warmest (3.8 °C year average), 71st sunniest (about 1,358 hours of sunshine a year) and 63rd wettest (669 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pieksämäki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Varkaus is 0.5 °C warmer in July and about as warm in February, with 52 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.4 °C), averages 25.8 °C more than its coolest, February (-8.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 202 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Varkaus has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 202 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Varkaus itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (62.315°N, 27.873°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-216-2078), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
