Kouvola at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Kouvola is 36th warmest (4.7 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 1,407 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (728 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Karhula, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Kouvola is about as warm in July and 0.6 °C cooler in February, with 53 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.6 °C), averages 24.6 °C more than its coolest, February (-7.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 50 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 119% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kouvola has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 50 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kouvola itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (60.867°N, 26.700°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-231-2067), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
