Kontiolahti weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kontiolahti, Finland (North Karelia), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kontiolahti at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Kontiolahti is 71st warmest (3.0 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 1,548 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (743 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Joensuu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Kontiolahti is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 26.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 257 km from the sea.

Why Kontiolahti has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 257 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.4 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kontiolahti itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (62.760°N, 29.847°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-212-2098), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
