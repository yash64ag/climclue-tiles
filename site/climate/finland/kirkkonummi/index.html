Kirkkonummi weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kirkkonummi, Finland (Uusimaa), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kirkkonummi at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Kirkkonummi is 15th warmest (5.3 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 1,495 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (742 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Espoo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Kirkkonummi is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 22.3 °C more than its coolest, February (-5.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kirkkonummi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kirkkonummi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kirkkonummi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (60.124°N, 24.439°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-238-2044), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
