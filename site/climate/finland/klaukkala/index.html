Klaukkala at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Klaukkala is 20th warmest (5.0 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 1,537 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (738 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nurmijärvi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Klaukkala is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.3 °C), averages 23.4 °C more than its coolest, February (-6.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Why Klaukkala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Klaukkala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (60.382°N, 24.751°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-236-2047), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
