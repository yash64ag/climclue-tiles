Kauhava weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kauhava, Finland (South Ostrobothnia), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kauhava is warmest in July, with 22.7 °C (73 °F) days and 11.1 °C nights, and coolest in February, with -3.1 °C (26 °F) days and -11.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (84 mm); February is driest (28 mm).

Kauhava at a glance

Among the 78 towns ClimaClue covers in Finland, Kauhava is the 4th least sunny (about 1,316 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (609 mm a year). Compared with Seinäjoki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Kauhava is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 24.2 °C more than its coolest, February (-7.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Why Kauhava has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kauhava itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (63.103°N, 23.071°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-208-2030), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
