Longyearbyen at a glance

Its warmest month, July (5.8 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, February (-14.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, May, September, October, November and December. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 5 km from Longyearbyen by up to 2.9 °C (5.3 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Longyearbyen

No month in Longyearbyen is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are June (1.8 °C, 22 mm), July (5.8 °C, 34 mm) and August (4.7 °C, 41 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (5.8 °C), August (4.7 °C) and June (1.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): May (17 mm), June (22 mm) and April (26 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (266 h), April (226 h) and June (206 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Longyearbyen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Low sun and long winters keep it cold; even the warmest month stays below 10 °C, too cool for trees. Cold air holds little moisture, so precipitation is usually light. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
