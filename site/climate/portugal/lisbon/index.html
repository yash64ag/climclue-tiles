Lisbon at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Portugal, Lisbon is the 3rd warmest (16.1 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,740 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (724 mm a year). Compared with Amadora, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Lisbon is 0.7 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.4 °C), averages 10.2 °C more than its coolest, January (11.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Lisbon measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.0 °C (3.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Best time to visit Lisbon

Why Lisbon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
