Porto at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Portugal, Porto is the 3rd least sunny (about 2,422 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,432 mm a year). Compared with Braga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (47 km away), Porto is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 1.6 °C warmer in January, with 140 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.9 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, January (10.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.1 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 8 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Porto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
