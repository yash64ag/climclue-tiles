Funchal at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Portugal, Funchal is the 2nd warmest (16.3 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,143 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (2,100 mm a year). Compared with Queluz, the nearest covered town with different numbers (968 km away), Funchal is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 1.1 °C warmer in February, with 1,367 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.8 °C), averages 7.9 °C more than its coolest, February (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.4 °C (53 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Funchal, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Funchal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
