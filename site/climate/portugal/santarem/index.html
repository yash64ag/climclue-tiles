Santarém at a glance

Among the 173 towns ClimaClue covers in Portugal, Santarém is 47th warmest (16.2 °C year average), 83rd sunniest (about 2,680 hours of sunshine a year) and 160th wettest (660 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Azambuja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Santarém is about as warm in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.7 °C), averages 12.9 °C more than its coolest, January (9.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 61 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santarém, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santarém has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 61 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santarém itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.234°N, 8.686°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-447-1713), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
