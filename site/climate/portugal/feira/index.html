Feira at a glance

Among the 173 towns ClimaClue covers in Portugal, Feira is 148th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 112th sunniest (about 2,461 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,727 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with São João da Madeira, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Feira is about as warm in August and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 137 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.5 °C), averages 11.1 °C more than its coolest, January (9.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.4 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 17 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 24 km from Feira measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 151% of the station’s). Why Feira has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Feira itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.925°N, 8.543°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-430-1714), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
