Amadora at a glance

Among the 173 towns ClimaClue covers in Portugal, Amadora is 82nd warmest (15.8 °C year average), 47th sunniest (about 2,731 hours of sunshine a year) and 93rd wettest (751 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Falagueira, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Amadora is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.8 °C), averages 9.6 °C more than its coolest, January (11.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.1 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 21 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 8 km from Amadora measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.6 °C (4.7 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Amadora has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Amadora itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.754°N, 9.231°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-452-1707), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
