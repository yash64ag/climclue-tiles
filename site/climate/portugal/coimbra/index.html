Coimbra at a glance

Among the 173 towns ClimaClue covers in Portugal, Coimbra is 109th warmest (15.5 °C year average), 98th sunniest (about 2,551 hours of sunshine a year) and 72nd wettest (996 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santo António dos Olivais, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Coimbra is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.8 °C), averages 12.6 °C more than its coolest, January (9.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.1 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 42 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Coimbra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 42 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Coimbra itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.207°N, 8.420°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-437-1715), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
