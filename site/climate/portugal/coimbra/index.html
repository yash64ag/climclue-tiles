Coimbra at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Portugal, Coimbra is 6th warmest (15.6 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 2,551 hours of sunshine a year) and 5th wettest (995 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Leiria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (61 km away), Coimbra is 1.7 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 145 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.8 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, January (9.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 42 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Coimbra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 42 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
