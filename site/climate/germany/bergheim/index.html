Bergheim at a glance

Among the 962 towns ClimaClue covers in Germany, Bergheim is 63rd warmest (10.5 °C year average), 799th sunniest (about 1,439 hours of sunshine a year) and 661st wettest (767 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Elsdorf, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Bergheim is 0.1 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.8 °C), averages 16.1 °C more than its coolest, January (2.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 218 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Bergheim has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 218 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bergheim itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.956°N, 6.640°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-330-1866), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 63,558, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
