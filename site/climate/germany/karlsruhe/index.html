Karlsruhe at a glance

Among the 962 towns ClimaClue covers in Germany, Karlsruhe is 6th warmest (11.2 °C year average), 89th sunniest (about 1,653 hours of sunshine a year) and 300th wettest (883 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ettlingen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Karlsruhe is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 166 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.6 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 444 km from the sea.

Why Karlsruhe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 444 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Karlsruhe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (49.009°N, 8.404°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-349-1884), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 283,799, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
