Rostock at a glance

Among the 80 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Germany, Rostock is 72nd warmest (8.9 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 1,652 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (663 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lübeck, the nearest covered town with different numbers (98 km away), Rostock is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 77 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.9 °C), averages 17.1 °C more than its coolest, January (0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 11 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Rostock has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
