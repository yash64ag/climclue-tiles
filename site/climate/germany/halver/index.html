Halver at a glance

Among the 962 towns ClimaClue covers in Germany, Halver is 920th warmest (8.2 °C year average), 774th sunniest (about 1,442 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (1,407 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kierspe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Halver is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. The area lies about 252 km from the sea.

Why Halver has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 252 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Halver itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (51.186°N, 7.498°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-328-1874), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,650, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
