Ulm at a glance

Among the 80 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Germany, Ulm is 74th warmest (8.8 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 1,644 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (861 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Reutlingen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (59 km away), Ulm is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.1 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 380 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 115% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ulm has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 380 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
