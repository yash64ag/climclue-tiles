Seesen weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Seesen, Germany (Lower Saxony), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Seesen at a glance

Among the 962 towns ClimaClue covers in Germany, Seesen is 887th warmest (8.5 °C year average), 582nd sunniest (about 1,511 hours of sunshine a year) and 403rd wettest (850 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Clausthal-Zellerfeld, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Seesen is 2.3 °C warmer in July and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 629 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.4 °C), averages 17.3 °C more than its coolest, January (0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 241 km from the sea.

Why Seesen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 241 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Seesen itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (51.891°N, 10.178°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-321-1901), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
