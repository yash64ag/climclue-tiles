Danané at a glance

Among the 182 towns ClimaClue covers in Ivory Coast, Danané is 176th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 44th sunniest (about 2,148 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (1,933 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mahapleu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Danané is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 59 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.8 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, August (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.5 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 260 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Danané, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Danané has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 260 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Danané itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.260°N, 8.155°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-767-1718), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 78,614, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
