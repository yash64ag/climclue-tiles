Zagné at a glance

Among the 182 towns ClimaClue covers in Ivory Coast, Zagné is 141st warmest (25.6 °C year average), 133rd sunniest (about 2,009 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (1,612 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nizahon II, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Zagné is 0.2 °C cooler in February and about as warm in August, with 133 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.9 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 199 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Zagné, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Zagné has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 199 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zagné itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.210°N, 7.483°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-777-1725), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
