San-Pédro at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ivory Coast, San-Pédro is the least sunny (about 1,827 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Soubré, the nearest covered town with different numbers (115 km away), San-Pédro is about as warm in April and 0.3 °C cooler in August, with 230 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.9 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.2 °C (74 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San-Pédro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San-Pédro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
