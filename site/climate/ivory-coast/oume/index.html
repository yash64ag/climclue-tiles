Oumé at a glance

Among the 182 towns ClimaClue covers in Ivory Coast, Oumé is 106th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 132nd sunniest (about 2,009 hours of sunshine a year) and 150th wettest (1,177 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Doukouya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Oumé is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C cooler in August, with 24 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.5 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.6 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 145 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Oumé, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Oumé

For mild weather, the best months to visit Oumé are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (27.5 °C), March (27.4 °C) and April (27.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (14 mm), December (23 mm) and February (57 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (209 h), May (208 h) and January (200 h).

Why Oumé has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 145 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Oumé itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.383°N, 5.418°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-776-1745), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 58,606, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
