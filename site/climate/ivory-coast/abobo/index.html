Abobo at a glance

Among the 182 towns ClimaClue covers in Ivory Coast, Abobo is the 2nd wettest (2,100 mm a year). Compared with Abidjan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Abobo is 0.2 °C cooler in March and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 216 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.9 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, August (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in August. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Abobo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Abobo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Abobo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.416°N, 4.016°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-785-1759), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
