Séguéla at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ivory Coast, Séguéla is the 5th warmest (26.3 °C year average) and the 2nd sunniest (about 2,275 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Man, the nearest covered town with different numbers (115 km away), Séguéla is 1.1 °C warmer in March and 0.7 °C warmer in August, with 363 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.3 °C), averages 3.6 °C more than its coolest, August (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.9 °C (68 °F), in January. The area lies about 345 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Séguéla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Séguéla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 345 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-760-1733), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
