Bandariba at a glance

Compared with Willemstad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Bandariba is 0.1 °C warmer in September and about as warm in February, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (27.5 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, February (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.4 °C (72 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Bandariba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bandariba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.093°N, 68.831°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-719-1111), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
