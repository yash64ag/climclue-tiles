Qoryooley at a glance

Among the 42 towns ClimaClue covers in Somalia, Qoryooley is the 5th wettest (600 mm a year). Compared with Marka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Qoryooley is 0.1 °C warmer in April and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.1 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, July (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.9 °C (70 °F), in August. The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qoryooley, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Qoryooley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qoryooley itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.788°N, 44.530°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-822-2245), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
