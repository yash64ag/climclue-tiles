Gebiley weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Gebiley, Somalia (Woqooyi Galbeed), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Gebiley is warmest in June, with 28.5 °C (83 °F) days and 17.2 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 24.9 °C (77 °F) days and 8.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (103 mm); January is driest (6 mm).

Gebiley at a glance

Among the 42 towns ClimaClue covers in Somalia, Gebiley is the 3rd coolest (20.5 °C year average). Compared with Tog Wajaale, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Gebiley is 1.2 °C warmer in June and about as warm in December, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (22.8 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, December (16.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.7 °C (48 °F), in December. The area lies about 114 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gebiley, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gebiley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 114 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gebiley itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.700°N, 43.624°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-742-2236), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 77,320, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
