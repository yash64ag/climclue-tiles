Jawhar at a glance

Among the 42 towns ClimaClue covers in Somalia, Jawhar is 21st warmest (27.1 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 3,096 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (500 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wanlaweyn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (70 km away), Jawhar is 0.8 °C cooler in March and 0.4 °C cooler in July, with 68 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.8 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, July (25.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.3 °C (70 °F), in August. The area lies about 65 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jawhar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jawhar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 65 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jawhar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.781°N, 45.500°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-812-2255), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 47,086, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
