Mogadishu at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Somalia, Mogadishu is the 3rd wettest (562 mm a year). Compared with Marka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (73 km away), Mogadishu is about as warm in April and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 27 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.9 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, July (24.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.1 °C (74 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mogadishu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Mogadishu

For mild weather, the best months to visit Mogadishu are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (27.9 °C), March (27.4 °C) and May (26.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (2 mm), January (5 mm) and March (11 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (284 h), January (283 h) and March (281 h).

Why Mogadishu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
