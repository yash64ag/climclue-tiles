Qā’emīyeh at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Qā’emīyeh is 87th warmest (21.4 °C year average), 53rd sunniest (about 3,188 hours of sunshine a year) and 38th wettest (762 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kāzerūn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Qā’emīyeh is about as warm in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 132 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.1 °C), averages 24.2 °C more than its coolest, January (8.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 106 km from the sea.

Why Qā’emīyeh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 106 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.2 °C. The 10 km area around Qā’emīyeh, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.2 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qā’emīyeh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.852°N, 51.588°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-541-2315), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
