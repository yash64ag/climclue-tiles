Isfahan at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iran, Isfahan is 51st warmest (16.3 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 3,185 hours of sunshine a year) and 94th wettest (151 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Khomeynī Shahr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Isfahan is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.9 °C), averages 28.3 °C more than its coolest, January (1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 321 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Isfahan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Isfahan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 321 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
