Kord Kūy at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Kord Kūy is 134th warmest (18.2 °C year average), 342nd sunniest (about 2,719 hours of sunshine a year) and 75th wettest (554 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bandar-e Torkaman, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Kord Kūy is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 36 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.5 °C), averages 19.0 °C more than its coolest, January (8.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Why Kord Kūy has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.0 °C. The 10 km area around Kord Kūy, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.6 °C a year, 1.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kord Kūy itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.793°N, 54.112°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-472-2341), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
