Alvand weather by month and climate

Alvand at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Alvand is 317th warmest (13.8 °C year average), 385th sunniest (about 2,440 hours of sunshine a year) and 125th wettest (453 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bīdestān, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Alvand is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 62 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 114 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alvand, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Alvand has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 114 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alvand itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.189°N, 50.064°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-478-2300), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
