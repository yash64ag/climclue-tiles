Shabestar at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Shabestar is 381st warmest (12.0 °C year average), 345th sunniest (about 2,717 hours of sunshine a year) and 234th wettest (310 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Marand, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Shabestar is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.5 °C), averages 27.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 280 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shabestar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shabestar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 280 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shabestar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.180°N, 45.703°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-458-2257), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
