Chālūs at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Chālūs is 191st warmest (16.9 °C year average), 395th sunniest (about 2,154 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (1,058 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nowshahr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Chālūs is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in February, with 37 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.3 °C), averages 16.6 °C more than its coolest, February (8.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Chālūs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chālūs itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.655°N, 51.420°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-473-2314), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
