Lordegān at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Lordegān is 294th warmest (14.7 °C year average), 87th sunniest (about 3,157 hours of sunshine a year) and 46th wettest (722 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Borūjen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (66 km away), Lordegān is 4.6 °C warmer in July and 2.4 °C warmer in January, with 375 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 30.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 178 km from the sea.

Why Lordegān has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 178 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.7 °C. The 10 km area around Lordegān, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.4 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lordegān itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.513°N, 50.827°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-524-2308), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
