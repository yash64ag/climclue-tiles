Sahand at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Sahand is 385th warmest (11.9 °C year average), 336th sunniest (about 2,731 hours of sunshine a year) and 268th wettest (283 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oskū, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Sahand is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.6 °C), averages 27.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 246 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sahand, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Sahand has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 246 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sahand itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.951°N, 46.110°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-2261), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 82,494, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
