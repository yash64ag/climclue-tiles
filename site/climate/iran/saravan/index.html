Sarāvān at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Sarāvān is the 2nd sunniest (about 3,306 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Jālq, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Sarāvān is 2.5 °C cooler in July and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.2 °C), averages 21.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 245 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sarāvān, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sarāvān has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 245 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sarāvān itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.371°N, 62.331°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-566-2423), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 60,014, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
