Amīr Kolā weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Amīr Kolā, Iran (Māzandarān), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Amīr Kolā at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Amīr Kolā is 170th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 398th sunniest (about 2,106 hours of sunshine a year) and 33rd wettest (827 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bābol, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Amīr Kolā is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.9 °C), averages 19.2 °C more than its coolest, January (7.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Why Amīr Kolā has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-474-2326), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
