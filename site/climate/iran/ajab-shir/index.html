Ajab Shīr at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Ajab Shīr is 315th warmest (13.9 °C year average), 335th sunniest (about 2,732 hours of sunshine a year) and 191st wettest (354 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bonāb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Ajab Shīr is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C warmer in January, with 46 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.4 °C), averages 27.2 °C more than its coolest, January (0.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 283 km from the sea.

Why Ajab Shīr has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 283 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-465-2258), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
