Arsanjān at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Arsanjān is 145th warmest (17.9 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 3,244 hours of sunshine a year) and 199th wettest (341 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sa‘ādat Shahr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Arsanjān is 1.4 °C warmer in July and 2.0 °C warmer in January, with 79 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.3 °C), averages 25.4 °C more than its coolest, January (4.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 262 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Arsanjān, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Arsanjān has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 262 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.4 °C. The 10 km area around Arsanjān, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 15.5 °C a year, 2.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Arsanjān itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.912°N, 53.308°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-540-2333), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
