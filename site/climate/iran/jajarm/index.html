Jājarm at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Jājarm is 265th warmest (15.5 °C year average), 250th sunniest (about 2,850 hours of sunshine a year) and 369th wettest (170 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Āshkhāneh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (83 km away), Jājarm is 0.9 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 160 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.2 °C), averages 25.8 °C more than its coolest, January (2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 222 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jājarm, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jājarm has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 222 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jājarm itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.950°N, 56.380°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-470-2363), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
