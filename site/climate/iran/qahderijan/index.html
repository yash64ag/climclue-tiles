Qahderījān weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Qahderījān, Iran (Isfahan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Qahderījān at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Qahderījān is 235th warmest (16.1 °C year average), 75th sunniest (about 3,169 hours of sunshine a year) and 367th wettest (173 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Falāvarjān, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Qahderījān is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.9 °C), averages 28.8 °C more than its coolest, January (1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 302 km from the sea.

Why Qahderījān has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 302 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qahderījān itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.577°N, 51.454°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-514-2314), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
