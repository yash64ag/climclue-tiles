Ahram at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Ahram is 32nd warmest (26.3 °C year average), 95th sunniest (about 3,129 hours of sunshine a year) and 257th wettest (293 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with ‘Ālī Shahr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Ahram is 1.8 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (36.4 °C), averages 22.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.6 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ahram, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ahram has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ahram itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.883°N, 51.275°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-551-2312), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,198, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
