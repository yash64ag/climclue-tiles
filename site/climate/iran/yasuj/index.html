Yasuj at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Yasuj is 358th warmest (12.9 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 3,232 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (1,110 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nūrābād, Fars, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Yasuj is 8.4 °C cooler in July and 7.8 °C cooler in January, with 383 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.1 °C), averages 24.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 159 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Yasuj, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Yasuj has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 159 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yasuj itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.668°N, 51.588°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-533-2315), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 96,786, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
