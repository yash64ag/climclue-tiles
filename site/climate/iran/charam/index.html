Charām at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Charām is 88th warmest (21.4 °C year average), 131st sunniest (about 3,075 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (943 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dehdasht, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Charām is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 208 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.3 °C), averages 24.9 °C more than its coolest, January (8.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 102 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Charām, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Charām has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 102 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.9 °C. The 10 km area around Charām, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.8 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Charām itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.756°N, 50.745°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-532-2307), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
