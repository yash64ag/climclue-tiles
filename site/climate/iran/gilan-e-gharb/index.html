Gīlān-e Gharb at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Gīlān-e Gharb is 110th warmest (19.5 °C year average), 93rd sunniest (about 3,135 hours of sunshine a year) and 71st wettest (578 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sarpol-e Z̄ahāb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Gīlān-e Gharb is 1.9 °C cooler in July and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 90 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.7 °C), averages 26.5 °C more than its coolest, January (6.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 490 km from the sea.

Why Gīlān-e Gharb has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 490 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C. The 10 km area around Gīlān-e Gharb, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.0 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gīlān-e Gharb itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.142°N, 45.920°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
