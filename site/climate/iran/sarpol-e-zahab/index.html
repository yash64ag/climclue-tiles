Sarpol-e Z̄ahāb at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Sarpol-e Z̄ahāb is 91st warmest (21.0 °C year average), 108th sunniest (about 3,105 hours of sunshine a year) and 55th wettest (667 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Qaşr-e Shīrīn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Sarpol-e Z̄ahāb is 1.3 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 82 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.6 °C), averages 27.1 °C more than its coolest, January (7.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 469 km from the sea.

Why Sarpol-e Z̄ahāb has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 469 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown.
