Ṣāleḥīeh weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ṣāleḥīeh, Iran (Tehran), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ṣāleḥīeh is warmest in July, with 38.3 °C (101 °F) days and 25.4 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 6.6 °C (44 °F) days and -2.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (57 mm); August is driest (1 mm).

Ṣāleḥīeh at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Ṣāleḥīeh is 168th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 291st sunniest (about 2,799 hours of sunshine a year) and 245th wettest (302 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Golestān, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Ṣāleḥīeh is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.8 °C), averages 29.5 °C more than its coolest, January (2.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 135 km from the sea.

Why Ṣāleḥīeh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 135 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ṣāleḥīeh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.505°N, 51.191°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-484-2311), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
