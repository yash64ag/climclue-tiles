Bāqershahr at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Bāqershahr is 165th warmest (17.6 °C year average), 253rd sunniest (about 2,846 hours of sunshine a year) and 218th wettest (322 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kahrīzak, Tehran (35.5°N 51.4°E), the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Bāqershahr is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.2 °C), averages 29.7 °C more than its coolest, January (2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 127 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bāqershahr, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bāqershahr has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 127 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-484-2314), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
