Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Shahrak-e Ţāleqānī is 69th warmest (23.6 °C year average), 161st sunniest (about 2,989 hours of sunshine a year) and 70th wettest (580 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Līkak, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Shahrak-e Ţāleqānī is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Why Shahrak-e Ţāleqānī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 80 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-531-2301), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
