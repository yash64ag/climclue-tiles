Qom at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iran, Qom is 24th warmest (19.4 °C year average), 68th sunniest (about 2,810 hours of sunshine a year) and 93rd wettest (159 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shahrak-e Pardīsān, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Qom is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.0 °C), averages 28.4 °C more than its coolest, January (4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 238 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qom, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Qom has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 238 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
