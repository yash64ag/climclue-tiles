Zābol at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iran, Zābol is the driest (50 mm a year). Compared with Zahedan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (181 km away), Zābol is 6.2 °C warmer in July and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 34 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (36.1 °C), averages 27.6 °C more than its coolest, January (8.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 637 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Zābol, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Zābol has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 637 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
