Bīrjand at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iran, Bīrjand is 59th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 3,117 hours of sunshine a year) and 83rd wettest (202 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Torbat-e Ḩeydarīyeh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (268 km away), Bīrjand is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 2.1 °C warmer in January, with 67 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.1 °C), averages 23.8 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 671 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bīrjand, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bīrjand has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 671 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
