Dorūd at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iran, Dorūd is 72nd warmest (14.8 °C year average), 30th sunniest (about 2,966 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (507 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Borūjerd, the nearest covered town with different numbers (53 km away), Dorūd is 0.6 °C warmer in July and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.7 °C), averages 28.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 334 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dorūd, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dorūd has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 334 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.9 °C. The 10 km area around Dorūd, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.4 °C a year, 2.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dorūd itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.492°N, 49.062°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-505-2290), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
