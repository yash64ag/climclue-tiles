Manjīl weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Manjīl, Iran (Gilan Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Manjīl is warmest in July, with 31.6 °C (89 °F) days and 19.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 7.8 °C (46 °F) days and -1.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around April (53 mm); June is driest (13 mm).

Manjīl at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Manjīl is 259th warmest (15.7 °C year average), 404th sunniest (about 2,041 hours of sunshine a year) and 141st wettest (430 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pādegān-e Manjīl, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Manjīl is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 16 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.4 °C), averages 22.1 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 89 km from the sea.

Why Manjīl has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 89 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.1 °C. The 10 km area around Manjīl, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.9 °C a year, 2.8 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Manjīl itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.744°N, 49.401°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-472-2294), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
