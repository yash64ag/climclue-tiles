Kāshān at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Kāshān is 106th warmest (19.8 °C year average), 205th sunniest (about 2,912 hours of sunshine a year) and 395th wettest (139 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kashan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Kāshān is 0.1 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.2 °C), averages 27.6 °C more than its coolest, January (5.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 291 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kāshān, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kāshān has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 291 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kāshān itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.002°N, 51.439°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-499-2314), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
