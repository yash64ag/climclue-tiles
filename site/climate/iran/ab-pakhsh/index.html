Āb Pakhsh at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Āb Pakhsh is 41st warmest (26.0 °C year average), 126th sunniest (about 3,084 hours of sunshine a year) and 227th wettest (315 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Borāzjān, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Āb Pakhsh is 0.4 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 66 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (36.3 °C), averages 22.5 °C more than its coolest, January (13.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Why Āb Pakhsh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Āb Pakhsh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.359°N, 51.074°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-546-2310), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
