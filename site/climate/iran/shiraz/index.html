Shiraz at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Shiraz is the 4th sunniest (about 3,282 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Shahr-e Ṣadrā, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Shiraz is 2.8 °C warmer in July and 3.6 °C warmer in January, with 126 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.6 °C), averages 23.9 °C more than its coolest, January (5.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 178 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shiraz, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations at a similar height, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Shiraz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 178 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.9 °C. The 10 km area around Shiraz, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.4 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shiraz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.610°N, 52.531°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-543-2325), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
