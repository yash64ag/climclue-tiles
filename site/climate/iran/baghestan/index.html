Bāghestān at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Bāghestān is 214th warmest (16.4 °C year average), 270th sunniest (about 2,823 hours of sunshine a year) and 220th wettest (321 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nasir Abaad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Bāghestān is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.0 °C), averages 29.9 °C more than its coolest, January (1.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 124 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bāghestān, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bāghestān has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 124 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bāghestān itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.647°N, 51.134°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-483-2311), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
