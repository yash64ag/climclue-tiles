Bābol at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Iran, Bābol is the 4th least sunny (about 2,106 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Qā’em Shahr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Bābol is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 38 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.7 °C), averages 19.3 °C more than its coolest, January (7.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Why Bābol has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bābol itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.551°N, 52.679°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-474-2326), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
