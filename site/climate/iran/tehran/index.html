Why Tehran has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 117 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Tehran climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Tehran?
July, with an average of 31.3 °C (88 °F), day highs around 35.7 °C (96 °F) and night lows around 24.5 °C (76 °F).
What is the coldest month in Tehran?
January, with an average of 1.1 °C (34 °F), day highs around 5.9 °C (43 °F) and night lows around -4.5 °C (24 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Tehran?
March, with 71 mm (2.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 384 mm (15.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Tehran?
August, with 3 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Tehran?
June, with about 332 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Tehran?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 84 cm (33.1 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 4 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Tehran?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: April, May and October.
What type of climate does Tehran have?
Mediterranean, hot summer (Köppen Csa). Hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.0 × 11 km, that contains Tehran’s GeoNames location (35.694°N, 51.422°E; ClimaClue area sq-483-2314), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 7,153,309 (GeoNames).
