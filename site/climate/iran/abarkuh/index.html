Abarkūh at a glance

Among the 423 towns ClimaClue covers in Iran, Abarkūh is 130th warmest (18.4 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 3,185 hours of sunshine a year) and 415th wettest (90 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Şafāshahr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Abarkūh is 5.5 °C warmer in July and 5.1 °C warmer in January, with 247 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.1 °C), averages 26.2 °C more than its coolest, January (5.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 321 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Abarkūh, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Abarkūh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 321 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Abarkūh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.129°N, 53.282°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-528-2332), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
