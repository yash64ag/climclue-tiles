Abu Dhabi at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Arab Emirates, Abu Dhabi is the 3rd coolest (27.5 °C year average) and the driest (71 mm a year). Compared with Musaffah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Abu Dhabi is 0.9 °C cooler in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (34.6 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, January (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.2 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Abu Dhabi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Abu Dhabi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
