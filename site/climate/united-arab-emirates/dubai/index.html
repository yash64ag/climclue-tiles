Why Dubai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 30 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Dubai climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Dubai?
August, with an average of 35.5 °C (96 °F), day highs around 43.3 °C (110 °F) and night lows around 29.0 °C (84 °F).
What is the coldest month in Dubai?
January, with an average of 18.8 °C (66 °F), day highs around 23.8 °C (75 °F) and night lows around 13.2 °C (56 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Dubai?
March, with 31 mm (1.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 116 mm (4.6 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Dubai?
June, with 0 mm (0.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Dubai?
May, with about 340 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Dubai?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Dubai?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Dubai’s GeoNames location (25.077°N, 55.309°E; ClimaClue area sq-589-2353), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 3,790,000 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
