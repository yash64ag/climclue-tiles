Fujairah at a glance

Among the 31 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Arab Emirates, Fujairah is the 4th warmest (28.0 °C year average). Compared with Ţarīf Kalbā, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Fujairah is 0.5 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.2 °C), averages 13.8 °C more than its coolest, January (20.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.3 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Fujairah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Fujairah

For mild weather, the best months to visit Fujairah are January, February, March and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (34.2 °C), July (33.9 °C) and August (32.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): June (1 mm), September (1 mm) and August (1 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (330 h), June (324 h) and July (304 h).

Why Fujairah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fujairah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.116°N, 56.341°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-588-2363), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 118,933, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
