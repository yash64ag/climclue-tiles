Al Ain City at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Arab Emirates, Al Ain City is the 2nd warmest (27.9 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,455 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (97 mm a year). Compared with Dubai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (108 km away), Al Ain City is 0.7 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (36.0 °C), averages 18.0 °C more than its coolest, January (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.1 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 101 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Ain City, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Ain City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 101 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
