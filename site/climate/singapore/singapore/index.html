Why Singapore has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Singapore climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Singapore?
May, with an average of 27.6 °C (82 °F), day highs around 28.7 °C (84 °F) and night lows around 26.3 °C (79 °F).
What is the coldest month in Singapore?
January, with an average of 25.9 °C (79 °F), day highs around 27.3 °C (81 °F) and night lows around 24.9 °C (77 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Singapore?
December, with 315 mm (12.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 2,410 mm (94.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Singapore?
February, with 120 mm (4.7 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Singapore?
March, with about 193 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Singapore?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Singapore?
No month meets the definition used here (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation).
What type of climate does Singapore have?
Tropical rainforest (Köppen Af). Hot and wet all year, with no real dry season. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Singapore’s GeoNames location (1.290°N, 103.850°E; ClimaClue area sq-827-2838), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 5,638,700 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
