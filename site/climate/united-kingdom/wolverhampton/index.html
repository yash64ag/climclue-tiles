Wolverhampton at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Wolverhampton is 81st warmest (9.7 °C year average), 80th sunniest (about 1,309 hours of sunshine a year) and 47th wettest (770 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Walsall, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Wolverhampton is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in February, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.3 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, February (4.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 124 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 17 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 102% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Wolverhampton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 124 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
