Cleethorpes at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Cleethorpes is 466th warmest (10.0 °C year average), 386th sunniest (about 1,405 hours of sunshine a year) and 683rd wettest (651 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Grimsby, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Cleethorpes is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.4 °C), averages 11.7 °C more than its coolest, February (4.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cleethorpes, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Cleethorpes

For mild weather, the best months to visit Cleethorpes are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (16.4 °C), August (16.4 °C) and September (14.2 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (43 mm), May (48 mm) and April (50 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): June (188 h), May (184 h) and July (176 h).

Why Cleethorpes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cleethorpes itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.560°N, 0.032°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-304-1799), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 38,996, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
