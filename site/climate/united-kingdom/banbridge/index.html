Banbridge at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Banbridge is 652nd warmest (9.5 °C year average), 680th sunniest (about 1,272 hours of sunshine a year) and 191st wettest (1,030 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Craigavon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Banbridge is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 101 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.4 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, January (4.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Why Banbridge has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Banbridge itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.350°N, 6.283°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-296-1737), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,173, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
