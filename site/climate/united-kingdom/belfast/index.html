Belfast at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Belfast is 57th warmest (10.0 °C year average), 79th sunniest (about 1,309 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (1,016 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Glasgow, the nearest covered town with different numbers (176 km away), Belfast is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C warmer in February, with 371 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.1 °C), averages 9.3 °C more than its coolest, February (5.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Belfast has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Belfast itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.597°N, 5.925°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-294-1740), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 348,005, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
