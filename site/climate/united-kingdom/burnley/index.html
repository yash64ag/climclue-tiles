Burnley at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Burnley is the coolest (8.4 °C year average), the least sunny (about 1,223 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,505 mm a year). Compared with Blackburn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Burnley is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 78 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (14.7 °C), averages 11.4 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 57 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Burnley, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Burnley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 57 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
