Bournemouth at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Bournemouth is the 2nd warmest (11.1 °C year average) and the 4th sunniest (about 1,674 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Poole, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Bournemouth is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (17.1 °C), averages 11.1 °C more than its coolest, February (6.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Bournemouth has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
