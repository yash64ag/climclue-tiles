Doncaster at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Doncaster is 370th warmest (10.2 °C year average), 503rd sunniest (about 1,326 hours of sunshine a year) and 604th wettest (669 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bentley, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Doncaster is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 10 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.0 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, January (4.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 85 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Doncaster, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Doncaster

For mild weather, the best months to visit Doncaster are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (17.0 °C), August (16.7 °C) and June (14.7 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (42 mm), March (49 mm) and May (50 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): June (181 h), May (176 h) and July (163 h).

Why Doncaster has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 85 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Doncaster itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.523°N, 1.131°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-304-1788), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 113,566, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
