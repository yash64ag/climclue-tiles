Newcastle under Lyme at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Newcastle under Lyme is 94th warmest (9.3 °C year average), 94th sunniest (about 1,269 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (932 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Stoke-on-Trent, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Newcastle under Lyme is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.8 °C), averages 11.8 °C more than its coolest, February (4.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 80 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 116% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Newcastle under Lyme has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 80 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
