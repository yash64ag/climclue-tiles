Burton upon Trent at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Burton upon Trent is 67th warmest (9.8 °C year average), 84th sunniest (about 1,307 hours of sunshine a year) and 54th wettest (750 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Derby, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Burton upon Trent is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.6 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, February (4.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 126 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Burton upon Trent, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Burton upon Trent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 126 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
