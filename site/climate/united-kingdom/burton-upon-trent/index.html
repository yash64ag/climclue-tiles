Why Burton upon Trent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 126 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Burton upon Trent climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Burton upon Trent?
July, with an average of 16.6 °C (62 °F), day highs around 20.2 °C (68 °F) and night lows around 12.3 °C (54 °F).
What is the coldest month in Burton upon Trent?
February, with an average of 4.3 °C (40 °F), day highs around 6.9 °C (44 °F) and night lows around 1.3 °C (34 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Burton upon Trent?
October, with 76 mm (3.0 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 750 mm (29.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Burton upon Trent?
February, with 49 mm (1.9 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Burton upon Trent?
June, with about 176 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Burton upon Trent?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 30 cm (11.7 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time for mild weather in Burton upon Trent?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: July and August.
What type of climate does Burton upon Trent have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 6.7 × 11 km, that contains Burton upon Trent’s GeoNames location (52.807°N, 1.643°W; ClimaClue area sq-311-1783), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 122,199 (GeoNames).
