Newport at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Newport is 18th warmest (10.6 °C year average), 49th sunniest (about 1,418 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (1,099 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cardiff, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Newport is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.1 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, February (5.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Newport, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Newport has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
