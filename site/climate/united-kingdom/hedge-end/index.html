Hedge End at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Hedge End is 106th warmest (10.8 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 1,605 hours of sunshine a year) and 332nd wettest (835 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bishopstoke, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Hedge End is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 28 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.4 °C), averages 12.2 °C more than its coolest, February (5.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hedge End, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Why Hedge End has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hedge End itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.912°N, 1.301°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-330-1786), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
