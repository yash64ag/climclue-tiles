Bearsden at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Bearsden is 718th warmest (9.3 °C year average), 777th sunniest (about 1,222 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,683 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Clydebank, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Bearsden is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.5 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, January (4.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 1.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 50 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 9 km from Bearsden measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 135% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care. Why Bearsden has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 50 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bearsden itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (55.915°N, 4.333°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-280-1756), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 28,470, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
