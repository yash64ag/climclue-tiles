Reading at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Reading is 32nd warmest (10.4 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 1,488 hours of sunshine a year) and 67th wettest (701 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Basingstoke, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Reading is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 123 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.3 °C), averages 12.6 °C more than its coolest, February (4.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 83 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Reading has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 83 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
