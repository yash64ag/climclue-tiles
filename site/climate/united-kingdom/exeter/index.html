Exeter at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Exeter is 25th warmest (10.5 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 1,524 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (971 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Plymouth, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Exeter is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 1.0 °C cooler in February, with 348 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.4 °C), averages 11.1 °C more than its coolest, February (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 26 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Exeter, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Exeter has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 26 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
