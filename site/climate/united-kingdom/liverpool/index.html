Liverpool at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Liverpool is 48th warmest (10.2 °C year average), 62nd sunniest (about 1,359 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (895 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Birkenhead, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Liverpool is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 37 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.9 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, February (5.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 17 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 22 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Liverpool are July and August.

Why Liverpool has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
