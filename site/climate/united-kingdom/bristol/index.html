Bristol at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Bristol is 36th warmest (10.4 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 1,477 hours of sunshine a year) and 27th wettest (896 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bath, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Bristol is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 35 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, February (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bristol, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bristol has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

July, with an average of 16.9 °C (62 °F), day highs around 20.4 °C (69 °F) and night lows around 12.7 °C (55 °F).
What is the coldest month in Bristol?
February, with an average of 5.0 °C (41 °F), day highs around 7.6 °C (46 °F) and night lows around 1.9 °C (35 °F).

Does it snow in Bristol?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 15 cm (5.8 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; it usually melts within days.
