Wigan at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Wigan is 62nd warmest (10.0 °C year average), 77th sunniest (about 1,310 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (1,015 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with St Helens, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Wigan is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 76 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.1 °C), averages 11.3 °C more than its coolest, February (4.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Wigan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Wigan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Wigan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.543°N, 2.637°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-304-1773), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
