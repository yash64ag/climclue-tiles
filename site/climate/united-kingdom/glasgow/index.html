Glasgow at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Glasgow is the 5th wettest (1,385 mm a year). Compared with Edinburgh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (67 km away), Glasgow is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 507 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.3 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, December (4.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 46 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 11 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 111% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Glasgow has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 46 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the coldest month in Glasgow?

Which month gets the most rain in Glasgow?

Does it snow in Glasgow?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 14 cm (5.4 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; it usually melts within days.
