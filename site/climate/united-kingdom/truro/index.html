Truro at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Truro is 14th warmest (11.1 °C year average), 70th sunniest (about 1,593 hours of sunshine a year) and 193rd wettest (1,022 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Falmouth, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Truro is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.5 °C), averages 10.0 °C more than its coolest, February (6.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 4.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 97% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Truro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Truro climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Truro?
August, with an average of 16.5 °C (62 °F), day highs around 19.5 °C (67 °F) and night lows around 13.6 °C (56 °F).
What is the coldest month in Truro?
February, with an average of 6.5 °C (44 °F), day highs around 8.9 °C (48 °F) and night lows around 4.1 °C (39 °F).

Yes. About 1.7 cm (0.7 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in February; it usually melts within days.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Truro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.265°N, 5.054°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-337-1749), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 23,041, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
