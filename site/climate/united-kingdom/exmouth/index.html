Exmouth at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Exmouth is 40th warmest (11.0 °C year average), 77th sunniest (about 1,572 hours of sunshine a year) and 312th wettest (851 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Exeter, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Exmouth is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.5 °C warmer in February, with 61 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.6 °C), averages 10.6 °C more than its coolest, February (6.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Exmouth, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Exmouth has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Exmouth itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.617°N, 3.402°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-333-1765), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
