Cheltenham at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Cheltenham is 54th warmest (10.0 °C year average), 52nd sunniest (about 1,415 hours of sunshine a year) and 38th wettest (825 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gloucester, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Cheltenham is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in February, with 29 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.8 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, February (4.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 83 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cheltenham, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cheltenham has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 83 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
