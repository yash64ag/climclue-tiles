Swindon at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, Swindon is 58th warmest (10.0 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 1,455 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (813 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oxford, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Swindon is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 133 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.6 °C), averages 12.2 °C more than its coolest, February (4.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 84 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 16 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Swindon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 84 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
