West Bromwich at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, West Bromwich is 86th warmest (9.6 °C year average), 71st sunniest (about 1,324 hours of sunshine a year) and 44th wettest (782 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dudley, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), West Bromwich is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 50 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.2 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, February (4.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 133 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 11 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why West Bromwich has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 133 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for West Bromwich itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (52.519°N, 1.994°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-314-1780), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
