York at a glance

Among the 104 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United Kingdom, York is 75th warmest (9.7 °C year average), 74th sunniest (about 1,316 hours of sunshine a year) and 88th wettest (650 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Leeds, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), York is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 268 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.4 °C), averages 12.2 °C more than its coolest, January (4.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 64 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of York, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit York

For mild weather, the best months to visit York are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.

Why York has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 64 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
