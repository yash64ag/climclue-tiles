Inverness weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Inverness, United Kingdom (Scotland), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Inverness is warmest in July, with 19.2 °C (67 °F) days and 11.8 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 6.9 °C (44 °F) days and 1.5 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 50 mm in April to 96 mm in October.

Inverness at a glance

Among the 804 towns ClimaClue covers in the United Kingdom, Inverness is the 2nd least sunny (about 1,102 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Elgin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (57 km away), Inverness is 1.2 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C cooler in December, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.5 °C), averages 11.3 °C more than its coolest, December (4.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Why Inverness has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Inverness itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (57.479°N, 4.224°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-265-1757), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
