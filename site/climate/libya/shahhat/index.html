Shahhat at a glance

Among the 59 towns ClimaClue covers in Libya, Shahhat is the coolest (16.2 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 3,084 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (581 mm a year). Compared with Al Bayḑā’, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Shahhat is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 77 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.5 °C), averages 13.1 °C more than its coolest, February (9.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.2 °C (43 °F), in February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shahhat, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shahhat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.1 °C. The 10 km area around Shahhat, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.5 °C a year, 1.3 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shahhat itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.804°N, 21.866°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-511-2018), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
