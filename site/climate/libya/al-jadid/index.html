Al Jadīd at a glance

Among the 19 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Libya, Al Jadīd is the warmest (23.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,603 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (9 mm a year). Compared with Sirte, the nearest covered town with different numbers (509 km away), Al Jadīd is 7.7 °C warmer in June and 3.0 °C cooler in January, with 211 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (31.6 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 503 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Jadīd, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Jadīd has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 503 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
