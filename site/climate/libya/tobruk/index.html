Tobruk at a glance

Among the 59 towns ClimaClue covers in Libya, Tobruk is 43rd warmest (20.0 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 3,167 hours of sunshine a year) and 45th wettest (161 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Darnah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (145 km away), Tobruk is 0.5 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 138 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.2 °C), averages 12.2 °C more than its coolest, January (14.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.6 °C (49 °F), in February. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tobruk, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tobruk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tobruk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.090°N, 23.954°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-519-2039), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
