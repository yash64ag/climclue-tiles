Al Abyār at a glance

Among the 59 towns ClimaClue covers in Libya, Al Abyār is 51st warmest (18.9 °C year average), 53rd sunniest (about 3,175 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (300 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Al Marj, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Al Abyār is 1.2 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 116 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.9 °C), averages 14.7 °C more than its coolest, January (11.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in February. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Abyār, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Abyār has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-518-2005), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
