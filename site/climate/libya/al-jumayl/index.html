Al Jumayl weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Al Jumayl, Libya (An Nuqat al Khams), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Al Jumayl is warmest in August, with 32.9 °C (91 °F) days and 24.4 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 17.5 °C (64 °F) days and 8.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around December (42 mm); July is driest (0 mm).

Al Jumayl at a glance

Among the 59 towns ClimaClue covers in Libya, Al Jumayl is 14th warmest (21.0 °C year average), 29th sunniest (about 3,242 hours of sunshine a year) and 42nd wettest (195 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sīdī Bin Zīnah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Al Jumayl is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.6 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, January (13.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Jumayl, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Jumayl has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Jumayl itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.853°N, 12.061°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-511-1920), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
