Tripoli at a glance

Among the 19 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Libya, Tripoli is 12th warmest (20.4 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 3,219 hours of sunshine a year) and 9th wettest (290 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tājūrā’, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Tripoli is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.4 °C), averages 14.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.2 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tripoli, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tripoli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
