Al Qubbah at a glance

Among the 59 towns ClimaClue covers in Libya, Al Qubbah is the 3rd coolest (16.6 °C year average), the least sunny (about 3,078 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (442 mm a year). Compared with Shahhat, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Al Qubbah is 0.8 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 140 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.3 °C), averages 13.6 °C more than its coolest, February (9.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Qubbah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Qubbah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Qubbah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.757°N, 22.241°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-512-2022), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
