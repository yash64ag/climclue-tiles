Mangina at a glance

Among the 115 towns ClimaClue covers in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Mangina is 91st warmest (21.8 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 2,116 hours of sunshine a year) and 64th wettest (1,473 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Beni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Mangina is 1.5 °C warmer in February and 1.3 °C warmer in November, with 1,383 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.1 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, November (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.3 °C (60 °F), in November. The area lies about 1,263 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mangina, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mangina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 1,263 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mangina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.604°N, 29.310°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-833-2093), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 55,439, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
