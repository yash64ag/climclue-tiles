Masina at a glance

Among the 115 towns ClimaClue covers in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Masina is 15th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 104th sunniest (about 1,801 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (1,449 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kinshasa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Masina is about as warm in March and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.4 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, July (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.9 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 375 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Masina, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Masina

For mild weather, the best months to visit Masina are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): March (26.4 °C), April (26.3 °C) and February (26.2 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (2 mm), June (7 mm) and August (7 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (173 h), March (169 h) and February (166 h).

Why Masina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 375 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Masina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.384°S, 15.391°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-883-1953), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 485,167, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
