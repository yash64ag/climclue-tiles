Mbuji-Mayi at a glance

Among the 38 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Mbuji-Mayi is 18th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,255 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (1,482 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gandajika, the nearest covered town with different numbers (79 km away), Mbuji-Mayi is about as warm in May and 0.3 °C warmer in November, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.9 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, November (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.1 °C (65 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,160 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mbuji-Mayi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mbuji-Mayi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,160 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
