Bumba at a glance

Among the 38 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Bumba is 6th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 2,070 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (1,665 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lisala, the nearest covered town with different numbers (106 km away), Bumba is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.2 °C warmer in August, with 31 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.9 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, August (24.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.3 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,403 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bumba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bumba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 1,403 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
