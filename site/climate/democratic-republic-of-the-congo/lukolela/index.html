Lukolela at a glance

Among the 115 towns ClimaClue covers in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Lukolela is 10th warmest (25.7 °C year average), 62nd sunniest (about 2,107 hours of sunshine a year) and 30th wettest (1,711 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Inongo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (156 km away), Lukolela is 1.0 °C cooler in February and 0.8 °C cooler in October, with 170 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.5 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, October (25.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.0 °C (70 °F), in October. The area lies about 724 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lukolela, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lukolela has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 724 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lukolela itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.060°S, 17.182°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-850-1971), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
