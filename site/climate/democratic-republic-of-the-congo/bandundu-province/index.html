Bandundu Province at a glance

Among the 38 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Bandundu Province is the warmest (26.0 °C year average). Compared with Kikwit, the nearest covered town with different numbers (249 km away), Bandundu Province is 1.0 °C warmer in March and 0.9 °C warmer in November, with 53 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.5 °C), averages 1.0 °C more than its coolest, November (25.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 622 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bandundu Province, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bandundu Province has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 622 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bandundu Province itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.317°S, 17.381°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-873-1973), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
