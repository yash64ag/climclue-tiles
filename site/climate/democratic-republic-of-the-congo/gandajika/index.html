Gandajika at a glance

Among the 115 towns ClimaClue covers in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Gandajika is 67th warmest (24.0 °C year average), 29th sunniest (about 2,350 hours of sunshine a year) and 47th wettest (1,545 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Luputa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (54 km away), Gandajika is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.5 °C warmer in December, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.1 °C), averages 2.0 °C more than its coolest, December (23.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.1 °C (63 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,191 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gandajika, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Gandajika

For mild weather, the best months to visit Gandajika are May, June and July.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June and July.

Why Gandajika has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,191 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gandajika itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.745°S, 23.953°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-907-2039), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
