Lodja at a glance

Among the 115 towns ClimaClue covers in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Lodja is 56th warmest (24.5 °C year average), 102nd sunniest (about 1,896 hours of sunshine a year) and 50th wettest (1,542 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lukula, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Lodja is 0.7 °C warmer in February and 0.6 °C warmer in November, with 364 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.0 °C), averages 1.0 °C more than its coolest, November (24.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.3 °C (65 °F), in September. The area lies about 1,263 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lodja, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Lodja

For mild weather, the best months to visit Lodja are June and July.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June and July.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (25.0 °C), March (24.9 °C) and July (24.8 °C).

Why Lodja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,263 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lodja itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.521°S, 23.600°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-875-2036), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
