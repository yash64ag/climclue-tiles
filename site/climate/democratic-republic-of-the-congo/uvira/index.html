Uvira at a glance

Among the 115 towns ClimaClue covers in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Uvira is the 5th driest (935 mm a year). Compared with Bukavu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (106 km away), Uvira is 3.8 °C warmer in September and 3.1 °C warmer in July, with 493 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (25.0 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, July (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,116 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Uvira, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Uvira has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,116 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Uvira itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.395°S, 29.138°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-873-2091), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
