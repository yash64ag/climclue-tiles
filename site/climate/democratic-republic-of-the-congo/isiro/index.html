Isiro at a glance

Among the 38 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Isiro is the 3rd wettest (2,167 mm a year). Compared with Bakwa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (152 km away), Isiro is 2.5 °C cooler in February and 1.3 °C cooler in July, with 506 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.6 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, July (21.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.2 °C (67 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,533 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Isiro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Isiro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 1,533 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Isiro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.774°N, 27.616°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-812-2076), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
