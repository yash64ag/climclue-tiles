Kikwit at a glance

Among the 38 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Kikwit is 9th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 2,149 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (1,435 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ilebo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (211 km away), Kikwit is 0.6 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in November, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, November (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.4 °C (69 °F), in July. The area lies about 710 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kikwit, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kikwit has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 710 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
