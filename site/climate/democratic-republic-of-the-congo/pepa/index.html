Pepa at a glance

Among the 115 towns ClimaClue covers in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Pepa is the coolest (17.4 °C year average) and the 2nd wettest (2,950 mm a year). Compared with Kituku, the nearest covered town with different numbers (49 km away), Pepa is 7.4 °C cooler in September and 7.6 °C cooler in June, with 1,164 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (19.2 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, June (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.1 °C (50 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,020 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pepa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pepa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,020 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pepa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.698°S, 29.777°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-916-2097), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 24,767, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
