Mbandaka at a glance

Among the 38 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Mbandaka is the 5th warmest (25.6 °C year average) and the 5th wettest (1,753 mm a year). Compared with Bandundu Province, the nearest covered town with different numbers (387 km away), Mbandaka is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 1.5 °C cooler in September, with 252 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.4 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, September (24.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.4 °C (72 °F), in August. The area lies about 894 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mbandaka, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mbandaka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 894 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
