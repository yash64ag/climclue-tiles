Saint-Denis at a glance

Among the 14 towns ClimaClue covers in Reunion, Saint-Denis is the 3rd warmest (24.0 °C year average). Compared with Sainte-Marie, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Saint-Denis is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 403 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.5 °C), averages 5.1 °C more than its coolest, August (21.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Saint-Denis has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Saint-Denis itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.882°S, 55.450°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1048-2354), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
