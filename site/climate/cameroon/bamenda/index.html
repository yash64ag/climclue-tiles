Bamenda at a glance

Among the 87 towns ClimaClue covers in Cameroon, Bamenda is 74th warmest (20.7 °C year average), 44th sunniest (about 1,982 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (2,662 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bafut, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Bamenda is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 414 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.8 °C), averages 3.6 °C more than its coolest, August (19.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.4 °C (60 °F), in April. The area lies about 220 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bamenda, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Bamenda

For mild weather, the best months to visit Bamenda are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (22.8 °C), March (22.4 °C) and January (21.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (11 mm), January (14 mm) and February (28 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (255 h), January (246 h) and November (223 h).

Why Bamenda has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 220 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bamenda itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.960°N, 10.146°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-780-1901), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 420,445, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
