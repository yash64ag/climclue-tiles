Oku at a glance

Among the 87 towns ClimaClue covers in Cameroon, Oku is the coolest (13.3 °C year average) and the 5th wettest (4,059 mm a year). Compared with Fundong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Oku is 4.8 °C cooler in March and 4.9 °C cooler in August, with 547 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (15.1 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, August (11.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.6 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 267 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Oku, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Oku has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 267 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C. The 10 km area around Oku, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 15.4 °C a year, 2.1 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Oku itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.250°N, 10.433°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-777-1904), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
