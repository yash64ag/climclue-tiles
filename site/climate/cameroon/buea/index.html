Buea at a glance

Among the 87 towns ClimaClue covers in Cameroon, Buea is the 4th least sunny (about 1,565 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (4,916 mm a year). Compared with Mutengene, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Buea is 3.4 °C cooler in March and 3.4 °C cooler in August, with 657 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (22.7 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, August (20.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.4 °C (63 °F), in September. The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Buea, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Buea has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 12.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Buea itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.153°N, 9.242°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-798-1892), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
