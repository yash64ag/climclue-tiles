Melong at a glance

Among the 87 towns ClimaClue covers in Cameroon, Melong is 68th warmest (22.0 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 1,850 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (2,834 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kekem, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Melong is 0.3 °C cooler in February and 0.4 °C cooler in August, with 235 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.3 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, August (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in December. The area lies about 145 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Melong, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Melong

For mild weather, the best months to visit Melong are January, February and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (23.3 °C), March (23.1 °C) and April (22.7 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (19 mm), December (22 mm) and February (46 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (220 h), January (217 h) and November (194 h).

Why Melong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 145 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Melong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.122°N, 9.961°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-788-1899), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 76,716, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
