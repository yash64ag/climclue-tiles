Mokolo at a glance

Among the 87 towns ClimaClue covers in Cameroon, Mokolo is 39th warmest (24.5 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 2,933 hours of sunshine a year) and 65th wettest (1,435 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mora, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Mokolo is 2.4 °C cooler in April and 1.2 °C cooler in January, with 625 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.3 °C), averages 8.0 °C more than its coolest, January (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.2 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 891 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mokolo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mokolo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 891 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mokolo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.742°N, 13.802°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-732-1938), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
