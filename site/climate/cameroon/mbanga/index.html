Mbanga at a glance

Among the 87 towns ClimaClue covers in Cameroon, Mbanga is 29th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 67th sunniest (about 1,720 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (2,715 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Njombé, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Mbanga is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 68 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.4 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, August (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.1 °C (70 °F), in September. The area lies about 78 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mbanga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mbanga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 78 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mbanga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.502°N, 9.567°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-794-1895), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
