Monrovia weather in May

Average May weather in Monrovia, Liberia: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In May, Monrovia averages 30.9 °C (88 °F) days and 22.0 °C (72 °F) nights, with 317 mm (12.5 in) of rain over the month and about 159 hours of sunshine.

How May compares in Monrovia

May is the 4th warmest of the 12, the 6th wettest of the 12 and the 5th least sunny of the 12 in Monrovia. Compared with April: 0.4 °C cooler, 130 mm more precipitation and 13 hours less sunshine. June is 0.8 °C cooler than May on average.

Monrovia in May: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Monrovia in May?
In May, Monrovia averages a day high of 30.9 °C (88 °F) and a night low of 22.0 °C (72 °F), with 317 mm (12.5 in) of precipitation and about 159 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is May a good time to visit Monrovia?
No month in Monrovia is mild by the rule used here, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Monrovia in May?
317 mm (12.5 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making May the 6th wettest of the 12. The wettest month is August (525 mm), the driest January (32 mm).
Does it snow in Monrovia in May?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for May in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Monrovia get in May?
About 159 hours of bright sunshine, the 5th least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Monrovia in May?
About 28.9 °C (84 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, warm for swimming; its warmest month is May (28.9 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Monrovia in May?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes for humid air and a rain jacket or umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
