Linstead at a glance

Among the 12 towns ClimaClue covers in Jamaica, Linstead is the 2nd coolest (26.2 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 2,515 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Spanish Town, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Linstead is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 374 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, January (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Linstead, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Linstead

For mild weather, the best months to visit Linstead are January, February and December.

Why Linstead has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Linstead itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.137°N, 77.032°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-658-1029), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
