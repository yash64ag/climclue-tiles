I-n-Tillit at a glance

Among the 58 towns ClimaClue covers in Mali, I-n-Tillit is the 3rd warmest (30.4 °C year average). Compared with Gao, the nearest covered town with different numbers (92 km away), I-n-Tillit is 0.2 °C cooler in May and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 73 mm more precipitation a year. Why I-n-Tillit has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,068 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for I-n-Tillit itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.546°N, 0.461°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-684-1795), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
