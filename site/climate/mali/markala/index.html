Markala at a glance

Among the 58 towns ClimaClue covers in Mali, Markala is 22nd warmest (29.0 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 3,075 hours of sunshine a year) and 36th wettest (574 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dougabougou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Markala is 0.3 °C cooler in May and about as warm in January, with 16 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.8 °C), averages 9.6 °C more than its coolest, January (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 947 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Markala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Markala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 947 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Markala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.684°N, 6.074°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-703-1739), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
