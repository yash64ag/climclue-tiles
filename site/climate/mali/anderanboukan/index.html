Andéranboukan at a glance

Among the 58 towns ClimaClue covers in Mali, Andéranboukan is 7th warmest (29.8 °C year average), 48th sunniest (about 2,884 hours of sunshine a year) and 52nd wettest (240 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gao, the nearest covered town with different numbers (341 km away), Andéranboukan is 1.0 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 70 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.9 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, January (22.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.9 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,012 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Andéranboukan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,012 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-685-1830), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
