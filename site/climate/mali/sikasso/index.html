Sikasso at a glance

Among the 58 towns ClimaClue covers in Mali, Sikasso is 50th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 53rd sunniest (about 2,765 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,078 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Finnkolo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Sikasso is 0.1 °C warmer in April and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (31.3 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, January (24.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 701 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sikasso, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sikasso has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 701 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sikasso itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.318°N, 5.667°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-726-1743), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 349,324, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
