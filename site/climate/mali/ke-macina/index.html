Ké-Macina at a glance

Among the 58 towns ClimaClue covers in Mali, Ké-Macina is the 5th sunniest (about 3,091 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Moninnpébougou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Ké-Macina is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.3 °C), averages 10.0 °C more than its coolest, January (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 985 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ké-Macina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 985 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ké-Macina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.964°N, 5.358°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-700-1746), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
