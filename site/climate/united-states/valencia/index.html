Valencia at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Valencia is 125th warmest (17.1 °C year average), 72nd sunniest (about 3,158 hours of sunshine a year) and 262nd wettest (415 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Clarita, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Valencia is 0.7 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 60 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.8 °C), averages 14.8 °C more than its coolest, December (9.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Valencia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
