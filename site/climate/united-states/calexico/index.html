Calexico at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Calexico is 65th warmest (24.2 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 3,652 hours of sunshine a year) and 3124th wettest (78 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Centro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Calexico is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in December, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Why Calexico has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 139 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Calexico itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.679°N, 115.499°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-513-645), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
