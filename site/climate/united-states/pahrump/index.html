Pahrump weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Pahrump, United States (Nevada), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Pahrump at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Pahrump is 485th warmest (19.3 °C year average), 64th sunniest (about 3,609 hours of sunshine a year) and 3062nd wettest (220 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Summerlin South, the nearest covered town with different numbers (60 km away), Pahrump is about as warm in July and 0.5 °C warmer in December, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.6 °C), averages 23.8 °C more than its coolest, December (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 1.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 348 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 9 km from Pahrump measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.7 °C (4.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Pahrump has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 348 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.8 °C. The 10 km area around Pahrump, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.1 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pahrump itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.208°N, 115.984°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-477-640), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
