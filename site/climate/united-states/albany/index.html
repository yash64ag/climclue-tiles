Albany at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Albany is 294th warmest (9.9 °C year average), 296th sunniest (about 2,351 hours of sunshine a year) and 128th wettest (1,090 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Springfield, Massachusetts, the nearest covered town with different numbers (114 km away), Albany is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 123 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.8 °C), averages 26.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 177 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 11 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Albany are May and September.

Why Albany has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 177 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
