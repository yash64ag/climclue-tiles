Las Cruces at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Las Cruces is 99th warmest (17.8 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 3,619 hours of sunshine a year) and 316th wettest (217 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Paso, the nearest covered town with different numbers (67 km away), Las Cruces is 0.8 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in December, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 21.0 °C more than its coolest, December (6.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 620 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 87% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Las Cruces has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 620 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
