Why Las Cruces has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 620 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Las Cruces climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Las Cruces?
July, with an average of 27.8 °C (82 °F), day highs around 34.1 °C (93 °F) and night lows around 21.3 °C (70 °F).
What is the coldest month in Las Cruces?
December, with an average of 6.8 °C (44 °F), day highs around 13.5 °C (56 °F) and night lows around 1.4 °C (35 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Las Cruces?
August, with 45 mm (1.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 217 mm (8.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Las Cruces?
March, with 6 mm (0.2 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Las Cruces?
June, with about 374 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Las Cruces?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 3.6 cm (1.4 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time for mild weather in Las Cruces?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: April, May, September and October.
What type of climate does Las Cruces have?
Cold desert (Köppen BWk). Very dry, with cold winters. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.4 × 11 km, that contains Las Cruces’s GeoNames location (32.312°N, 106.778°W; ClimaClue area sq-516-732), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 101,643 (GeoNames).
