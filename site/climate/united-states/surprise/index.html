Surprise at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Surprise is the 2nd sunniest (about 3,817 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Peoria, Arizona, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Surprise is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.8 °C), averages 23.0 °C more than its coolest, December (11.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.9 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 278 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 99% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Surprise has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 278 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
