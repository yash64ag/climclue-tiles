Why Waterbury has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Waterbury climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Waterbury?
July, with an average of 22.9 °C (73 °F), day highs around 27.9 °C (82 °F) and night lows around 18.1 °C (65 °F).
What is the coldest month in Waterbury?
January, with an average of -2.1 °C (28 °F), day highs around 2.0 °C (36 °F) and night lows around -6.3 °C (21 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Waterbury?
October, with 124 mm (4.9 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,358 mm (53.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Waterbury?
January, with 94 mm (3.7 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Waterbury?
July, with about 275 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Waterbury?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 128 cm (50.3 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 4 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Waterbury?
No month meets the definition used here (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation).

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.3 × 11 km, that contains Waterbury’s GeoNames location (41.558°N, 73.052°W; ClimaClue area sq-424-1069), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 108,802 (GeoNames).
