Murrieta weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Murrieta, United States (California), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Murrieta at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Murrieta is 820th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 266th sunniest (about 3,203 hours of sunshine a year) and 3005th wettest (287 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wildomar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Murrieta is about as warm in August and 0.5 °C warmer in December, with 15 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.2 °C), averages 12.9 °C more than its coolest, December (11.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Murrieta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Murrieta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Murrieta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.554°N, 117.214°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-504-627), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
