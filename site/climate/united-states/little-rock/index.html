Little Rock at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Little Rock is 137th warmest (16.9 °C year average), 126th sunniest (about 3,027 hours of sunshine a year) and 50th wettest (1,320 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Memphis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (209 km away), Little Rock is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 39 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 23.1 °C more than its coolest, January (4.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 534 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Little Rock has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 534 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
