Nashville at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Nashville is 177th warmest (15.4 °C year average), 269th sunniest (about 2,486 hours of sunshine a year) and 61st wettest (1,276 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Murfreesboro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Nashville is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 82 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 23.2 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 645 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Nashville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 645 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
