Holt at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Holt is 2848th warmest (9.3 °C year average), 2705th sunniest (about 2,356 hours of sunshine a year) and 2248th wettest (896 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lansing, Michigan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Holt is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Why Holt has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 785 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Holt itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.641°N, 84.515°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-413-954), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
