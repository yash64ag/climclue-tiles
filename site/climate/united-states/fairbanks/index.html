Fairbanks at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Fairbanks is the 2nd coolest (-1.8 °C year average). Compared with Badger, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Fairbanks is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.8 °C), averages 37.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-20.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 432 km from the sea.

Why Fairbanks has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 432 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 37.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fairbanks itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (64.838°N, 147.716°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 4.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-191-322), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
