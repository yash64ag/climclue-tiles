Tulsa at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Tulsa is 160th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 169th sunniest (about 2,758 hours of sunshine a year) and 133rd wettest (1,078 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Broken Arrow, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Tulsa is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 66 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 25.1 °C more than its coolest, January (3.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 741 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Tulsa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 741 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
