Why Aurora, Illinois has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,051 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

When is the best time for mild weather in Aurora, Illinois?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.3 × 11 km, that contains Aurora’s GeoNames location (41.761°N, 88.320°W; ClimaClue area sq-422-916), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 200,661 (GeoNames).
