Victorville at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Victorville is 122nd warmest (17.2 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 3,328 hours of sunshine a year) and 321st wettest (189 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Bernardino, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Victorville is 1.2 °C warmer in July and 2.9 °C cooler in December, with 368 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.3 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, December (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 129 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 119% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Victorville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 129 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
