Cocoa weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Cocoa, United States (Florida), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Cocoa at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Cocoa is 220th warmest (22.5 °C year average), 755th sunniest (about 2,933 hours of sunshine a year) and 642nd wettest (1,299 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rockledge, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Cocoa is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 16 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.0 °C), averages 9.9 °C more than its coolest, January (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.0 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Cocoa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cocoa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.386°N, 80.742°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-556-992), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
