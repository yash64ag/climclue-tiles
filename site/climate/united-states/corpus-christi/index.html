Why Corpus Christi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

When is the best time for mild weather in Corpus Christi?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: February, March, April, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.8 × 11 km, that contains Corpus Christi’s GeoNames location (27.801°N, 97.396°W; ClimaClue area sq-561-826), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 316,239 (GeoNames).
