Corpus Christi at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Corpus Christi is 34th warmest (22.0 °C year average), 219th sunniest (about 2,600 hours of sunshine a year) and 196th wettest (871 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with McAllen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (196 km away), Corpus Christi is 1.6 °C cooler in August and 1.7 °C cooler in January, with 299 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.4 °C), averages 14.2 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.6 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 12 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Corpus Christi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
