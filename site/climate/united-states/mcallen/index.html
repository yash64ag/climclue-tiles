Why McAllen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 92 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

McAllen climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in McAllen?
August, with an average of 30.1 °C (86 °F), day highs around 36.1 °C (97 °F) and night lows around 25.5 °C (78 °F).
What is the coldest month in McAllen?
January, with an average of 15.9 °C (61 °F), day highs around 22.7 °C (73 °F) and night lows around 12.3 °C (54 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in McAllen?
September, with 104 mm (4.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 572 mm (22.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in McAllen?
March, with 24 mm (1.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in McAllen?
July, with about 291 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in McAllen?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in McAllen?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, October, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10.0 × 11 km, that contains McAllen’s GeoNames location (26.203°N, 98.230°W; ClimaClue area sq-577-817), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 140,269 (GeoNames).
