Sylvania at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Sylvania is 2540th warmest (10.2 °C year average), 2717th sunniest (about 2,349 hours of sunshine a year) and 1950th wettest (1,004 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Toledo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Sylvania is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 47 mm more precipitation a year. Why Sylvania has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 684 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.8 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sylvania itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.719°N, 83.713°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-422-962), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
