Albuquerque at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Albuquerque is 195th warmest (14.4 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 3,371 hours of sunshine a year) and 304th wettest (276 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Las Cruces, the nearest covered town with different numbers (308 km away), Albuquerque is 1.9 °C cooler in July and 4.2 °C cooler in December, with 59 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.9 °C), averages 23.3 °C more than its coolest, December (2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 744 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 115% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Albuquerque has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 744 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
