Albuquerque at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Albuquerque is 194th warmest (14.5 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 3,371 hours of sunshine a year) and 301st wettest (272 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Las Cruces, the nearest covered town with different numbers (308 km away), Albuquerque is 2.2 °C cooler in July and 4.5 °C cooler in December, with 70 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.0 °C), averages 23.4 °C more than its coolest, December (2.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 744 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Albuquerque has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 744 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Albuquerque itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.084°N, 106.651°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-489-733), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
