Concord at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Concord is 189th warmest (14.8 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 3,192 hours of sunshine a year) and 244th wettest (472 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Antioch, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Concord is 1.3 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 24 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 10.3 °C more than its coolest, January (9.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 38 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 2 km from Concord measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.2 °C (5.7 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Concord has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 38 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
