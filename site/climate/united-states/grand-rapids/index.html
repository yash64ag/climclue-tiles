Grand Rapids at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Grand Rapids is 312th warmest (9.3 °C year average), 309th sunniest (about 2,204 hours of sunshine a year) and 181st wettest (929 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lansing, the nearest covered town with different numbers (94 km away), Grand Rapids is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.1 °C), averages 26.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 880 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Grand Rapids has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 880 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
