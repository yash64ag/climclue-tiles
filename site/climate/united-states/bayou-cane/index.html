Bayou Cane at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Bayou Cane is 385th warmest (20.4 °C year average), 1257th sunniest (about 2,657 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (1,774 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Houma, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Bayou Cane is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.4 °C), averages 15.4 °C more than its coolest, January (11.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 53 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 111% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Bayou Cane has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 53 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bayou Cane itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.624°N, 90.751°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-543-892), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 19,355, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
