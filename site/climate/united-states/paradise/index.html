Paradise at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Paradise is the 5th driest (142 mm a year). Compared with Las Vegas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Paradise is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in December, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.7 °C), averages 24.9 °C more than its coolest, December (8.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 377 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 133% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Paradise has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 377 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Paradise itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.097°N, 115.147°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-479-648), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
