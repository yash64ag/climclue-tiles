East Hampton at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, East Hampton is 173rd warmest (15.5 °C year average), 188th sunniest (about 2,687 hours of sunshine a year) and 102nd wettest (1,183 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Newport News, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), East Hampton is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.1 °C), averages 21.5 °C more than its coolest, January (4.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why East Hampton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
