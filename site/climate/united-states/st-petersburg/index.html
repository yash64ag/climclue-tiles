St. Petersburg at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, St. Petersburg is 29th warmest (22.4 °C year average), 139th sunniest (about 2,918 hours of sunshine a year) and 36th wettest (1,362 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Clearwater, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), St. Petersburg is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.6 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 119% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why St. Petersburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
