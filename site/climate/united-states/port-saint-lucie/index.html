Port Saint Lucie at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Port Saint Lucie is 14th warmest (23.6 °C year average), 129th sunniest (about 3,006 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (1,424 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with West Palm Beach, the nearest covered town with different numbers (71 km away), Port Saint Lucie is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 200 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.6 °C), averages 9.3 °C more than its coolest, January (18.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.5 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 16 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 89% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Port Saint Lucie has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
