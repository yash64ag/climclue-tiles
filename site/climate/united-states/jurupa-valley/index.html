Jurupa Valley at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Jurupa Valley is 591st warmest (18.7 °C year average), 235th sunniest (about 3,219 hours of sunshine a year) and 2710th wettest (472 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Glen Avon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Jurupa Valley is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 13.7 °C more than its coolest, December (12.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.5 °C (42 °F), in December. The area lies about 58 km from the sea.

Why Jurupa Valley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 58 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jurupa Valley itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.993°N, 117.516°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-500-624), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 21,930, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
