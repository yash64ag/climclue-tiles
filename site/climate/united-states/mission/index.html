Mission weather by month and climate

Mission at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Mission is 105th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 2712th sunniest (about 2,352 hours of sunshine a year) and 2595th wettest (548 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Alton, Texas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Mission is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (30.1 °C), averages 14.2 °C more than its coolest, January (15.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.5 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 102 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Mission has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 102 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mission itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.216°N, 98.325°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-577-816), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 83,298, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
