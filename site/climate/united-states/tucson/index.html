Tucson at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Tucson is 36th warmest (21.6 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 3,734 hours of sunshine a year) and 299th wettest (311 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chandler, the nearest covered town with different numbers (148 km away), Tucson is 3.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 97 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.4 °C), averages 20.3 °C more than its coolest, December (11.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 241 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tucson has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 241 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
