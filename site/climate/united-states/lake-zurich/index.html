Lake Zurich at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Lake Zurich is 2806th warmest (9.2 °C year average), 2500th sunniest (about 2,459 hours of sunshine a year) and 2110th wettest (956 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vernon Hills, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Lake Zurich is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 1,037 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Lake Zurich has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,037 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lake Zurich itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.197°N, 88.093°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-418-919), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 19,993, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
