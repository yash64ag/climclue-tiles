Cutler at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Cutler is 74th warmest (24.5 °C year average), 461st sunniest (about 3,117 hours of sunshine a year) and 173rd wettest (1,510 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Palmetto Bay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Cutler is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.5 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, January (19.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.1 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Cutler has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cutler itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.615°N, 80.311°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-583-996), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
