St. Louis at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, St. Louis is 206th warmest (13.6 °C year average), 225th sunniest (about 2,580 hours of sunshine a year) and 117th wettest (1,137 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Springfield, Illinois, the nearest covered town with different numbers (139 km away), St. Louis is 1.7 °C warmer in July and 2.0 °C warmer in January, with 138 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.3 °C), averages 26.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 931 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why St. Louis has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 931 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
