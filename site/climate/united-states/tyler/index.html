Tyler at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Tyler is 72nd warmest (18.7 °C year average), 184th sunniest (about 2,708 hours of sunshine a year) and 112th wettest (1,157 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mesquite, the nearest covered town with different numbers (130 km away), Tyler is 0.9 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 134 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.4 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (8.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 315 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 96% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tyler has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 315 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
