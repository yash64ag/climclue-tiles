Artesia at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Artesia is 619th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 323rd sunniest (about 3,179 hours of sunshine a year) and 2922nd wettest (337 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cerritos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Artesia is about as warm in August and about as warm in December, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.5 °C), averages 9.4 °C more than its coolest, December (14.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in December. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Artesia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Artesia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.866°N, 118.083°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-501-619), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
