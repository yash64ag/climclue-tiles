Alamogordo at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Alamogordo is 1031st warmest (16.3 °C year average), 121st sunniest (about 3,475 hours of sunshine a year) and 2737th wettest (452 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Las Cruces, the nearest covered town with different numbers (101 km away), Alamogordo is 2.3 °C cooler in June and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 251 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (25.8 °C), averages 19.9 °C more than its coolest, January (5.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 707 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Alamogordo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 707 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-510-740), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
