Virginia Beach at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Virginia Beach is 178th warmest (15.4 °C year average), 195th sunniest (about 2,666 hours of sunshine a year) and 79th wettest (1,224 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chesapeake, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Virginia Beach is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.6 °C), averages 20.5 °C more than its coolest, January (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Virginia Beach has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
