Rockford at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Rockford is 304th warmest (9.5 °C year average), 280th sunniest (about 2,470 hours of sunshine a year) and 184th wettest (915 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Elgin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (72 km away), Rockford is about as warm in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 28.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 1,119 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 99% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Rockford has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,119 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.8 °C.
