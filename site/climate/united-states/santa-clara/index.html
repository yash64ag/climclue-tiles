Santa Clara at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Santa Clara is 1145th warmest (15.6 °C year average), 352nd sunniest (about 3,167 hours of sunshine a year) and 2558th wettest (583 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Jose, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Santa Clara is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.6 °C), averages 10.6 °C more than its coolest, January (10.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 3 km from Santa Clara by up to 2.1 °C (3.7 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations at a similar height, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Santa Clara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa Clara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.354°N, 121.955°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-466-580), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 126,215, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
