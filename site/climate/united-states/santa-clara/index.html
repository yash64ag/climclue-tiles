Santa Clara at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Santa Clara is 191st warmest (14.8 °C year average), 69th sunniest (about 3,167 hours of sunshine a year) and 219th wettest (585 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Jose, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Santa Clara is 0.3 °C cooler in September and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 60 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (19.4 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, January (9.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Why Santa Clara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
