Spokane at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Spokane is 289th warmest (10.0 °C year average), 278th sunniest (about 2,476 hours of sunshine a year) and 242nd wettest (479 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tri-Cities, the nearest covered town with different numbers (207 km away), Spokane is 4.0 °C cooler in July and 2.5 °C cooler in December, with 277 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.4 °C), averages 23.9 °C more than its coolest, December (-1.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 374 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 117% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Spokane has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 374 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
