Why Spokane has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 374 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Spokane climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Spokane?
July, with an average of 22.4 °C (72 °F), day highs around 29.4 °C (85 °F) and night lows around 15.2 °C (59 °F).
What is the coldest month in Spokane?
December, with an average of -1.4 °C (29 °F), day highs around 1.3 °C (34 °F) and night lows around -5.0 °C (23 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Spokane?
December, with 70 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 479 mm (18.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Spokane?
August, with 14 mm (0.6 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Spokane?
July, with about 377 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Spokane?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 136 cm (53.3 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 5 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Spokane?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: June, July, August and September.
What type of climate does Spokane have?
Continental, hot dry summer (Köppen Dsa). Hot, dry summers and cold, snowy winters. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 7.5 × 11 km, that contains Spokane’s GeoNames location (47.660°N, 117.429°W; ClimaClue area sq-363-625), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 229,447 (GeoNames).
