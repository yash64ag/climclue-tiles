Santa Rosa at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Santa Rosa is 185th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 98th sunniest (about 3,094 hours of sunshine a year) and 168th wettest (970 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vallejo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Santa Rosa is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 351 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.4 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, January (8.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 118% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Santa Rosa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
