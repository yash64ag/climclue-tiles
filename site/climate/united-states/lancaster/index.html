Lancaster at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Lancaster is 128th warmest (17.0 °C year average), 52nd sunniest (about 3,193 hours of sunshine a year) and 320th wettest (198 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Palmdale, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Lancaster is 1.8 °C warmer in July and 0.8 °C warmer in December, with 112 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.1 °C), averages 17.7 °C more than its coolest, December (8.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 86 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 8 km from Lancaster measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.3 °C (4.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Lancaster has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 86 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
