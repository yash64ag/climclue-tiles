Dallas at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Dallas is 68th warmest (18.9 °C year average), 160th sunniest (about 2,794 hours of sunshine a year) and 163rd wettest (983 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Irving, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Dallas is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.4 °C), averages 21.5 °C more than its coolest, January (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 404 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Dallas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 404 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
