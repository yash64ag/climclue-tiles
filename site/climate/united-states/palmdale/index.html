Palmdale weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Palmdale, United States (California), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Palmdale at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Palmdale is 888th warmest (16.9 °C year average), 315th sunniest (about 3,184 hours of sunshine a year) and 3042nd wettest (241 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vincent, California (34.5°N 118.1°W), the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Palmdale is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in December, with 126 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.9 °C), averages 17.2 °C more than its coolest, December (8.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 1.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 76 km from the sea.

Why Palmdale has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 76 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.2 °C. The 10 km area around Palmdale, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 15.6 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Palmdale itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.579°N, 118.116°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-494-618), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
