Chula Vista at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Chula Vista is 139th warmest (16.8 °C year average), 109th sunniest (about 3,068 hours of sunshine a year) and 303rd wettest (286 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Diego, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Chula Vista is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.1 °C), averages 7.5 °C more than its coolest, January (13.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.9 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 117% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Chula Vista has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
