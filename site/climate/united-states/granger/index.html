Granger at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Granger is 2649th warmest (10.0 °C year average), 2416th sunniest (about 2,469 hours of sunshine a year) and 1754th wettest (1,052 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mishawaka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Granger is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. The area lies about 873 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Granger has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 873 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Granger itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.753°N, 86.111°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-422-938), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,465, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
