Thousand Oaks at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Thousand Oaks is 155th warmest (16.3 °C year average), 90th sunniest (about 3,120 hours of sunshine a year) and 248th wettest (441 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Simi Valley, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Thousand Oaks is 1.3 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.9 °C), averages 10.3 °C more than its coolest, December (11.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Thousand Oaks, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Thousand Oaks has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
