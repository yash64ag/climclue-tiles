Independence at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Independence is 225th warmest (12.8 °C year average), 178th sunniest (about 2,725 hours of sunshine a year) and 126th wettest (1,108 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kansas City, Missouri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Independence is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 78 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.9 °C), averages 27.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,047 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Independence has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,047 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
