Cleveland at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Cleveland is 269th warmest (10.7 °C year average), 319th sunniest (about 2,136 hours of sunshine a year) and 147th wettest (1,029 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Akron, the nearest covered town with different numbers (49 km away), Cleveland is 0.9 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 26.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 516 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Cleveland has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 516 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
