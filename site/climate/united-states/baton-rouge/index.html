Baton Rouge at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Baton Rouge is the 4th wettest (1,694 mm a year). Compared with Lafayette, the nearest covered town with different numbers (84 km away), Baton Rouge is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 34 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.1 °C), averages 16.7 °C more than its coolest, January (10.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.7 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 84 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Baton Rouge has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 84 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
