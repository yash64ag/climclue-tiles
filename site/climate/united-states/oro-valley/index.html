Oro Valley at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Oro Valley is 337th warmest (21.1 °C year average), 48th sunniest (about 3,702 hours of sunshine a year) and 2877th wettest (361 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Casas Adobes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Oro Valley is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in December, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Why Oro Valley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 247 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Oro Valley itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.391°N, 110.966°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-516-690), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
