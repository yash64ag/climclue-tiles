Vineyard at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Vineyard is 984th warmest (16.6 °C year average), 92nd sunniest (about 3,529 hours of sunshine a year) and 2604th wettest (538 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Florin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Vineyard is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 5 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.4 °C), averages 16.1 °C more than its coolest, December (8.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 3.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 119 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 12 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Vineyard has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 119 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vineyard itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.464°N, 121.347°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-455-586), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 24,836, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
