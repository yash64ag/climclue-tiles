Pompano Beach at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Pompano Beach is 9th warmest (24.2 °C year average), 110th sunniest (about 3,068 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (1,528 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fort Lauderdale, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Pompano Beach is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 8.5 °C more than its coolest, January (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Pompano Beach has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
