Chicago at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Chicago is 281st warmest (10.3 °C year average), 279th sunniest (about 2,470 hours of sunshine a year) and 141st wettest (1,064 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Naperville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Chicago is 0.7 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.1 °C), averages 28.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 997 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Chicago has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 997 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
