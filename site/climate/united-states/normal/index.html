Normal at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Normal is 2171st warmest (11.0 °C year average), 1773rd sunniest (about 2,534 hours of sunshine a year) and 1847th wettest (1,032 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bloomington, Illinois, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Normal is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. The area lies about 1,084 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Normal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,084 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.3 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Normal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.514°N, 88.991°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-434-910), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
