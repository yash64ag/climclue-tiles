Why Garland has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 415 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Garland climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Garland?
August, with an average of 29.1 °C (84 °F), day highs around 34.2 °C (94 °F) and night lows around 24.9 °C (77 °F).
What is the coldest month in Garland?
January, with an average of 7.5 °C (45 °F), day highs around 13.7 °C (57 °F) and night lows around 3.7 °C (39 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Garland?
May, with 142 mm (5.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,075 mm (42.3 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Garland?
August, with 53 mm (2.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Garland?
July, with about 327 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Garland?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 5.7 cm (2.2 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in February; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time for mild weather in Garland?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: April and September.
What type of climate does Garland have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.3 × 11 km, that contains Garland’s GeoNames location (32.913°N, 96.639°W; ClimaClue area sq-510-833), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 236,897 (GeoNames).
