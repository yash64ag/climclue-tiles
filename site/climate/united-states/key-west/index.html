Key West at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Key West is the 2nd warmest (24.9 °C year average). Compared with Marco Island, the nearest covered town with different numbers (154 km away), Key West is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 2.5 °C warmer in January, with 339 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.1 °C), averages 6.9 °C more than its coolest, January (21.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Key West has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Key West itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.555°N, 81.782°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-594-982), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
