Jacksonville at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Jacksonville is 50th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 148th sunniest (about 2,845 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (1,457 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gainesville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (99 km away), Jacksonville is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 164 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.3 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 11 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Jacksonville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
