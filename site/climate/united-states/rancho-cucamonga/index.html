Rancho Cucamonga at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Rancho Cucamonga is 144th warmest (16.6 °C year average), 43rd sunniest (about 3,235 hours of sunshine a year) and 213th wettest (646 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ontario, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Rancho Cucamonga is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 2.0 °C cooler in December, with 113 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.9 °C), averages 15.4 °C more than its coolest, December (9.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 76 km from the sea.

Why Rancho Cucamonga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 76 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.4 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
