Dumas at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Dumas is 1441st warmest (13.5 °C year average), 234th sunniest (about 3,221 hours of sunshine a year) and 2711th wettest (472 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Amarillo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (73 km away), Dumas is about as warm in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.8 °C), averages 23.8 °C more than its coolest, January (2.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 965 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Dumas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 965 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dumas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.866°N, 101.973°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-481-780), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,001, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
