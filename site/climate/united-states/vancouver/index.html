Vancouver at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Vancouver is 246th warmest (11.7 °C year average), 304th sunniest (about 2,297 hours of sunshine a year) and 56th wettest (1,297 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Portland, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Vancouver is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 99 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (19.8 °C), averages 15.5 °C more than its coolest, December (4.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 1.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 109 km from the sea.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Vancouver are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.

Why Vancouver has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 109 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
