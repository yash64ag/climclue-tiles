Muskogee at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Muskogee is 1005th warmest (16.4 °C year average), 1074th sunniest (about 2,745 hours of sunshine a year) and 1273rd wettest (1,174 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tahlequah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (41 km away), Muskogee is 0.9 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 76 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 691 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Muskogee has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 691 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Muskogee itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.748°N, 95.370°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-482-846), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
