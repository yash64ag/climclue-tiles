Oxnard at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Oxnard is 145th warmest (16.6 °C year average), 92nd sunniest (about 3,113 hours of sunshine a year) and 263rd wettest (413 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thousand Oaks, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Oxnard is 1.1 °C cooler in August and 1.9 °C warmer in January, with 67 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.4 °C), averages 7.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.0 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Oxnard has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
