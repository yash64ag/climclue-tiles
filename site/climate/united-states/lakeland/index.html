Lakeland at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Lakeland is 33rd warmest (22.0 °C year average), 142nd sunniest (about 2,898 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (1,378 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Brandon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Lakeland is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 23 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.1 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, January (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.1 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 68 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Lakeland has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 68 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
