Makakilo at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Makakilo is 177th warmest (23.2 °C year average), 884th sunniest (about 2,887 hours of sunshine a year) and 2262nd wettest (892 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Makakilo City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Makakilo is 0.4 °C cooler in September and 0.4 °C cooler in February, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (24.9 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, February (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.3 °C (63 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Makakilo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Makakilo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.352°N, 158.087°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-626-219), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
