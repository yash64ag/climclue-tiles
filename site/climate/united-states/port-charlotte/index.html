Port Charlotte at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Port Charlotte is 185th warmest (23.1 °C year average), 653rd sunniest (about 3,024 hours of sunshine a year) and 489th wettest (1,338 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Punta Gorda Isles, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Port Charlotte is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, January (16.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.8 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Port Charlotte has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Port Charlotte itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.976°N, 82.091°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-570-979), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
