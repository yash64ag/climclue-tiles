Greeley at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Greeley is 284th warmest (10.2 °C year average), 127th sunniest (about 3,021 hours of sunshine a year) and 280th wettest (351 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fort Collins, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Greeley is 1.4 °C warmer in July and 0.6 °C cooler in December, with 41 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 25.7 °C more than its coolest, December (-2.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,281 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Greeley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,281 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
