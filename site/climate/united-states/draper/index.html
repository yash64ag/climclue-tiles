Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Draper, United States (Utah), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Why Draper has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 943 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Draper itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.525°N, 111.864°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-434-681), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
