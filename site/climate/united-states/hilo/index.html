Hilo at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Hilo is the wettest (4,712 mm a year). Compared with Kīhei, the nearest covered town with different numbers (182 km away), Hilo is 2.2 °C cooler in August and 1.2 °C cooler in February, with 3,796 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.0 °C), averages 2.9 °C more than its coolest, February (21.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.7 °C (62 °F), in February. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 5 km from Hilo measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 146% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Hilo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hilo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.730°N, 155.091°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-642-249), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 43,263, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
