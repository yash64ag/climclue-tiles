San Francisco at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, San Francisco is 215th warmest (13.2 °C year average), 120th sunniest (about 3,048 hours of sunshine a year) and 205th wettest (727 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oakland, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), San Francisco is 2.2 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 113 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.1 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, January (9.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.9 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station under 1 km from San Francisco measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 1.9 °C (3.4 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why San Francisco has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
