Athens at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Athens is 146th warmest (16.6 °C year average), 300th sunniest (about 2,341 hours of sunshine a year) and 82nd wettest (1,221 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sandy Springs, the nearest covered town with different numbers (92 km away), Athens is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 145 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 20.2 °C more than its coolest, January (6.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 322 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Athens has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 322 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the coldest month in Athens?
January, with an average of 6.2 °C (43 °F), day highs around 11.3 °C (52 °F) and night lows around 2.2 °C (36 °F).

Does it snow in Athens?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 6.2 cm (2.4 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; it usually melts within days.
