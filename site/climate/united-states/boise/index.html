Boise at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Boise is 253rd warmest (11.5 °C year average), 138th sunniest (about 2,921 hours of sunshine a year) and 266th wettest (402 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tri-Cities, the nearest covered town with different numbers (376 km away), Boise is 1.8 °C cooler in July and 1.6 °C cooler in December, with 200 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.6 °C), averages 25.2 °C more than its coolest, December (-0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 637 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Boise has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 637 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
