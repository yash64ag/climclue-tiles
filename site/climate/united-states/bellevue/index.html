Bellevue at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Bellevue is 263rd warmest (11.0 °C year average), 323rd sunniest (about 2,098 hours of sunshine a year) and 143rd wettest (1,061 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Seattle, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Bellevue is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in December, with 15 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (17.9 °C), averages 12.9 °C more than its coolest, December (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 27 km from the sea.

Why Bellevue has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 27 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
