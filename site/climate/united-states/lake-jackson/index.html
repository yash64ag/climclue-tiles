Lake Jackson at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Lake Jackson is 330th warmest (21.2 °C year average), 1385th sunniest (about 2,622 hours of sunshine a year) and 569th wettest (1,317 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Angleton, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Lake Jackson is about as warm in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Why Lake Jackson has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 30 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lake Jackson itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.034°N, 95.434°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-549-845), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
