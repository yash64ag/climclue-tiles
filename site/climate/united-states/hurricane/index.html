Hurricane at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Hurricane is 987th warmest (16.5 °C year average), 136th sunniest (about 3,431 hours of sunshine a year) and 3003rd wettest (290 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Washington, Utah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Hurricane is 1.8 °C cooler in July and 1.4 °C cooler in December, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.0 °C), averages 26.1 °C more than its coolest, December (4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 581 km from the sea.

Why Hurricane has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 581 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.1 °C. The 10 km area around Hurricane, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 15.0 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hurricane itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.175°N, 113.290°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-468-667), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
