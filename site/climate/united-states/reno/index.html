Reno at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Reno is 250th warmest (11.7 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 3,498 hours of sunshine a year) and 303rd wettest (266 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Roseville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (154 km away), Reno is 0.8 °C cooler in July and 7.6 °C cooler in December, with 371 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.3 °C), averages 23.3 °C more than its coolest, December (1.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 297 km from the sea.

Why Reno has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 297 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.3 °C. The 10 km area around Reno, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 10.5 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Reno itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.530°N, 119.814°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-444-601), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
