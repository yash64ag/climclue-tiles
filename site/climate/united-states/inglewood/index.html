Inglewood at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Inglewood is 115th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 70th sunniest (about 3,165 hours of sunshine a year) and 273rd wettest (371 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Torrance, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Inglewood is 1.2 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.8 °C), averages 10.1 °C more than its coolest, December (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Inglewood has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-500-616), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
