Green Bay at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Green Bay is 323rd warmest (7.9 °C year average), 288th sunniest (about 2,433 hours of sunshine a year) and 202nd wettest (787 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Milwaukee, the nearest covered town with different numbers (165 km away), Green Bay is 0.9 °C cooler in July and 2.7 °C cooler in January, with 102 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.9 °C), averages 29.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 956 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Green Bay has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 956 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Green Bay itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.519°N, 88.020°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-394-919), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
