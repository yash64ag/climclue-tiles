Kent at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Kent is 255th warmest (11.4 °C year average), 318th sunniest (about 2,138 hours of sunshine a year) and 98th wettest (1,188 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Renton, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Kent is about as warm in August and about as warm in December, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.8 °C), averages 13.8 °C more than its coolest, December (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 2.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 58 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 121% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 58 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
