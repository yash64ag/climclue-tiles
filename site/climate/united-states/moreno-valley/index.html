Moreno Valley at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Moreno Valley is 814th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 215th sunniest (about 3,256 hours of sunshine a year) and 2802nd wettest (412 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Loma Linda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Moreno Valley is 0.9 °C cooler in August and 0.8 °C cooler in December, with 114 mm less precipitation a year. Why Moreno Valley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 76 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Moreno Valley itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.938°N, 117.231°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-500-627), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
