Marion, Illinois at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Marion, Illinois is 1295th warmest (14.4 °C year average), 1393rd sunniest (about 2,620 hours of sunshine a year) and 1245th wettest (1,179 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Carbondale, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Marion, Illinois is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. The area lies about 816 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Marion, Illinois has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 816 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Marion itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.731°N, 88.933°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-462-910), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
