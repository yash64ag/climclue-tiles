Spanish Springs at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Spanish Springs is 1702nd warmest (12.4 °C year average), 95th sunniest (about 3,520 hours of sunshine a year) and 3078th wettest (207 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sun Valley, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Spanish Springs is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C warmer in December, with 49 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.7 °C), averages 22.5 °C more than its coolest, December (2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 311 km from the sea.

Why Spanish Springs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 311 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.5 °C. The 10 km area around Spanish Springs, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 11.2 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Spanish Springs itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.649°N, 119.707°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-443-602), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
