Doral at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Doral is 70th warmest (24.2 °C year average), 486th sunniest (about 3,108 hours of sunshine a year) and 122nd wettest (1,543 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fountainebleau, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Doral is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 8.1 °C more than its coolest, January (19.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.7 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 30 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Doral has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 30 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Doral itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.820°N, 80.355°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-581-996), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
