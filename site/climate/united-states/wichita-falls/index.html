Wichita Falls at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Wichita Falls is 95th warmest (17.9 °C year average), 123rd sunniest (about 3,039 hours of sunshine a year) and 201st wettest (806 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Denton, the nearest covered town with different numbers (148 km away), Wichita Falls is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 209 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.4 °C), averages 23.6 °C more than its coolest, January (5.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 596 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Wichita Falls has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 596 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
