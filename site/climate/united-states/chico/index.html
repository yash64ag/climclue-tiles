Chico at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Chico is 82nd warmest (18.4 °C year average), 26th sunniest (about 3,495 hours of sunshine a year) and 193rd wettest (880 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Roseville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (118 km away), Chico is 4.0 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in December, with 222 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.1 °C), averages 20.1 °C more than its coolest, December (8.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 171 km from the sea.

Why Chico has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 171 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
