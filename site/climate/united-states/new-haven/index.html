New Haven at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, New Haven is 264th warmest (11.0 °C year average), 233rd sunniest (about 2,548 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (1,381 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bridgeport, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), New Haven is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 137 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.8 °C), averages 23.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Why New Haven has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
