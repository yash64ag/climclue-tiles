Colorado Springs at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Colorado Springs is 2870th warmest (9.0 °C year average), 417th sunniest (about 3,142 hours of sunshine a year) and 2784th wettest (426 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Security-Widefield, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Colorado Springs is 1.9 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in December, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. The area lies about 1,141 km from the sea (deep inland).

Best time to visit Colorado Springs

For mild weather, the best months to visit Colorado Springs are June, July, August and September.

Why Colorado Springs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,141 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Colorado Springs itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.834°N, 104.821°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-451-751), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 456,568, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
