Colorado Springs at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Colorado Springs is 323rd warmest (7.8 °C year average), 83rd sunniest (about 3,142 hours of sunshine a year) and 247th wettest (464 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pueblo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (67 km away), Colorado Springs is 5.4 °C cooler in July and 2.3 °C cooler in December, with 100 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.8 °C), averages 22.9 °C more than its coolest, December (-3.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 1,141 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Colorado Springs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,141 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
