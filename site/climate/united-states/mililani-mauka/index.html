Mililani Mauka at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Mililani Mauka is 162nd warmest (23.4 °C year average), 792nd sunniest (about 2,917 hours of sunshine a year) and 1501st wettest (1,123 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mililani Mauka / Launani Valley, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Mililani Mauka is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 4.7 °C more than its coolest, January (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.8 °C (62 °F), in February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 17 km from Mililani Mauka by up to 1.8 °C (3.3 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Mililani Mauka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mililani Mauka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.478°N, 157.995°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-625-220), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 21,075, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
