Why Sterling Heights has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 672 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Sterling Heights climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Sterling Heights?
July, with an average of 22.9 °C (73 °F), day highs around 27.7 °C (82 °F) and night lows around 18.8 °C (66 °F).
What is the coldest month in Sterling Heights?
January, with an average of -4.0 °C (25 °F), day highs around -0.5 °C (31 °F) and night lows around -7.8 °C (18 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Sterling Heights?

What is the driest month in Sterling Heights?

What is the sunniest month in Sterling Heights?

Does it snow in Sterling Heights?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 116 cm (45.7 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 4 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Sterling Heights?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: May, June, July, August and September.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.2 × 11 km, that contains Sterling Heights’s GeoNames location (42.580°N, 83.030°W; ClimaClue area sq-414-969), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 132,052 (GeoNames).
