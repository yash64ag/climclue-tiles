Pampa at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Pampa is 1340th warmest (14.0 °C year average), 299th sunniest (about 3,187 hours of sunshine a year) and 2534th wettest (607 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Amarillo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (86 km away), Pampa is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 57 mm more precipitation a year. The area lies about 878 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Pampa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 878 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pampa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.536°N, 100.960°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-484-790), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
