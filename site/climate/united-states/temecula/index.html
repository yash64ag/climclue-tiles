Temecula at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Temecula is 827th warmest (17.2 °C year average), 279th sunniest (about 3,195 hours of sunshine a year) and 2929th wettest (333 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Murrieta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Temecula is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in December, with 46 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.1 °C), averages 12.7 °C more than its coolest, December (11.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Temecula, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Temecula has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Temecula itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.494°N, 117.148°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-505-628), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
