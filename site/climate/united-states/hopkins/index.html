Hopkins weather by month and climate

Hopkins at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Hopkins is 3068th warmest (7.5 °C year average), 1316th sunniest (about 2,641 hours of sunshine a year) and 2340th wettest (858 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Minnetonka Mills, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Hopkins is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 10 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 1,200 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Hopkins has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,200 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hopkins itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.925°N, 93.463°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-390-865), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
