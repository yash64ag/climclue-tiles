El Cerrito at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, El Cerrito is 1294th warmest (14.4 °C year average), 503rd sunniest (about 3,100 hours of sunshine a year) and 2505th wettest (642 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Albany, California, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), El Cerrito is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 22 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.1 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, January (9.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Why El Cerrito has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El Cerrito itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.916°N, 122.312°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-576), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
