Irvine at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Irvine is 135th warmest (16.9 °C year average), 76th sunniest (about 3,157 hours of sunshine a year) and 288th wettest (321 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Costa Mesa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Irvine is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.3 °C), averages 8.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.9 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Irvine measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.7 °C (4.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Irvine has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
