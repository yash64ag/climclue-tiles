San Jacinto at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, San Jacinto is 628th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 190th sunniest (about 3,291 hours of sunshine a year) and 2852nd wettest (375 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hemet, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), San Jacinto is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in December, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.8 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, December (11.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 3.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 79 km from the sea.

Why San Jacinto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 79 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Jacinto itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.784°N, 116.959°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-502-630), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 46,951, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
