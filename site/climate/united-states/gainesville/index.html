Gainesville at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Gainesville is 49th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 145th sunniest (about 2,882 hours of sunshine a year) and 58th wettest (1,294 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jacksonville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (99 km away), Gainesville is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 164 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.8 °C), averages 14.4 °C more than its coolest, January (12.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 82 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Gainesville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 82 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
