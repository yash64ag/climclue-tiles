Tri-Cities at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Tri-Cities is 207th warmest (13.6 °C year average), 292nd sunniest (about 2,393 hours of sunshine a year) and 319th wettest (202 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Spokane, the nearest covered town with different numbers (207 km away), Tri-Cities is 4.0 °C warmer in July and 2.5 °C warmer in December, with 277 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 25.4 °C more than its coolest, December (1.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 307 km from the sea.

Why Tri-Cities has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 307 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
