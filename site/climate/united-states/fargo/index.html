Fargo at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Fargo is the 2nd coolest (5.9 °C year average). Compared with Minneapolis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (345 km away), Fargo is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 2.7 °C cooler in January, with 221 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.8 °C), averages 33.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,184 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 3 km from Fargo measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.1 °C (3.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Fargo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,184 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
