College Station at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, College Station is 52nd warmest (20.1 °C year average), 211th sunniest (about 2,617 hours of sunshine a year) and 144th wettest (1,060 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cypress, the nearest covered town with different numbers (95 km away), College Station is 0.8 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 194 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.1 °C), averages 18.9 °C more than its coolest, January (10.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 189 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why College Station has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 189 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
