La Palma at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, La Palma is 614th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 323rd sunniest (about 3,179 hours of sunshine a year) and 2935th wettest (331 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cerritos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), La Palma is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in December, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.6 °C), averages 9.5 °C more than its coolest, December (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.2 °C (47 °F), in December. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Why La Palma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Palma itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.846°N, 118.047°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-501-619), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
