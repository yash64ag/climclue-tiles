Seattle at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Seattle is the 5th least sunny (about 2,095 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Bellevue, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Seattle is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in December, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (17.9 °C), averages 12.8 °C more than its coolest, December (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 2.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 111% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Seattle has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
