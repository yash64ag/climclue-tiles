Scottsdale at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Scottsdale is 28th warmest (22.7 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 3,790 hours of sunshine a year) and 306th wettest (244 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tempe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Scottsdale is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 24 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.5 °C), averages 23.4 °C more than its coolest, December (11.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 293 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Scottsdale has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 293 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
