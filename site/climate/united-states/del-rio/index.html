Del Rio at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Del Rio is 319th warmest (21.4 °C year average), 1688th sunniest (about 2,548 hours of sunshine a year) and 2644th wettest (513 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Eagle Pass, the nearest covered town with different numbers (82 km away), Del Rio is 1.0 °C cooler in August and 1.6 °C cooler in December, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Why Del Rio has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 413 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Del Rio itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.363°N, 100.897°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-546-791), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
