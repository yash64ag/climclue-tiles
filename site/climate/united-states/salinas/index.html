Salinas at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Salinas is 212th warmest (13.4 °C year average), 81st sunniest (about 3,145 hours of sunshine a year) and 267th wettest (398 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Jose, the nearest covered town with different numbers (77 km away), Salinas is 2.9 °C cooler in September and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 126 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (16.8 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, January (9.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 2 km from Salinas measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 1.6 °C (2.9 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Salinas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
