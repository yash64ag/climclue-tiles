Why Las Vegas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 386 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Las Vegas climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Las Vegas?
July, with an average of 33.8 °C (93 °F), day highs around 39.9 °C (104 °F) and night lows around 26.4 °C (80 °F).
What is the coldest month in Las Vegas?
December, with an average of 8.8 °C (48 °F), day highs around 14.5 °C (58 °F) and night lows around 4.2 °C (40 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Las Vegas?
February, with 28 mm (1.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 141 mm (5.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Las Vegas?
June, with 3 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Las Vegas?
June, with about 397 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Las Vegas?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 2.3 cm (0.9 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time for mild weather in Las Vegas?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: March, April and October.
What type of climate does Las Vegas have?
Hot desert (Köppen BWh). Very dry and hot: far more water could evaporate than ever falls. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.0 × 11 km, that contains Las Vegas’s GeoNames location (36.175°N, 115.137°W; ClimaClue area sq-478-648), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 641,903 (GeoNames).
