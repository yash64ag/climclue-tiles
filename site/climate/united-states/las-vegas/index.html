Las Vegas at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Las Vegas is the 3rd driest (141 mm a year). Compared with Sunrise Manor, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Las Vegas is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.8 °C), averages 24.9 °C more than its coolest, December (8.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 386 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Las Vegas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 386 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
