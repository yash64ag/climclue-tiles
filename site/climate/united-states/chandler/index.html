Chandler at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Chandler is 24th warmest (23.0 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 3,804 hours of sunshine a year) and 317th wettest (214 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gilbert, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Chandler is 0.2 °C warmer in July and about as warm in December, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.6 °C), averages 23.3 °C more than its coolest, December (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in December. The area lies about 273 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 15 km from Chandler measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.1 °C (3.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Chandler has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 273 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
