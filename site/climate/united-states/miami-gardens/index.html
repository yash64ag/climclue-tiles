Miami Gardens at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Miami Gardens is the 5th warmest (24.3 °C year average). Compared with Pembroke Pines, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Miami Gardens is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 7.8 °C more than its coolest, January (19.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Miami Gardens has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
