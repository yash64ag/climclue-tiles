West Palm Beach at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, West Palm Beach is 12th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 124th sunniest (about 3,032 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (1,624 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pompano Beach, the nearest covered town with different numbers (54 km away), West Palm Beach is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 96 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 8.4 °C more than its coolest, January (19.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.3 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why West Palm Beach has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
