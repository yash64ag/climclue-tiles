San Bernardino at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, San Bernardino is 90th warmest (18.1 °C year average), 35th sunniest (about 3,285 hours of sunshine a year) and 224th wettest (557 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rialto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), San Bernardino is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.4 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, December (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.9 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 93 km from the sea.

Why San Bernardino has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 93 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
