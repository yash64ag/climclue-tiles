Lexington at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Lexington is 214th warmest (13.2 °C year average), 312th sunniest (about 2,191 hours of sunshine a year) and 77th wettest (1,232 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lexington-Fayette, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Lexington is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.6 °C), averages 23.5 °C more than its coolest, January (1.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 701 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Lexington has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 701 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
