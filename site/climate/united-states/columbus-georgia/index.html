Columbus, Georgia at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Columbus, Georgia is 88th warmest (18.2 °C year average), 162nd sunniest (about 2,783 hours of sunshine a year) and 67th wettest (1,263 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Montgomery, the nearest covered town with different numbers (124 km away), Columbus, Georgia is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 102 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.3 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, January (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 270 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Columbus, Georgia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 270 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
