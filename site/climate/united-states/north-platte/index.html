North Platte at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, North Platte is 2713th warmest (9.7 °C year average), 802nd sunniest (about 2,911 hours of sunshine a year) and 2632nd wettest (519 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kearney, the nearest covered town with different numbers (149 km away), North Platte is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 165 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 1,396 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why North Platte has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 1,396 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for North Platte itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.124°N, 100.765°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-428-792), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
