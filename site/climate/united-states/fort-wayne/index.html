Fort Wayne at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Fort Wayne is 279th warmest (10.5 °C year average), 197th sunniest (about 2,653 hours of sunshine a year) and 156th wettest (994 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with South Bend, the nearest covered town with different numbers (112 km away), Fort Wayne is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 39 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 776 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 102% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Fort Wayne has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 776 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fort Wayne itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.131°N, 85.129°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-428-948), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
