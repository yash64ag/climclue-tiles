How February compares in New York City

February is the 2nd coolest of the 12, the driest month and the 4th least sunny of the 12 in New York City. Compared with January: 1.3 °C warmer, 6 mm less precipitation and 2 hours more sunshine. March is 4.1 °C warmer than February on average.

New York City in February: questions and answers

What is the weather like in New York City in February?
In February, New York City averages a day high of 4.8 °C (41 °F) and a night low of -3.2 °C (26 °F), with 76 mm (3.0 in) of precipitation and about 162 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is February a good time to visit New York City?
February is not one of New York City’s mild months; those are June and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in New York City in February?
76 mm (3.0 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making February the driest month of the year. The wettest month is July (114 mm), the driest February (76 mm).
Does it snow in New York City in February?
Yes, on average: ERA5-Land gives about 26 cm (10.1 in) of fresh snow in February (1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), and snow lies on the ground with an average depth of 5.9 cm.
How many hours of sunshine does New York City get in February?
About 162 hours of bright sunshine, the 4th least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for New York City in February?
Going by the averages: a warm coat, a hat and gloves, a compact umbrella and waterproof boots. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
