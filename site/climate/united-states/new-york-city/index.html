New York City at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, New York City is 231st warmest (12.3 °C year average), 259th sunniest (about 2,499 hours of sunshine a year) and 104th wettest (1,181 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manhattan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), New York City is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 31 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.6 °C), averages 25.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why New York City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
