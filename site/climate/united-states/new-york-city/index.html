New York City at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, New York City is 1644th warmest (12.7 °C year average), 2070th sunniest (about 2,499 hours of sunshine a year) and 1165th wettest (1,193 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hoboken, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), New York City is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.9 °C), averages 24.7 °C more than its coolest, January (0.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Why New York City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

New York City climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in New York City?
July, with an average of 24.9 °C (77 °F), day highs around 29.8 °C (86 °F) and night lows around 20.0 °C (68 °F).
What is the coldest month in New York City?
January, with an average of 0.2 °C (32 °F), day highs around 4.1 °C (39 °F) and night lows around -3.6 °C (26 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in New York City?
July, with 115 mm (4.5 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,193 mm (47.0 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in New York City?
February, with 77 mm (3.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in New York City?
July, with about 267 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in New York City?
Yes. About 57 cm (22.5 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in January; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 2 months.

How warm is the sea in New York City?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for New York City itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.714°N, 74.006°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-432-1059), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 8,804,190, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
