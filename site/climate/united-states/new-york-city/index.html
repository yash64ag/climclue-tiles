New York City at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, New York City is 232nd warmest (12.3 °C year average), 259th sunniest (about 2,499 hours of sunshine a year) and 100th wettest (1,193 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jersey City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), New York City is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.6 °C), averages 25.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why New York City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for New York City itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.714°N, 74.006°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-432-1059), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
