San Benito at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, San Benito is 153rd warmest (23.6 °C year average), 1545th sunniest (about 2,580 hours of sunshine a year) and 2485th wettest (675 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Harlingen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), San Benito is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.9 °C), averages 14.0 °C more than its coolest, January (15.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.3 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 45 km from the sea.

Why San Benito has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 45 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Benito itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.133°N, 97.631°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-578-823), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 24,496, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
