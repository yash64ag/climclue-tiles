Fresno at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Fresno is 96th warmest (17.9 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 3,489 hours of sunshine a year) and 289th wettest (321 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Clovis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Fresno is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 47 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 19.1 °C more than its coolest, January (8.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 184 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Fresno has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 184 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
