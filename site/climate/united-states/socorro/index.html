Socorro at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Socorro is 537th warmest (18.8 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 3,636 hours of sunshine a year) and 3064th wettest (218 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Socorro Mission Number 1 Colonia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Socorro is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.9 °C), averages 21.3 °C more than its coolest, December (7.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 599 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 88% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Socorro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 599 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Socorro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.655°N, 106.303°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-523-736), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 33,222, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
