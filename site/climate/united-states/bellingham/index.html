Bellingham at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Bellingham is the 2nd least sunny (about 1,832 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Anacortes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Bellingham is 1.6 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C cooler in December, with 321 mm more precipitation a year. Why Bellingham has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bellingham itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (48.760°N, 122.488°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-352-575), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
