Laredo at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Laredo is 13th warmest (23.8 °C year average), 308th sunniest (about 2,218 hours of sunshine a year) and 239th wettest (488 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with McAllen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (193 km away), Laredo is 1.4 °C warmer in August and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 84 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (31.4 °C), averages 17.1 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.2 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 218 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Laredo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 218 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
