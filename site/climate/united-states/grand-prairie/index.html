Grand Prairie at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Grand Prairie is 64th warmest (19.0 °C year average), 159th sunniest (about 2,797 hours of sunshine a year) and 178th wettest (938 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Irving, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Grand Prairie is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 27 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.6 °C), averages 21.7 °C more than its coolest, January (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 408 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Grand Prairie has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 408 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
