Oakland at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Oakland is 199th warmest (14.1 °C year average), 86th sunniest (about 3,122 hours of sunshine a year) and 217th wettest (614 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Francisco, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Oakland is 2.2 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 113 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.3 °C), averages 9.4 °C more than its coolest, January (8.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Oakland measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.1 °C (3.7 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Oakland has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
