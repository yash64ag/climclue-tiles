San Jose at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, San Jose is 187th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 64th sunniest (about 3,178 hours of sunshine a year) and 232nd wettest (525 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Clara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), San Jose is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 60 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (19.8 °C), averages 10.4 °C more than its coolest, January (9.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Why San Jose has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
