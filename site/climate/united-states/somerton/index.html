Somerton at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Somerton is the 3rd sunniest (about 3,868 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (72 mm a year). Compared with San Luis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Somerton is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (33.5 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, December (13.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.9 °C (43 °F), in December. The area lies about 111 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 7 km from Somerton by up to 2.6 °C (4.6 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Somerton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 111 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Somerton itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.596°N, 114.710°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-514-652), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,048, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
