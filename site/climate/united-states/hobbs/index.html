Hobbs at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Hobbs is 1010th warmest (16.4 °C year average), 351st sunniest (about 3,170 hours of sunshine a year) and 2824th wettest (398 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Carlsbad, New Mexico, the nearest covered town with different numbers (107 km away), Hobbs is 2.2 °C cooler in July and 1.4 °C cooler in January, with 61 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 20.7 °C more than its coolest, January (5.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 800 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Hobbs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 800 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hobbs itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.703°N, 103.136°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-512-768), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
