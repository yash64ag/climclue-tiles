Palm Bay at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Palm Bay is 28th warmest (22.9 °C year average), 135th sunniest (about 2,950 hours of sunshine a year) and 42nd wettest (1,340 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Port Saint Lucie, the nearest covered town with different numbers (86 km away), Palm Bay is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 96 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.2 °C), averages 9.7 °C more than its coolest, January (17.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.1 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Palm Bay has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Palm Bay itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.034°N, 80.589°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-559-994), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
