Santa Clarita at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Santa Clarita is 151st warmest (16.4 °C year average), 71st sunniest (about 3,160 hours of sunshine a year) and 243rd wettest (475 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Valencia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Santa Clarita is 0.7 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 60 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.1 °C), averages 14.4 °C more than its coolest, December (9.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santa Clarita, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santa Clarita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.4 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
