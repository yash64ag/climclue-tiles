New Bedford at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, New Bedford is 270th warmest (10.6 °C year average), 244th sunniest (about 2,524 hours of sunshine a year) and 52nd wettest (1,315 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Providence, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), New Bedford is 1.3 °C cooler in July and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.6 °C), averages 22.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why New Bedford has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
