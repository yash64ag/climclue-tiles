Indianapolis weather in September

Average September weather in Indianapolis, United States: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In September, Indianapolis averages 25.6 °C (78 °F) days and 12.8 °C (55 °F) nights, with 78 mm (3.1 in) of rain over the month and about 231 hours of sunshine.

How September compares in Indianapolis

September is the 4th warmest of the 12, the 4th driest of the 12 and the 5th sunniest of the 12 in Indianapolis. Compared with August: 4.1 °C cooler, 4 mm less precipitation and 42 hours less sunshine. October is 6.6 °C cooler than September on average.

Indianapolis in September: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Indianapolis in September?
In September, Indianapolis averages a day high of 25.6 °C (78 °F) and a night low of 12.8 °C (55 °F), with 78 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation and about 231 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is September a good time to visit Indianapolis?
For mild weather, yes: September is one of Indianapolis’s mild months (August and September), counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Indianapolis in September?
78 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making September the 4th driest of the 12. The wettest month is May (129 mm), the driest February (60 mm).
Does it snow in Indianapolis in September?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for September in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Indianapolis get in September?
About 231 hours of bright sunshine, the 5th sunniest of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Indianapolis in September?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes, an extra layer for cooler evenings and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
