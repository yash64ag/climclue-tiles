San Diego at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, San Diego is 136th warmest (16.9 °C year average), 115th sunniest (about 3,061 hours of sunshine a year) and 305th wettest (273 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chula Vista, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), San Diego is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.1 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, January (13.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.5 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 19 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why San Diego has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
