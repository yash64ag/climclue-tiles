Fullerton at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Fullerton is 112th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 3,186 hours of sunshine a year) and 284th wettest (337 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Garden Grove, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Fullerton is 0.7 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.8 °C), averages 10.0 °C more than its coolest, December (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.6 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 118% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Fullerton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
