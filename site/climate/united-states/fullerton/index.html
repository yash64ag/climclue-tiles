Fullerton at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Fullerton is 605th warmest (18.5 °C year average), 307th sunniest (about 3,186 hours of sunshine a year) and 2901st wettest (347 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Anaheim, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Fullerton is 0.2 °C warmer in August and about as warm in December, with 22 mm more precipitation a year. Why Fullerton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C. The 10 km area around Fullerton, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.5 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fullerton itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.870°N, 117.925°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-501-620), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
