San Antonio at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, San Antonio is 47th warmest (20.5 °C year average), 228th sunniest (about 2,571 hours of sunshine a year) and 200th wettest (822 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Austin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (118 km away), San Antonio is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.1 °C), averages 18.2 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 227 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why San Antonio has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 227 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
