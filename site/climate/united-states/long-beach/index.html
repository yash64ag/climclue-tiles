Long Beach at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Long Beach is 118th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 73rd sunniest (about 3,158 hours of sunshine a year) and 291st wettest (317 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Torrance, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Long Beach is 0.2 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 21 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.9 °C), averages 8.9 °C more than its coolest, January (13.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 102% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Long Beach has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
