Norfolk at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Norfolk is 170th warmest (15.6 °C year average), 193rd sunniest (about 2,667 hours of sunshine a year) and 91st wettest (1,207 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Newport News, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Norfolk is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.1 °C), averages 21.3 °C more than its coolest, January (4.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Norfolk

For mild weather, the best months to visit Norfolk are May and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (26.1 °C), August (25.3 °C) and June (23.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (81 mm), February (83 mm) and November (85 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): June (279 h), July (277 h) and May (271 h).

Why Norfolk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
