Bakersfield at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Bakersfield is 91st warmest (18.0 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 3,278 hours of sunshine a year) and 318th wettest (202 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Visalia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (109 km away), Bakersfield is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C warmer in December, with 128 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.6 °C), averages 18.0 °C more than its coolest, December (9.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 124 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 123% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Bakersfield has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 124 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
