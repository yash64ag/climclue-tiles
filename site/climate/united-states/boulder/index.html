Boulder at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Boulder is 319th warmest (8.8 °C year average), 119th sunniest (about 3,050 hours of sunshine a year) and 249th wettest (462 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Westminster, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Boulder is 1.3 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.9 °C), averages 23.0 °C more than its coolest, December (-2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,219 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Boulder has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,219 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
