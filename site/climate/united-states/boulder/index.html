Boulder at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Boulder is 319th warmest (8.8 °C year average), 119th sunniest (about 3,050 hours of sunshine a year) and 242nd wettest (460 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Westminster, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Boulder is 1.5 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.7 °C), averages 22.9 °C more than its coolest, December (-2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,219 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 3 km from Boulder measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.9 °C (5.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Boulder has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,219 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Boulder itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.015°N, 105.271°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-439-747), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
