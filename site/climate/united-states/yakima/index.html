Yakima at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Yakima is 2321st warmest (10.6 °C year average), 2826th sunniest (about 2,259 hours of sunshine a year) and 3084th wettest (198 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ellensburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Yakima is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 1.0 °C warmer in December, with 47 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.5 °C), averages 23.7 °C more than its coolest, December (-1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 196 km from the sea.

Why Yakima has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 196 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yakima itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.602°N, 120.506°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-373-594), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 93,701, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
