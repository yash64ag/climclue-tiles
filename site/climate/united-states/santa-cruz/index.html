Santa Cruz at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Santa Cruz is 1620th warmest (13.0 °C year average), 543rd sunniest (about 3,085 hours of sunshine a year) and 2452nd wettest (759 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Live Oak, California, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Santa Cruz is 0.3 °C cooler in September and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (15.5 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, January (11.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.5 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 3 km from Santa Cruz measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 3.3 °C (6.0 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Santa Cruz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa Cruz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.974°N, 122.031°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-470-579), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 64,220, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
