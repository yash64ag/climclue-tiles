Lubbock at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Lubbock is 163rd warmest (15.8 °C year average), 78th sunniest (about 3,154 hours of sunshine a year) and 233rd wettest (522 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Midland, the nearest covered town with different numbers (177 km away), Lubbock is 1.2 °C cooler in July and 2.2 °C cooler in January, with 141 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (4.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 764 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Lubbock has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 764 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
