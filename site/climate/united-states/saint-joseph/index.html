Saint Joseph weather by month and climate

Saint Joseph at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Saint Joseph is 1807th warmest (12.1 °C year average), 1119th sunniest (about 2,726 hours of sunshine a year) and 2115th wettest (954 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Leavenworth, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Saint Joseph is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 121 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 1,128 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Saint Joseph has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,128 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Saint Joseph itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.769°N, 94.847°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-442-851), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
