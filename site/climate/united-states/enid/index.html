Enid at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Enid is 1189th warmest (15.2 °C year average), 682nd sunniest (about 2,999 hours of sunshine a year) and 2285th wettest (883 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ponca City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (79 km away), Enid is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 59 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 26.1 °C more than its coolest, January (2.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 806 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Enid has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 806 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Enid itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.396°N, 97.878°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-476-821), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
