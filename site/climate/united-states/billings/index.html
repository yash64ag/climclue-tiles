Billings at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Billings is 301st warmest (9.6 °C year average), 176th sunniest (about 2,735 hours of sunshine a year) and 274th wettest (368 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Salt Lake City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (622 km away), Billings is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C cooler in December, with 204 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.9 °C), averages 26.9 °C more than its coolest, December (-3.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,082 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Billings has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,082 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
