Cheshire weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Cheshire, United States (Connecticut), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Why Cheshire has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the coldest month in Cheshire?
January, with an average of -1.6 °C (29 °F), day highs around 3.0 °C (37 °F) and night lows around -6.1 °C (21 °F).

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cheshire itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.499°N, 72.901°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-425-1070), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 29,443, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
