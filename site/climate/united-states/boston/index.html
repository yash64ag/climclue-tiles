Boston at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Boston is 280th warmest (10.3 °C year average), 250th sunniest (about 2,518 hours of sunshine a year) and 75th wettest (1,233 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cambridge, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Boston is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.3 °C), averages 24.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 111% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Boston has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
