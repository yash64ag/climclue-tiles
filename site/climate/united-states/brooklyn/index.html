Brooklyn at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Brooklyn is 233rd warmest (12.3 °C year average), 251st sunniest (about 2,518 hours of sunshine a year) and 83rd wettest (1,218 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with New York City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Brooklyn is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 37 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.4 °C), averages 24.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Brooklyn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
