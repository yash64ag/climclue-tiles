Louisville at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Louisville is 205th warmest (13.7 °C year average), 286th sunniest (about 2,462 hours of sunshine a year) and 121st wettest (1,128 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lexington, the nearest covered town with different numbers (116 km away), Louisville is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 103 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.6 °C), averages 24.8 °C more than its coolest, January (0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 811 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 99% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Louisville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 811 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
