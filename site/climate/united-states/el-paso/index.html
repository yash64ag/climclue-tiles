Why El Paso has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 600 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

El Paso climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in El Paso?
July, with an average of 28.6 °C (83 °F), day highs around 34.7 °C (94 °F) and night lows around 22.1 °C (72 °F).
What is the coldest month in El Paso?
December, with an average of 7.3 °C (45 °F), day highs around 14.4 °C (58 °F) and night lows around 1.6 °C (35 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in El Paso?
August, with 47 mm (1.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 239 mm (9.4 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in El Paso?
March, with 7 mm (0.3 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in El Paso?
May, with about 377 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in El Paso?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 3.0 cm (1.2 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in December; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time for mild weather in El Paso?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: April, May, September and October.
What type of climate does El Paso have?
Hot desert (Köppen BWh). Very dry and hot: far more water could evaporate than ever falls. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.5 × 11 km, that contains El Paso’s GeoNames location (31.759°N, 106.487°W; ClimaClue area sq-522-735), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 678,815 (GeoNames).
