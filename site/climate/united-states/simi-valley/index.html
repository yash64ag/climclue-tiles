Simi Valley at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Simi Valley is 147th warmest (16.5 °C year average), 84th sunniest (about 3,138 hours of sunshine a year) and 254th wettest (449 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thousand Oaks, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Simi Valley is 1.4 °C warmer in August and 0.6 °C cooler in December, with 31 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.9 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, December (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Simi Valley, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Simi Valley has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
