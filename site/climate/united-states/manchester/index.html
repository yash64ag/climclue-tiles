Manchester at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Manchester is 308th warmest (9.4 °C year average), 268th sunniest (about 2,488 hours of sunshine a year) and 110th wettest (1,160 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lowell, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Manchester is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 83 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.4 °C), averages 26.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 58 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Manchester has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 58 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
