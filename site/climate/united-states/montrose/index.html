Montrose at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Montrose is 2850th warmest (9.3 °C year average), 312th sunniest (about 3,185 hours of sunshine a year) and 3024th wettest (262 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Clifton, Colorado, the nearest covered town with different numbers (84 km away), Montrose is 3.6 °C cooler in July and 0.9 °C cooler in December, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 945 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Montrose has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 945 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Montrose itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.478°N, 107.876°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-455-721), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
