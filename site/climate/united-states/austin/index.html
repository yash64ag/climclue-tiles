Austin at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Austin is 51st warmest (20.2 °C year average), 213th sunniest (about 2,613 hours of sunshine a year) and 194th wettest (877 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Round Rock, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Austin is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 46 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.4 °C), averages 19.2 °C more than its coolest, January (10.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 233 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Austin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 233 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the coldest month in Austin?

Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 1.0 cm (0.4 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; it usually melts within days.
