Provo at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Provo is 307th warmest (9.4 °C year average), 132nd sunniest (about 2,995 hours of sunshine a year) and 237th wettest (497 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with West Jordan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Provo is 2.0 °C cooler in July and 2.4 °C cooler in December, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.1 °C), averages 27.6 °C more than its coolest, December (-3.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 930 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Provo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 930 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
