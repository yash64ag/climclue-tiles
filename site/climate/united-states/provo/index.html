Provo weather by month and climate

Provo at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Provo is 2260th warmest (10.8 °C year average), 689th sunniest (about 2,995 hours of sunshine a year) and 2758th wettest (441 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Orem, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Provo is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.4 °C), averages 27.7 °C more than its coolest, December (-2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 930 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Provo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 930 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.7 °C. The 10 km area around Provo, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 9.4 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Provo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.234°N, 111.659°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-437-683), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
