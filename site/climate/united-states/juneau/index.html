Juneau at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Juneau is the least sunny (about 1,509 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (2,891 mm a year). Compared with Eagle River, the nearest covered town with different numbers (908 km away), Juneau is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 8.1 °C warmer in January, with 2,266 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (14.0 °C), averages 15.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 26 km from the sea.

Why Juneau has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 26 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.9 °C. The 10 km area around Juneau, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 2.1 °C a year, 3.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Juneau climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Juneau?
July, with an average of 14.0 °C (57 °F), day highs around 17.8 °C (64 °F) and night lows around 10.3 °C (51 °F).
What is the coldest month in Juneau?
January, with an average of -1.9 °C (29 °F), day highs around 0.6 °C (33 °F) and night lows around -4.4 °C (24 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Juneau?
September, with 410 mm (16.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 2,891 mm (113.8 in) in a typical year.

What is the sunniest month in Juneau?

Yes. About 214 cm (84.3 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in December; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 12 months.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Juneau itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.302°N, 134.420°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-256-455), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 31,555, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
