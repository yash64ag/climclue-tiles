Newport Beach weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Newport Beach, United States (California), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Newport Beach at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Newport Beach is 905th warmest (16.8 °C year average), 386th sunniest (about 3,158 hours of sunshine a year) and 2956th wettest (318 mm a year), near the middle on each. Why Newport Beach has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Newport Beach itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.619°N, 117.929°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-503-620), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
