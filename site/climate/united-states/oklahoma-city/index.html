Oklahoma City at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Oklahoma City is 163rd warmest (15.9 °C year average), 125th sunniest (about 3,031 hours of sunshine a year) and 187th wettest (906 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Norman, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Oklahoma City is about as warm in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 73 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.1 °C), averages 24.8 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 703 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 12 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 97% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Oklahoma City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 703 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Oklahoma City itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.468°N, 97.516°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-485-824), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
