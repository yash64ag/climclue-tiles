Fallbrook at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Fallbrook is 806th warmest (17.7 °C year average), 360th sunniest (about 3,165 hours of sunshine a year) and 2962nd wettest (316 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Temecula, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Fallbrook is 1.4 °C cooler in August and 0.4 °C warmer in December, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.6 °C), averages 10.3 °C more than its coolest, December (13.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in December. The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Fallbrook, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Fallbrook has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fallbrook itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.376°N, 117.251°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-506-627), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,534, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
