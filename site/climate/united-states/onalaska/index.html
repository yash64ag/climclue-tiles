Onalaska at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Onalaska is 2956th warmest (8.4 °C year average), 1510th sunniest (about 2,589 hours of sunshine a year) and 2436th wettest (795 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with North La Crosse, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Onalaska is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.8 °C), averages 31.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,173 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Onalaska has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,173 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Onalaska itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.884°N, 91.235°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-401-887), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 18,468, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
