Hayward at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Hayward is 194th warmest (14.5 °C year average), 79th sunniest (about 3,150 hours of sunshine a year) and 231st wettest (526 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fremont, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Hayward is 0.8 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 16 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.1 °C), averages 10.0 °C more than its coolest, January (9.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Hayward has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
