The Woodlands at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, The Woodlands is 418th warmest (20.1 °C year average), 1641st sunniest (about 2,561 hours of sunshine a year) and 821st wettest (1,258 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Spring, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), The Woodlands is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 57 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.1 °C), averages 17.3 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.2 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 88 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of The Woodlands, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why The Woodlands has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 88 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for The Woodlands itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.158°N, 95.489°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-538-845), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
