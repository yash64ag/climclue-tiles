Newark at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Newark is 235th warmest (12.2 °C year average), 267th sunniest (about 2,490 hours of sunshine a year) and 76th wettest (1,232 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jersey City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Newark is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 52 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.6 °C), averages 25.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 42 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Newark has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 42 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
