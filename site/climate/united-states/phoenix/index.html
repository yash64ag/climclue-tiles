Phoenix at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Phoenix is the 3rd sunniest (about 3,815 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Tempe Junction, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Phoenix is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (35.0 °C), averages 23.5 °C more than its coolest, December (11.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in December. The area lies about 274 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 95% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Phoenix

For mild weather, the best months to visit Phoenix are March, April, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, April, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (35.0 °C), August (34.0 °C) and June (33.0 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): June (1 mm), May (3 mm) and April (8 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): June (404 h), May (397 h) and July (373 h).

Why Phoenix has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 274 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Phoenix itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.448°N, 112.074°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-505-679), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
