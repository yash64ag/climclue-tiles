Why Queens has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Queens climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Queens?
July, with an average of 24.4 °C (76 °F), day highs around 27.7 °C (82 °F) and night lows around 21.1 °C (70 °F).
What is the coldest month in Queens?
January, with an average of 0.1 °C (32 °F), day highs around 3.5 °C (38 °F) and night lows around -3.2 °C (26 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Queens?
July, with 113 mm (4.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,199 mm (47.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Queens?
February, with 77 mm (3.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Queens?
July, with about 266 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Queens?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 83 cm (32.6 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in February; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 4 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Queens?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: September.
What type of climate does Queens have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.4 × 11 km, that contains Queens’s GeoNames location (40.681°N, 73.837°W; ClimaClue area sq-433-1061), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 2,316,841 (GeoNames).
