Queens at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Queens is 230th warmest (12.3 °C year average), 245th sunniest (about 2,523 hours of sunshine a year) and 93rd wettest (1,199 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Brooklyn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Queens is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.4 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Queens has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
