Blythe at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Blythe is the 4th sunniest (about 3,847 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Yuma, the nearest covered town with different numbers (98 km away), Blythe is 0.7 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C cooler in December, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.9 °C), averages 22.6 °C more than its coolest, December (12.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in December. The area lies about 234 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Blythe by up to 2.6 °C (4.7 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Blythe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 234 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Blythe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.610°N, 114.596°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-503-654), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
