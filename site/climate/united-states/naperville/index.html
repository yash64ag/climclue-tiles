Naperville at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Naperville is 291st warmest (10.1 °C year average), 277th sunniest (about 2,478 hours of sunshine a year) and 157th wettest (990 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Aurora, Illinois, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Naperville is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.3 °C), averages 27.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 1,035 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Naperville has this climate

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Naperville itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.786°N, 88.147°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-422-918), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
