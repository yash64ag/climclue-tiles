Fort Pierce at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Fort Pierce is 154th warmest (23.5 °C year average), 695th sunniest (about 2,994 hours of sunshine a year) and 232nd wettest (1,442 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Florida Ridge, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Fort Pierce is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 9.3 °C more than its coolest, January (18.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.4 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Fort Pierce has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fort Pierce itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.447°N, 80.326°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-565-996), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
