Lincoln at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Lincoln is 259th warmest (11.2 °C year average), 179th sunniest (about 2,724 hours of sunshine a year) and 203rd wettest (793 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Omaha, the nearest covered town with different numbers (79 km away), Lincoln is 0.7 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 84 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.4 °C), averages 29.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 1,267 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Lincoln has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,267 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
