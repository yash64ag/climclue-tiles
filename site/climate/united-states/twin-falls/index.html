Twin Falls at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Twin Falls is 2166th warmest (11.0 °C year average), 725th sunniest (about 2,972 hours of sunshine a year) and 3036th wettest (248 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pocatello, the nearest covered town with different numbers (168 km away), Twin Falls is 1.2 °C warmer in July and 2.0 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.7 °C), averages 25.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 806 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Twin Falls has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 806 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Twin Falls itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.563°N, 114.461°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-414-655), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
