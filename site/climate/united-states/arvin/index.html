Arvin at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Arvin is 748th warmest (17.6 °C year average), 202nd sunniest (about 3,275 hours of sunshine a year) and 3027th wettest (259 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lamont, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Arvin is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in December, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.6 °C), averages 17.1 °C more than its coolest, December (9.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 3.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 124 km from the sea.

Why Arvin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 124 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Arvin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.209°N, 118.828°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-487-611), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,876, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
