How January compares in Denver

January is the 2nd coolest of the 12, the driest month and the 4th least sunny of the 12 in Denver. Compared with December: 0.5 °C warmer, 5 mm less precipitation and 19 hours more sunshine. February is 1.6 °C warmer than January on average.

Denver in January: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Denver in January?
In January, Denver averages a day high of 6.9 °C (44 °F) and a night low of -6.7 °C (20 °F), with 14 mm (0.6 in) of precipitation and about 212 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is January a good time to visit Denver?
January is not one of Denver’s mild months; those are June, July, August and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Denver in January?
14 mm (0.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making January the driest month of the year. The wettest month is May (61 mm), the driest January (14 mm).
Does it snow in Denver in January?
Yes, on average: ERA5-Land gives about 22 cm (8.5 in) of fresh snow in January (1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), and snow lies on the ground with an average depth of 15 cm.
How many hours of sunshine does Denver get in January?
About 212 hours of bright sunshine, the 4th least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Denver in January?
Going by the averages: a warm coat, a hat and gloves, an extra layer for cooler evenings and waterproof boots. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
