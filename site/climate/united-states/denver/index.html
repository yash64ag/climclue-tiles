Denver weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Denver, United States (Colorado), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Denver is warmest in July, with 31.0 °C (88 °F) days and 14.4 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 5.7 °C (42 °F) days and -9.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around May (61 mm); January is driest (15 mm).

Denver at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Denver is 2700th warmest (9.8 °C year average), 575th sunniest (about 3,069 hours of sunshine a year) and 2772nd wettest (433 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wheat Ridge, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Denver is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 31 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.7 °C), averages 24.5 °C more than its coolest, December (-1.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,210 km from the sea (deep inland).

Best time to visit Denver

For mild weather, the best months to visit Denver are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.

Why Denver has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,210 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Denver itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.739°N, 104.985°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-442-750), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 729,019, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
