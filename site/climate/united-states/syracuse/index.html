Syracuse at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Syracuse is 315th warmest (9.2 °C year average), 322nd sunniest (about 2,105 hours of sunshine a year) and 116th wettest (1,140 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rochester, New York, the nearest covered town with different numbers (120 km away), Syracuse is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 169 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.9 °C), averages 26.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 321 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 117% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Syracuse has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 321 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
