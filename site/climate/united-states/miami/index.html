Miami at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Miami is the warmest (24.4 °C year average). Compared with Hialeah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Miami is about as warm in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 32 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 7.4 °C more than its coolest, January (20.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.4 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Miami

For mild weather, the best months to visit Miami are January, February, March, April, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (27.8 °C), July (27.7 °C) and September (27.2 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (48 mm), December (53 mm) and February (68 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (303 h), May (297 h) and April (291 h).

Why Miami has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Miami itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.774°N, 80.194°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-582-998), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 487,014, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
