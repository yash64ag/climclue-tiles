Milwaukee at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Milwaukee is 317th warmest (8.9 °C year average), 287th sunniest (about 2,435 hours of sunshine a year) and 188th wettest (894 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Elgin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (116 km away), Milwaukee is 0.3 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 38 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.8 °C), averages 27.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 1,058 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Milwaukee has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,058 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
