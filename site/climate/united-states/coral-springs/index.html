Coral Springs at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Coral Springs is 10th warmest (24.1 °C year average), 105th sunniest (about 3,076 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (1,550 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pompano Beach, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Coral Springs is about as warm in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 22 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 8.7 °C more than its coolest, January (19.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 85% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Coral Springs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
