Tampa at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Tampa is 31st warmest (22.2 °C year average), 144th sunniest (about 2,893 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (1,348 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Brandon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Tampa is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.4 °C), averages 12.2 °C more than its coolest, January (15.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.6 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 115% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tampa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
