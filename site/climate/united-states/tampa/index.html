Why Tampa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Tampa climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Tampa?
July, with an average of 27.4 °C (81 °F), day highs around 31.2 °C (88 °F) and night lows around 24.3 °C (76 °F).
What is the coldest month in Tampa?
January, with an average of 15.3 °C (59 °F), day highs around 20.8 °C (69 °F) and night lows around 11.6 °C (53 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Tampa?
August, with 223 mm (8.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,348 mm (53.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Tampa?
November, with 50 mm (2.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Tampa?
May, with about 309 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Tampa?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Tampa?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, October, November and December.
What type of climate does Tampa have?
Humid subtropical (Köppen Cfa). Hot, humid summers and mild winters, with rain all year. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.8 × 11 km, that contains Tampa’s GeoNames location (27.948°N, 82.458°W; ClimaClue area sq-560-975), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 414,547 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
