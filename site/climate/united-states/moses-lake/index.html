Moses Lake at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Moses Lake is 2115th warmest (11.1 °C year average), 2834th sunniest (about 2,253 hours of sunshine a year) and 3081st wettest (205 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wenatchee, the nearest covered town with different numbers (84 km away), Moses Lake is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.8 °C), averages 24.8 °C more than its coolest, December (-0.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 253 km from the sea.

Why Moses Lake has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 253 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Moses Lake itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.130°N, 119.278°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-368-607), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
