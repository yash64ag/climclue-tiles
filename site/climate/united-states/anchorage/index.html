Anchorage at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Anchorage is the 4th coolest (3.1 °C year average). Compared with Eagle River, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Anchorage is about as warm in July and 1.4 °C warmer in January, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.2 °C), averages 23.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 2 km from Anchorage by up to 2.6 °C (4.6 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Anchorage has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

What is the warmest month in Anchorage?

What is the coldest month in Anchorage?
January, with an average of -8.5 °C (17 °F), day highs around -4.9 °C (23 °F) and night lows around -12.0 °C (10 °F).

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Anchorage itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (61.218°N, 149.900°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-227-301), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 289,600, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
