Anchorage at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Anchorage is the coolest (2.5 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 2,019 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Everett, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2,283 km away), Anchorage is 1.1 °C cooler in July and 13.6 °C cooler in January, with 461 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (14.8 °C), averages 23.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Anchorage has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
