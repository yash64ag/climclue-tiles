La Verne at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, La Verne is 674th warmest (18.3 °C year average), 243rd sunniest (about 3,215 hours of sunshine a year) and 2541st wettest (595 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Dimas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), La Verne is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C cooler in December, with 5 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.0 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, December (13.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in December. The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of La Verne, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why La Verne has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C. The 10 km area around La Verne, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.1 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Verne itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.101°N, 117.768°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-498-622), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 32,681, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
