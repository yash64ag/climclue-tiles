Ann Arbor at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Ann Arbor is 306th warmest (9.4 °C year average), 295th sunniest (about 2,359 hours of sunshine a year) and 190th wettest (888 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Detroit, the nearest covered town with different numbers (57 km away), Ann Arbor is 0.8 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 79 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.3 °C), averages 26.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 707 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 118% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ann Arbor has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 707 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.4 °C.
