The Bronx at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, The Bronx is 236th warmest (12.1 °C year average), 247th sunniest (about 2,519 hours of sunshine a year) and 88th wettest (1,213 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yonkers, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), The Bronx is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.4 °C), averages 25.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why The Bronx has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
