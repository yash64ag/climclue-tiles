Salt Lake City at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Salt Lake City is 283rd warmest (10.2 °C year average), 134th sunniest (about 2,975 hours of sunshine a year) and 221st wettest (572 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with West Valley City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Salt Lake City is 0.8 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 109 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.4 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 959 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Salt Lake City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 959 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
