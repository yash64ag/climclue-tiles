Salt Lake City at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Salt Lake City is 2083rd warmest (11.3 °C year average), 722nd sunniest (about 2,975 hours of sunshine a year) and 2673rd wettest (497 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with South Salt Lake, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Salt Lake City is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 959 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Salt Lake City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 959 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.9 °C. The 10 km area around Salt Lake City, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 10.2 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Salt Lake City itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.761°N, 111.891°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-432-681), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
