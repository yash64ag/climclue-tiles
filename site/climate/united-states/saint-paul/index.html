Saint Paul at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Saint Paul is the 4th coolest (7.6 °C year average). Compared with Minneapolis, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Saint Paul is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.8 °C), averages 32.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,179 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Saint Paul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,179 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
