Why Staten Island has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Staten Island climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Staten Island?
July, with an average of 24.6 °C (76 °F), day highs around 28.2 °C (83 °F) and night lows around 20.9 °C (70 °F).
What is the coldest month in Staten Island?
January, with an average of -0.1 °C (32 °F), day highs around 3.6 °C (38 °F) and night lows around -3.5 °C (26 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Staten Island?
July, with 121 mm (4.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,218 mm (47.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Staten Island?
February, with 78 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Staten Island?
July, with about 267 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Staten Island?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 85 cm (33.3 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in February; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 4 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Staten Island?
No month meets the definition used here (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation).
What type of climate does Staten Island have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.5 × 11 km, that contains Staten Island’s GeoNames location (40.562°N, 74.140°W; ClimaClue area sq-434-1058), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 468,730 (GeoNames).
