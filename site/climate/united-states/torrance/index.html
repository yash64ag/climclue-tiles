Torrance at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Torrance is 124th warmest (17.1 °C year average), 77th sunniest (about 3,156 hours of sunshine a year) and 283rd wettest (337 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Inglewood, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Torrance is 1.0 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C warmer in December, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.8 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, December (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.8 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Torrance has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
