Torrance at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Torrance is 849th warmest (17.1 °C year average), 391st sunniest (about 3,156 hours of sunshine a year) and 2932nd wettest (332 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Redondo Beach, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Torrance is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.6 °C), averages 8.6 °C more than its coolest, December (13.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.9 °C (46 °F), in December. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Torrance has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Torrance itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.836°N, 118.341°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-501-616), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
