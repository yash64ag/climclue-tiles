Charlotte at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Charlotte is 159th warmest (16.0 °C year average), 168th sunniest (about 2,763 hours of sunshine a year) and 113th wettest (1,147 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with High Point, the nearest covered town with different numbers (111 km away), Charlotte is 0.9 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 36 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 21.4 °C more than its coolest, January (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 262 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Charlotte has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 262 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
