Tacoma at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Tacoma is 2088th warmest (11.2 °C year average), 2958th sunniest (about 2,140 hours of sunshine a year) and 1380th wettest (1,152 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with University Place, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Tacoma is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in December, with 47 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.6 °C), averages 13.9 °C more than its coolest, December (4.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 1.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Why Tacoma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Tacoma climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Tacoma?
August, with an average of 18.6 °C (65 °F), day highs around 25.0 °C (77 °F) and night lows around 12.2 °C (54 °F).
What is the coldest month in Tacoma?
December, with an average of 4.7 °C (40 °F), day highs around 7.8 °C (46 °F) and night lows around 1.5 °C (35 °F).

Does it snow in Tacoma?
Yes. About 6.9 cm (2.7 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in December; it usually melts within days.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tacoma itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.253°N, 122.444°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-367-575), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 222,906, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
