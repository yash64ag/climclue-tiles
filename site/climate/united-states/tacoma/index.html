Tacoma at a glance

Among the 328 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the United States, Tacoma is 258th warmest (11.3 °C year average), 317th sunniest (about 2,140 hours of sunshine a year) and 107th wettest (1,174 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kent, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Tacoma is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.6 °C), averages 13.5 °C more than its coolest, December (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, December, averages 2.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 118% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tacoma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
