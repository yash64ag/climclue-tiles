Panama City at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Panama City is 395th warmest (20.5 °C year average), 910th sunniest (about 2,873 hours of sunshine a year) and 210th wettest (1,459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lynn Haven, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Panama City is about as warm in July and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 15.9 °C more than its coolest, January (11.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 119% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Panama City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Panama City climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Panama City?
July, with an average of 27.8 °C (82 °F), day highs around 32.1 °C (90 °F) and night lows around 23.5 °C (74 °F).
What is the coldest month in Panama City?
January, with an average of 11.9 °C (53 °F), day highs around 17.4 °C (63 °F) and night lows around 6.4 °C (44 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Panama City?
August, with 184 mm (7.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,459 mm (57.5 in) in a typical year.

What is the sunniest month in Panama City?
May, with about 311 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Panama City?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Panama City itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.159°N, 85.660°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-538-943), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 38,286, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
