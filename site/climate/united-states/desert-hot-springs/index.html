Desert Hot Springs at a glance

Among the 3129 towns ClimaClue covers in the United States, Desert Hot Springs is 299th warmest (22.1 °C year average), 155th sunniest (about 3,393 hours of sunshine a year) and 3057th wettest (226 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Palm Springs, California, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Desert Hot Springs is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 1.4 °C cooler in December, with 27 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.4 °C), averages 19.8 °C more than its coolest, December (12.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.5 °C (42 °F), in December. The area lies about 121 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 15 km from Desert Hot Springs by up to 2.9 °C (5.2 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Desert Hot Springs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 121 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Desert Hot Springs itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.962°N, 116.504°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-500-634), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 28,335, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
