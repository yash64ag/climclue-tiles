La Paz at a glance

Among the 39 towns ClimaClue covers in Bolivia, La Paz is the 3rd coolest (8.3 °C year average) and the 4th least sunny (about 2,349 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Achocalla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), La Paz is 0.8 °C cooler in November and 0.6 °C cooler in July, with 129 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (9.9 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, July (5.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June, July and August. The area lies about 330 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of La Paz, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit La Paz

No month in La Paz is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are January (9.4 °C, 229 mm), November (9.9 °C, 91 mm) and December (9.8 °C, 153 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): November (9.9 °C), December (9.8 °C) and January (9.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (15 mm), June (18 mm) and May (23 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (246 h), June (244 h) and August (237 h).

Why La Paz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Low sun and long winters keep it cold; even the warmest month stays below 10 °C, too cool for trees. Cold air holds little moisture, so precipitation is usually light. About 330 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C. The 10 km area around La Paz, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 6.3 °C a year, 2.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Paz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.500°S, 68.150°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1004-1118), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,004,652, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
