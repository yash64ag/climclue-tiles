Sacaba at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Bolivia, Sacaba is the 2nd least sunny (about 2,461 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,925 mm a year). Compared with Cochabamba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Sacaba is 2.0 °C cooler in November and 2.3 °C cooler in July, with 418 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (11.8 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, July (7.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 1.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 481 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sacaba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sacaba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 481 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 8.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
