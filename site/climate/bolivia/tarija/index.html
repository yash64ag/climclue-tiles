Tarija at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Bolivia, Tarija is the 2nd warmest (16.3 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,901 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (529 mm a year). Compared with Potosí, the nearest covered town with different numbers (242 km away), Tarija is 9.0 °C warmer in December and 7.9 °C warmer in July, with 58 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (18.6 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, July (12.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.2 °C (43 °F), in July. The area lies about 558 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tarija, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tarija has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 558 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tarija itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.535°S, 64.730°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1055-1152), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
