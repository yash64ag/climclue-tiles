Tarija at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Bolivia, Tarija is the 2nd warmest (16.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,901 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (570 mm a year). Compared with Potosí, the nearest covered town with different numbers (242 km away), Tarija is 8.8 °C warmer in December and 7.6 °C warmer in July, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (18.3 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, July (12.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.9 °C (43 °F), in July. The area lies about 558 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tarija, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tarija has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 558 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
