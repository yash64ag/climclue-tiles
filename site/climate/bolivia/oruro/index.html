Oruro at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Bolivia, Oruro is the 3rd coolest (9.7 °C year average). Compared with Quillacollo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (108 km away), Oruro is 4.6 °C cooler in November and 6.6 °C cooler in July, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (12.1 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, July (6.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June, July and August. The area lies about 363 km from the sea.

Why Oruro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 363 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Oruro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.972°S, 67.093°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1019-1129), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
