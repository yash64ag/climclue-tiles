Oruro at a glance

Among the 9 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Bolivia, Oruro is the 3rd coolest (9.4 °C year average). Compared with Quillacollo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (108 km away), Oruro is 4.0 °C cooler in November and 5.7 °C cooler in July, with 90 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (11.7 °C), averages 5.6 °C more than its coolest, July (6.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June, July and August. The area lies about 363 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Oruro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Oruro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 363 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
