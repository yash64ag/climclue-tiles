Cochabamba at a glance

Among the 39 towns ClimaClue covers in Bolivia, Cochabamba is 25th warmest (14.6 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 2,464 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (1,204 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tiquipaya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Cochabamba is 0.7 °C warmer in November and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 158 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (16.2 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, July (12.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 471 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cochabamba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cochabamba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 471 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C. The 10 km area around Cochabamba, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.3 °C a year, 2.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cochabamba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.382°S, 66.160°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1013-1138), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
