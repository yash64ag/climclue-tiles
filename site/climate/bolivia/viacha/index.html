Viacha at a glance

Among the 39 towns ClimaClue covers in Bolivia, Viacha is the 4th coolest (8.4 °C year average). Compared with Laja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Viacha is 0.3 °C cooler in November and 0.3 °C cooler in July, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (10.2 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, July (5.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in May, June, July and August. The area lies about 307 km from the sea.

Why Viacha has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 307 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Viacha itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.650°S, 68.300°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1006-1117), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 86,218, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
