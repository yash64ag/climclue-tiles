Trinidad at a glance

Among the 39 towns ClimaClue covers in Bolivia, Trinidad is the 3rd warmest (25.3 °C year average), the 5th least sunny (about 2,384 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,940 mm a year). Compared with San Borja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (199 km away), Trinidad is 0.3 °C warmer in October and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (26.7 °C), averages 4.1 °C more than its coolest, July (22.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.0 °C (61 °F), in July. The area lies about 710 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Trinidad, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Trinidad

For mild weather, the best months to visit Trinidad are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): October (26.7 °C), November (26.6 °C) and January (26.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (37 mm), June (41 mm) and August (49 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): August (239 h), July (227 h) and September (216 h).

Why Trinidad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 710 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Trinidad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.832°S, 64.903°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-988-1150), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 84,259, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
