Why San Juan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 294 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

San Juan climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in San Juan?
January, with an average of 25.6 °C (78 °F), day highs around 31.6 °C (89 °F) and night lows around 18.9 °C (66 °F).
What is the coldest month in San Juan?
July, with an average of 8.6 °C (47 °F), day highs around 16.9 °C (62 °F) and night lows around 2.5 °C (37 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in San Juan?
February, with 17 mm (0.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 97 mm (3.8 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in San Juan?
June, with 3 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in San Juan?
December, with about 335 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in San Juan?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in San Juan?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: February, March, April, October, November and December.
What type of climate does San Juan have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.5 × 11 km, that contains San Juan’s GeoNames location (31.537°S, 68.526°W; ClimaClue area sq-1155-1114), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 109,123 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
