Juan José Castelli at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Juan José Castelli is 7th warmest (22.1 °C year average), 111th sunniest (about 2,677 hours of sunshine a year) and 130th wettest (1,099 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tres Isletas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Juan José Castelli is 0.3 °C warmer in January and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 60 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (27.6 °C), averages 11.8 °C more than its coolest, July (15.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 954 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Juan José Castelli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 954 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Juan José Castelli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.947°S, 60.620°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1099-1193), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
