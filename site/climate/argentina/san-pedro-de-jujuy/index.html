San Pedro de Jujuy weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in San Pedro de Jujuy, Argentina (Jujuy), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

San Pedro de Jujuy is warmest in December, with 31.6 °C (89 °F) days and 18.7 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 21.0 °C (70 °F) days and 6.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around February (151 mm); August is driest (4 mm).

San Pedro de Jujuy at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, San Pedro de Jujuy is 49th warmest (20.4 °C year average), 290th sunniest (about 2,280 hours of sunshine a year) and 258th wettest (681 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Palpalá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), San Pedro de Jujuy is 3.4 °C warmer in December and 1.6 °C warmer in July, with 405 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (25.1 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, July (13.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.1 °C (43 °F), in July. The area lies about 582 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Pedro de Jujuy, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Pedro de Jujuy has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 582 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Pedro de Jujuy itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.231°S, 64.866°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1082-1151), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
