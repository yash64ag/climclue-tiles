San Rafael at a glance

Among the 45 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Argentina, San Rafael is 39th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 2,951 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (404 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mendoza, the nearest covered town with different numbers (198 km away), San Rafael is 0.2 °C warmer in January and 0.4 °C cooler in July, with 157 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.1 °C), averages 17.0 °C more than its coolest, July (7.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 1.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 332 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Rafael, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Rafael has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 332 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
