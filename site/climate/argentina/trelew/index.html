Trelew at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Trelew is 284th warmest (14.5 °C year average), 186th sunniest (about 2,592 hours of sunshine a year) and 307th wettest (221 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rawson, Chubut, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Trelew is 1.4 °C warmer in January and 0.6 °C cooler in July, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.3 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, July (6.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Trelew, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Trelew has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Trelew itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.249°S, 65.305°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1272-1146), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 97,915, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
