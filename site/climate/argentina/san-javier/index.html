San Javier at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, San Javier is 74th warmest (19.3 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 2,811 hours of sunshine a year) and 50th wettest (1,265 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with La Paz, the nearest covered town with different numbers (33 km away), San Javier is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 36 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.9 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, July (12.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.9 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 445 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Javier, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Javier has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 445 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Javier itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.581°S, 59.930°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1145-1200), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,606, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
