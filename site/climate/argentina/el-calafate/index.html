El Calafate weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in El Calafate, Argentina (Santa Cruz), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

El Calafate is warmest in January, with 17.9 °C (64 °F) days and 7.2 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 3.5 °C (38 °F) days and -3.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around April (34 mm); November is driest (9 mm).

El Calafate at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, El Calafate is the 3rd coolest (6.5 °C year average). Compared with Río Gallegos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (255 km away), El Calafate is 1.3 °C cooler in January and 1.7 °C cooler in July, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (12.5 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, July (0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in May, June, July, August and September. The area lies about 168 km from the sea.

Why El Calafate has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 168 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C. The 10 km area around El Calafate, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 5.3 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El Calafate itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.341°S, 72.277°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1343-1077), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
