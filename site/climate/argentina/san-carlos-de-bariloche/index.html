San Carlos de Bariloche at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, San Carlos de Bariloche is the 5th coolest (8.3 °C year average). Compared with El Bolsón, the nearest covered town with different numbers (93 km away), San Carlos de Bariloche is 2.3 °C cooler in February and 1.4 °C cooler in July, with 219 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (15.1 °C), averages 13.0 °C more than its coolest, July (2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June, July, August and September. The area lies about 129 km from the sea.

Why San Carlos de Bariloche has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 129 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Carlos de Bariloche itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.146°S, 71.308°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1251-1086), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
