San Salvador de Jujuy at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, San Salvador de Jujuy is 150th warmest (17.4 °C year average), 280th sunniest (about 2,345 hours of sunshine a year) and 56th wettest (1,226 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Palpalá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), San Salvador de Jujuy is 0.6 °C cooler in January and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 140 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.4 °C), averages 9.2 °C more than its coolest, July (12.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 540 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Salvador de Jujuy, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Salvador de Jujuy has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 540 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.2 °C. The 10 km area around San Salvador de Jujuy, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.0 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Salvador de Jujuy itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.193°S, 65.293°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1081-1147), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
