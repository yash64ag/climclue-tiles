Buenos Aires at a glance

Among the 45 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Argentina, Buenos Aires is 20th warmest (16.9 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 2,433 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (1,142 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lanús, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Buenos Aires is about as warm in January and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 27 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.6 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, July (10.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in July. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Buenos Aires, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

No month in Buenos Aires is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are April (17.4 °C, 105 mm), November (19.3 °C, 114 mm) and December (21.9 °C, 113 mm).

Why Buenos Aires has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
