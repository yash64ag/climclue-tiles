Concordia at a glance

Among the 45 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Argentina, Concordia is 10th warmest (18.9 °C year average), 24th sunniest (about 2,585 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (1,297 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Paraná, the nearest covered town with different numbers (241 km away), Concordia is 0.7 °C warmer in January and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 149 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.9 °C), averages 13.9 °C more than its coolest, July (12.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 323 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Concordia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Concordia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 323 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Concordia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.392°S, 58.017°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1153-1219), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
