Zapala at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Zapala is 310th warmest (10.3 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 2,841 hours of sunshine a year) and 275th wettest (378 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cutral-Có, the nearest covered town with different numbers (72 km away), Zapala is 3.4 °C cooler in January and 2.2 °C cooler in July, with 146 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (18.6 °C), averages 16.3 °C more than its coolest, July (2.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in May, June, July, August and September. The area lies about 286 km from the sea.

Why Zapala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 286 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zapala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.901°S, 70.067°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1229-1099), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
