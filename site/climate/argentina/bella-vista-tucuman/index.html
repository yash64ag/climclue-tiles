Bella Vista, Tucuman at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Bella Vista, Tucuman is 60th warmest (19.7 °C year average), 242nd sunniest (about 2,487 hours of sunshine a year) and 40th wettest (1,323 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Famaillá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Bella Vista, Tucuman is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 86 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.3 °C), averages 12.9 °C more than its coolest, July (12.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in July. The area lies about 539 km from the sea.

Why Bella Vista, Tucuman has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 539 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bella Vista itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.034°S, 65.302°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1110-1146), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
