La Rioja at a glance

Among the 45 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Argentina, La Rioja is the 5th sunniest (about 3,015 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Catamarca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (148 km away), La Rioja is 0.6 °C warmer in January and 2.5 °C cooler in July, with 157 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (26.4 °C), averages 15.5 °C more than its coolest, July (10.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.7 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 445 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of La Rioja, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why La Rioja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 445 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
