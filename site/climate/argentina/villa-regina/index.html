Villa Regina at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Villa Regina is 248th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 2,829 hours of sunshine a year) and 285th wettest (292 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with General Roca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Villa Regina is about as warm in January and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 34 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.8 °C), averages 17.8 °C more than its coolest, July (7.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 0.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 271 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Villa Regina, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Villa Regina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 271 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Villa Regina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.096°S, 67.084°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1230-1129), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
