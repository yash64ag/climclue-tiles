Ushuaia weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ushuaia, Argentina (Tierra del Fuego), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ushuaia at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Ushuaia is the coolest (4.6 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 1,682 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Río Grande, the nearest covered town with different numbers (120 km away), Ushuaia is 0.2 °C cooler in January and 3.4 °C cooler in July, with 443 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (10.3 °C), averages 11.4 °C more than its coolest, July (-1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in May, June, July, August and September. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Ushuaia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ushuaia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.811°S, 68.316°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1388-1116), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
