Punta Alta weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Punta Alta, Argentina (Buenos Aires), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Punta Alta is warmest in January, with 30.3 °C (87 °F) days and 15.5 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 13.6 °C (56 °F) days and 3.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (92 mm); August is driest (30 mm).

Punta Alta at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Punta Alta is 268th warmest (15.4 °C year average), 271st sunniest (about 2,407 hours of sunshine a year) and 261st wettest (653 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bahía Blanca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Punta Alta is 0.6 °C cooler in January and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.9 °C), averages 14.5 °C more than its coolest, July (8.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Punta Alta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Punta Alta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Punta Alta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.880°S, 62.075°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1228-1179), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
