Puerto Iguazú weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Puerto Iguazú, Argentina (Misiones), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Puerto Iguazú at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Puerto Iguazú is the 4th least sunny (about 1,915 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Colonia Wanda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Puerto Iguazú is 0.6 °C warmer in January and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (26.5 °C), averages 9.7 °C more than its coolest, July (16.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.6 °C (51 °F), in July. The area lies about 603 km from the sea (deep inland).

Best time to visit Puerto Iguazú

For mild weather, the best months to visit Puerto Iguazú are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): January (26.5 °C), December (26.0 °C) and February (25.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (84 mm), July (96 mm) and March (128 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (218 h), November (201 h) and January (185 h).

Why Puerto Iguazú has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 603 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Puerto Iguazú itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.599°S, 54.574°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1095-1254), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
