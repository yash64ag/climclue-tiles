San Luis at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, San Luis is 175th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 3,021 hours of sunshine a year) and 119th wettest (1,110 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with La Punta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), San Luis is 0.2 °C warmer in January and about as warm in July, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.1 °C), averages 14.5 °C more than its coolest, July (9.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 490 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Luis, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit San Luis

For mild weather, the best months to visit San Luis are April and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): January (24.1 °C), December (23.4 °C) and February (23.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): June (10 mm), August (15 mm) and July (19 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (314 h), December (310 h) and November (296 h).

Why San Luis has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 490 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Luis itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.291°S, 66.325°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1172-1136), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 169,947, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
