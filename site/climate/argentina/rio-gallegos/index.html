Río Gallegos at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Río Gallegos is the 4th coolest (8.1 °C year average) and the 3rd least sunny (about 1,807 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with El Calafate, the nearest covered town with different numbers (255 km away), Río Gallegos is 1.3 °C warmer in January and 1.7 °C warmer in July, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (13.8 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, July (1.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June and July. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Why Río Gallegos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Río Gallegos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (51.625°S, 69.252°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1356-1107), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
