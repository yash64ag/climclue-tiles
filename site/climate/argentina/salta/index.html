Salta at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Salta is the 5th least sunny (about 1,989 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with San Lorenzo, Salta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Salta is 1.0 °C warmer in January and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 397 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.1 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, July (11.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 515 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Salta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Salta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 515 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Salta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.806°S, 65.420°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1088-1145), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
