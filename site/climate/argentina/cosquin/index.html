Cosquín at a glance

Among the 318 towns ClimaClue covers in Argentina, Cosquín is 228th warmest (16.5 °C year average), 116th sunniest (about 2,667 hours of sunshine a year) and 238th wettest (853 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Unquillo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Cosquín is 0.2 °C cooler in January and 0.4 °C cooler in July, with 201 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.4 °C), averages 13.0 °C more than its coolest, July (9.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 666 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cosquín, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Cosquín has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 666 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cosquín itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.245°S, 64.466°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1152-1155), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
