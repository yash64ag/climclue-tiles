Colón at a glance

Among the 20 towns ClimaClue covers in Panama, Colón is the 5th warmest (26.3 °C year average). Compared with Cativá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Colón is 0.2 °C warmer in April and 0.2 °C warmer in November, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.6 °C), averages 0.8 °C more than its coolest, November (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.9 °C (73 °F), in October. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Colón, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Colón has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Colón itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.360°N, 79.900°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-746-1001), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
