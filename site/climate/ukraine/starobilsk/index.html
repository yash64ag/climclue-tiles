Starobilsk at a glance

Among the 272 towns ClimaClue covers in Ukraine, Starobilsk is 108th warmest (8.7 °C year average), 100th sunniest (about 1,967 hours of sunshine a year) and 200th wettest (540 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Siverskodonetsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Starobilsk is about as warm in July and 0.5 °C cooler in February, with 33 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.4 °C), averages 27.1 °C more than its coolest, February (-4.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 234 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Starobilsk, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Starobilsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 234 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Starobilsk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (49.279°N, 38.908°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-347-2189), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
