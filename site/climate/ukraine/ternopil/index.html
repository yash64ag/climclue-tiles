Ternopil at a glance

Among the 43 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ukraine, Ternopil is the 5th coolest (8.0 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 1,617 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Ivano-Frankivsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (95 km away), Ternopil is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 63 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.0 °C), averages 22.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 524 km from the sea.

Why Ternopil has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 524 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ternopil itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (49.554°N, 25.591°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-344-2055), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
