Bakhmut at a glance

Among the 272 towns ClimaClue covers in Ukraine, Bakhmut is 101st warmest (8.7 °C year average), 103rd sunniest (about 1,966 hours of sunshine a year) and 219th wettest (527 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kostyantynivka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Bakhmut is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.0 °C), averages 26.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 168 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bakhmut, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bakhmut has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 168 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bakhmut itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (48.594°N, 37.999°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-354-2179), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
