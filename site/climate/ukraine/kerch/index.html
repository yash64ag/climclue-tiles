Kerch at a glance

Among the 272 towns ClimaClue covers in Ukraine, Kerch is the 5th sunniest (about 2,242 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Feodosiya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (93 km away), Kerch is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 1.1 °C cooler in February, with 80 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 22.7 °C more than its coolest, February (0.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 97% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Kerch has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Kerch climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Kerch?
July, with an average of 23.6 °C (74 °F), day highs around 28.3 °C (83 °F) and night lows around 18.9 °C (66 °F).
What is the coldest month in Kerch?
February, with an average of 0.9 °C (34 °F), day highs around 4.1 °C (39 °F) and night lows around -2.2 °C (28 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Kerch?

Does it snow in Kerch?
Yes. About 43 cm (17.1 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in February; it usually melts within days.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kerch itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.357°N, 36.475°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-386-2164), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 148,932, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
