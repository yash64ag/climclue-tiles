Broken Hill at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Broken Hill is the driest (241 mm a year). Compared with Mildura, the nearest covered town with different numbers (256 km away), Broken Hill is 1.1 °C warmer in January and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 38 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (26.3 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, July (9.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 356 km from the sea.

Why Broken Hill has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 356 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Broken Hill itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.965°S, 141.451°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1159-3214), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
