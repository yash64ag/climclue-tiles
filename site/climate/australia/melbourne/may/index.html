Melbourne weather in May

Average May weather in Melbourne, Australia: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In May, Melbourne averages 16.5 °C (62 °F) days and 8.5 °C (47 °F) nights, with 49 mm (1.9 in) of rain over the month and about 127 hours of sunshine.

How May compares in Melbourne

May is the 5th coolest of the 12, the 4th driest of the 12 and the 3rd least sunny of the 12 in Melbourne. Compared with April: 2.5 °C cooler, 5 mm less precipitation and 33 hours less sunshine. June is 2.2 °C cooler than May on average.

Melbourne in May: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Melbourne in May?
In May, Melbourne averages a day high of 16.5 °C (62 °F) and a night low of 8.5 °C (47 °F), with 49 mm (1.9 in) of precipitation and about 127 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is May a good time to visit Melbourne?
May is not one of Melbourne’s mild months; those are January, February, March, April, November and December, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Melbourne in May?
49 mm (1.9 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making May the 4th driest of the 12. The wettest month is November (66 mm), the driest January (41 mm).
Does it snow in Melbourne in May?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for May in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Melbourne get in May?
About 127 hours of bright sunshine, the 3rd least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Melbourne in May?
About 15.5 °C (60 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, cold for most swimmers; its warmest month is February (20.1 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Melbourne in May?
Going by the averages: a jacket or sweater. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
