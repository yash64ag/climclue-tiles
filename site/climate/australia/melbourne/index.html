Melbourne at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Melbourne is the 3rd least sunny (about 2,197 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (625 mm a year). Compared with Geelong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Melbourne is 0.8 °C warmer in February and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 83 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (19.6 °C), averages 9.7 °C more than its coolest, July (9.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Melbourne measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 1.8 °C (3.3 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Best time to visit Melbourne

For mild weather, the best months to visit Melbourne are January, February, March, April, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, November and December.

Why Melbourne has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
