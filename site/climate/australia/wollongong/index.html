Wollongong at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Wollongong is 14th warmest (16.8 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 2,436 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,149 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sydney, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Wollongong is 0.8 °C cooler in January and 1.1 °C cooler in July, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.1 °C), averages 8.9 °C more than its coolest, July (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.8 °C (50 °F), in July. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 90% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Wollongong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
