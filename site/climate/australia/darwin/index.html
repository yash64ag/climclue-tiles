Why Darwin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Darwin climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Darwin?
October, with an average of 29.1 °C (84 °F), day highs around 34.3 °C (94 °F) and night lows around 24.8 °C (77 °F).
What is the coldest month in Darwin?
July, with an average of 24.9 °C (77 °F), day highs around 30.9 °C (88 °F) and night lows around 19.5 °C (67 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Darwin?
January, with 472 mm (18.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,856 mm (73.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Darwin?
July, with 1 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Darwin?
August, with about 313 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Darwin?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Darwin?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: July.
What type of climate does Darwin have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Darwin’s GeoNames location (12.461°S, 130.842°E; ClimaClue area sq-964-3108), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 139,902 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
