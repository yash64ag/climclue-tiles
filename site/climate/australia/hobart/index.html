Hobart at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Hobart is the 2nd coolest (12.0 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,136 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (560 mm a year). Compared with Geelong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (583 km away), Hobart is 2.4 °C cooler in February and 2.3 °C cooler in July, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (16.4 °C), averages 8.6 °C more than its coolest, July (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Hobart has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
