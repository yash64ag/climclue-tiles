Central Coast at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Central Coast is 12th warmest (17.8 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 2,491 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (1,237 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sydney, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Central Coast is 0.3 °C warmer in January and 0.4 °C cooler in July, with 82 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.1 °C), averages 9.3 °C more than its coolest, July (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.1 °C (50 °F), in July. The area lies about 19 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Central Coast has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
