Logan City at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Logan City is 21st warmest (20.3 °C year average), 29th sunniest (about 2,812 hours of sunshine a year) and 26th wettest (1,023 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Capalaba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Logan City is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.8 °C cooler in July, with 161 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.2 °C), averages 10.5 °C more than its coolest, July (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 30 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Logan City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 30 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Logan City itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.639°S, 153.109°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1116-3331), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 345,098, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
