Geelong at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Geelong is the 5th coolest (14.2 °C year average), the 4th least sunny (about 2,222 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (542 mm a year). Compared with Melbourne, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Geelong is 0.8 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 83 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (18.8 °C), averages 8.7 °C more than its coolest, July (10.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in July. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 100% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Geelong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
