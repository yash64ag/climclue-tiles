Mount Isa weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Mount Isa, Australia (Queensland), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Mount Isa is warmest in December, with 37.7 °C (100 °F) days and 23.1 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 25.1 °C (77 °F) days and 9.3 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (118 mm); August is driest (3 mm).

Mount Isa at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Mount Isa is the 5th warmest (25.1 °C year average) and the 3rd sunniest (about 3,411 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Alice Springs, the nearest covered town with different numbers (666 km away), Mount Isa is 1.8 °C warmer in December and 5.0 °C warmer in July, with 179 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (30.4 °C), averages 13.2 °C more than its coolest, July (17.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 350 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Mount Isa

For mild weather, the best months to visit Mount Isa are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): December (30.4 °C), January (30.1 °C) and February (29.5 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (3 mm), September (5 mm) and July (7 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): August (311 h), October (305 h) and September (298 h).

Why Mount Isa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 350 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mount Isa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.725°S, 139.497°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1047-3194), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 18,317, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
