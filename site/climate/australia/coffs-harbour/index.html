Coffs Harbour weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Coffs Harbour, Australia (New South Wales), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Coffs Harbour is warmest in January, with 27.5 °C (82 °F) days and 19.1 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 20.2 °C (68 °F) days and 8.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (214 mm); September is driest (60 mm).

Coffs Harbour at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Coffs Harbour is the 5th wettest (1,566 mm a year). Compared with Grafton, the nearest covered town with different numbers (71 km away), Coffs Harbour is 0.8 °C cooler in January and 1.6 °C warmer in July, with 481 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (23.3 °C), averages 8.9 °C more than its coolest, July (14.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Coffs Harbour has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Coffs Harbour itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.296°S, 153.114°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1142-3331), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
