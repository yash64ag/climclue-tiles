Perth at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Perth is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,908 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Mandurah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (65 km away), Perth is 1.2 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 108 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.4 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, July (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.8 °C (50 °F), in July. The area lies about 19 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Perth has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
