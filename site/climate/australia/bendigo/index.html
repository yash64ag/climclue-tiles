Bendigo at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Bendigo is the 4th coolest (14.1 °C year average), the 5th least sunny (about 2,325 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (529 mm a year). Compared with Ballarat, the nearest covered town with different numbers (98 km away), Bendigo is 3.5 °C warmer in February and 1.1 °C warmer in July, with 178 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (21.1 °C), averages 13.6 °C more than its coolest, July (7.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 151 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 95% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Bendigo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 151 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
