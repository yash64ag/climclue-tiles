Townsville weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Townsville, Australia (Queensland), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Townsville at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Townsville is 6th warmest (24.2 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,975 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (1,092 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cairns, the nearest covered town with different numbers (283 km away), Townsville is 0.8 °C warmer in January and 1.0 °C cooler in July, with 920 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (27.6 °C), averages 8.1 °C more than its coolest, July (19.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.8 °C (57 °F), in July. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 102% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Townsville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Townsville itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.266°S, 146.806°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1032-3268), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
