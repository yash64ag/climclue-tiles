Sunshine Coast at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Sunshine Coast is the 5th warmest (20.4 °C year average), the 5th sunniest (about 2,832 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,601 mm a year). Compared with Brisbane, the nearest covered town with different numbers (90 km away), Sunshine Coast is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 531 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (24.8 °C), averages 9.6 °C more than its coolest, July (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Sunshine Coast has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
