Adelaide at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Adelaide is the 5th driest (645 mm a year). Compared with Bendigo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (551 km away), Adelaide is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 3.9 °C warmer in July, with 116 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (21.4 °C), averages 10.0 °C more than its coolest, July (11.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.2 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 2 km from Adelaide measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.0 °C (3.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Adelaide are January, February, March, April, October, November and December.

Why Adelaide has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
