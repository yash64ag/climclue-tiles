Ballarat at a glance

Among the 21 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Australia, Ballarat is the coolest (11.8 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 2,136 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Geelong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (79 km away), Ballarat is 1.1 °C cooler in February and 3.6 °C cooler in July, with 165 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (17.6 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, July (6.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 97 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 112% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ballarat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 97 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
