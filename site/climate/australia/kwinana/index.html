Kwinana at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Kwinana is 28th warmest (18.5 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 2,858 hours of sunshine a year) and 40th wettest (786 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rockingham, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Kwinana is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C cooler in July, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.3 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, July (13.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Kwinana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kwinana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.230°S, 115.781°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1162-2957), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,433, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
