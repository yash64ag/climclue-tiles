Bundaberg at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Bundaberg is 11th warmest (21.9 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 2,891 hours of sunshine a year) and 27th wettest (974 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hervey Bay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (63 km away), Bundaberg is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 156 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (26.0 °C), averages 9.2 °C more than its coolest, July (16.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.0 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Bundaberg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bundaberg itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.866°S, 152.348°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1088-3323), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 73,747, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
