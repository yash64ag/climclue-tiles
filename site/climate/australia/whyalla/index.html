Whyalla at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Whyalla is the 2nd driest (262 mm a year). Compared with Gawler, the nearest covered town with different numbers (206 km away), Whyalla is 1.6 °C warmer in February and 1.4 °C warmer in July, with 240 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.3 °C), averages 12.6 °C more than its coolest, July (11.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.8 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Whyalla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Whyalla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.033°S, 137.565°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1170-3175), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
