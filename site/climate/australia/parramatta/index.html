Parramatta at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Parramatta is 43rd warmest (17.4 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 2,497 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (877 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Liverpool, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Parramatta is about as warm in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 36 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.8 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, July (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in July. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 90% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Parramatta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Parramatta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.818°S, 151.003°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1178-3310), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
