Sydney at a glance

Among the 89 towns ClimaClue covers in Australia, Sydney is 38th warmest (17.8 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 2,488 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (1,097 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Parramatta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Sydney is 0.4 °C cooler in February and 1.8 °C warmer in July, with 219 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.0 °C), averages 8.9 °C more than its coolest, July (13.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Sydney has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Sydney climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Sydney?
February, with an average of 22.0 °C (72 °F), day highs around 25.6 °C (78 °F) and night lows around 18.5 °C (65 °F).
What is the coldest month in Sydney?
July, with an average of 13.1 °C (56 °F), day highs around 17.8 °C (64 °F) and night lows around 8.4 °C (47 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Sydney?
February, with 129 mm (5.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,097 mm (43.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Sydney?
September, with 60 mm (2.4 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Sydney?
December, with about 238 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Sydney?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sydney itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.868°S, 151.207°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1178-3312), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 5,638,830, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
