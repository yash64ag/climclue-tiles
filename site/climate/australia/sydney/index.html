Why Sydney has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Sydney climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Sydney?
February, with an average of 21.9 °C (72 °F), day highs around 23.5 °C (74 °F) and night lows around 20.4 °C (69 °F).
What is the coldest month in Sydney?
July, with an average of 13.3 °C (56 °F), day highs around 15.6 °C (60 °F) and night lows around 10.8 °C (51 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Sydney?
February, with 136 mm (5.3 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,155 mm (45.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Sydney?
September, with 62 mm (2.4 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Sydney?
December, with about 238 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Sydney?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Sydney?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, September, October, November and December.
What type of climate does Sydney have?
Oceanic (Köppen Cfb). Mild all year, with rain in every season. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.2 × 11 km, that contains Sydney’s GeoNames location (33.868°S, 151.207°E; ClimaClue area sq-1178-3312), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 5,638,830 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
