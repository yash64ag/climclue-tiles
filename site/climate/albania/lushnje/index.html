Lushnjë at a glance

Among the 24 towns ClimaClue covers in Albania, Lushnjë is the 3rd driest (846 mm a year). Compared with Peqin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Lushnjë is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 104 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.8 °C), averages 18.2 °C more than its coolest, January (7.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lushnjë, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lushnjë has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lushnjë itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.942°N, 19.705°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-430-1997), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
