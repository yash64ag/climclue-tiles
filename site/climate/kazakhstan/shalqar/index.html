Shalqar at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Shalqar is 44th warmest (7.3 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 2,693 hours of sunshine a year) and 68th wettest (194 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Embi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (155 km away), Shalqar is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 73 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.4 °C), averages 36.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 512 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shalqar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shalqar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 512 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 36.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shalqar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.832°N, 59.619°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-361-2396), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
