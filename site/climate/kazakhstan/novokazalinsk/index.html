Novokazalinsk at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Novokazalinsk is the driest (138 mm a year). Compared with Baikonur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (94 km away), Novokazalinsk is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 36.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 703 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Novokazalinsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 703 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 36.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Novokazalinsk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.848°N, 62.153°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-381-2421), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
