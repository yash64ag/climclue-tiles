Kalbatau weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kalbatau, Kazakhstan (Abai Region), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kalbatau is warmest in July, with 29.2 °C (85 °F) days and 14.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -9.6 °C (15 °F) days and -19.6 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 17 mm in February to 46 mm in July.

Kalbatau at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Kalbatau is 57th warmest (4.3 °C year average), 40th sunniest (about 2,550 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (323 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ust-Kamenogorsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (103 km away), Kalbatau is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 149 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.9 °C), averages 36.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-14.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,964 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kalbatau, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kalbatau has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,964 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 36.5 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kalbatau itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (49.330°N, 81.573°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-346-2615), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,047, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
