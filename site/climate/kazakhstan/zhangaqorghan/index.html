Zhangaqorghan at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Zhangaqorghan is the 2nd sunniest (about 2,929 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Shiyeli, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Zhangaqorghan is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.1 °C), averages 33.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,085 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Zhangaqorghan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,085 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zhangaqorghan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.907°N, 67.246°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-400-2472), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
