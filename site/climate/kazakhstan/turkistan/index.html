Turkistan at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Kazakhstan, Turkistan is the warmest (13.6 °C year average) and the sunniest (about 2,952 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Shymkent, the nearest covered town with different numbers (155 km away), Turkistan is 3.4 °C warmer in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 381 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.3 °C), averages 31.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,149 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 125% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Turkistan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,149 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
