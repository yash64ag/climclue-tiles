Petropavl weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Petropavl, Kazakhstan (North Kazakhstan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Petropavl is warmest in July, with 25.2 °C (77 °F) days and 13.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -11.1 °C (12 °F) days and -19.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (68 mm); March is driest (17 mm).

Petropavl at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Petropavl is the 4th coolest (2.4 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 2,162 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Kokshetau, the nearest covered town with different numbers (177 km away), Petropavl is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 79 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.5 °C), averages 34.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-15.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,287 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Petropavl has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,287 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 34.9 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Petropavl itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.873°N, 69.151°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-291-2491), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
