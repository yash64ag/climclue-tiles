Atyrau at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Atyrau is 32nd warmest (10.2 °C year average), 44th sunniest (about 2,545 hours of sunshine a year) and 74th wettest (172 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Balykshi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Atyrau is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.0 °C), averages 33.0 °C more than its coolest, February (-6.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Why Atyrau has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Atyrau itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.105°N, 51.884°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-368-2318), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 290,700, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
