Almaty at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Almaty is the 2nd wettest (926 mm a year). Compared with Burunday, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Almaty is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 139 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.3 °C), averages 28.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,852 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 3 km from Almaty by up to 2.4 °C (4.3 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Almaty

For mild weather, the best months to visit Almaty are July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (24.3 °C), August (23.4 °C) and June (21.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (36 mm), September (41 mm) and February (45 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (317 h), August (302 h) and June (294 h).

Why Almaty has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,852 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Almaty itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.252°N, 76.912°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-407-2569), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
