Balqash weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Balqash, Kazakhstan (Karaganda), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Balqash is warmest in July, with 30.3 °C (87 °F) days and 19.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -7.4 °C (19 °F) days and -16.3 °C nights. Most rain falls around November (17 mm); September is driest (4 mm).

Balqash at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Balqash is the 5th driest (156 mm a year). Compared with Ushtobe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (291 km away), Balqash is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 123 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.0 °C), averages 36.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,662 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Balqash has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,662 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 36.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Balqash itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.845°N, 74.982°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-371-2549), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
