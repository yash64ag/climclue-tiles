Ushtobe at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Ushtobe is 40th warmest (8.5 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 2,664 hours of sunshine a year) and 58th wettest (278 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Taldykorgan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Ushtobe is 2.0 °C warmer in July and 2.2 °C cooler in January, with 127 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.4 °C), averages 36.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-10.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,922 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ushtobe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Ushtobe

For mild weather, the best months to visit Ushtobe are May, June, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, August and September.

Why Ushtobe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,922 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 36.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ushtobe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.253°N, 77.983°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-387-2579), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
