Balykshi at a glance

Among the 83 towns ClimaClue covers in Kazakhstan, Balykshi is 29th warmest (10.2 °C year average), 42nd sunniest (about 2,547 hours of sunshine a year) and 76th wettest (171 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Atyrau, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Balykshi is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.3 °C), averages 33.6 °C more than its coolest, February (-6.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 99% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Balykshi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Balykshi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.067°N, 51.867°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-369-2318), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
