Yangon at a glance

Among the 36 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Myanmar, Yangon is 10th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 2,574 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (2,761 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mingala Tangnyunt, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Yangon is 0.1 °C warmer in April and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (31.3 °C), averages 7.0 °C more than its coolest, January (24.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 60 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Yangon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Yangon

For mild weather, the best months to visit Yangon are January and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (31.3 °C), May (29.6 °C) and March (29.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (1 mm), February (3 mm) and December (5 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (306 h), February (285 h) and January (282 h).

Why Yangon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 60 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
