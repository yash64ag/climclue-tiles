Lewe at a glance

Among the 163 towns ClimaClue covers in Myanmar, Lewe is 101st warmest (26.5 °C year average), 78th sunniest (about 2,507 hours of sunshine a year) and 125th wettest (1,385 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nay Pyi Taw, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Lewe is 0.2 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 41 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (31.4 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, January (21.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.2 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 238 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lewe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Lewe

For mild weather, the best months to visit Lewe are January, February and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (31.4 °C), May (29.9 °C) and March (28.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (3 mm), March (3 mm) and February (3 mm).

Why Lewe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 238 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lewe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.636°N, 96.110°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-643-2761), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,208, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
