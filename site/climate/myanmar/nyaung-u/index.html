Nyaung-U at a glance

Among the 163 towns ClimaClue covers in Myanmar, Nyaung-U is the 4th warmest (27.8 °C year average) and the 3rd driest (736 mm a year). Compared with Pakokku, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Nyaung-U is 0.3 °C cooler in April and about as warm in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (32.4 °C), averages 10.0 °C more than its coolest, January (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.6 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 221 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nyaung-U, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Nyaung-U are January and December.

Why Nyaung-U has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 221 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nyaung-U itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.201°N, 94.908°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-627-2749), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 48,528, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
