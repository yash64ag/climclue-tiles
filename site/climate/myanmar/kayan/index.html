Kayan at a glance

Among the 163 towns ClimaClue covers in Myanmar, Kayan is 28th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 2,601 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (3,199 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thongwa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Kayan is 0.4 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 46 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.8 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, January (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.9 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kayan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kayan

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kayan are January and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (30.8 °C), May (29.6 °C) and March (28.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (1 mm), February (3 mm) and December (5 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (308 h), February (286 h) and January (282 h).

Why Kayan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kayan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.908°N, 96.560°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-670-2765), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
