Kyauktan, Sagaing Region at a glance

Among the 163 towns ClimaClue covers in Myanmar, Kyauktan, Sagaing Region is 123rd warmest (25.3 °C year average), 141st sunniest (about 2,276 hours of sunshine a year) and 108th wettest (1,762 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Katha, the nearest covered town with different numbers (47 km away), Kyauktan, Sagaing Region is 0.8 °C cooler in April and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 184 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.7 °C), averages 9.3 °C more than its coolest, January (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 473 km from the sea.

Why Kyauktan, Sagaing Region has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 473 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kyauktan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.278°N, 95.895°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-597-2758), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
