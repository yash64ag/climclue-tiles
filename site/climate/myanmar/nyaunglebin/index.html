Nyaunglebin at a glance

Among the 163 towns ClimaClue covers in Myanmar, Nyaunglebin is 93rd warmest (26.8 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 2,602 hours of sunshine a year) and 81st wettest (2,607 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shwegyin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Nyaunglebin is 0.2 °C warmer in April and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 582 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (31.5 °C), averages 9.1 °C more than its coolest, January (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.6 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 91 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nyaunglebin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nyaunglebin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 91 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nyaunglebin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.954°N, 96.722°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-660-2767), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
