Paung at a glance

Among the 163 towns ClimaClue covers in Myanmar, Paung is the 4th wettest (4,937 mm a year). Compared with Hmaw Bi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Paung is 1.0 °C cooler in April and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 727 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.1 °C), averages 4.6 °C more than its coolest, January (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Paung, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Paung has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Paung itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.618°N, 97.459°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-673-2774), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
