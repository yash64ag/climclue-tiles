Kutkai at a glance

Among the 163 towns ClimaClue covers in Myanmar, Kutkai is the 2nd coolest (18.7 °C year average) and the 4th least sunny (about 2,194 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Namhkam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (49 km away), Kutkai is 3.6 °C cooler in June and 3.6 °C cooler in January, with 156 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (21.8 °C), averages 9.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 613 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kutkai, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kutkai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 613 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kutkai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.458°N, 97.938°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-605-2779), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 24,963, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
