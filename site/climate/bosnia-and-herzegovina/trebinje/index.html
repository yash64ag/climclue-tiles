Trebinje at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in Bosnia and Herzegovina, Trebinje is the 2nd warmest (14.3 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,187 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (2,181 mm a year). Compared with Mostar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (83 km away), Trebinje is 1.9 °C cooler in July and 1.5 °C warmer in January, with 385 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.5 °C), averages 17.9 °C more than its coolest, January (5.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 18 km from Trebinje measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 4.0 °C (7.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Trebinje has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.9 °C. The 10 km area around Trebinje, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.4 °C a year, 1.8 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Trebinje itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.712°N, 18.344°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-412-1983), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
