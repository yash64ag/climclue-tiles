Sarajevo weather in January

Average January weather in Sarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In January, Sarajevo averages 4.1 °C (39 °F) days and -2.7 °C (27 °F) nights, with 70 mm (2.7 in) of rain and snow over the month and about 66 hours of sunshine.

How January compares in Sarajevo

January is the coolest month, the driest month and the 2nd least sunny of the 12 in Sarajevo. Compared with December: 1.1 °C cooler, 23 mm less precipitation and 15 hours more sunshine. February is 1.3 °C warmer than January on average.

Sarajevo in January: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Sarajevo in January?
In January, Sarajevo averages a day high of 4.1 °C (39 °F) and a night low of -2.7 °C (27 °F), with 70 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation and about 66 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is January a good time to visit Sarajevo?
January is not one of Sarajevo’s mild months; those are May, June, July, August and September, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Sarajevo in January?
70 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making January the driest month of the year. The wettest month is November (99 mm), the driest January (70 mm).
Does it snow in Sarajevo in January?
Yes, on average: ERA5-Land gives about 32 cm (12.5 in) of fresh snow in January (1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), and snow lies on the ground with an average depth of 2.8 cm.
How many hours of sunshine does Sarajevo get in January?
About 66 hours of bright sunshine, the 2nd least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Sarajevo in January?
Going by the averages: a warm coat, a hat and gloves, a compact umbrella and waterproof boots. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
