Ilidža at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in Bosnia and Herzegovina, Ilidža is the 5th coolest (11.3 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 1,798 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Sarajevo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Ilidža is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 97 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.2 °C), averages 20.2 °C more than its coolest, January (0.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 131 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 115% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ilidža has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 131 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ilidža itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.831°N, 18.307°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-401-1983), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
