Ibarra at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ecuador, Ibarra is 12th warmest (15.1 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 1,665 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (928 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Quito, the nearest covered town with different numbers (78 km away), Ibarra is 4.0 °C warmer in April and 3.2 °C warmer in July, with 1,334 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (15.3 °C), averages 0.7 °C more than its coolest, July (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.1 °C (50 °F), in August. The area lies about 150 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ibarra, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ibarra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 150 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
