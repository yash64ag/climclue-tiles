Milagro at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ecuador, Milagro is the 3rd least sunny (about 954 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Eloy Alfaro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Milagro is 0.3 °C cooler in April and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 182 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (25.6 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, August (23.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.3 °C (69 °F), in August. The area lies about 90 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Milagro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Milagro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 90 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Milagro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.134°S, 79.593°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-861-1004), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
