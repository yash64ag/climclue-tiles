Playas at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Playas is 40th warmest (23.4 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 1,457 hours of sunshine a year) and 65th wettest (615 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Posorja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Playas is 0.3 °C warmer in March and 0.5 °C warmer in September, with 101 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (25.5 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, September (21.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in September. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Playas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Playas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Playas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.632°S, 80.388°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-866-996), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
