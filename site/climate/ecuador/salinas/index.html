Salinas at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Salinas is the 2nd driest (403 mm a year). Compared with La Libertad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Salinas is 0.2 °C cooler in February and 0.2 °C cooler in September, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (25.2 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, September (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.3 °C (65 °F), in September. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Salinas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Salinas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Salinas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.215°S, 80.952°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-862-990), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
