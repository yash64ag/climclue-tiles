Bahía de Caráquez at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Bahía de Caráquez is 9th warmest (24.9 °C year average), 43rd sunniest (about 1,096 hours of sunshine a year) and 64th wettest (623 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chone, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Bahía de Caráquez is about as warm in April and 0.6 °C cooler in September, with 353 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.2 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, September (23.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.2 °C (68 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bahía de Caráquez, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bahía de Caráquez has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bahía de Caráquez itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.600°S, 80.424°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-845-995), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
