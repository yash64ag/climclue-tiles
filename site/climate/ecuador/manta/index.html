Manta at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ecuador, Manta is the driest (415 mm a year). Compared with Portoviejo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (33 km away), Manta is 0.2 °C warmer in March and 0.9 °C cooler in August, with 236 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (25.9 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, August (22.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.0 °C (70 °F), in September. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Manta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Manta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
