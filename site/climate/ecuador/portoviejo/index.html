Portoviejo at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Portoviejo is 8th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 41st sunniest (about 1,153 hours of sunshine a year) and 63rd wettest (624 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Montecristi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Portoviejo is 0.8 °C warmer in April and 1.1 °C warmer in August, with 55 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.1 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, August (24.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.4 °C (63 °F), in August. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Portoviejo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Portoviejo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Portoviejo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.058°S, 80.451°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-850-995), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
