Huaquillas at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Huaquillas is 12th warmest (24.9 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 1,754 hours of sunshine a year) and 66th wettest (596 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Arenillas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Huaquillas is 0.4 °C warmer in March and 0.5 °C warmer in August, with 246 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.4 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, August (23.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Huaquillas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Huaquillas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Huaquillas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.475°S, 80.231°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-874-997), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
