Montalvo at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Montalvo is the 4th least sunny (about 760 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Babahoyo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Montalvo is 0.3 °C cooler in March and 0.7 °C cooler in August, with 481 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (25.3 °C), averages 2.7 °C more than its coolest, August (22.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.3 °C (60 °F), in August. The area lies about 145 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Montalvo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Montalvo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 145 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.7 °C. The 10 km area around Montalvo, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 22.2 °C a year, 1.8 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Montalvo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.790°S, 79.288°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-857-1007), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
