El Empalme at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, El Empalme is 30th warmest (24.0 °C year average), 63rd sunniest (about 891 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (2,365 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Quevedo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), El Empalme is 0.3 °C warmer in April and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 243 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (25.1 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, July (22.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in August. The area lies about 110 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of El Empalme, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why El Empalme has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 110 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El Empalme itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.045°S, 79.638°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-850-1003), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
