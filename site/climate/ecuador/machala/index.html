Machala at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ecuador, Machala is 8th warmest (23.7 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 1,550 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (852 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cuenca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (114 km away), Machala is 11.8 °C warmer in March and 9.4 °C warmer in August, with 54 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (25.8 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, August (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.7 °C (67 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Machala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Machala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Machala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.259°S, 79.959°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-872-1000), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
