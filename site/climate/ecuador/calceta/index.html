Calceta at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Calceta is 9th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 50th sunniest (about 1,026 hours of sunshine a year) and 52nd wettest (857 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chone, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Calceta is 0.3 °C warmer in March and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 118 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.4 °C), averages 2.0 °C more than its coolest, July (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.2 °C (65 °F), in August. The area lies about 50 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Calceta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Calceta

For mild weather, the best months to visit Calceta are June, July, August, September, October and November.

Why Calceta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 50 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Calceta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.846°S, 80.164°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-848-998), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,286, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
