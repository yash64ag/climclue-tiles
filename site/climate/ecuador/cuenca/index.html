Cuenca at a glance

Among the 17 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ecuador, Cuenca is 13th warmest (13.2 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 1,702 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (875 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Milagro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (108 km away), Cuenca is 11.1 °C cooler in January and 11.2 °C cooler in July, with 610 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (13.8 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, July (11.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in August. The area lies about 111 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cuenca, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cuenca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 111 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
