Cariamanga at a glance

Among the 71 towns ClimaClue covers in Ecuador, Cariamanga is the sunniest (about 2,038 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Catamayo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Cariamanga is 4.9 °C cooler in August and 5.3 °C cooler in November, with 548 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.9 °C), averages 0.8 °C more than its coolest, November (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.1 °C (48 °F), in November. The area lies about 139 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cariamanga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cariamanga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 139 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cariamanga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.328°S, 79.556°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-883-1004), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,862, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
