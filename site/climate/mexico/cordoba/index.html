Córdoba at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Córdoba is the least sunny (about 1,661 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Orizaba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Córdoba is 2.2 °C warmer in May and 1.2 °C warmer in January, with 91 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.6 °C), averages 6.2 °C more than its coolest, January (17.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.7 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 99 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 79% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Córdoba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 99 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Córdoba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.884°N, 96.926°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-651-830), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
