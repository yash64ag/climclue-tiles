Acatzingo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Acatzingo is 464th warmest (16.8 °C year average), 232nd sunniest (about 2,425 hours of sunshine a year) and 325th wettest (764 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Los Reyes de Juárez, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Acatzingo is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (18.6 °C), averages 4.7 °C more than its coolest, January (14.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.4 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 167 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 122% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Acatzingo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 167 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Acatzingo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.982°N, 97.784°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-650-822), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
