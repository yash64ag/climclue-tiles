Acapulco de Juárez at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Acapulco de Juárez is 8th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 43rd sunniest (about 2,498 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (1,236 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chilpancingo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (89 km away), Acapulco de Juárez is 6.6 °C warmer in May and 8.5 °C warmer in January, with 47 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.3 °C), averages 2.0 °C more than its coolest, January (25.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.9 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 1 km from Acapulco de Juárez measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.1 °C (3.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Acapulco de Juárez has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.0 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
