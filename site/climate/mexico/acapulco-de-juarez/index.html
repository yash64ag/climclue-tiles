Acapulco de Juárez at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Acapulco de Juárez is 9th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 181st sunniest (about 2,498 hours of sunshine a year) and 153rd wettest (1,158 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Atoyac de Álvarez, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Acapulco de Juárez is 0.4 °C cooler in May and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 158 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.1 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, January (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.8 °C (69 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 85% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Acapulco de Juárez has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C. The 10 km area around Acapulco de Juárez, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 26.2 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Acapulco de Juárez itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.849°N, 99.909°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-671-800), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
