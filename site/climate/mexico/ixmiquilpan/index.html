Ixmiquilpan at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Ixmiquilpan is 384th warmest (18.8 °C year average), 147th sunniest (about 2,555 hours of sunshine a year) and 558th wettest (423 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Progreso de Obregón, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Ixmiquilpan is 1.4 °C warmer in May and 1.2 °C warmer in January, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (21.8 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, January (15.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.9 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 217 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ixmiquilpan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 217 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ixmiquilpan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.484°N, 99.218°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-635-807), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
