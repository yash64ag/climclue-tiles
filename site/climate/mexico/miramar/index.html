Miramar at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Miramar is 31st warmest (24.3 °C year average), 102nd sunniest (about 2,092 hours of sunshine a year) and 30th wettest (1,149 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tampico, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Miramar is 0.2 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 16 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.4 °C), averages 7.4 °C more than its coolest, January (19.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.4 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 115% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Miramar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
