Sabinas at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Sabinas is 208th warmest (22.9 °C year average), 293rd sunniest (about 2,299 hours of sunshine a year) and 528th wettest (476 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nueva Rosita, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Sabinas is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (30.7 °C), averages 17.6 °C more than its coolest, January (13.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.9 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 379 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Sabinas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 379 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sabinas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.856°N, 101.117°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-561-788), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
