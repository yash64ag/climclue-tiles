Petatlán at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Petatlán is 34th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 251st sunniest (about 2,383 hours of sunshine a year) and 161st wettest (1,136 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ixtapa-Zihuatanejo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Petatlán is 0.4 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 49 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.7 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.3 °C (65 °F), in March. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 115% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Petatlán has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Petatlán itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.538°N, 101.269°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-664-787), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
