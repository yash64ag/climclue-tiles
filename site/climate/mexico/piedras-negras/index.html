Piedras Negras at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Piedras Negras is 48th warmest (22.5 °C year average), 55th sunniest (about 2,411 hours of sunshine a year) and 105th wettest (546 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad Acuña, the nearest covered town with different numbers (81 km away), Piedras Negras is 0.6 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 36 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (31.1 °C), averages 18.8 °C more than its coolest, January (12.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 359 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 121% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Piedras Negras has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 359 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
