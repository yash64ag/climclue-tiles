Nogales, Veracruz at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Nogales, Veracruz is 352nd warmest (19.1 °C year average), 596th sunniest (about 1,888 hours of sunshine a year) and 78th wettest (1,564 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Río Blanco, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Nogales, Veracruz is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 49 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (20.9 °C), averages 4.5 °C more than its coolest, January (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.9 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 116 km from the sea.

Why Nogales, Veracruz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 116 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.5 °C. The 10 km area around Nogales, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.4 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nogales itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.828°N, 97.163°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-651-828), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
