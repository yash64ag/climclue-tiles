Tequixquiac at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Tequixquiac is 471st warmest (16.7 °C year average), 262nd sunniest (about 2,354 hours of sunshine a year) and 486th wettest (539 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Juan Zitlaltepec, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Tequixquiac is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (19.5 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 231 km from the sea.

Why Tequixquiac has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 231 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tequixquiac itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.909°N, 99.145°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-640-808), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
