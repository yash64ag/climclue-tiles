Crucecita at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Crucecita is 17th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 146th sunniest (about 2,558 hours of sunshine a year) and 130th wettest (1,226 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Miahuatlán de Porfirio Díaz, the nearest covered town with different numbers (79 km away), Crucecita is 6.2 °C warmer in May and 7.8 °C warmer in January, with 189 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.8 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, January (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.4 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 20 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 92% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Crucecita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Crucecita itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.769°N, 96.135°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-682-838), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
