Uruapan at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Uruapan is 90th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 59th sunniest (about 2,353 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (1,274 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Apatzingán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Uruapan is 11.3 °C cooler in May and 10.4 °C cooler in January, with 501 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (19.9 °C), averages 5.3 °C more than its coolest, January (14.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 172 km from the sea.

Why Uruapan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 172 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
