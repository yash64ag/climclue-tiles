Huixquilucan at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Huixquilucan is 603rd warmest (13.2 °C year average), 481st sunniest (about 2,092 hours of sunshine a year) and 140th wettest (1,187 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jesús del Monte, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Huixquilucan is 0.8 °C cooler in May and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 140 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (15.4 °C), averages 5.3 °C more than its coolest, January (10.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 280 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 9 km from Huixquilucan by up to 3.3 °C (5.9 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Huixquilucan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 280 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.3 °C. The 10 km area around Huixquilucan, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 11.9 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Huixquilucan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.360°N, 99.350°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-646-806), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
