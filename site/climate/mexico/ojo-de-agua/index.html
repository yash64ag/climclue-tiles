Ojo de Agua at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Ojo de Agua is 97th warmest (17.0 °C year average), 78th sunniest (about 2,148 hours of sunshine a year) and 96th wettest (576 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tepexpan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Ojo de Agua is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (19.8 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 237 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 3 km from Ojo de Agua measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.8 °C (5.0 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Ojo de Agua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 237 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
