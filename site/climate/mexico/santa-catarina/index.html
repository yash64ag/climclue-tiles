Santa Catarina weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Santa Catarina, Mexico (Nuevo León), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Santa Catarina is warmest in August, with 32.3 °C (90 °F) days and 21.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 20.0 °C (68 °F) days and 9.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (152 mm); November is driest (14 mm).

Santa Catarina at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Santa Catarina is 257th warmest (21.6 °C year average), 589th sunniest (about 1,917 hours of sunshine a year) and 473rd wettest (570 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Pedro Garza García, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Santa Catarina is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 45 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.8 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, January (14.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.0 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 311 km from the sea.

Why Santa Catarina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 311 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C. The 10 km area around Santa Catarina, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.4 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa Catarina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.673°N, 100.458°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-583-795), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 304,052, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
