Nava at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Nava is 226th warmest (22.4 °C year average), 255th sunniest (about 2,361 hours of sunshine a year) and 507th wettest (512 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Allende, Coahuila, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Nava is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (30.6 °C), averages 18.1 °C more than its coolest, January (12.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.0 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 365 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 11 km from Nava measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 3.1 °C (5.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Nava has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 365 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nava itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.421°N, 100.766°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-555-792), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,192, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
