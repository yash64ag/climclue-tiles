Ocotlán at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Ocotlán is 319th warmest (19.7 °C year average), 112th sunniest (about 2,613 hours of sunshine a year) and 344th wettest (744 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jamay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Ocotlán is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.1 °C), averages 7.5 °C more than its coolest, January (15.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.0 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 218 km from the sea.

Why Ocotlán has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 218 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ocotlán itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.351°N, 102.768°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-636-772), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
