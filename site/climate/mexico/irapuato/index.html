Irapuato weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Irapuato, Mexico (Guanajuato), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Irapuato is warmest in May, with 32.4 °C (90 °F) days and 16.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 25.0 °C (77 °F) days and 7.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (175 mm); March is driest (5 mm).

Irapuato at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Irapuato is 308th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 250th sunniest (about 2,385 hours of sunshine a year) and 409th wettest (656 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Salamanca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Irapuato is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.2 °C), averages 8.1 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.4 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 318 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Irapuato has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 318 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Irapuato itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.671°N, 101.356°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-633-786), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 380,941, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
