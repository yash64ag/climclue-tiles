Santa María Atzompa at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Santa María Atzompa is 330th warmest (20.0 °C year average), 573rd sunniest (about 1,991 hours of sunshine a year) and 465th wettest (586 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oaxaca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Santa María Atzompa is 0.1 °C cooler in April and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (21.8 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, January (17.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.7 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 154 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 85% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Santa María Atzompa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 154 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C. The 10 km area around Santa María Atzompa, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.3 °C a year, 1.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa María Atzompa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.102°N, 96.777°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-668-832), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
