Loma Bonita at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Loma Bonita is 86th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 379th sunniest (about 2,166 hours of sunshine a year) and 33rd wettest (2,008 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tuxtepec, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Loma Bonita is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 510 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.1 °C), averages 7.2 °C more than its coolest, January (21.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.4 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 79 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 17 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 125% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Loma Bonita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 79 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-658-841), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
