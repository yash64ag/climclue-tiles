La Paz at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, La Paz is the 3rd driest (223 mm a year). Compared with San José del Cabo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (136 km away), La Paz is 2.0 °C warmer in August and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 220 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.8 °C), averages 11.1 °C more than its coolest, January (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.8 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 123% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why La Paz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
