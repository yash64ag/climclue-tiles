La Paz at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, La Paz is 169th warmest (24.1 °C year average), 69th sunniest (about 2,671 hours of sunshine a year) and 613th wettest (223 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San José del Cabo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (136 km away), La Paz is 0.8 °C warmer in August and 1.5 °C cooler in January, with 174 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.6 °C), averages 11.0 °C more than its coolest, January (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 123% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why La Paz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Paz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.142°N, 110.313°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-598-696), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
