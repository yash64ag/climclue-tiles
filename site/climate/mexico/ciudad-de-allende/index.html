Ciudad de Allende at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Ciudad de Allende is 253rd warmest (21.6 °C year average), 594th sunniest (about 1,917 hours of sunshine a year) and 280th wettest (834 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santiago, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Ciudad de Allende is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 15 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 261 km from the sea.

Why Ciudad de Allende has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 261 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C. The 10 km area around Ciudad de Allende, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.4 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ciudad de Allende itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.281°N, 100.019°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-587-799), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
