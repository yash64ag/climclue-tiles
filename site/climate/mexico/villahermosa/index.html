Villahermosa weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Villahermosa, Mexico (Tabasco), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Villahermosa is warmest in May, with 35.9 °C (97 °F) days and 22.9 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 27.9 °C (82 °F) days and 17.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (327 mm); March is driest (37 mm).

Villahermosa at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Villahermosa is 42nd warmest (26.4 °C year average), 462nd sunniest (about 2,105 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (1,916 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Playas del Rosario, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Villahermosa is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 107 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.4 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, January (22.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.8 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Villahermosa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Villahermosa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.986°N, 92.939°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-660-870), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
