Morelia at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Morelia is 80th warmest (18.5 °C year average), 94th sunniest (about 2,107 hours of sunshine a year) and 66th wettest (766 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Salamanca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (97 km away), Morelia is 2.3 °C cooler in May and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 139 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (21.8 °C), averages 6.6 °C more than its coolest, January (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 227 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 7 km from Morelia measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.2 °C (5.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Morelia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 227 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
