Cabo San Lucas at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Cabo San Lucas is 41st warmest (23.3 °C year average), 24th sunniest (about 2,655 hours of sunshine a year) and 130th wettest (321 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San José del Cabo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Cabo San Lucas is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C warmer in February, with 122 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.4 °C), averages 7.2 °C more than its coolest, February (20.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.5 °C (65 °F), in March. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 3 km from Cabo San Lucas measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 1.9 °C (3.5 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Cabo San Lucas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
