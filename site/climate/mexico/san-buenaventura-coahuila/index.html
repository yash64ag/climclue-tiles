San Buenaventura, Coahuila at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, San Buenaventura, Coahuila is 276th warmest (21.0 °C year average), 333rd sunniest (about 2,230 hours of sunshine a year) and 586th wettest (341 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Frontera, Coahuila, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), San Buenaventura, Coahuila is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.8 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, January (12.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 406 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 13 km from San Buenaventura by up to 3.3 °C (6.0 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why San Buenaventura, Coahuila has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 406 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Buenaventura itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.062°N, 101.549°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-569-784), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
