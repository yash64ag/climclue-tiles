Iguala de la Independencia at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Iguala de la Independencia is 6th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 374th sunniest (about 2,167 hours of sunshine a year) and 274th wettest (842 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad de Huitzuco, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Iguala de la Independencia is 1.6 °C warmer in May and 1.4 °C warmer in December, with 51 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.7 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, December (25.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.0 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 175 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 88% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Iguala de la Independencia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 175 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Iguala de la Independencia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.345°N, 99.538°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-656-804), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
