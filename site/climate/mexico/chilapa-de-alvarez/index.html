Chilapa de Álvarez at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Chilapa de Álvarez is 277th warmest (21.0 °C year average), 367th sunniest (about 2,174 hours of sunshine a year) and 195th wettest (1,056 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tixtla de Guerrero, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Chilapa de Álvarez is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.0 °C), averages 4.2 °C more than its coolest, January (18.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.3 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 111 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 117% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Chilapa de Álvarez has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 111 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.2 °C. The 10 km area around Chilapa de Álvarez, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.7 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chilapa de Álvarez itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.594°N, 99.177°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-664-808), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
