Ticul at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Ticul is 85th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 163rd sunniest (about 2,531 hours of sunshine a year) and 151st wettest (1,162 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oxkutzkab, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Ticul is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 39 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.6 °C), averages 6.4 °C more than its coolest, January (22.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.7 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 104 km from the sea.

Why Ticul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 104 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ticul itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.399°N, 89.535°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-636-904), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
