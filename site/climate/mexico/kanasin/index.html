Kanasín at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Kanasín is 50th warmest (26.4 °C year average), 81st sunniest (about 2,648 hours of sunshine a year) and 215th wettest (1,014 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mérida, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Kanasín is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.8 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.0 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 46 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 95% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Kanasín has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 46 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kanasín itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.935°N, 89.558°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-630-904), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
