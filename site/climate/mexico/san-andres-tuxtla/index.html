San Andrés Tuxtla at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, San Andrés Tuxtla is the 5th wettest (2,957 mm a year). Compared with Santiago Tuxtla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), San Andrés Tuxtla is 0.6 °C cooler in May and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 49 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (26.9 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, January (20.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Why San Andrés Tuxtla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Andrés Tuxtla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.449°N, 95.213°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-655-847), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
