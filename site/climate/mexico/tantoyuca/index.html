Tantoyuca weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Tantoyuca, Mexico (Veracruz), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Tantoyuca is warmest in June, with 33.5 °C (92 °F) days and 22.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 24.1 °C (75 °F) days and 13.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (297 mm); March is driest (32 mm).

Tantoyuca at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Tantoyuca is 172nd warmest (24.0 °C year average), 477th sunniest (about 2,096 hours of sunshine a year) and 91st wettest (1,446 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Huejutla de Reyes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Tantoyuca is 0.3 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 92 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.1 °C), averages 9.5 °C more than its coolest, January (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.1 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 94 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 17 km from Tantoyuca measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 1.8 °C (3.3 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Tantoyuca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 94 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tantoyuca itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.351°N, 98.226°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-626-817), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
