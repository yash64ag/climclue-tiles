Romita weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Romita, Mexico (Guanajuato), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Romita is warmest in May, with 32.5 °C (91 °F) days and 15.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 24.6 °C (76 °F) days and 7.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (175 mm); March is driest (6 mm).

Romita at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Romita is 321st warmest (20.1 °C year average), 310th sunniest (about 2,275 hours of sunshine a year) and 405th wettest (661 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Silao de la Victoria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Romita is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.1 °C), averages 8.2 °C more than its coolest, January (15.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 336 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 2 km from Romita measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.9 °C (5.2 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Romita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 336 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Romita itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.872°N, 101.517°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-631-784), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 21,176, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
