Sayula at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Sayula is 290th warmest (20.7 °C year average), 199th sunniest (about 2,478 hours of sunshine a year) and 288th wettest (817 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad Guzmán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Sayula is 1.7 °C warmer in May and 1.4 °C warmer in January, with 53 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.7 °C), averages 6.2 °C more than its coolest, January (17.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 123 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 17 km from Sayula measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 5.4 °C (9.7 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Sayula has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 123 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.2 °C. The 10 km area around Sayula, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.5 °C a year, 4.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sayula itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.884°N, 103.601°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-641-763), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 26,789, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
