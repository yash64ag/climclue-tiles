Tlalnepantla at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Tlalnepantla is 117th warmest (15.8 °C year average), 98th sunniest (about 2,101 hours of sunshine a year) and 77th wettest (705 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad López Mateos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Tlalnepantla is 0.8 °C warmer in May and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 109 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (18.4 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, January (12.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.4 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 251 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 87% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tlalnepantla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 251 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
