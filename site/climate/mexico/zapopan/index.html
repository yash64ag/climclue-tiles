Zapopan at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Zapopan is 305th warmest (20.4 °C year average), 86th sunniest (about 2,647 hours of sunshine a year) and 244th wettest (901 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guadalajara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Zapopan is 0.2 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 37 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.2 °C), averages 7.6 °C more than its coolest, January (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.9 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 208 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Zapopan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 208 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zapopan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.721°N, 103.387°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-632-766), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
