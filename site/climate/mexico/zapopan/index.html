Zapopan at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Zapopan is 57th warmest (21.1 °C year average), 26th sunniest (about 2,647 hours of sunshine a year) and 50th wettest (864 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guadalajara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Zapopan is 0.8 °C warmer in May and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.9 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, January (17.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.1 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 208 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 87% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Zapopan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 208 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
