Metepec at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Metepec is 611th warmest (13.2 °C year average), 528th sunniest (about 2,055 hours of sunshine a year) and 186th wettest (1,070 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Salvador Tizatlalli, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Metepec is 0.1 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (15.1 °C), averages 4.7 °C more than its coolest, January (10.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 266 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Metepec measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 152% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Metepec has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 266 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Metepec itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.252°N, 99.605°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-647-803), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 28,205, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
