Manzanillo at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Manzanillo is 16th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 2,401 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (1,149 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad de Villa de Álvarez, the nearest covered town with different numbers (66 km away), Manzanillo is 1.6 °C warmer in July and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 344 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.9 °C), averages 3.6 °C more than its coolest, January (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.1 °C (66 °F), in March. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 9 km from Manzanillo measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.1 °C (5.5 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Manzanillo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
