Manzanillo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Manzanillo is 23rd warmest (26.9 °C year average), 242nd sunniest (about 2,401 hours of sunshine a year) and 190th wettest (1,066 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cihuatlán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Manzanillo is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C warmer in February, with 5 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.3 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, February (25.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.8 °C (64 °F), in March. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 9 km from Manzanillo by up to 2.5 °C (4.5 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations at a similar height, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Manzanillo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C. The 10 km area around Manzanillo, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 25.2 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Manzanillo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.117°N, 104.342°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-648-756), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
