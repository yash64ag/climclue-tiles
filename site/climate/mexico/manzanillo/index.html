Manzanillo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Manzanillo is 71st warmest (25.9 °C year average), 242nd sunniest (about 2,401 hours of sunshine a year) and 190th wettest (1,066 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cihuatlán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Manzanillo is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in March, with 5 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.7 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, March (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in March. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 9 km from Manzanillo measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.5 °C (4.5 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Manzanillo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Manzanillo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.117°N, 104.342°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-648-756), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
