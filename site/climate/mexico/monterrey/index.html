Monterrey at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Monterrey is 62nd warmest (20.5 °C year average), 133rd sunniest (about 1,887 hours of sunshine a year) and 79th wettest (688 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guadalupe, Nuevo León, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Monterrey is 0.6 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 12.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.7 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 302 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Monterrey measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.7 °C (4.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Monterrey has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 302 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
