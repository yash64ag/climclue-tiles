Monterrey at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Monterrey is 249th warmest (21.7 °C year average), 599th sunniest (about 1,887 hours of sunshine a year) and 434th wettest (627 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guadalupe, Nuevo León, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Monterrey is 0.2 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 13.6 °C more than its coolest, January (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 302 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 112% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Monterrey has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 302 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.6 °C. The 10 km area around Monterrey, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.5 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Monterrey itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.684°N, 100.317°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-583-796), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
