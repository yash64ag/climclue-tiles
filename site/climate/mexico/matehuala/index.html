Matehuala weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Matehuala, Mexico (San Luis Potosí), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Matehuala is warmest in June, with 30.5 °C (87 °F) days and 15.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 21.5 °C (71 °F) days and 5.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (74 mm); November is driest (10 mm).

Matehuala at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Matehuala is 373rd warmest (19.0 °C year average), 286th sunniest (about 2,309 hours of sunshine a year) and 540th wettest (456 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad Victoria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (153 km away), Matehuala is 5.4 °C cooler in June and 2.9 °C cooler in January, with 193 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (22.7 °C), averages 9.3 °C more than its coolest, January (13.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.4 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 306 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 2 km from Matehuala measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 1.7 °C (3.0 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Matehuala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 306 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Matehuala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.648°N, 100.643°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-603-793), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
