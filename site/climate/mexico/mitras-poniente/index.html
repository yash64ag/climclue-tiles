Mitras Poniente at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Mitras Poniente is 243rd warmest (21.8 °C year average), 605th sunniest (about 1,831 hours of sunshine a year) and 516th wettest (499 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Parque Industrial Ciudad Mitras, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Mitras Poniente is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 27 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 13.5 °C more than its coolest, January (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 314 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 13 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 89% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Mitras Poniente has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 314 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.5 °C. The 10 km area around Mitras Poniente, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.6 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mitras Poniente itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.776°N, 100.426°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-582-795), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
