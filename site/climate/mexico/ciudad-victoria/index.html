Ciudad Victoria at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Ciudad Victoria is 175th warmest (23.8 °C year average), 447th sunniest (about 2,114 hours of sunshine a year) and 417th wettest (649 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad Mante, the nearest covered town with different numbers (112 km away), Ciudad Victoria is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 1.8 °C cooler in January, with 419 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.0 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, January (16.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.9 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 153 km from the sea.

Why Ciudad Victoria has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 153 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ciudad Victoria itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.741°N, 99.144°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-602-808), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
