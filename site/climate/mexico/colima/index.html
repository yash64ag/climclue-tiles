Colima at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Colima is 25th warmest (24.4 °C year average), 89th sunniest (about 2,116 hours of sunshine a year) and 58th wettest (805 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad Guzmán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (57 km away), Colima is 5.6 °C warmer in May and 7.4 °C warmer in January, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (26.6 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, January (22.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 59 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Colima has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 59 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
