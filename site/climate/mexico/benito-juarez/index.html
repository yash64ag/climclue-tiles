Benito Juarez at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Benito Juarez is 122nd warmest (15.4 °C year average), 115th sunniest (about 2,023 hours of sunshine a year) and 54th wettest (821 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mexico City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Benito Juarez is 0.8 °C cooler in May and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 102 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (17.6 °C), averages 5.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.2 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 263 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 8 km from Benito Juarez measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.0 °C (5.4 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Benito Juarez has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 263 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
