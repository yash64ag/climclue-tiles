Ixtlán del Río at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Ixtlán del Río is 223rd warmest (22.2 °C year average), 121st sunniest (about 2,606 hours of sunshine a year) and 291st wettest (806 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Magdalena, the nearest covered town with different numbers (43 km away), Ixtlán del Río is 1.2 °C warmer in June and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (25.6 °C), averages 7.4 °C more than its coolest, January (18.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 104 km from the sea.

Why Ixtlán del Río has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 104 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.4 °C. The 10 km area around Ixtlán del Río, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.4 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ixtlán del Río itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.035°N, 104.369°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-629-756), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
