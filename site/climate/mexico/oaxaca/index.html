Oaxaca at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Oaxaca is 69th warmest (20.1 °C year average), 127th sunniest (about 1,991 hours of sunshine a year) and 91st wettest (597 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tuxtepec, the nearest covered town with different numbers (131 km away), Oaxaca is 6.0 °C cooler in April and 4.1 °C cooler in January, with 1,921 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (21.9 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, January (18.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.2 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 144 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 87% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Oaxaca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 144 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Oaxaca itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.060°N, 96.725°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-669-832), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
