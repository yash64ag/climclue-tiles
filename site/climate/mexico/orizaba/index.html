Orizaba weather by month and climate

Orizaba at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Orizaba is 359th warmest (19.4 °C year average), 617th sunniest (about 1,747 hours of sunshine a year) and 52nd wettest (1,845 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ixtaczoquitlán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Orizaba is 0.7 °C cooler in June and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (21.4 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, January (16.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.3 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 107 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 17 km from Orizaba measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 3.0 °C (5.4 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Orizaba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 107 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Orizaba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.852°N, 97.100°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-651-829), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
