Chilpancingo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Chilpancingo is 245th warmest (21.8 °C year average), 397th sunniest (about 2,152 hours of sunshine a year) and 191st wettest (1,063 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tixtla de Guerrero, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Chilpancingo is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.7 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, January (19.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.5 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 94 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 122% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Chilpancingo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 94 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C. The 10 km area around Chilpancingo, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.7 °C a year, 3.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chilpancingo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.552°N, 99.501°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-664-804), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
