Nuevo Laredo weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nuevo Laredo, Mexico (Tamaulipas), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nuevo Laredo is warmest in August, with 38.3 °C (101 °F) days and 24.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 20.9 °C (70 °F) days and 8.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (72 mm); February is driest (22 mm).

Nuevo Laredo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Nuevo Laredo is 180th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 350th sunniest (about 2,194 hours of sunshine a year) and 518th wettest (490 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Anáhuac, the nearest covered town with different numbers (66 km away), Nuevo Laredo is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (31.4 °C), averages 17.0 °C more than its coolest, January (14.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.0 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 215 km from the sea.

Why Nuevo Laredo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 215 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nuevo Laredo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.476°N, 99.516°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-565-804), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
