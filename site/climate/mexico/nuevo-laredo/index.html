Nuevo Laredo at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Nuevo Laredo is 37th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 67th sunniest (about 2,194 hours of sunshine a year) and 110th wettest (512 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Piedras Negras, the nearest covered town with different numbers (168 km away), Nuevo Laredo is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 2.1 °C warmer in January, with 34 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (31.4 °C), averages 16.9 °C more than its coolest, January (14.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.3 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 215 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 13 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 99% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Nuevo Laredo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 215 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
