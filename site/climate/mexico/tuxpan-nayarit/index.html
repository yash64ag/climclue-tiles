Tuxpan, Nayarit at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Tuxpan, Nayarit is 102nd warmest (25.6 °C year average), 87th sunniest (about 2,641 hours of sunshine a year) and 113th wettest (1,306 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santiago Ixcuintla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Tuxpan, Nayarit is about as warm in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 95 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.9 °C), averages 7.3 °C more than its coolest, January (21.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.7 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 17 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tuxpan, Nayarit has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-620-747), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
