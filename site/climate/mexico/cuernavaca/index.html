Cuernavaca at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Cuernavaca is 305th warmest (20.2 °C year average), 449th sunniest (about 2,112 hours of sunshine a year) and 177th wettest (1,093 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tres de Mayo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Cuernavaca is 1.6 °C cooler in May and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (22.7 °C), averages 5.0 °C more than its coolest, January (17.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.5 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 248 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 83% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Cuernavaca

For mild weather, the best months to visit Cuernavaca are January, February, March, April, May, October, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, May, October, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (22.7 °C), April (22.5 °C) and June (21.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (6 mm), March (9 mm) and February (9 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (218 h), February (195 h) and January (194 h).

Why Cuernavaca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 248 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.0 °C. The 10 km area around Cuernavaca, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.8 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cuernavaca itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.926°N, 99.231°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-650-807), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 338,650, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
