Santa Anita at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Santa Anita is 329th warmest (20.0 °C year average), 102nd sunniest (about 2,632 hours of sunshine a year) and 255th wettest (885 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lomas del Sur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Santa Anita is 0.5 °C warmer in May and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.8 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 198 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 114% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Santa Anita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 198 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa Anita itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.550°N, 103.444°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-634-765), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,320, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
