Acayucan weather by month and climate

Acayucan at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Acayucan is 106th warmest (25.5 °C year average), 414th sunniest (about 2,144 hours of sunshine a year) and 59th wettest (1,793 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jáltipan de Morelos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Acayucan is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 403 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.8 °C), averages 7.0 °C more than its coolest, January (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.1 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 61 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 21 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Acayucan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 61 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Acayucan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.950°N, 94.914°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-660-850), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
