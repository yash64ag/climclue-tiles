Salamanca at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Salamanca is 67th warmest (20.2 °C year average), 52nd sunniest (about 2,429 hours of sunshine a year) and 87th wettest (627 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Irapuato, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Salamanca is 0.1 °C cooler in May and about as warm in January, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.1 °C), averages 7.9 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 312 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 10 km from Salamanca measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.6 °C (6.4 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Salamanca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 312 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
