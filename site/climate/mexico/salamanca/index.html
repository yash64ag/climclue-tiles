Why Salamanca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 312 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Salamanca climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Salamanca?
May, with an average of 24.1 °C (75 °F), day highs around 31.3 °C (88 °F) and night lows around 15.5 °C (60 °F).
What is the coldest month in Salamanca?
January, with an average of 16.1 °C (61 °F), day highs around 23.9 °C (75 °F) and night lows around 8.4 °C (47 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Salamanca?
July, with 163 mm (6.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 627 mm (24.7 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Salamanca?
March, with 6 mm (0.2 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Salamanca?
March, with about 240 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Salamanca?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Salamanca?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, May, October, November and December.
What type of climate does Salamanca have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Salamanca’s GeoNames location (20.570°N, 101.198°W; ClimaClue area sq-634-788), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 160,682 (GeoNames).
