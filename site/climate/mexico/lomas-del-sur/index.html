Lomas del Sur at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Lomas del Sur is 356th warmest (19.5 °C year average), 117th sunniest (about 2,608 hours of sunshine a year) and 258th wettest (870 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tlajomulco de Zúñiga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Lomas del Sur is 0.4 °C cooler in May and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 78 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.3 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, January (15.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 192 km from the sea.

Why Lomas del Sur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 192 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lomas del Sur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.492°N, 103.418°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-635-765), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
