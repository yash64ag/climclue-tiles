Chihuahua weather in September

Average September weather in Chihuahua, Mexico: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In September, Chihuahua averages 29.6 °C (85 °F) days and 15.6 °C (60 °F) nights, with 70 mm (2.8 in) of rain over the month and about 221 hours of sunshine.

How September compares in Chihuahua

September is the 5th warmest of the 12, the 3rd wettest of the 12 and the 3rd least sunny of the 12 in Chihuahua. Compared with August: 1.7 °C cooler, 19 mm less precipitation and 25 hours less sunshine. October is 3.6 °C cooler than September on average.

Chihuahua in September: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Chihuahua in September?
In September, Chihuahua averages a day high of 29.6 °C (85 °F) and a night low of 15.6 °C (60 °F), with 70 mm (2.8 in) of precipitation and about 221 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is September a good time to visit Chihuahua?
For mild weather, yes: September is one of Chihuahua’s mild months (March, April, May, August, September and October), counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Chihuahua in September?
70 mm (2.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making September the 3rd wettest of the 12. The wettest month is August (89 mm), the driest February (5 mm).
Does it snow in Chihuahua in September?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for September in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Chihuahua get in September?
About 221 hours of bright sunshine, the 3rd least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Chihuahua in September?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes, an extra layer for cooler evenings and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
