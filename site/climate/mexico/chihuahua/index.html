Chihuahua weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Chihuahua, Mexico, from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Chihuahua is warmest in June, with 34.8 °C (95 °F) days and 18.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 18.7 °C (66 °F) days and 1.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (89 mm); February is driest (5 mm).

Chihuahua at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Chihuahua is 385th warmest (18.8 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 3,019 hours of sunshine a year) and 575th wettest (377 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Juan Aldama, Chihuahua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Chihuahua is 1.5 °C cooler in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (26.7 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 407 km from the sea.

Why Chihuahua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 407 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chihuahua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.635°N, 106.089°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-553-739), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 925,762, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
