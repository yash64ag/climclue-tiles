Pánuco at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Pánuco is 116th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 489th sunniest (about 2,089 hours of sunshine a year) and 209th wettest (1,024 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ébano, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Pánuco is 0.2 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 54 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (29.1 °C), averages 9.7 °C more than its coolest, January (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.5 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 52 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 118% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Pánuco has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 52 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pánuco itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.054°N, 98.182°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-619-818), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
