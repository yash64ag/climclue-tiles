Cortazar weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Cortazar, Mexico (Guanajuato), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Cortazar is warmest in May, with 32.2 °C (90 °F) days and 15.4 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 24.4 °C (76 °F) days and 7.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (153 mm); December is driest (6 mm).

Cortazar at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Cortazar is 322nd warmest (20.1 °C year average), 223rd sunniest (about 2,427 hours of sunshine a year) and 439th wettest (621 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Villagrán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Cortazar is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.8 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 308 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 99% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Cortazar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 308 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cortazar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.483°N, 100.964°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-635-790), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 61,658, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
