Yautepec weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Yautepec, Mexico (Morelos), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Yautepec at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Yautepec is 203rd warmest (23.1 °C year average), 396th sunniest (about 2,153 hours of sunshine a year) and 169th wettest (1,113 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jiutepec, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Yautepec is 0.9 °C warmer in May and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (25.9 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, January (20.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.9 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 246 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 6 km from Yautepec measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 1.9 °C (3.4 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Yautepec has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 246 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yautepec itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.882°N, 99.067°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-651-809), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 105,780, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
