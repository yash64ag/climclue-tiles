Mezcales at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Mezcales is 118th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 135th sunniest (about 2,577 hours of sunshine a year) and 152nd wettest (1,159 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bucerías, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Mezcales is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 5.4 °C more than its coolest, January (22.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.3 °C (60 °F), in February. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 7 km from Mezcales measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.7 °C (4.9 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Mezcales has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mezcales itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.730°N, 105.283°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-632-747), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
