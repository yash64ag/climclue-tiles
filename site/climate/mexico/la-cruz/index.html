La Cruz at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, La Cruz is 156th warmest (24.2 °C year average), 104th sunniest (about 2,627 hours of sunshine a year) and 360th wettest (724 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Costa Rica, the nearest covered town with different numbers (90 km away), La Cruz is 0.9 °C cooler in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.9 °C), averages 9.9 °C more than its coolest, January (18.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.1 °C (52 °F), in February. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why La Cruz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Cruz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.921°N, 106.892°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-600-731), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
