San José del Cabo at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, San José del Cabo is 38th warmest (23.7 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 2,648 hours of sunshine a year) and 121st wettest (442 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cabo San Lucas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), San José del Cabo is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 122 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 8.3 °C more than its coolest, January (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 128% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why San José del Cabo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
