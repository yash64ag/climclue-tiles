Mérida at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Mérida is 6th warmest (26.4 °C year average), 31st sunniest (about 2,635 hours of sunshine a year) and 37th wettest (1,021 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Campeche, the nearest covered town with different numbers (156 km away), Mérida is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 357 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.1 °C), averages 6.2 °C more than its coolest, January (22.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.9 °C (66 °F), in January. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 99% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Mérida has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
