Río Grande at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Río Grande is 423rd warmest (17.3 °C year average), 107th sunniest (about 2,621 hours of sunshine a year) and 578th wettest (371 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Juan Aldama, Zacatecas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (63 km away), Río Grande is 0.1 °C cooler in June and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 82 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (22.3 °C), averages 11.0 °C more than its coolest, January (11.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 321 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 2 km from Río Grande by up to 2.9 °C (5.2 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Río Grande has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 321 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Río Grande itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.827°N, 103.034°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-601-769), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
