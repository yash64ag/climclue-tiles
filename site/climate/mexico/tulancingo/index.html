Tulancingo at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Tulancingo is 135th warmest (14.1 °C year average), 51st sunniest (about 2,434 hours of sunshine a year) and 113th wettest (499 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pachuca de Soto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Tulancingo is 0.7 °C warmer in May and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 87 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (16.4 °C), averages 5.6 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 151 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 92% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tulancingo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 151 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
