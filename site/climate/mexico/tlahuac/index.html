Tláhuac at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Tláhuac is 484th warmest (16.3 °C year average), 569th sunniest (about 2,005 hours of sunshine a year) and 303rd wettest (786 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Juan Ixtayopan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Tláhuac is 0.3 °C warmer in May and about as warm in January, with 133 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (18.8 °C), averages 5.6 °C more than its coolest, January (13.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 261 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 4 km from Tláhuac by up to 2.0 °C (3.6 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Tláhuac

For mild weather, the best months to visit Tláhuac are March, April, May and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, April, May and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (18.8 °C), June (18.3 °C) and April (18.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (6 mm), February (9 mm) and November (11 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (216 h), February (194 h) and January (187 h).

Why Tláhuac has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 261 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tláhuac itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.287°N, 99.005°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-647-809), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 305,076, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
