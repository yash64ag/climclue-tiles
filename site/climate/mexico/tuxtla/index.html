Tuxtla at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Tuxtla is 40th warmest (23.4 °C year average), 130th sunniest (about 1,938 hours of sunshine a year) and 40th wettest (1,007 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Cristóbal de las Casas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Tuxtla is 10.1 °C warmer in May and 8.7 °C warmer in January, with 604 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (25.8 °C), averages 5.1 °C more than its coolest, January (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 123 km from the sea.

Why Tuxtla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 123 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.1 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
