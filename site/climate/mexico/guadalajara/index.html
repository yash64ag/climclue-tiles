Guadalajara at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Guadalajara is 65th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 30th sunniest (about 2,638 hours of sunshine a year) and 47th wettest (875 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zapopan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Guadalajara is 0.8 °C cooler in May and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.1 °C), averages 7.6 °C more than its coolest, January (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.5 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 208 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 90% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Guadalajara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 208 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
