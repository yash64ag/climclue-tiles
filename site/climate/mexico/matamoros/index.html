Matamoros at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Matamoros is 244th warmest (21.9 °C year average), 209th sunniest (about 2,458 hours of sunshine a year) and 612th wettest (229 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Pedro, Coahuila (25.4°N 103.2°W), the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Matamoros is 0.1 °C warmer in June and about as warm in January, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.6 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.1 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 419 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Matamoros has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 419 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Matamoros itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.527°N, 103.228°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-584-767), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
