Aguascalientes at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Aguascalientes is 77th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 2,707 hours of sunshine a year) and 109th wettest (515 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guadalupe, Zacatecas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (99 km away), Aguascalientes is 3.3 °C warmer in May and 3.6 °C warmer in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (22.6 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.7 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 323 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Aguascalientes measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.6 °C (6.4 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Best time to visit Aguascalientes

Why Aguascalientes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 323 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
