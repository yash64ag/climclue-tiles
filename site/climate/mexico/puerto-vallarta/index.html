Puerto Vallarta at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Puerto Vallarta is 18th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 37th sunniest (about 2,566 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (1,252 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tepic, the nearest covered town with different numbers (105 km away), Puerto Vallarta is 4.2 °C warmer in July and 4.6 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.4 °C), averages 5.3 °C more than its coolest, January (22.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.6 °C (65 °F), in February. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Puerto Vallarta measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.3 °C (6.0 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Best time to visit Puerto Vallarta

Why Puerto Vallarta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
