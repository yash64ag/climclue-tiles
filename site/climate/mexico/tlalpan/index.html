Tlalpan at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Tlalpan is 132nd warmest (15.1 °C year average), 112th sunniest (about 2,037 hours of sunshine a year) and 42nd wettest (889 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Coyoacán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Tlalpan is 0.3 °C cooler in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 57 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (17.2 °C), averages 5.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 269 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 86% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tlalpan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 269 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers
