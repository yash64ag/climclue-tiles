Anáhuac weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Anáhuac, Mexico (Nuevo León), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Anáhuac is warmest in August, with 38.4 °C (101 °F) days and 24.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 21.1 °C (70 °F) days and 7.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (87 mm); November is driest (16 mm).

Anáhuac at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Anáhuac is 177th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 434th sunniest (about 2,129 hours of sunshine a year) and 519th wettest (490 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nuevo Laredo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (66 km away), Anáhuac is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (31.5 °C), averages 17.2 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.6 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 269 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Anáhuac has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 269 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Anáhuac itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.241°N, 100.133°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-567-798), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,628, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
