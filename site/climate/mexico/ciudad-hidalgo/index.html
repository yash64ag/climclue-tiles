Ciudad Hidalgo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Ciudad Hidalgo is 458th warmest (16.7 °C year average), 395th sunniest (about 2,155 hours of sunshine a year) and 254th wettest (885 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Maravatío de Ocampo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Ciudad Hidalgo is 0.9 °C cooler in May and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 128 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (19.2 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 249 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 9 km from Ciudad Hidalgo by up to 2.0 °C (3.5 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Ciudad Hidalgo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 249 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C. The 10 km area around Ciudad Hidalgo, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 15.7 °C a year, 1.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ciudad Hidalgo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.690°N, 100.555°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-643-794), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 60,542, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
