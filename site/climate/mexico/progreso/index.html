Progreso at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Progreso is 96th warmest (25.7 °C year average), 165th sunniest (about 2,529 hours of sunshine a year) and 307th wettest (783 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mérida, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Progreso is 0.8 °C cooler in June and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 233 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.7 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, January (22.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.1 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Progreso has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Progreso itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.282°N, 89.663°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-627-903), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 37,369, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
