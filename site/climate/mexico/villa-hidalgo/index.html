Villa Hidalgo weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Villa Hidalgo, Mexico (Jalisco), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Villa Hidalgo is warmest in May, with 31.1 °C (88 °F) days and 13.4 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 22.5 °C (73 °F) days and 4.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (172 mm); March is driest (3 mm).

Villa Hidalgo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Villa Hidalgo is 411th warmest (18.2 °C year average), 61st sunniest (about 2,695 hours of sunshine a year) and 442nd wettest (616 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Calvillo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Villa Hidalgo is 1.6 °C cooler in May and 1.8 °C cooler in January, with 78 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (22.3 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 290 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 13 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Villa Hidalgo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 290 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Villa Hidalgo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.676°N, 102.588°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-623-774), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
