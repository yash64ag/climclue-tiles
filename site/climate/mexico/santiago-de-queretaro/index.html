Santiago de Querétaro at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Santiago de Querétaro is 84th warmest (17.9 °C year average), 39th sunniest (about 2,547 hours of sunshine a year) and 104th wettest (549 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Celaya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Santiago de Querétaro is 2.4 °C cooler in May and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 51 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (21.3 °C), averages 7.2 °C more than its coolest, January (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.1 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 321 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Santiago de Querétaro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 321 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
