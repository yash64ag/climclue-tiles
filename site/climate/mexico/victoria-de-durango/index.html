Victoria de Durango at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Victoria de Durango is 93rd warmest (17.4 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 2,641 hours of sunshine a year) and 118th wettest (459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mazatlán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (200 km away), Victoria de Durango is 4.6 °C cooler in June and 9.2 °C cooler in January, with 326 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (22.6 °C), averages 11.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 203 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Victoria de Durango has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 203 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Victoria de Durango itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.020°N, 104.658°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-599-753), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
