Tuzantla at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Tuzantla is 36th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 352nd sunniest (about 2,188 hours of sunshine a year) and 225th wettest (976 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Heróica Zitácuaro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Tuzantla is 10.5 °C warmer in May and 9.7 °C warmer in January, with 323 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.6 °C), averages 6.6 °C more than its coolest, January (24.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.9 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 215 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 18 km from Tuzantla measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 8.1 °C (14.5 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Tuzantla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 215 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.6 °C. The 10 km area around Tuzantla, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 25.1 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tuzantla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.203°N, 100.567°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-647-794), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
