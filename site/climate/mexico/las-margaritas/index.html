Las Margaritas at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Las Margaritas is 398th warmest (18.5 °C year average), 372nd sunniest (about 2,170 hours of sunshine a year) and 77th wettest (1,590 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Comitán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Las Margaritas is about as warm in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 126 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (20.1 °C), averages 4.1 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.0 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 170 km from the sea.

Why Las Margaritas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 170 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Las Margaritas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.313°N, 91.982°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-676-880), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
