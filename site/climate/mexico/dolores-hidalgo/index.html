Dolores Hidalgo at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Dolores Hidalgo is 410th warmest (18.2 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 2,698 hours of sunshine a year) and 514th wettest (502 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Miguel de Allende, the nearest covered town with different numbers (33 km away), Dolores Hidalgo is 0.5 °C cooler in May and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (21.7 °C), averages 7.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 356 km from the sea.

Why Dolores Hidalgo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 356 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dolores Hidalgo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.156°N, 100.932°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-628-790), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
