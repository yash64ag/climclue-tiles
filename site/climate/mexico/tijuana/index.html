Why Tijuana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Tijuana climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Tijuana?
August, with an average of 20.4 °C (69 °F), day highs around 23.1 °C (74 °F) and night lows around 18.6 °C (65 °F).
What is the coldest month in Tijuana?
January, with an average of 13.6 °C (56 °F), day highs around 16.6 °C (62 °F) and night lows around 11.0 °C (52 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Tijuana?
February, with 58 mm (2.3 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 285 mm (11.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Tijuana?
August, with 2 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Tijuana?
July, with about 306 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Tijuana?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Tijuana?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: May, June, July, August, September, October and November.
What type of climate does Tijuana have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.4 × 11 km, that contains Tijuana’s GeoNames location (32.503°N, 117.004°W; ClimaClue area sq-514-629), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 1,922,523 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
