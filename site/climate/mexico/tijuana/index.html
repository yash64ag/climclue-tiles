Tijuana at a glance

Among the 141 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Mexico, Tijuana is 103rd warmest (16.5 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 3,067 hours of sunshine a year) and 133rd wettest (285 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rosarito, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Tijuana is 0.4 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.4 °C), averages 6.9 °C more than its coolest, January (13.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.0 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 19 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tijuana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
