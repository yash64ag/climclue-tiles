Tepic at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Tepic is 270th warmest (21.1 °C year average), 105th sunniest (about 2,626 hours of sunshine a year) and 96th wettest (1,395 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Xalisco, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Tepic is 0.2 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 71 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (23.6 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, January (17.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.8 °C (50 °F), in February. The area lies about 53 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Tepic has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 53 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tepic itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.507°N, 104.893°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-624-751), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
