Pinotepa Nacional at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Pinotepa Nacional is 51st warmest (26.3 °C year average), 97th sunniest (about 2,637 hours of sunshine a year) and 117th wettest (1,297 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ometepec, the nearest covered town with different numbers (54 km away), Pinotepa Nacional is 0.7 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 208 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.6 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, January (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in February. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pinotepa Nacional, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pinotepa Nacional has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pinotepa Nacional itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.340°N, 98.053°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-676-819), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
