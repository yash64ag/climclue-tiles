Acajete at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Acajete is 570th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 326th sunniest (about 2,242 hours of sunshine a year) and 293rd wettest (801 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tepatlaxco de Hidalgo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Acajete is 0.6 °C cooler in May and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 30 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (16.9 °C), averages 5.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 181 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Acajete has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 181 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.1 °C. The 10 km area around Acajete, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.8 °C a year, 1.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Acajete itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.112°N, 97.952°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-648-820), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
