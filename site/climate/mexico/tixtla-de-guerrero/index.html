Tixtla de Guerrero at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, Tixtla de Guerrero is 266th warmest (21.3 °C year average), 358th sunniest (about 2,177 hours of sunshine a year) and 182nd wettest (1,076 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chilpancingo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Tixtla de Guerrero is 0.4 °C cooler in May and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (23.3 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, January (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.2 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 108 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tixtla de Guerrero has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 108 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C. The 10 km area around Tixtla de Guerrero, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.8 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tixtla de Guerrero itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.567°N, 99.398°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-664-806), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
