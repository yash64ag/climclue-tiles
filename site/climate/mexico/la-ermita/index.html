La Ermita at a glance

Among the 624 towns ClimaClue covers in Mexico, La Ermita is 390th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 345th sunniest (about 2,202 hours of sunshine a year) and 384th wettest (687 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Centro Familiar la Soledad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), La Ermita is 0.2 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (22.6 °C), averages 8.2 °C more than its coolest, January (14.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 352 km from the sea.

Why La Ermita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 352 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Ermita itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.152°N, 101.733°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-628-782), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
