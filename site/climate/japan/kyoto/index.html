Kyoto at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Kyoto is 454th warmest (15.7 °C year average), 1071st sunniest (about 1,674 hours of sunshine a year) and 846th wettest (1,435 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kamigyō-ku, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Kyoto is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.0 °C), averages 23.3 °C more than its coolest, January (4.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kyoto, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Kyoto

No month in Kyoto is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are May (18.9 °C, 152 mm), September (23.8 °C, 167 mm) and October (17.4 °C, 118 mm).

Why Kyoto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Kyoto climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Kyoto?
August, with an average of 28.0 °C (82 °F), day highs around 32.9 °C (91 °F) and night lows around 23.9 °C (75 °F).
What is the coldest month in Kyoto?
January, with an average of 4.7 °C (40 °F), day highs around 8.9 °C (48 °F) and night lows around 1.1 °C (34 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Kyoto?
June, with 198 mm (7.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,435 mm (56.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Kyoto?
December, with 57 mm (2.2 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Kyoto?
May, with about 189 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Kyoto?
Yes. About 27 cm (10.7 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in February; it usually melts within days.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kyoto itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.021°N, 135.754°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-489-3157), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,463,723, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
