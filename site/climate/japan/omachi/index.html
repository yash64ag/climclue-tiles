Ōmachi at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Ōmachi is 1155th warmest (10.1 °C year average), 986th sunniest (about 1,752 hours of sunshine a year) and 661st wettest (1,542 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hotaka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Ōmachi is 2.1 °C cooler in August and 1.9 °C cooler in January, with 301 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.4 °C), averages 26.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 58 km from the sea.

Why Ōmachi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 58 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ōmachi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.500°N, 137.867°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-474-3178), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
