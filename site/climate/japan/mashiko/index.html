Mashiko at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Mashiko is 920th warmest (13.9 °C year average), 435th sunniest (about 1,858 hours of sunshine a year) and 1115th wettest (1,294 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mooka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Mashiko is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.9 °C), averages 23.1 °C more than its coolest, January (2.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 50 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mashiko, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Mashiko has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 50 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mashiko itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.467°N, 140.100°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-475-3201), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 26,045, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
