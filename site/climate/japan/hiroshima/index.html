Hiroshima at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Hiroshima is 102nd warmest (15.9 °C year average), 65th sunniest (about 1,896 hours of sunshine a year) and 109th wettest (1,604 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hatsukaichi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Hiroshima is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 191 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.4 °C), averages 22.4 °C more than its coolest, January (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hiroshima, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hiroshima has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.4 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
