Katsuta at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Katsuta is 215th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 116th sunniest (about 1,858 hours of sunshine a year) and 195th wettest (1,401 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hitachi-Naka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Katsuta is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 21 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.3 °C), averages 19.7 °C more than its coolest, January (5.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Katsuta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Katsuta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Katsuta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.383°N, 140.533°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-476-3205), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
