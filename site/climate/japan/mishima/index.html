Mishima at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Mishima is 108th warmest (16.1 °C year average), 110th sunniest (about 1,859 hours of sunshine a year) and 72nd wettest (1,752 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Numazu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Mishima is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.5 °C), averages 20.2 °C more than its coolest, January (6.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 21 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mishima, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mishima has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.2 °C. The 10 km area around Mishima, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 14.8 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mishima itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.117°N, 138.917°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-488-3189), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
