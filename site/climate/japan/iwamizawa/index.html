Iwamizawa weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Iwamizawa, Japan (Hokkaido), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Iwamizawa at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Iwamizawa is 1177th warmest (8.1 °C year average), 1059th sunniest (about 1,686 hours of sunshine a year) and 623rd wettest (1,582 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bibai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Iwamizawa is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.6 °C), averages 27.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Why Iwamizawa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Iwamizawa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.200°N, 141.760°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-407-3217), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
