Nemuro at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Nemuro is the coolest (5.8 °C year average). Compared with Shibetsu, Hokkaido (43.7°N 145.1°E), the nearest covered town with different numbers (52 km away), Nemuro is 2.9 °C cooler in August and 3.1 °C warmer in February, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (15.7 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, February (-3.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April and December. The area lies about 8 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Nemuro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nemuro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.324°N, 145.575°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-406-3255), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
