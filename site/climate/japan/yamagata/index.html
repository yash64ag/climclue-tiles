Yamagata at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Yamagata is 261st warmest (11.3 °C year average), 254th sunniest (about 1,699 hours of sunshine a year) and 101st wettest (1,649 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sendai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Yamagata is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 2.1 °C cooler in January, with 131 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.1 °C), averages 24.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 62 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Yamagata, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Yamagata has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 62 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
