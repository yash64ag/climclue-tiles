Kajiki weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kajiki, Japan (Kagoshima), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kajiki is warmest in August, with 32.1 °C (90 °F) days and 24.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 12.9 °C (55 °F) days and 2.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (425 mm); December is driest (65 mm).

Kajiki at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Kajiki is 55th warmest (18.1 °C year average), 436th sunniest (about 1,858 hours of sunshine a year) and 206th wettest (2,213 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hamanoichi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Kajiki is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 164 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.1 °C), averages 20.3 °C more than its coolest, January (7.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kajiki, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kajiki has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kajiki itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.733°N, 130.667°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-522-3106), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,997, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
