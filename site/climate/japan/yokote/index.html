Yokote weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Yokote, Japan (Akita), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Yokote is warmest in August, with 28.6 °C (83 °F) days and 20.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.8 °C (37 °F) days and -3.7 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 128 mm in February to 282 mm in July.

Yokote at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Yokote is 1109th warmest (11.5 °C year average), 1040th sunniest (about 1,699 hours of sunshine a year) and 155th wettest (2,345 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rokugō, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Yokote is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.4 °C), averages 24.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 53 km from the sea.

Why Yokote has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 53 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yokote itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.317°N, 140.550°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-446-3205), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
