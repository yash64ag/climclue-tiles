Masaki-chō weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Masaki-chō, Japan (Ehime), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Masaki-chō is warmest in August, with 31.8 °C (89 °F) days and 24.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 10.9 °C (52 °F) days and 3.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (221 mm); December is driest (41 mm).

Masaki-chō at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Masaki-chō is 131st warmest (17.3 °C year average), 177th sunniest (about 1,926 hours of sunshine a year) and 1106th wettest (1,304 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Iyo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Masaki-chō is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.1 °C), averages 21.0 °C more than its coolest, January (7.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Masaki-chō has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Masaki-chō itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.788°N, 132.711°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-502-3127), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
