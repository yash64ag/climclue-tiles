Kitamoto weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kitamoto, Japan (Saitama), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kitamoto is warmest in August, with 31.2 °C (88 °F) days and 22.9 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 9.4 °C (49 °F) days and -1.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (210 mm); December is driest (38 mm).

Kitamoto at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Kitamoto is 730th warmest (15.2 °C year average), 291st sunniest (about 1,888 hours of sunshine a year) and 1020th wettest (1,355 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Okegawa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Kitamoto is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.0 °C), averages 23.2 °C more than its coolest, January (3.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 62 km from the sea.

Why Kitamoto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 62 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kitamoto itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.033°N, 139.538°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-479-3195), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 66,022, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
