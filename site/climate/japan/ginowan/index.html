Ginowan at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Ginowan is the 5th warmest (22.5 °C year average) and the 4th wettest (2,661 mm a year). Compared with Urasoe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Ginowan is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 232 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.5 °C), averages 10.3 °C more than its coolest, January (17.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.2 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ginowan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ginowan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.263°N, 127.761°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-577-3077), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
