Nishio weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nishio, Japan (Aichi), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nishio is warmest in August, with 31.2 °C (88 °F) days and 23.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 10.2 °C (50 °F) days and 2.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (231 mm); December is driest (45 mm).

Nishio at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Nishio is 364th warmest (16.4 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 2,018 hours of sunshine a year) and 650th wettest (1,556 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hekinan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Nishio is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.3 °C), averages 21.2 °C more than its coolest, January (6.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Nishio has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nishio itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.867°N, 137.050°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-491-3170), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
