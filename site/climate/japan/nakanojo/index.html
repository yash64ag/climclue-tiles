Nakanojō at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Nakanojō is 1038th warmest (12.5 °C year average), 490th sunniest (about 1,850 hours of sunshine a year) and 1181st wettest (1,124 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Minakami, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Nakanojō is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 246 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.8 °C), averages 24.2 °C more than its coolest, January (0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 99 km from the sea.

Why Nakanojō has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 99 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.2 °C. The 10 km area around Nakanojō, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 11.4 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nakanojō itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.587°N, 138.841°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-474-3188), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
