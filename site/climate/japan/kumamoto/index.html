Kumamoto at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Kumamoto is the wettest (2,904 mm a year). Compared with Kotō, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Kumamoto is 1.2 °C cooler in August and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 447 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.9 °C), averages 20.8 °C more than its coolest, January (6.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kumamoto, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kumamoto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
