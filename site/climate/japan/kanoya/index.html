Kanoya at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Kanoya is the 3rd wettest (2,724 mm a year). Compared with Kagoshima, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Kanoya is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 624 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 19.1 °C more than its coolest, January (8.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 19 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kanoya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kanoya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kanoya itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.383°N, 130.850°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-526-3108), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
