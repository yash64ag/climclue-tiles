Shinagawa weather by month and climate

Shinagawa at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Shinagawa is 920th warmest (14.3 °C year average), 436th sunniest (about 1,858 hours of sunshine a year) and 370th wettest (1,868 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tōon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Shinagawa is 2.2 °C cooler in August and 2.9 °C cooler in January, with 427 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.7 °C), averages 22.9 °C more than its coolest, January (2.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 45 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shinagawa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shinagawa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 45 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.9 °C. The 10 km area around Shinagawa, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.7 °C a year, 1.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shinagawa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.636°N, 133.006°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-503-3130), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
