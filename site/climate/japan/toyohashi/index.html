Toyohashi at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Toyohashi is 325th warmest (16.6 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 2,048 hours of sunshine a year) and 538th wettest (1,668 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Toyokawa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Toyohashi is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 40 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 21.5 °C more than its coolest, January (6.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Toyohashi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Toyohashi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Toyohashi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.767°N, 137.383°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-492-3173), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
