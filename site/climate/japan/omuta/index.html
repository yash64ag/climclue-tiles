Ōmuta at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Ōmuta is 17th warmest (17.2 °C year average), 178th sunniest (about 1,826 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (2,268 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Saga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Ōmuta is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 395 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 20.7 °C more than its coolest, January (7.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ōmuta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ōmuta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
