Ishinomaki at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Ishinomaki is 255th warmest (12.0 °C year average), 63rd sunniest (about 1,898 hours of sunshine a year) and 269th wettest (1,220 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ōsaki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Ishinomaki is 1.5 °C cooler in August and 0.7 °C warmer in February, with 287 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.1 °C), averages 20.8 °C more than its coolest, February (2.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ishinomaki, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ishinomaki has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
