Muramatsu at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Muramatsu is 980th warmest (13.7 °C year average), 1097th sunniest (about 1,658 hours of sunshine a year) and 196th wettest (2,241 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gosen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Muramatsu is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.5 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 42 km from the sea.

Why Muramatsu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 42 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Muramatsu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.693°N, 139.171°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-463-3191), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
