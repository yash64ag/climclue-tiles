Fuchū at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Fuchū is 201st warmest (15.1 °C year average), 121st sunniest (about 1,855 hours of sunshine a year) and 196th wettest (1,398 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kokubunji, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Fuchū is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.5 °C), averages 22.5 °C more than its coolest, January (4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 38 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Fuchū, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Fuchū has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 38 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fuchū itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.675°N, 139.482°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-483-3194), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
