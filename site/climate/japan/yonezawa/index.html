Yonezawa at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Yonezawa is 1102nd warmest (11.7 °C year average), 1058th sunniest (about 1,687 hours of sunshine a year) and 713th wettest (1,509 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Takahata, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Yonezawa is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 54 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 24.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 78 km from the sea.

Why Yonezawa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 78 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yonezawa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.910°N, 140.117°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-3201), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
