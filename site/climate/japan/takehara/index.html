Takehara at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Takehara is 290th warmest (16.2 °C year average), 167th sunniest (about 1,930 hours of sunshine a year) and 1138th wettest (1,257 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mihara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Takehara is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 1.2 °C warmer in January, with 16 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 21.7 °C more than its coolest, January (5.9 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Takehara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Takehara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Takehara climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Takehara?
August, with an average of 27.6 °C (82 °F), day highs around 31.7 °C (89 °F) and night lows around 23.7 °C (75 °F).
What is the coldest month in Takehara?
January, with an average of 5.9 °C (43 °F), day highs around 10.0 °C (50 °F) and night lows around 1.8 °C (35 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Takehara?
June, with 209 mm (8.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,257 mm (49.5 in) in a typical year.

What is the sunniest month in Takehara?

Does it snow in Takehara?
Yes. About 5.8 cm (2.3 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in February; it usually melts within days.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Takehara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.338°N, 132.917°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-496-3129), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,506, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
