Suwa at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Suwa is 1098th warmest (11.7 °C year average), 572nd sunniest (about 1,839 hours of sunshine a year) and 1182nd wettest (1,120 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shimosuwa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Suwa is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.4 °C), averages 26.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 120 km from the sea.

Why Suwa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 120 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.1 °C. The 10 km area around Suwa, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 9.6 °C a year, 2.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Suwa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.038°N, 138.113°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-479-3181), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
