Mitoyo at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Mitoyo is 268th warmest (16.3 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 2,011 hours of sunshine a year) and 750th wettest (1,486 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kan’onjichō, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Mitoyo is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C cooler in February, with 34 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 21.6 °C more than its coolest, February (6.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mitoyo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Mitoyo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mitoyo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.211°N, 133.675°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-497-3136), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 65,713, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
