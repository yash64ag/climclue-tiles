Komoro weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Komoro, Japan (Nagano), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Komoro is warmest in August, with 29.3 °C (85 °F) days and 19.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 5.2 °C (41 °F) days and -6.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (132 mm); December is driest (22 mm).

Komoro at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Komoro is the 3rd driest (843 mm a year). Compared with Miyota, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Komoro is 1.9 °C warmer in August and 1.7 °C warmer in January, with 210 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.4 °C), averages 24.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 99 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Komoro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Komoro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 99 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.9 °C. The 10 km area around Komoro, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 9.5 °C a year, 2.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Komoro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.317°N, 138.433°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-476-3184), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
