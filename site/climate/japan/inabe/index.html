Inabe at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Inabe is 668th warmest (15.2 °C year average), 374th sunniest (about 1,868 hours of sunshine a year) and 533rd wettest (1,673 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tōin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Inabe is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.1 °C), averages 22.9 °C more than its coolest, January (4.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Inabe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Inabe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Inabe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.113°N, 136.572°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-488-3165), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 44,973, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
