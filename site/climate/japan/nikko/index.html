Nikkō weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nikkō, Japan (Tochigi), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nikkō is warmest in August, with 27.2 °C (81 °F) days and 19.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 4.0 °C (39 °F) days and -5.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (371 mm); December is driest (52 mm).

Nikkō at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Nikkō is 1126th warmest (11.1 °C year average), 696th sunniest (about 1,826 hours of sunshine a year) and 232nd wettest (2,134 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Imaichi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Nikkō is 0.9 °C cooler in August and 1.4 °C cooler in January, with 77 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.1 °C), averages 23.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 101 km from the sea.

Why Nikkō has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 101 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nikkō itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.750°N, 139.617°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-472-3196), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
