Iwakuni at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Iwakuni is 43rd warmest (16.7 °C year average), 84th sunniest (about 1,877 hours of sunshine a year) and 105th wettest (1,620 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hatsukaichi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Iwakuni is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 174 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 21.4 °C more than its coolest, January (6.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Iwakuni has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
