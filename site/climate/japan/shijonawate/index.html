Shijōnawate weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Shijōnawate, Japan (Osaka), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Shijōnawate is warmest in August, with 32.1 °C (90 °F) days and 22.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 8.2 °C (47 °F) days and -0.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (244 mm); December is driest (58 mm).

Shijōnawate at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Shijōnawate is 679th warmest (15.3 °C year average), 776th sunniest (about 1,816 hours of sunshine a year) and 513th wettest (1,700 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ikoma, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Shijōnawate is 0.6 °C cooler in August and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 117 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.5 °C), averages 23.5 °C more than its coolest, January (4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Why Shijōnawate has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.5 °C. The 10 km area around Shijōnawate, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.3 °C a year, 1.0 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shijōnawate itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.733°N, 135.683°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-492-3156), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
