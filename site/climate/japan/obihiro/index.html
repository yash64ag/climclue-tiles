Obihiro weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Obihiro, Japan (Hokkaido), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Obihiro is warmest in August, with 24.5 °C (76 °F) days and 16.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -2.1 °C (28 °F) days and -13.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (152 mm); February is driest (26 mm).

Obihiro at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Obihiro is 1194th warmest (7.1 °C year average), 242nd sunniest (about 1,902 hours of sunshine a year) and 1205th wettest (954 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Otofuke, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Obihiro is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.4 °C), averages 28.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 53 km from the sea.

Why Obihiro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 53 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Obihiro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.917°N, 143.204°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-410-3232), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
