Ibaraki at a glance

Among the 276 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Japan, Ibaraki is 112th warmest (15.8 °C year average), 244th sunniest (about 1,765 hours of sunshine a year) and 98th wettest (1,657 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Takatsuki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Ibaraki is 0.5 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 65 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 23.2 °C more than its coolest, January (4.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ibaraki, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ibaraki has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
