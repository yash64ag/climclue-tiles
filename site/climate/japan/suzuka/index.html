Suzuka at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Suzuka is 426th warmest (16.2 °C year average), 191st sunniest (about 1,917 hours of sunshine a year) and 494th wettest (1,718 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kawage, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Suzuka is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.4 °C), averages 21.6 °C more than its coolest, January (5.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Suzuka, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Suzuka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Suzuka climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Suzuka?
August, with an average of 27.4 °C (81 °F), day highs around 31.3 °C (88 °F) and night lows around 23.5 °C (74 °F).
What is the coldest month in Suzuka?
January, with an average of 5.7 °C (42 °F), day highs around 9.9 °C (50 °F) and night lows around 1.5 °C (35 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Suzuka?
September, with 260 mm (10.3 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,718 mm (67.6 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Suzuka?
December, with 47 mm (1.9 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Suzuka?
August, with about 194 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Suzuka?
Yes. About 5.8 cm (2.3 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in February; it usually melts within days.

How warm is the sea in Suzuka?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Suzuka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.883°N, 136.583°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-491-3165), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 195,670, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
