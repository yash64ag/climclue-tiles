Miyota at a glance

Among the 1213 towns ClimaClue covers in Japan, Miyota is 1154th warmest (10.1 °C year average), 593rd sunniest (about 1,836 hours of sunshine a year) and 1192nd wettest (1,054 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Komoro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Miyota is 1.9 °C cooler in August and 1.7 °C cooler in January, with 210 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.6 °C), averages 24.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 104 km from the sea.

Why Miyota has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 104 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.8 °C. The 10 km area around Miyota, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 8.8 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Miyota itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.337°N, 138.513°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-476-3185), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
