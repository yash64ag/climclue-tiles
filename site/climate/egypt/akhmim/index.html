Akhmīm at a glance

Among the 72 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Egypt, Akhmīm is 7th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 3,766 hours of sunshine a year) and 67th wettest (3 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sohag, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Akhmīm is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (33.0 °C), averages 18.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.0 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 222 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Akhmīm, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Akhmīm has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 222 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
