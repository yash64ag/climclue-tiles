Suez at a glance

Among the 72 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Egypt, Suez is 27th warmest (22.2 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 3,481 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (19 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ismailia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (74 km away), Suez is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.4 °C), averages 15.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.5 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Suez, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Suez has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
