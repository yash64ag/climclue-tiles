Al Badārī at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Al Badārī is 46th warmest (24.1 °C year average), 44th sunniest (about 3,745 hours of sunshine a year) and 187th wettest (5 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Şidfā, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Al Badārī is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.4 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.5 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 229 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Al Badārī, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Al Badārī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 229 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Badārī itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.993°N, 31.416°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-570-2114), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
