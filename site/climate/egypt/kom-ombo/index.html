Kom Ombo at a glance

Among the 72 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Egypt, Kom Ombo is the warmest (27.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,850 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (2 mm a year). Compared with Aswān, the nearest covered town with different numbers (43 km away), Kom Ombo is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.8 °C), averages 18.2 °C more than its coolest, January (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.6 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 213 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kom Ombo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kom Ombo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 213 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
