Fāqūs at a glance

Among the 72 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Egypt, Fāqūs is 49th warmest (21.3 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 3,372 hours of sunshine a year) and 26th wettest (34 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Abū Kabīr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Fāqūs is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 14.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Fāqūs, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Fāqūs has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
