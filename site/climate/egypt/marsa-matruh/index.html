Marsá Maţrūḩ at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Marsá Maţrūḩ is the 5th wettest (182 mm a year). Compared with An-Najaylah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Marsá Maţrūḩ is 0.9 °C warmer in August and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.8 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.6 °C (49 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Marsá Maţrūḩ has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Marsá Maţrūḩ itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.353°N, 27.237°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-526-2072), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
