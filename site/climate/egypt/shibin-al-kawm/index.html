Shibīn al Kawm at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Shibīn al Kawm is 131st warmest (21.5 °C year average), 198th sunniest (about 3,305 hours of sunshine a year) and 98th wettest (32 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Birkat as Sab‘, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Shibīn al Kawm is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 14.2 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 112 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shibīn al Kawm, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shibīn al Kawm has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 112 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shibīn al Kawm itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.553°N, 31.009°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-534-2110), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
