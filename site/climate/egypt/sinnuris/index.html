Sinnūris at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Sinnūris is 81st warmest (22.8 °C year average), 93rd sunniest (about 3,424 hours of sunshine a year) and 151st wettest (11 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ţāmiyah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Sinnūris is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.1 °C), averages 16.3 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 156 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sinnūris, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sinnūris has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 156 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sinnūris itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.413°N, 30.866°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-545-2108), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
