Hurghada at a glance

Among the 72 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Egypt, Hurghada is 9th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 3,764 hours of sunshine a year) and 53rd wettest (6 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Qina, the nearest covered town with different numbers (163 km away), Hurghada is 2.9 °C cooler in August and 2.4 °C warmer in January, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (30.6 °C), averages 13.3 °C more than its coolest, January (17.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.1 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hurghada, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hurghada has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
