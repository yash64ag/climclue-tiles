Alexandria at a glance

Among the 72 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Egypt, Alexandria is the 2nd coolest (20.4 °C year average), the least sunny (about 3,256 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (198 mm a year). Compared with Kafr ad Dawwār, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Alexandria is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.3 °C), averages 11.7 °C more than its coolest, February (14.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.1 °C (54 °F), in February. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alexandria, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Alexandria has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
