Abū Qīr at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Abū Qīr is the 3rd least sunny (about 3,260 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (209 mm a year). Compared with Alexandria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Abū Qīr is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.2 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, February (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.7 °C (49 °F), in February. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Abū Qīr has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Abū Qīr itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.311°N, 30.061°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-526-2100), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
