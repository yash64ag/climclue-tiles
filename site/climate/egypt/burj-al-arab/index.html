Burj al ‘Arab weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Burj al ‘Arab, Egypt (Alexandria), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Burj al ‘Arab at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Burj al ‘Arab is the 4th coolest (20.2 °C year average). Compared with Burj al ‘Arab al Jadīdah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Burj al ‘Arab is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.2 °C), averages 12.0 °C more than its coolest, January (14.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.8 °C (48 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Burj al ‘Arab has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Burj al ‘Arab itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.903°N, 29.553°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-530-2095), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
