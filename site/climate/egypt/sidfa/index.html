Şidfā at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Şidfā is 45th warmest (24.1 °C year average), 47th sunniest (about 3,742 hours of sunshine a year) and 195th wettest (4 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Al Badārī, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Şidfā is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.4 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.5 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 237 km from the sea.

Why Şidfā has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 237 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Şidfā itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.966°N, 31.380°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-570-2113), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
