Rosetta at a glance

Among the 72 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Egypt, Rosetta is the 5th coolest (20.5 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 3,265 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (179 mm a year). Compared with Idkū, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Rosetta is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.4 °C), averages 11.8 °C more than its coolest, February (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rosetta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Rosetta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
