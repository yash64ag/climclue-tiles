Rosetta at a glance

Among the 237 towns ClimaClue covers in Egypt, Rosetta is the 4th least sunny (about 3,265 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Idkū, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Rosetta is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.6 °C), averages 12.0 °C more than its coolest, February (14.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.1 °C (50 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Rosetta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rosetta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.400°N, 30.417°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-525-2104), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
