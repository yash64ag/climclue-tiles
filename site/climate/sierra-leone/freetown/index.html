Freetown at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sierra Leone, Freetown is the 3rd warmest (25.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,088 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (3,695 mm a year). Compared with Bo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (175 km away), Freetown is 0.6 °C warmer in December and about as warm in August, with 1,078 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (25.9 °C), averages 2.0 °C more than its coolest, August (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.3 °C (74 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Freetown, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Freetown has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.0 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
