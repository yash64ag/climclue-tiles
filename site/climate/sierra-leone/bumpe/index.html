Bumpe at a glance

Among the 13 towns ClimaClue covers in Sierra Leone, Bumpe is the 3rd least sunny (about 1,860 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Bo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Bumpe is about as warm in March and 0.2 °C warmer in August, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.7 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, August (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 79 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bumpe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Bumpe

No month in Bumpe is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are July (24.4 °C, 378 mm), September (24.5 °C, 389 mm) and October (24.9 °C, 323 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): March (27.7 °C), February (27.4 °C) and April (27.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (10 mm), December (13 mm) and February (17 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (195 h), March (190 h) and February (189 h).

Why Bumpe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 79 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bumpe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.892°N, 11.905°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-761-1680), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,123, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
