Why Benguela has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Benguela climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Benguela?
April, with an average of 26.9 °C (81 °F), day highs around 30.1 °C (86 °F) and night lows around 24.8 °C (77 °F).
What is the coldest month in Benguela?
August, with an average of 19.9 °C (68 °F), day highs around 23.2 °C (74 °F) and night lows around 17.6 °C (64 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Benguela?
March, with 124 mm (4.9 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 339 mm (13.3 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Benguela?
June, with 1 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Benguela?
April, with about 210 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Benguela?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Benguela?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: June, July, August, September and October.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Benguela’s GeoNames location (12.577°S, 13.403°E; ClimaClue area sq-965-1934), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 555,124 (GeoNames).
