Benguela at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Angola, Benguela is the 5th least sunny (about 2,058 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (293 mm a year). Compared with Navegantes, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Benguela is about as warm in April and about as warm in August, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.0 °C), averages 7.1 °C more than its coolest, August (19.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Benguela, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Benguela has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Benguela itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.577°S, 13.403°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-965-1934), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
