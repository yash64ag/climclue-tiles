Vila Flor at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Angola, Vila Flor is 8th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 2,297 hours of sunshine a year) and 103rd wettest (521 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Camama, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Vila Flor is 0.1 °C cooler in March and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.2 °C), averages 5.4 °C more than its coolest, July (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.0 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Vila Flor, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Vila Flor has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vila Flor itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.980°S, 13.308°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-929-1933), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
