Cabinda weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Cabinda, Angola, from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Cabinda is warmest in March, with 30.7 °C (87 °F) days and 22.7 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 25.4 °C (78 °F) days and 17.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around November (161 mm); July is driest (2 mm).

Cabinda at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Angola, Cabinda is the least sunny (about 1,268 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Cacongo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Cabinda is 0.2 °C warmer in March and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.7 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, July (21.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cabinda, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Cabinda

For mild weather, the best months to visit Cabinda are June, July, August, September and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August, September and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): March (26.7 °C), April (26.4 °C) and February (26.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (2 mm), June (2 mm) and August (3 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (150 h), April (148 h) and February (123 h).

Why Cabinda has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cabinda itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.562°S, 12.195°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-895-1921), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
