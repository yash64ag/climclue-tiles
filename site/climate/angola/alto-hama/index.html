Alto Hama at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Angola, Alto Hama is 120th warmest (19.5 °C year average), 91st sunniest (about 2,188 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (1,214 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Londuimbali, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Alto Hama is 0.7 °C warmer in September and 0.4 °C warmer in June, with 209 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (21.6 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, June (17.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.8 °C (50 °F), in June. The area lies about 208 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alto Hama, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Alto Hama has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 208 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alto Hama itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.233°S, 15.550°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-962-1955), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
