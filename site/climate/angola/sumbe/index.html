Sumbe at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Angola, Sumbe is 53rd warmest (23.7 °C year average), 107th sunniest (about 2,125 hours of sunshine a year) and 100th wettest (531 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Porto Amboim, the nearest covered town with different numbers (53 km away), Sumbe is 0.2 °C cooler in March and 0.5 °C cooler in August, with 75 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.5 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, August (19.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.8 °C (60 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sumbe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sumbe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sumbe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.206°S, 13.844°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-952-1938), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
