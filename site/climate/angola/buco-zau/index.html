Buco Zau at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Angola, Buco Zau is the 3rd least sunny (about 1,508 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Belize, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Buco Zau is 0.4 °C warmer in March and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 54 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.2 °C), averages 4.9 °C more than its coolest, July (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.8 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 74 km from the sea.

Best time to visit Buco Zau

For mild weather, the best months to visit Buco Zau are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): March (26.2 °C), April (26.2 °C) and February (25.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (4 mm), June (7 mm) and August (11 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): February (171 h), April (166 h) and March (158 h).

Why Buco Zau has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Buco Zau itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.767°S, 12.550°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-887-1925), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 40,953, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
