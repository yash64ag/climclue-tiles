Luanda at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Angola, Luanda is 11th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 2,306 hours of sunshine a year) and 33rd wettest (459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Samba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Luanda is 0.1 °C cooler in March and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 67 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.4 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, July (21.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.2 °C (65 °F), in July. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Luanda, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Luanda has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
