Dundo at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Angola, Dundo is 39th warmest (24.2 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 2,312 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (1,447 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chitato, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Dundo is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in November, with 92 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.5 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, November (23.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in June. The area lies about 843 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dundo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dundo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 843 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dundo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.366°S, 20.816°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-913-2008), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
