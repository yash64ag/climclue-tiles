Panguíla at a glance

Among the 51 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Angola, Panguíla is 10th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 30th sunniest (about 2,290 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (569 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cacuaco, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Panguíla is 0.3 °C cooler in March and about as warm in July, with 82 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.3 °C), averages 5.7 °C more than its coolest, July (21.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Panguíla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Panguíla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
