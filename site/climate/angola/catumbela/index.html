Catumbela weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Catumbela, Angola (Benguela), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Catumbela is warmest in April, with 30.3 °C (87 °F) days and 23.2 °C nights, and coolest in August, with 23.6 °C (74 °F) days and 16.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (151 mm); June is driest (2 mm).

Catumbela at a glance

Among the 136 towns ClimaClue covers in Angola, Catumbela is 44th warmest (24.0 °C year average), 125th sunniest (about 2,041 hours of sunshine a year) and 123rd wettest (416 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lobito, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Catumbela is about as warm in April and about as warm in August, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.8 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, August (20.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in July. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Catumbela, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Catumbela has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Catumbela itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.430°S, 13.547°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-964-1935), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
