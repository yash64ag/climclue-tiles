Helsingborg at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sweden, Helsingborg is the 2nd warmest (8.0 °C year average) and the sunniest (about 1,591 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Malmö, the nearest covered town with different numbers (53 km away), Helsingborg is 0.5 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 16.7 °C more than its coolest, January (0.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 17 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Helsingborg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
