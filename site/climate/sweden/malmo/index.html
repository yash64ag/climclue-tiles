Malmö weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Malmö, Sweden (Skåne), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Malmö is warmest in July, with 21.3 °C (70 °F) days and 13.9 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.4 °C (36 °F) days and -1.5 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 36 mm in February to 72 mm in August.

Malmö at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Malmö is the 3rd warmest (8.4 °C year average). Compared with Limhamn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Malmö is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.6 °C), averages 17.1 °C more than its coolest, January (0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Malmö has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Malmö itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (55.606°N, 13.001°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-283-1930), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 362,133, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
