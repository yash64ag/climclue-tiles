Uddevalla at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Uddevalla is 33rd warmest (7.3 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 1,523 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (1,006 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Trollhättan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Uddevalla is about as warm in July and about as warm in February, with 132 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 17.9 °C more than its coolest, February (-1.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Uddevalla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.9 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Uddevalla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.348°N, 11.942°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-256-1919), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
