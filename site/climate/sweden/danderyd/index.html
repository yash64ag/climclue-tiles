Danderyd at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Danderyd is 63rd warmest (6.5 °C year average), 71st sunniest (about 1,485 hours of sunshine a year) and 89th wettest (587 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sollentuna, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Danderyd is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.3 °C), averages 19.8 °C more than its coolest, February (-2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Danderyd has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Danderyd itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (59.404°N, 18.024°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-245-1980), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
