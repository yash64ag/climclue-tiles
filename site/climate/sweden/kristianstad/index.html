Kristianstad at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Kristianstad is 19th warmest (7.9 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 1,547 hours of sunshine a year) and 54th wettest (667 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hässleholm, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Kristianstad is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in February, with 102 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 17.0 °C more than its coolest, February (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 13 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kristianstad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kristianstad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (56.031°N, 14.152°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-279-1941), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
