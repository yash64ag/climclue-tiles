Stockholm weather in July

Average July weather in Stockholm, Sweden: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In July, Stockholm averages 21.4 °C (71 °F) days and 13.0 °C (55 °F) nights, with 69 mm (2.7 in) of rain over the month and about 234 hours of sunshine.

How July compares in Stockholm

July is the warmest month, the wettest month and the 3rd sunniest of the 12 in Stockholm. Compared with June: 3.1 °C warmer, 12 mm more precipitation and 22 hours less sunshine. August is 0.9 °C cooler than July on average.

Stockholm in July: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Stockholm in July?
In July, Stockholm averages a day high of 21.4 °C (71 °F) and a night low of 13.0 °C (55 °F), with 69 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation and about 234 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is July a good time to visit Stockholm?
For mild weather, yes: July is one of Stockholm’s mild months (July and August), counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Stockholm in July?
69 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making July the wettest month of the year. The wettest month is July (69 mm), the driest February (30 mm).
Does it snow in Stockholm in July?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for July in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Stockholm get in July?
About 234 hours of bright sunshine, the 3rd sunniest of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Stockholm in July?
About 16.1 °C (61 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, cold for most swimmers; its warmest month is August (16.8 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Stockholm in July?
Going by the averages: light layers and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
