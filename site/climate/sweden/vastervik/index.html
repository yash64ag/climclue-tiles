Västervik at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Västervik is 26th warmest (7.5 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 1,553 hours of sunshine a year) and 64th wettest (634 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Oskarshamn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (56 km away), Västervik is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.5 °C), averages 18.3 °C more than its coolest, February (-0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 12 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Västervik has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Västervik itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (57.758°N, 16.637°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-262-1966), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
