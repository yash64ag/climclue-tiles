Mölndal at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Mölndal is 20th warmest (7.8 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 1,528 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (994 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Västra Frölunda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Mölndal is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C cooler in February, with 81 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.0 °C), averages 17.4 °C more than its coolest, February (-0.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Mölndal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mölndal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (57.655°N, 12.014°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-263-1920), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
