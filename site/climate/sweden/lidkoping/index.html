Lidköping weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Lidköping, Sweden (Västra Götaland), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Lidköping at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Lidköping is 31st warmest (7.3 °C year average), 42nd sunniest (about 1,504 hours of sunshine a year) and 55th wettest (662 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Skövde, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Lidköping is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in February, with 51 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.5 °C), averages 19.0 °C more than its coolest, February (-1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 110 km from the sea.

Why Lidköping has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lidköping itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.505°N, 13.158°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-254-1931), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
