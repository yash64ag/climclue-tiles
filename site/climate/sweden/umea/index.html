Umeå at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Umeå is the 5th least sunny (about 1,436 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Örnsköldsvik, the nearest covered town with different numbers (97 km away), Umeå is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 23 mm more precipitation a year. Why Umeå has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Umeå itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (63.828°N, 20.260°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 4.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-201-2002), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
