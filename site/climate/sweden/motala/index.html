Motala at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Motala is 46th warmest (6.7 °C year average), 48th sunniest (about 1,495 hours of sunshine a year) and 76th wettest (613 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Linköping, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Motala is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in February, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.0 °C), averages 19.3 °C more than its coolest, February (-2.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April and December. The area lies about 113 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 20 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 115% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Motala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 113 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.3 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Motala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.537°N, 15.036°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-254-1950), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
