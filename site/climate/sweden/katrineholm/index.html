Katrineholm at a glance

Among the 99 towns ClimaClue covers in Sweden, Katrineholm is 48th warmest (6.6 °C year average), 38th sunniest (about 1,512 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (620 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Eskilstuna, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Katrineholm is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.2 °C), averages 19.7 °C more than its coolest, February (-2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 64 km from the sea.

Why Katrineholm has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 64 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.7 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Katrineholm itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.996°N, 16.207°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-250-1962), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
