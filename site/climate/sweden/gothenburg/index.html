Gothenburg at a glance

Among the 11 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Sweden, Gothenburg is the 3rd warmest (7.7 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 1,529 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (931 mm a year). Compared with Jönköping, the nearest covered town with different numbers (130 km away), Gothenburg is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 1.7 °C warmer in February, with 212 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.9 °C), averages 17.3 °C more than its coolest, February (-0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Gothenburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
