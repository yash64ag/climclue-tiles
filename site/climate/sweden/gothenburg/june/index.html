Gothenburg weather in June

Average June weather in Gothenburg, Sweden: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In June, Gothenburg averages 18.9 °C (66 °F) days and 10.2 °C (50 °F) nights, with 79 mm (3.1 in) of rain over the month and about 245 hours of sunshine.

How June compares in Gothenburg

June is the 3rd warmest of the 12, the 5th driest of the 12 and the sunniest month in Gothenburg. Compared with May: 3.1 °C warmer, 21 mm more precipitation and 21 hours more sunshine. July is 2.3 °C warmer than June on average.

Gothenburg in June: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Gothenburg in June?
In June, Gothenburg averages a day high of 18.9 °C (66 °F) and a night low of 10.2 °C (50 °F), with 79 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation and about 245 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is June a good time to visit Gothenburg?
June is not one of Gothenburg’s mild months; those are July and August, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Gothenburg in June?
79 mm (3.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making June the 5th driest of the 12. The wettest month is October (105 mm), the driest April (51 mm).
Does it snow in Gothenburg in June?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for June in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Gothenburg get in June?
About 245 hours of bright sunshine, the sunniest month of the year (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Gothenburg in June?
About 14.5 °C (58 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, cold for most swimmers; its warmest month is August (17.8 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Gothenburg in June?
Going by the averages: light layers and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
