Katumba at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Katumba is 203rd warmest (19.0 °C year average), 89th sunniest (about 2,793 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (1,822 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tukuyu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Katumba is 0.3 °C warmer in November and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 220 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (21.2 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, July (15.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 633 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Katumba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 633 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Katumba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.233°S, 33.617°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-932-2136), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
