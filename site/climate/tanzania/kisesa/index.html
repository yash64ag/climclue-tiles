Kisesa, Mwanza at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Kisesa, Mwanza is 129th warmest (22.4 °C year average), 87th sunniest (about 2,796 hours of sunshine a year) and 74th wettest (1,115 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mwanza, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Kisesa, Mwanza is 0.5 °C cooler in September and 0.6 °C cooler in December, with 127 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (22.9 °C), averages 1.0 °C more than its coolest, December (22.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in July. The area lies about 738 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kisesa, Mwanza has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 738 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kisesa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.550°S, 33.033°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-865-2130), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
