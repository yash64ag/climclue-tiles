Igugunu at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Igugunu is 143rd warmest (21.9 °C year average), 45th sunniest (about 2,874 hours of sunshine a year) and 215th wettest (662 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sepuka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Igugunu is 0.6 °C warmer in November and 0.7 °C warmer in July, with 59 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (23.4 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, July (20.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.8 °C (57 °F), in July. The area lies about 494 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Igugunu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Igugunu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 494 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Igugunu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.567°S, 34.633°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-885-2146), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,810, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
