Kakunyu at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Kakunyu is the least sunny (about 2,055 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Mutukula, the nearest covered town with different numbers (53 km away), Kakunyu is 0.5 °C cooler in February and 1.0 °C cooler in November, with 376 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (20.6 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, November (19.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.7 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,018 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kakunyu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,018 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C. The 10 km area around Kakunyu, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 21.1 °C a year, 1.2 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kakunyu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.006°S, 30.941°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-850-2109), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
