Sumbawanga at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Sumbawanga is 218th warmest (17.6 °C year average), 151st sunniest (about 2,683 hours of sunshine a year) and 86th wettest (1,075 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Matai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (39 km away), Sumbawanga is 1.4 °C cooler in October and 1.3 °C cooler in July, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (19.8 °C), averages 4.2 °C more than its coolest, July (15.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.7 °C (48 °F), in July. The area lies about 822 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sumbawanga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sumbawanga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 822 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sumbawanga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.967°S, 31.617°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-919-2116), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
