Mvomero at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Mvomero is 13th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 157th sunniest (about 2,667 hours of sunshine a year) and 94th wettest (1,053 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bagamoyo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Mvomero is 0.2 °C cooler in February and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.5 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, July (24.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.9 °C (66 °F), in August. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mvomero, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mvomero has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mvomero itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.250°S, 38.667°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-902-2186), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
