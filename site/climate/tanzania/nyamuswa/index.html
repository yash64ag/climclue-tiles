Nyamuswa at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Nyamuswa is 147th warmest (21.6 °C year average), 79th sunniest (about 2,804 hours of sunshine a year) and 95th wettest (1,053 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Butiama, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Nyamuswa is 0.9 °C warmer in September and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 66 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (22.6 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, January (21.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.1 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 671 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nyamuswa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nyamuswa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 671 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nyamuswa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.900°S, 34.017°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-858-2140), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
