Katoro at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Katoro is 138th warmest (22.1 °C year average), 158th sunniest (about 2,667 hours of sunshine a year) and 53rd wettest (1,184 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Buseresere, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Katoro is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C cooler in December, with 92 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.2 °C), averages 2.0 °C more than its coolest, December (21.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.3 °C (61 °F), in July. The area lies about 835 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Katoro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Katoro

For mild weather, the best months to visit Katoro are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (23.2 °C), September (23.1 °C) and July (22.7 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (5 mm), June (9 mm) and August (21 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (263 h), August (263 h) and June (252 h).

Why Katoro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 835 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Katoro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.000°S, 31.933°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-869-2119), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
