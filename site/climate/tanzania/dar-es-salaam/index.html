Dar es Salaam at a glance

Among the 227 towns ClimaClue covers in Tanzania, Dar es Salaam is the 4th warmest (26.0 °C year average). Compared with Mwandege, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Dar es Salaam is about as warm in February and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 57 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.8 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, August (24.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.9 °C (66 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dar es Salaam, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Dar es Salaam

For mild weather, the best months to visit Dar es Salaam are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (27.8 °C), January (27.5 °C) and March (27.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): September (20 mm), July (22 mm) and August (28 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): October (261 h), November (255 h) and September (246 h).

Why Dar es Salaam has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

What is the coldest month in Dar es Salaam?
August, with an average of 24.2 °C (76 °F), day highs around 29.6 °C (85 °F) and night lows around 18.9 °C (66 °F).

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dar es Salaam itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.823°S, 39.270°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-908-2192), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 5,383,728, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
