Jaruco at a glance

Among the 125 towns ClimaClue covers in Cuba, Jaruco is the sunniest (about 2,953 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Santa Cruz del Norte, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Jaruco is 0.5 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 144 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.5 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, January (21.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.4 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Jaruco has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jaruco itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.044°N, 82.009°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-609-979), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
