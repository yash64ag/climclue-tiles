Santiago de Cuba at a glance

Among the 19 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Cuba, Santiago de Cuba is the least sunny (about 2,367 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,225 mm a year). Compared with Palma Soriano, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Santiago de Cuba is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 89 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.2 °C (68 °F), in February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santiago de Cuba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Santiago de Cuba

For mild weather, the best months to visit Santiago de Cuba are January, February, March and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (27.8 °C), August (27.6 °C) and June (27.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (29 mm), February (35 mm) and December (36 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (225 h), July (224 h) and March (220 h).

Why Santiago de Cuba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
