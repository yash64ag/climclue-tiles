Las Tunas at a glance

Among the 19 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Cuba, Las Tunas is 4th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,574 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (1,320 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manzanillo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (71 km away), Las Tunas is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 84 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, January (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.9 °C (68 °F), in January. The area lies about 53 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Las Tunas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Las Tunas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 53 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
