Calimete at a glance

Among the 125 towns ClimaClue covers in Cuba, Calimete is 79th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 2,768 hours of sunshine a year) and 68th wettest (1,362 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Aguada de Pasajeros, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Calimete is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.6 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, January (21.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.4 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 59 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Calimete, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Calimete has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 59 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Calimete itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.534°N, 80.911°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-614-990), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
