Camagüey at a glance

Among the 125 towns ClimaClue covers in Cuba, Camagüey is 64th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 43rd sunniest (about 2,723 hours of sunshine a year) and 63rd wettest (1,369 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jimaguayú, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Camagüey is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.4 °C), averages 4.6 °C more than its coolest, January (22.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.4 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 64 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Camagüey, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Camagüey

For mild weather, the best months to visit Camagüey are January, February, March, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, November and December.

Why Camagüey has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 64 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Camagüey itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.381°N, 77.917°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-626-1020), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
