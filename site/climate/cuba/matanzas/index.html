Why Matanzas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Matanzas climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Matanzas?
August, with an average of 27.4 °C (81 °F), day highs around 30.2 °C (86 °F) and night lows around 25.4 °C (78 °F).
What is the coldest month in Matanzas?
January, with an average of 21.9 °C (72 °F), day highs around 24.8 °C (77 °F) and night lows around 19.9 °C (68 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Matanzas?
June, with 196 mm (7.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,336 mm (52.6 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Matanzas?
January, with 50 mm (2.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Matanzas?
April, with about 274 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Matanzas?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Matanzas?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, November and December.
What type of climate does Matanzas have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Matanzas’s GeoNames location (23.044°N, 81.583°W; ClimaClue area sq-609-984), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 146,733 (GeoNames).
