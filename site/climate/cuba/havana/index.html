Havana at a glance

Among the 125 towns ClimaClue covers in Cuba, Havana is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,950 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Regla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Havana is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 5.5 °C more than its coolest, January (22.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.8 °C (66 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Havana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Havana

For mild weather, the best months to visit Havana are January, February, March, April, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (27.9 °C), July (27.8 °C) and September (27.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): March (49 mm), December (54 mm) and January (57 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): September (300 h), October (284 h) and April (273 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Havana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Havana climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Havana?
August, with an average of 27.9 °C (82 °F), day highs around 31.8 °C (89 °F) and night lows around 24.2 °C (76 °F).
What is the coldest month in Havana?
January, with an average of 22.5 °C (72 °F), day highs around 26.2 °C (79 °F) and night lows around 18.8 °C (66 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Havana?
June, with 225 mm (8.9 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,368 mm (53.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Havana?
March, with 49 mm (1.9 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Havana?
September, with about 300 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Havana?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).

How warm is the sea in Havana?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Havana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.133°N, 82.383°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-608-976), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,163,824, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
