Ciego de Ávila at a glance

Among the 125 towns ClimaClue covers in Cuba, Ciego de Ávila is 51st warmest (25.4 °C year average), 61st sunniest (about 2,677 hours of sunshine a year) and 50th wettest (1,404 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Venezuela, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Ciego de Ávila is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 124 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.8 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, January (22.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.9 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ciego de Ávila, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ciego de Ávila has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ciego de Ávila itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.842°N, 78.760°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-621-1012), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
