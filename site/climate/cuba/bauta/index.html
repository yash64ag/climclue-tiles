Bauta weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bauta, Cuba (Artemisa), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bauta is warmest in August, with 32.5 °C (91 °F) days and 22.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 27.1 °C (81 °F) days and 16.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (245 mm); March is driest (49 mm).

Bauta at a glance

Among the 125 towns ClimaClue covers in Cuba, Bauta is 76th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 2,892 hours of sunshine a year) and 46th wettest (1,428 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Antonio de los Baños, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Bauta is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, January (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.6 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bauta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bauta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bauta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.982°N, 82.548°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-610-974), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
