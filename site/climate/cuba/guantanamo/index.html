Guantánamo at a glance

Among the 125 towns ClimaClue covers in Cuba, Guantánamo is the 4th warmest (26.9 °C year average) and the 5th least sunny (about 2,382 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Río Guayabal de Yateras, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Guantánamo is 3.7 °C warmer in July and 3.6 °C warmer in January, with 1,121 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.1 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, January (24.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.0 °C (66 °F), in January. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guantánamo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Guantánamo

For mild weather, the best months to visit Guantánamo are January and February.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and February.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (29.1 °C), August (28.9 °C) and June (28.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (28 mm), February (36 mm) and December (38 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (227 h), April (224 h) and March (220 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Guantánamo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Guantánamo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.144°N, 75.209°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-638-1047), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 272,801, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
