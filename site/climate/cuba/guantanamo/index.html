Guantánamo at a glance

Among the 19 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Cuba, Guantánamo is the 2nd warmest (26.4 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,382 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (1,185 mm a year). Compared with Santiago de Cuba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (65 km away), Guantánamo is 0.9 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 40 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, January (24.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.3 °C (69 °F), in February. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guantánamo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Guantánamo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
