Kyzyl-Suu weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kyzyl-Suu, Kyrgyzstan (Issyk-Kul), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kyzyl-Suu is warmest in July, with 23.5 °C (74 °F) days and 10.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.6 °C (35 °F) days and -9.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around May (69 mm); January is driest (18 mm).

Kyzyl-Suu at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Kyrgyzstan, Kyzyl-Suu is the 3rd coolest (6.8 °C year average). Compared with Karakol, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Kyzyl-Suu is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 4.7 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.8 °C), averages 20.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,944 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kyzyl-Suu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,944 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.9 °C. The 10 km area around Kyzyl-Suu, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 5.7 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kyzyl-Suu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.342°N, 78.006°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-416-2580), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
