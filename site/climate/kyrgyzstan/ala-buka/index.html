Ala-Buka at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Kyrgyzstan, Ala-Buka is the 4th sunniest (about 2,641 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Tash-Kumyr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (61 km away), Ala-Buka is 5.9 °C cooler in July and 4.5 °C cooler in January, with 45 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.4 °C), averages 25.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,404 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ala-Buka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,404 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.9 °C. The 10 km area around Ala-Buka, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 7.8 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ala-Buka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.410°N, 71.486°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-425-2514), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
