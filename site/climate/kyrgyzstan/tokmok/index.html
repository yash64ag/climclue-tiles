Tokmok weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Tokmok, Kyrgyzstan (Chuy Region), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Tokmok is warmest in July, with 29.8 °C (86 °F) days and 16.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.0 °C (36 °F) days and -8.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around May (82 mm); August is driest (22 mm).

Tokmok at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Kyrgyzstan, Tokmok is the 3rd least sunny (about 2,536 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Ivanovka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Tokmok is 0.9 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.9 °C), averages 26.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,722 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Tokmok has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,722 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tokmok itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.842°N, 75.301°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-411-2553), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
