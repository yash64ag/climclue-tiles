Ivanovka at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Kyrgyzstan, Ivanovka is 18th warmest (11.2 °C year average), 26th sunniest (about 2,541 hours of sunshine a year) and 7th wettest (551 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tokmok, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Ivanovka is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.0 °C), averages 26.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,697 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ivanovka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,697 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ivanovka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.888°N, 75.085°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-411-2550), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
