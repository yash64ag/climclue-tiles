Naryn weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Naryn, Kyrgyzstan (Naryn Region), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Naryn is warmest in July, with 23.2 °C (74 °F) days and 9.4 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -7.0 °C (19 °F) days and -17.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (55 mm); January is driest (11 mm).

Naryn at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Kyrgyzstan, Naryn is the coolest (3.6 °C year average) and the 3rd driest (324 mm a year). Compared with Balykchy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (116 km away), Naryn is 2.3 °C cooler in July and 8.1 °C cooler in January, with 74 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.3 °C), averages 28.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,778 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Naryn measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 3.1 °C (5.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Naryn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,778 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Naryn itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.428°N, 75.996°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-425-2559), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
