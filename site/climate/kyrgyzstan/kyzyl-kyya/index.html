Kyzyl-Kyya weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kyzyl-Kyya, Kyrgyzstan (Batken), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kyzyl-Kyya is warmest in July, with 32.7 °C (91 °F) days and 17.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 3.4 °C (38 °F) days and -5.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around May (52 mm); September is driest (13 mm).

Kyzyl-Kyya at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Kyrgyzstan, Kyzyl-Kyya is 9th warmest (12.7 °C year average), 21st sunniest (about 2,546 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (393 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Iradan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Kyzyl-Kyya is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.2 °C), averages 26.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,477 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kyzyl-Kyya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,477 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kyzyl-Kyya itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.257°N, 72.128°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-437-2521), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
