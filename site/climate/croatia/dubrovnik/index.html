Dubrovnik at a glance

Among the 24 towns ClimaClue covers in Croatia, Dubrovnik is the warmest (16.2 °C year average), the 4th sunniest (about 2,246 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,670 mm a year). Compared with Solin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (165 km away), Dubrovnik is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 551 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.9 °C), averages 16.3 °C more than its coolest, January (8.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 4.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 16 km from Dubrovnik measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (yearly rain and snow: these numbers have 157% of the station’s). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Dubrovnik has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.3 °C. The 10 km area around Dubrovnik, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 14.5 °C a year, 1.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Dubrovnik climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Dubrovnik?
August, with an average of 24.9 °C (77 °F), day highs around 30.6 °C (87 °F) and night lows around 19.2 °C (67 °F).
What is the coldest month in Dubrovnik?
January, with an average of 8.5 °C (47 °F), day highs around 12.1 °C (54 °F) and night lows around 5.0 °C (41 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Dubrovnik?
December, with 239 mm (9.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,670 mm (65.7 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Dubrovnik?
July, with 41 mm (1.6 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Dubrovnik?
July, with about 309 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Dubrovnik?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 16 cm (6.4 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time to visit Dubrovnik?
For mild weather (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July and August. Climate only, from 1981–2010 averages: crowds and prices are not considered.
How warm is the sea in Dubrovnik?
The nearby open sea averages 24.1 °C (75 °F) in August, its warmest month, and 12.6 °C (55 °F) in February. It is 20 °C (68 °F) or warmer in June, July, August and September. 1981–2010 averages (ERA5); beaches and shallow bays are often a little warmer in summer.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dubrovnik itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.641°N, 18.109°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-413-1981), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
