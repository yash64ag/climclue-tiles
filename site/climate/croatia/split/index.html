Split at a glance

Among the 3 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Croatia, Split is the warmest (13.9 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,367 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (1,128 mm a year). Compared with Rijeka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (257 km away), Split is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 3.9 °C warmer in January, with 618 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.9 °C), averages 16.9 °C more than its coolest, January (6.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 1 km from Split measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.6 °C (6.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Split has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
