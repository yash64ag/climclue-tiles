Solin at a glance

Among the 24 towns ClimaClue covers in Croatia, Solin is the sunniest (about 2,367 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,119 mm a year). Compared with Split, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Solin is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 115 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.4 °C), averages 17.2 °C more than its coolest, January (7.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 6 km from Solin measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.1 °C (3.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Solin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.2 °C. The 10 km area around Solin, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.9 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Solin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.543°N, 16.493°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-404-1964), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
