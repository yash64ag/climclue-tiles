Rijeka at a glance

Among the 3 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Croatia, Rijeka is the 2nd warmest (13.3 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,104 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,571 mm a year). Compared with Zagreb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (131 km away), Rijeka is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 2.6 °C warmer in January, with 630 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.5 °C), averages 20.3 °C more than its coolest, January (3.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Rijeka measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.8 °C (5.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Rijeka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C. The 10 km area around Rijeka, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.0 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rijeka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.327°N, 14.442°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-386-1944), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
