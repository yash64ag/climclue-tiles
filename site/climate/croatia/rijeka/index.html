Rijeka at a glance

Among the 24 towns ClimaClue covers in Croatia, Rijeka is the 2nd wettest (1,571 mm a year). Compared with Pula, the nearest covered town with different numbers (69 km away), Rijeka is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 2.2 °C cooler in January, with 675 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.1 °C), averages 18.6 °C more than its coolest, January (5.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station under 1 km from Rijeka by up to 2.8 °C (5.1 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Rijeka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.6 °C. The 10 km area around Rijeka, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.4 °C a year, 1.9 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the coldest month in Rijeka?

Does it snow in Rijeka?
Yes. About 24 cm (9.6 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in March; it usually melts within days.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rijeka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.327°N, 14.442°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-386-1944), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
