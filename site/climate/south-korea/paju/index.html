Paju at a glance

Among the 59 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Korea, Paju is 48th warmest (11.9 °C year average), 38th sunniest (about 2,351 hours of sunshine a year) and 22nd wettest (1,427 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Goyang-si, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Paju is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 29.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 48 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Paju, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Paju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 48 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
