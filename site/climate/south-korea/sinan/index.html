Sinan at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Sinan is 39th warmest (14.0 °C year average), 124th sunniest (about 2,292 hours of sunshine a year) and 75th wettest (1,353 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mokpo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Sinan is 1.0 °C cooler in August and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.3 °C), averages 21.7 °C more than its coolest, January (3.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sinan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Sinan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sinan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.826°N, 126.109°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-491-3061), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 53,150, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
