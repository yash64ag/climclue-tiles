Yeoju at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Yeoju is 103rd warmest (12.4 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 2,469 hours of sunshine a year) and 96th wettest (1,302 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pubal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Yeoju is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.5 °C), averages 28.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 91 km from the sea.

Why Yeoju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 91 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yeoju itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.296°N, 127.634°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-467-3076), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
