Santyoku weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Santyoku, South Korea (Gangwon-do), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Santyoku is warmest in August, with 28.6 °C (83 °F) days and 20.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 5.6 °C (42 °F) days and -4.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (260 mm); December is driest (31 mm).

Santyoku at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Santyoku is 75th warmest (13.2 °C year average), 55th sunniest (about 2,404 hours of sunshine a year) and 94th wettest (1,304 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Donghae City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Santyoku is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 79 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.7 °C), averages 23.9 °C more than its coolest, January (0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santyoku, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santyoku has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santyoku itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.441°N, 129.171°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-465-3091), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
