Gangneung at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Gangneung is 98th warmest (12.6 °C year average), 110th sunniest (about 2,327 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (1,559 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jumunjin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Gangneung is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 10 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 25.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 21 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Gangneung has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gangneung itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.753°N, 128.872°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-462-3088), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
