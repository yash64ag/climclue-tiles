Jeongeup weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Jeongeup, South Korea (Jeollabuk-do), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Jeongeup is warmest in August, with 30.7 °C (87 °F) days and 21.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 4.7 °C (40 °F) days and -4.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (302 mm); December is driest (38 mm).

Jeongeup at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Jeongeup is 65th warmest (13.4 °C year average), 61st sunniest (about 2,397 hours of sunshine a year) and 72nd wettest (1,359 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Buan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Jeongeup is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 119 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 26.1 °C more than its coolest, January (0.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 42 km from the sea.

Why Jeongeup has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 42 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jeongeup itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.600°N, 126.917°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-483-3069), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
