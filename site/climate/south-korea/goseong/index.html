Goseong at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Goseong is 10th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 83rd sunniest (about 2,365 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (1,609 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tongyeong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Goseong is 0.9 °C warmer in August and 1.5 °C cooler in January, with 93 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.6 °C), averages 24.5 °C more than its coolest, January (2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Goseong, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Goseong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Goseong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.976°N, 128.324°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-490-3083), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 26,704, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
