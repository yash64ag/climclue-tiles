Namyangju weather by month and climate

Namyangju at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Namyangju is 138th warmest (11.7 °C year average), 87th sunniest (about 2,358 hours of sunshine a year) and 64th wettest (1,390 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wabu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Namyangju is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 51 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.2 °C), averages 28.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 70 km from the sea.

Why Namyangju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 70 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Namyangju itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.637°N, 127.214°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-463-3072), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
