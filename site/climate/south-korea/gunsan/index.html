Gunsan at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Gunsan is 48th warmest (13.6 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 2,443 hours of sunshine a year) and 97th wettest (1,296 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Iksan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Gunsan is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.8 °C warmer in January, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 25.4 °C more than its coolest, January (0.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 21 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gunsan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gunsan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gunsan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.979°N, 126.711°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-480-3067), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
