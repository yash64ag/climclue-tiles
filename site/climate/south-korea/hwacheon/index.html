Hwacheon weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Hwacheon, South Korea (Gangwon-do), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Hwacheon at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Hwacheon is the 4th coolest (11.2 °C year average). Compared with Yanggu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Hwacheon is 0.6 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 145 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 29.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 77 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hwacheon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hwacheon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 77 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.6 °C. The 10 km area around Hwacheon, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 9.7 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hwacheon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.107°N, 127.706°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-458-3077), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
