Gwangju at a glance

Among the 59 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Korea, Gwangju is the 5th least sunny (about 2,279 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th wettest (1,666 mm a year). Compared with Masan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Gwangju is 0.9 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 209 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.4 °C), averages 25.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 64 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gwangju, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gwangju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 64 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
