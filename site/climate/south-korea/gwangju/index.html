Gwangju at a glance

Among the 59 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Korea, Gwangju is the 5th least sunny (about 2,279 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Masan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Gwangju is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 130 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 25.8 °C more than its coolest, January (0.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 64 km from the sea.

Why Gwangju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 64 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gwangju itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.155°N, 126.916°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-488-3069), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
