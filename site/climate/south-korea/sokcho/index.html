Sokcho at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Sokcho is 110th warmest (12.3 °C year average), 133rd sunniest (about 2,275 hours of sunshine a year) and 62nd wettest (1,398 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kosong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Sokcho is about as warm in August and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 36 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.4 °C), averages 25.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sokcho, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sokcho has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sokcho itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.207°N, 128.592°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-457-3085), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
