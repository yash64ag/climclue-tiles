Jenzan weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Jenzan, South Korea (Gyeongsangbuk-do), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Jenzan is warmest in August, with 31.0 °C (88 °F) days and 21.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 4.9 °C (41 °F) days and -5.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (249 mm); December is driest (18 mm).

Jenzan at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Jenzan is 47th warmest (13.6 °C year average), 35th sunniest (about 2,443 hours of sunshine a year) and 142nd wettest (1,079 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gumi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Jenzan is 0.2 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 128 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.3 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 108 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jenzan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jenzan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 108 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jenzan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.241°N, 128.298°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-477-3082), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
