Gyeongju at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Gyeongju is 26th warmest (14.2 °C year average), 99th sunniest (about 2,350 hours of sunshine a year) and 137th wettest (1,098 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yeonil, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Gyeongju is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 27 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.4 °C), averages 25.3 °C more than its coolest, January (1.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gyeongju, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Gyeongju

For mild weather, the best months to visit Gyeongju are May and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (26.4 °C), July (25.7 °C) and June (22.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (18 mm), January (27 mm) and February (34 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (243 h), April (215 h) and March (208 h).

Why Gyeongju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gyeongju itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.843°N, 129.212°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-481-3092), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
