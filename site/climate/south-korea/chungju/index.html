Chungju at a glance

Among the 59 towns of 100,000+ people covered in South Korea, Chungju is 36th warmest (12.5 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,446 hours of sunshine a year) and 54th wettest (1,134 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wŏnju, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Chungju is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 209 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 28.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 116 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chungju, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chungju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 116 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chungju itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.977°N, 127.929°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-470-3079), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
