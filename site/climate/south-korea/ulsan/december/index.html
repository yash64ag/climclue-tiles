Ulsan weather in December

Average December weather in Ulsan, South Korea: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In December, Ulsan averages 10.2 °C (50 °F) days and -0.4 °C (31 °F) nights, with 20 mm (0.8 in) of rain and snow over the month and about 190 hours of sunshine.

How December compares in Ulsan

December is the 3rd coolest of the 12, the driest month and the 6th sunniest of the 12 in Ulsan. Compared with November: 5.8 °C cooler, 18 mm less precipitation and 14 hours more sunshine. January is 2.3 °C cooler than December on average.

Ulsan in December: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Ulsan in December?
In December, Ulsan averages a day high of 10.2 °C (50 °F) and a night low of -0.4 °C (31 °F), with 20 mm (0.8 in) of precipitation and about 190 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is December a good time to visit Ulsan?
December is not one of Ulsan’s mild months; those are May and October, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Ulsan in December?
20 mm (0.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making December the driest month of the year. The wettest month is August (225 mm), the driest December (20 mm).
Does it snow in Ulsan in December?
Yes, on average: ERA5-Land gives about 0.5 cm (0.2 in) of fresh snow in December (1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow).
How many hours of sunshine does Ulsan get in December?
About 190 hours of bright sunshine, the 6th sunniest of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Ulsan in December?
About 15.6 °C (60 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, cold for most swimmers; its warmest month is August (24.6 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Ulsan in December?
Going by the averages: a jacket or sweater, a hat and gloves and an extra layer for cooler evenings. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
