Gapyeong at a glance

Among the 147 towns ClimaClue covers in South Korea, Gapyeong is 137th warmest (11.8 °C year average), 103rd sunniest (about 2,337 hours of sunshine a year) and 81st wettest (1,343 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chuncheon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Gapyeong is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 133 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.1 °C), averages 29.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 104 km from the sea.

Why Gapyeong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 104 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gapyeong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.831°N, 127.511°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-461-3075), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
