Kenscoff at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Haiti, Kenscoff is the coolest (17.0 °C year average) and the wettest (2,053 mm a year). Compared with Pétionville, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Kenscoff is 7.7 °C cooler in July and 7.8 °C cooler in January, with 402 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.5 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, January (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kenscoff, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kenscoff

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kenscoff are January and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and December.

Why Kenscoff has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C. The 10 km area around Kenscoff, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.4 °C a year, 3.4 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kenscoff itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.448°N, 72.284°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-655-1077), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
