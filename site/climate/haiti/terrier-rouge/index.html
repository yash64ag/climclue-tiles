Terrier Rouge at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Haiti, Terrier Rouge is 25th warmest (26.0 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 2,625 hours of sunshine a year) and 9th wettest (1,317 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cap-Haïtien, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Terrier Rouge is 0.8 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 491 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, January (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.6 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Terrier Rouge, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Terrier Rouge has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Terrier Rouge itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.636°N, 71.955°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-643-1080), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
