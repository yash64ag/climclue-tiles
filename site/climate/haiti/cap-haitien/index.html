Cap-Haïtien at a glance

Among the 32 towns ClimaClue covers in Haiti, Cap-Haïtien is the 5th coolest (25.6 °C year average) and the 4th wettest (1,808 mm a year). Compared with Lenbe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Cap-Haïtien is 0.2 °C cooler in August and about as warm in February, with 70 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.2 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, February (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.9 °C (66 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cap-Haïtien, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cap-Haïtien has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cap-Haïtien itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.759°N, 72.198°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-642-1078), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
