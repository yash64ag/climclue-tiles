Dublin at a glance

Among the 3 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ireland, Dublin is the 2nd warmest (10.3 °C year average), the sunniest (about 1,392 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (944 mm a year). Compared with Limerick, the nearest covered town with different numbers (175 km away), Dublin is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 220 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.8 °C), averages 10.0 °C more than its coolest, January (5.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 17 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 122% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Dublin are July and August.

Why Dublin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
