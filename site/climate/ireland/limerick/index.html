Limerick at a glance

Among the 3 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Ireland, Limerick is the 3rd warmest (10.1 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 1,253 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (1,164 mm a year). Compared with Cork, the nearest covered town with different numbers (86 km away), Limerick is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 88 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.6 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, January (5.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 65 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Limerick, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Limerick has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 65 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
