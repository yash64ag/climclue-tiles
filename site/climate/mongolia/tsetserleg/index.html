Tsetserleg weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Tsetserleg, Mongolia (Arkhangai Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Tsetserleg is warmest in July, with 22.2 °C (72 °F) days and 10.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -12.0 °C (10 °F) days and -24.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (80 mm); February is driest (3 mm).

Tsetserleg at a glance

Among the 24 towns ClimaClue covers in Mongolia, Tsetserleg is the 3rd wettest (304 mm a year). Compared with Bayanhongor, the nearest covered town with different numbers (153 km away), Tsetserleg is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 89 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.1 °C), averages 34.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-18.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,618 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Tsetserleg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 1,618 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 34.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tsetserleg itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.475°N, 101.454°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-365-2814), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
