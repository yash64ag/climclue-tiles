Altai weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Altai, Mongolia (Govi-Altai Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Altai is warmest in July, with 21.9 °C (71 °F) days and 9.9 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -9.2 °C (15 °F) days and -23.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (52 mm); January is driest (2 mm).

Altai at a glance

Among the 24 towns ClimaClue covers in Mongolia, Altai is the 5th sunniest (about 3,011 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Uliastay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (159 km away), Altai is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 3.9 °C warmer in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.9 °C), averages 32.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-16.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,936 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Altai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,936 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Altai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.372°N, 96.258°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-376-2762), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
