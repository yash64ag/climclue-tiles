Ciudad Bolivia at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Ciudad Bolivia is 100th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 176th sunniest (about 2,333 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (1,943 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Socopó, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Ciudad Bolivia is about as warm in March and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 272 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.1 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, July (25.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.6 °C (69 °F), in January. The area lies about 129 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ciudad Bolivia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ciudad Bolivia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 129 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ciudad Bolivia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.353°N, 70.571°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-756-1094), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 52,476, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
