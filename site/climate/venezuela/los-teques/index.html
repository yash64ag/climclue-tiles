Los Teques at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Los Teques is 205th warmest (20.8 °C year average), 189th sunniest (about 2,252 hours of sunshine a year) and 68th wettest (1,509 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Carrizal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Los Teques is 0.8 °C warmer in April and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 90 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (21.8 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, January (19.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.8 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Los Teques, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Los Teques has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Los Teques itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.350°N, 67.043°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-736-1129), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 252,242, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
