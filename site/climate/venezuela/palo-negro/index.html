Palo Negro at a glance

Among the 67 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Venezuela, Palo Negro is 40th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 2,654 hours of sunshine a year) and 44th wettest (1,019 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cagua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Palo Negro is 0.5 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 44 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (26.3 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, January (23.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 50 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 15 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Palo Negro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 50 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
