Maracay at a glance

Among the 67 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Venezuela, Maracay is 49th warmest (24.9 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 2,687 hours of sunshine a year) and 40th wettest (1,065 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Limón, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Maracay is 0.8 °C warmer in May and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (25.9 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.8 °C (68 °F), in January. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 6 km from Maracay measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.7 °C (4.9 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Maracay has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Maracay itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.250°N, 67.595°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-737-1124), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
