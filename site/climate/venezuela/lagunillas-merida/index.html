Lagunillas, Mérida at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Lagunillas, Mérida is 183rd warmest (23.3 °C year average), 190th sunniest (about 2,234 hours of sunshine a year) and 118th wettest (1,208 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ejido, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Lagunillas, Mérida is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 8 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (24.0 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, January (22.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 68 km from the sea.

Why Lagunillas, Mérida has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 68 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C. The 10 km area around Lagunillas, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.1 °C a year, 3.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lagunillas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.505°N, 71.389°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-754-1086), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
