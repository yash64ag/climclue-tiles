Barcelona at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Barcelona is 72nd warmest (26.6 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,871 hours of sunshine a year) and 189th wettest (706 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lecherías, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Barcelona is 0.8 °C warmer in May and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 44 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.5 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, January (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.9 °C (68 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Barcelona, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Barcelona has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Barcelona itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.138°N, 64.688°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-738-1153), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
