San Antonio del Táchira at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, San Antonio del Táchira is 96th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 202nd sunniest (about 1,963 hours of sunshine a year) and 122nd wettest (1,168 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ureña, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), San Antonio del Táchira is 1.4 °C cooler in September and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 450 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (27.3 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, January (25.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.6 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 169 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Antonio del Táchira, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Antonio del Táchira has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 169 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Antonio del Táchira itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.811°N, 72.444°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-761-1075), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
