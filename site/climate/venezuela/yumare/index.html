Yumare at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Yumare is 101st warmest (26.1 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 2,862 hours of sunshine a year) and 137th wettest (1,117 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Felipe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Yumare is 0.7 °C warmer in September and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 239 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (27.1 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (24.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.2 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Yumare, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Yumare

For mild weather, the best months to visit Yumare are January and February.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and February.
  • Warmest (average temperature): September (27.1 °C), May (26.9 °C) and August (26.7 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): March (27 mm), January (39 mm) and February (40 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (270 h), March (266 h) and December (254 h).

Why Yumare has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yumare itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.597°N, 68.673°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-734-1113), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,661, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
