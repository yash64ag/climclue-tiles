Why Mérida has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 17.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Mérida climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Mérida?
May, with an average of 15.7 °C (60 °F), day highs around 18.6 °C (65 °F) and night lows around 12.0 °C (54 °F).
What is the coldest month in Mérida?
January, with an average of 14.2 °C (57 °F), day highs around 17.7 °C (64 °F) and night lows around 10.2 °C (50 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Mérida?
May, with 199 mm (7.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,503 mm (59.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Mérida?
January, with 33 mm (1.3 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Mérida?
January, with about 237 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Mérida?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Mérida?
No month meets the definition used here (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation).
What type of climate does Mérida have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Mérida’s GeoNames location (8.579°N, 71.169°W; ClimaClue area sq-754-1088), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 300,000 (GeoNames).
