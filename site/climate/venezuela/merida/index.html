Mérida at a glance

Among the 67 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Venezuela, Mérida is the coolest (15.0 °C year average) and the 3rd least sunny (about 2,062 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Ejido, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Mérida is 3.7 °C cooler in May and 3.6 °C cooler in January, with 179 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (15.7 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, January (14.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.2 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 74 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 3 km from Mérida measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 6.1 °C (11.0 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Mérida has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 17.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
