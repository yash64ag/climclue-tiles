Puerto Ayacucho at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Puerto Ayacucho is 99th warmest (26.1 °C year average), 187th sunniest (about 2,265 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (2,591 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Biruaca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (243 km away), Puerto Ayacucho is 1.5 °C cooler in February and 1.6 °C cooler in July, with 991 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.8 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, July (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.6 °C (69 °F), in July. The area lies about 545 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Puerto Ayacucho, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Puerto Ayacucho has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 545 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Puerto Ayacucho itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.660°N, 67.583°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-783-1124), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
