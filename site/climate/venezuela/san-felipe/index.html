San Felipe at a glance

Among the 67 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Venezuela, San Felipe is 54th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 2,705 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (1,398 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yaritagua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), San Felipe is 0.3 °C cooler in September and 2.7 °C cooler in January, with 421 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (24.9 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.1 °C (66 °F), in January. The area lies about 70 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Felipe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Felipe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 70 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 9.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
