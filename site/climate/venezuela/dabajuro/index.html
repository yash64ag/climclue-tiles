Dabajuro at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Dabajuro is the warmest (28.8 °C year average). Compared with Mene de Mauroa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Dabajuro is 1.7 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in November, with 203 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.4 °C), averages 2.9 °C more than its coolest, November (27.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.6 °C (74 °F), in October. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dabajuro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dabajuro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dabajuro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.023°N, 70.678°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-729-1093), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
