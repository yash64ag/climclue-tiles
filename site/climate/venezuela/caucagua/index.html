Caucagua at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Caucagua is 125th warmest (25.7 °C year average), 51st sunniest (about 2,703 hours of sunshine a year) and 88th wettest (1,352 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guatire, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Caucagua is 1.0 °C warmer in May and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 78 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (26.7 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.4 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Caucagua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Caucagua

For mild weather, the best months to visit Caucagua are January and February.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and February.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (26.7 °C), September (26.4 °C) and October (26.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): March (31 mm), February (54 mm) and April (61 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (253 h), January (246 h) and February (233 h).

Why Caucagua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Caucagua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.283°N, 66.376°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-737-1136), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
