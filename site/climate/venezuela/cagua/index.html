Cagua at a glance

Among the 67 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Venezuela, Cagua is 46th warmest (24.7 °C year average), 44th sunniest (about 2,532 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (1,063 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Turmero, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Cagua is 1.3 °C warmer in April and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (25.8 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, January (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.0 °C (66 °F), in January. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 22 km from Cagua measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.0 °C (3.5 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Cagua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
