Táriba at a glance

Among the 213 towns ClimaClue covers in Venezuela, Táriba is 183rd warmest (23.1 °C year average), 207th sunniest (about 1,914 hours of sunshine a year) and 60th wettest (1,599 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Palmira, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Táriba is 1.3 °C warmer in September and 1.5 °C warmer in January, with 60 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (23.6 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, January (22.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.0 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 159 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Táriba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Táriba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 159 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C. The 10 km area around Táriba, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.6 °C a year, 2.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Táriba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.821°N, 72.223°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-761-1077), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
