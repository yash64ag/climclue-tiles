San Cristóbal at a glance

Among the 67 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Venezuela, San Cristóbal is the 2nd least sunny (about 1,918 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Táriba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), San Cristóbal is 2.3 °C warmer in April and 2.5 °C warmer in January, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (23.1 °C), averages 0.9 °C more than its coolest, January (22.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 169 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Cristóbal, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Cristóbal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 169 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
