Cyangugu at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in Rwanda, Cyangugu is 17th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 2,042 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (1,083 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bushenge, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Cyangugu is 1.2 °C warmer in September and 1.1 °C warmer in November, with 353 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (20.9 °C), averages 0.9 °C more than its coolest, November (19.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.4 °C (56 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,168 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cyangugu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Cyangugu

For mild weather, the best months to visit Cyangugu are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.

Why Cyangugu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,168 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cyangugu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.485°S, 28.908°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-864-2089), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
