Cyato at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in Rwanda, Cyato is 32nd warmest (18.3 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 1,971 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,861 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rangiro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Cyato is 0.6 °C cooler in September and 0.6 °C cooler in July, with 130 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (18.7 °C), averages 0.9 °C more than its coolest, July (17.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.7 °C (55 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,151 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cyato, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cyato has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,151 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.9 °C. The 10 km area around Cyato, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.5 °C a year, 1.2 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cyato itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.387°S, 29.193°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-863-2091), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 26,996, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
