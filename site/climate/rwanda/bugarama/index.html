Bugarama at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in Rwanda, Bugarama is the warmest (24.1 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 2,077 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (715 mm a year). Compared with Karengera, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Bugarama is 4.1 °C warmer in September and 3.6 °C warmer in July, with 617 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (24.8 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, July (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.0 °C (61 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,150 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bugarama, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bugarama has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,150 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C. The 10 km area around Bugarama, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 22.6 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bugarama itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.692°S, 29.008°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-866-2090), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 31,086, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
