Kibuye at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in Rwanda, Kibuye is 8th warmest (20.7 °C year average), 30th sunniest (about 1,953 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (1,346 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gitesi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Kibuye is 2.3 °C warmer in March and 2.3 °C warmer in November, with 160 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (20.9 °C), averages 0.7 °C more than its coolest, November (20.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.5 °C (58 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,141 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kibuye, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kibuye has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,141 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kibuye itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.060°S, 29.348°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-860-2093), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
