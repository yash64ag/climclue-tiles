Kigali at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in Rwanda, Kigali is 15th warmest (20.5 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 1,990 hours of sunshine a year) and 30th wettest (1,069 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kamuhanda / Ruyenzi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Kigali is about as warm in August and about as warm in November, with 101 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.4 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, November (19.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.7 °C (57 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,072 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kigali, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kigali

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kigali are January, February, June, July, August, September and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, June, July, August, September and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (21.4 °C), September (21.1 °C) and February (20.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (12 mm), June (20 mm) and August (33 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (208 h), June (200 h) and August (178 h).

Why Kigali has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,072 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kigali itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.950°S, 30.059°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-859-2100), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,132,686, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
