Kigali at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Rwanda, Kigali is the 3rd warmest (20.1 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,990 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,044 mm a year). Compared with Musanze, the nearest covered town with different numbers (69 km away), Kigali is 4.5 °C warmer in August and 3.6 °C warmer in November, with 517 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.1 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, November (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.3 °C (58 °F), in December. The area lies about 1,072 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kigali, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kigali has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,072 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
