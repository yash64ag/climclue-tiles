Why Coquimbo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Coquimbo climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Coquimbo?
January, with an average of 18.6 °C (65 °F), day highs around 22.6 °C (73 °F) and night lows around 16.6 °C (62 °F).
What is the coldest month in Coquimbo?
July, with an average of 12.4 °C (54 °F), day highs around 16.5 °C (62 °F) and night lows around 10.5 °C (51 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Coquimbo?
July, with 37 mm (1.5 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 114 mm (4.5 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Coquimbo?
January, with 1 mm (0.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Coquimbo?
December, with about 337 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Coquimbo?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Coquimbo?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.6 × 11 km, that contains Coquimbo’s GeoNames location (29.953°S, 71.339°W; ClimaClue area sq-1139-1086), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 161,317 (GeoNames).
