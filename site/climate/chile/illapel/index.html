Illapel at a glance

Among the 139 towns ClimaClue covers in Chile, Illapel is 21st warmest (16.0 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,847 hours of sunshine a year) and 117th wettest (414 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Los Vilos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Illapel is 4.3 °C warmer in January and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.2 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, July (10.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.5 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 47 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Illapel, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Illapel has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 47 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Illapel itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.633°S, 71.170°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1156-1088), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
