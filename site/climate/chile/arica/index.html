Why Arica has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Arica climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Arica?
February, with an average of 23.6 °C (75 °F), day highs around 27.5 °C (82 °F) and night lows around 21.2 °C (70 °F).
What is the coldest month in Arica?
August, with an average of 14.7 °C (58 °F), day highs around 17.0 °C (63 °F) and night lows around 12.3 °C (54 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Arica?
January, with 4 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 28 mm (1.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Arica?
April, with 2 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Arica?
October, with about 247 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Arica?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Arica?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, May, June, September, October, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Arica’s GeoNames location (18.476°S, 70.301°W; ClimaClue area sq-1024-1096), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 241,653 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
