Arica at a glance

Among the 37 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Chile, Arica is the 2nd warmest (18.8 °C year average) and the 2nd driest (28 mm a year). Compared with Iquique, the nearest covered town with different numbers (194 km away), Arica is 1.5 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in August, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (23.7 °C), averages 9.0 °C more than its coolest, August (14.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Arica, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Arica has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Arica itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.476°S, 70.301°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1024-1096), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
