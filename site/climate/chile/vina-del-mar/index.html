Viña del Mar at a glance

Among the 37 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Chile, Viña del Mar is 24th warmest (13.7 °C year average), 21st sunniest (about 2,594 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (560 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Valparaíso, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Viña del Mar is 0.6 °C warmer in January and 0.5 °C cooler in July, with 95 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (17.1 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (10.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Viña del Mar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Viña del Mar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
