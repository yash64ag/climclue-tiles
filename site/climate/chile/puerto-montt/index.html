Puerto Montt at a glance

Among the 37 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Chile, Puerto Montt is the 2nd coolest (11.1 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,437 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (2,190 mm a year). Compared with Osorno, the nearest covered town with different numbers (101 km away), Puerto Montt is 1.1 °C cooler in January and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 965 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (14.8 °C), averages 7.3 °C more than its coolest, July (7.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.1 °C (41 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Puerto Montt, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Puerto Montt has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
