Copiapó at a glance

Among the 37 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Chile, Copiapó is the 2nd warmest (18.1 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,158 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (27 mm a year). Compared with La Serena, the nearest covered town with different numbers (296 km away), Copiapó is 2.6 °C warmer in January and 1.7 °C warmer in July, with 98 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.8 °C), averages 7.9 °C more than its coolest, July (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 68 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Copiapó, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Copiapó has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 68 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
