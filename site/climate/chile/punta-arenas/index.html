Punta Arenas at a glance

Among the 37 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Chile, Punta Arenas is the coolest (5.7 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 1,832 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Puerto Montt, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1,309 km away), Punta Arenas is 5.4 °C cooler in January and 5.9 °C cooler in July, with 1,621 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (9.4 °C), averages 7.8 °C more than its coolest, July (1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June and July. The area lies about 13 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Punta Arenas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Punta Arenas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Low sun and long winters keep it cold; even the warmest month stays below 10 °C, too cool for trees. Cold air holds little moisture, so precipitation is usually light. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
