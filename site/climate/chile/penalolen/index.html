Peñalolén at a glance

Among the 37 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Chile, Peñalolén is 18th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 2,555 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (622 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santiago, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Peñalolén is 0.6 °C cooler in January and 1.2 °C cooler in July, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.9 °C), averages 14.4 °C more than its coolest, July (7.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 111 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Peñalolén, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Peñalolén has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 111 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.4 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
