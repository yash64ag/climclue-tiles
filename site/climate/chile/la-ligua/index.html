La Ligua at a glance

Among the 139 towns ClimaClue covers in Chile, La Ligua is 30th warmest (15.6 °C year average), 35th sunniest (about 2,680 hours of sunshine a year) and 101st wettest (547 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cabildo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), La Ligua is 2.1 °C cooler in January and about as warm in July, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (20.1 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, July (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in July. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of La Ligua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why La Ligua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for La Ligua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.452°S, 71.231°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1164-1087), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
