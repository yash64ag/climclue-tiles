El Monte at a glance

Among the 139 towns ClimaClue covers in Chile, El Monte is 33rd warmest (15.5 °C year average), 117th sunniest (about 2,524 hours of sunshine a year) and 118th wettest (412 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Talagante, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), El Monte is 0.1 °C cooler in January and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.2 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, July (8.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 74 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of El Monte, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why El Monte has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El Monte itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.680°S, 70.985°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1176-1090), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 26,459, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
