Los Andes at a glance

Among the 139 towns ClimaClue covers in Chile, Los Andes is 14th warmest (16.3 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 2,722 hours of sunshine a year) and 121st wettest (392 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Esteban, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Los Andes is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.9 °C), averages 13.8 °C more than its coolest, July (9.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 96 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Los Andes, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Los Andes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 96 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.8 °C. The 10 km area around Los Andes, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 14.9 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Los Andes itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.834°S, 70.598°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1168-1094), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
