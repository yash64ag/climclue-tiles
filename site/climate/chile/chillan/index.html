Chillán at a glance

Among the 37 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Chile, Chillán is 26th warmest (13.5 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,689 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,416 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Concepción, the nearest covered town with different numbers (88 km away), Chillán is 1.8 °C warmer in January and 0.7 °C cooler in July, with 243 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (19.4 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, July (8.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 4.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 79 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chillán, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chillán has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 79 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
