San Miguel at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in Costa Rica, San Miguel is 27th warmest (20.1 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 2,342 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (3,060 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Rafael Arriba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), San Miguel is 0.3 °C cooler in April and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 34 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (20.9 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, January (19.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.3 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 60 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Miguel, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit San Miguel

For mild weather, the best months to visit San Miguel are January, February and March.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and March.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (20.9 °C), May (20.7 °C) and June (20.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (64 mm), March (64 mm) and January (86 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (220 h), April (210 h) and January (205 h).

Why San Miguel has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 60 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C. The 10 km area around San Miguel, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.6 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Miguel itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.871°N, 84.061°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-741-959), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 28,827, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
