Purral at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in Costa Rica, Purral is the 4th coolest (19.6 °C year average). Compared with San Vicente de Moravia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Purral is 0.4 °C cooler in April and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 57 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (20.4 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, January (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.6 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 70 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Purral, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Purral has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 70 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Purral itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.957°N, 84.032°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-740-959), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 30,034, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
