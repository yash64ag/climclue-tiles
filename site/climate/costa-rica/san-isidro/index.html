San Isidro at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in Costa Rica, San Isidro is the wettest (5,199 mm a year). Compared with Paraíso, the nearest covered town with different numbers (56 km away), San Isidro is 2.9 °C warmer in April and 3.4 °C warmer in January, with 1,963 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (23.1 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, January (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Isidro, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Isidro has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Isidro itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.367°N, 83.697°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-746-963), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
