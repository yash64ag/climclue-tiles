Nicoya at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in Costa Rica, Nicoya is the 3rd warmest (26.3 °C year average) and the 3rd driest (2,195 mm a year). Compared with Cañas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Nicoya is 0.6 °C warmer in April and 0.2 °C cooler in October, with 170 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.6 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, October (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in November. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nicoya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nicoya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nicoya itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.150°N, 85.451°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-738-945), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
