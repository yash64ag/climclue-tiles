San Rafael Abajo at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in Costa Rica, San Rafael Abajo is the sunniest (about 2,426 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Tejar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), San Rafael Abajo is 1.3 °C warmer in April and 1.0 °C warmer in October, with 598 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (22.7 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, October (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in October. The area lies about 50 km from the sea.

Why San Rafael Abajo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 50 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Rafael Abajo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.831°N, 84.290°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-741-957), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
