Chacarita at a glance

Among the 36 towns ClimaClue covers in Costa Rica, Chacarita is the 2nd warmest (26.5 °C year average), the 4th sunniest (about 2,389 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (2,572 mm a year). Compared with Puntarenas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Chacarita is about as warm in March and about as warm in October, with 198 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.6 °C), averages 1.9 °C more than its coolest, October (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in October. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chacarita, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chacarita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chacarita itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.984°N, 84.779°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-740-952), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
