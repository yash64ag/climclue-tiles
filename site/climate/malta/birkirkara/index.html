Birkirkara at a glance

Among the 7 towns ClimaClue covers in Malta, Birkirkara is the 2nd coolest (18.9 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,880 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (577 mm a year). Compared with Qormi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Birkirkara is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, February (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.2 °C (50 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Birkirkara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Birkirkara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Birkirkara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.897°N, 14.461°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-481-1944), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
