Managua at a glance

Among the 5 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nicaragua, Managua is the 2nd warmest (27.9 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,648 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (1,367 mm a year). Compared with Masaya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Managua is 2.1 °C warmer in April and 1.3 °C warmer in October, with 120 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.9 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, October (26.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.5 °C (73 °F), in January. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Managua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Managua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
