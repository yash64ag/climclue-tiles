Masaya at a glance

Among the 5 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nicaragua, Masaya is the 2nd coolest (26.2 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 2,666 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,487 mm a year). Compared with Managua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Masaya is 2.1 °C cooler in April and 1.8 °C cooler in January, with 120 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.9 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, January (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.6 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Masaya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Masaya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
