San Rafael del Sur at a glance

Among the 37 towns ClimaClue covers in Nicaragua, San Rafael del Sur is 10th warmest (27.7 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 2,659 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (1,622 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Crucero, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), San Rafael del Sur is 5.3 °C warmer in April and 4.3 °C warmer in October, with 403 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.6 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, October (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.9 °C (71 °F), in October. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Why San Rafael del Sur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Rafael del Sur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.849°N, 86.438°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-721-935), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
