Zomba at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in Malawi, Zomba is the 4th least sunny (about 2,642 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Liwonde, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Zomba is 3.7 °C cooler in November and 3.2 °C cooler in July, with 215 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (23.7 °C), averages 7.2 °C more than its coolest, July (16.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.1 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 342 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Zomba, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Zomba

For mild weather, the best months to visit Zomba are April, May, June, July, August, September, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April, May, June, July, August, September, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): November (23.7 °C), October (22.8 °C) and December (22.8 °C).

Why Zomba has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 342 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C. The 10 km area around Zomba, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.4 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zomba itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.386°S, 35.319°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-993-2153), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 118,440, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
