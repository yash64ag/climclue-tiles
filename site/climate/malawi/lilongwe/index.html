Lilongwe at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malawi, Lilongwe is the warmest (20.7 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,754 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (810 mm a year). Compared with Zomba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (228 km away), Lilongwe is 1.7 °C warmer in November and 1.4 °C warmer in July, with 407 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (24.1 °C), averages 7.4 °C more than its coolest, July (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.9 °C (52 °F), in July. The area lies about 572 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lilongwe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lilongwe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 572 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
