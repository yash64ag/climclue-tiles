Why Lilongwe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 572 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Lilongwe climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Lilongwe?
November, with an average of 24.1 °C (75 °F), day highs around 29.4 °C (85 °F) and night lows around 18.8 °C (66 °F).
What is the coldest month in Lilongwe?
July, with an average of 16.6 °C (62 °F), day highs around 22.5 °C (73 °F) and night lows around 10.9 °C (52 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Lilongwe?
January, with 224 mm (8.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 810 mm (31.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Lilongwe?
June, with 1 mm (0.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Lilongwe?
October, with about 291 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Lilongwe?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Lilongwe?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: April, May, June, July, August, September, October and November.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Lilongwe’s GeoNames location (13.967°S, 33.787°E; ClimaClue area sq-979-2137), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 1,115,815 (GeoNames).
