Nkhotakota at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in Malawi, Nkhotakota is the 5th warmest (24.4 °C year average), the 4th sunniest (about 2,942 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,307 mm a year). Compared with Kasungu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (89 km away), Nkhotakota is 2.2 °C warmer in November and 3.5 °C warmer in July, with 484 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (26.9 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, July (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in July. The area lies about 629 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nkhotakota, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Nkhotakota

For mild weather, the best months to visit Nkhotakota are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): November (26.9 °C), October (26.4 °C) and December (25.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): September (2 mm), August (3 mm) and June (6 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): October (305 h), September (295 h) and August (291 h).

Why Nkhotakota has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 629 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nkhotakota itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.927°S, 34.296°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-969-2142), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 31,974, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
