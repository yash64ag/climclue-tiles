Kasungu at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in Malawi, Kasungu is the 2nd driest (823 mm a year). Compared with Mponela, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Kasungu is 1.6 °C warmer in November and 1.4 °C warmer in July, with 100 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (24.7 °C), averages 7.2 °C more than its coolest, July (17.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.3 °C (51 °F), in July. The area lies about 669 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kasungu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kasungu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 669 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kasungu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.033°S, 33.483°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-970-2134), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
