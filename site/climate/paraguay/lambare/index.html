Lambaré at a glance

Among the 30 towns ClimaClue covers in Paraguay, Lambaré is 13th warmest (22.4 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 2,744 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (1,448 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Villa Elisa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Lambaré is about as warm in January and about as warm in July, with 29 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (27.3 °C), averages 10.3 °C more than its coolest, July (16.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 854 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lambaré, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Lambaré

For mild weather, the best months to visit Lambaré are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): January (27.3 °C), February (26.5 °C) and December (26.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (50 mm), July (52 mm) and June (69 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (287 h), January (271 h) and November (268 h).

Why Lambaré has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 854 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lambaré itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.347°S, 57.606°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1093-1223), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
