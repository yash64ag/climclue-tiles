Concepción at a glance

Among the 30 towns ClimaClue covers in Paraguay, Concepción is the 3rd warmest (23.9 °C year average), the 5th sunniest (about 2,763 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (1,386 mm a year). Compared with Yby Yaú, the nearest covered town with different numbers (103 km away), Concepción is 0.7 °C warmer in January and 0.4 °C cooler in July, with 281 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (27.9 °C), averages 9.2 °C more than its coolest, July (18.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.9 °C (55 °F), in July. The area lies about 948 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Concepción, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Concepción has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 948 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Concepción itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.400°S, 57.432°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1073-1225), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
