Leiden at a glance

Among the 25 towns of 100,000+ people covered in The Netherlands, Leiden is the 4th sunniest (about 1,502 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (943 mm a year). Compared with Zoetermeer, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Leiden is about as warm in August and about as warm in February, with 27 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (17.6 °C), averages 13.8 °C more than its coolest, February (3.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Leiden, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Leiden has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

What is the coldest month in Leiden?
February, with an average of 3.8 °C (39 °F), day highs around 6.0 °C (43 °F) and night lows around 1.7 °C (35 °F).

Does it snow in Leiden?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 19 cm (7.4 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in January; it usually melts within days.
