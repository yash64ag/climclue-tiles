Hoofddorp at a glance

Among the 25 towns of 100,000+ people covered in The Netherlands, Hoofddorp is the sunniest (about 1,511 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Haarlem, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Hoofddorp is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in February, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (17.4 °C), averages 13.7 °C more than its coolest, February (3.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 112% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Hoofddorp has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
