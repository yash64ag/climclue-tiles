Dordrecht at a glance

Among the 25 towns of 100,000+ people covered in The Netherlands, Dordrecht is 13th warmest (10.3 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 1,458 hours of sunshine a year) and 10th wettest (911 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rotterdam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Dordrecht is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.9 °C), averages 14.6 °C more than its coolest, January (3.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 47 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 23 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Dordrecht has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 47 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
