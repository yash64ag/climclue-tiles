Spijkenisse at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in The Netherlands, Spijkenisse is 39th warmest (10.4 °C year average), 69th sunniest (about 1,477 hours of sunshine a year) and 84th wettest (906 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hoogvliet, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Spijkenisse is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.9 °C), averages 14.3 °C more than its coolest, January (3.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 30 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 15 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 106% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Spijkenisse has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 30 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Spijkenisse itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (51.845°N, 4.329°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-321-1843), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
