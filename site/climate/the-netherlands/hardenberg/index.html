Hardenberg at a glance

Among the 234 towns ClimaClue covers in The Netherlands, Hardenberg is 218th warmest (9.8 °C year average), 192nd sunniest (about 1,369 hours of sunshine a year) and 151st wettest (874 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hoogeveen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Hardenberg is 0.2 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.8 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, January (2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in February. The area lies about 102 km from the sea.

Why Hardenberg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 102 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hardenberg itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (52.576°N, 6.619°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-314-1866), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
