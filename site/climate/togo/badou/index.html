Badou at a glance

Among the 20 towns ClimaClue covers in Togo, Badou is the 3rd coolest (25.7 °C year average) and the wettest (1,663 mm a year). Compared with Atakpamé, the nearest covered town with different numbers (59 km away), Badou is about as warm in February and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 225 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.8 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, August (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.1 °C (68 °F), in December. The area lies about 179 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Badou, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Badou

No month in Badou is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are June (24.9 °C, 220 mm), July (24.0 °C, 221 mm) and August (23.9 °C, 228 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (27.8 °C), March (27.4 °C) and April (26.8 °C).

Why Badou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 179 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C. The 10 km area around Badou, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 24.1 °C a year, 1.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Badou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.583°N, 0.600°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-764-1806), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 24,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
