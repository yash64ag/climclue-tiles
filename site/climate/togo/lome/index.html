Lomé at a glance

Among the 3 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Togo, Lomé is the 2nd warmest (26.6 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 2,330 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (884 mm a year). Compared with Sokodé, the nearest covered town with different numbers (318 km away), Lomé is 0.7 °C cooler in March and 0.4 °C warmer in August, with 383 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.1 °C), averages 3.7 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.1 °C (74 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lomé, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lomé has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
