Pérgamos at a glance

Among the 16 towns ClimaClue covers in Cyprus, Pérgamos is the 3rd sunniest (about 3,172 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Aradíppou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Pérgamos is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.7 °C), averages 16.9 °C more than its coolest, January (11.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in February. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 120% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Pérgamos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pérgamos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.042°N, 33.708°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-489-2137), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
