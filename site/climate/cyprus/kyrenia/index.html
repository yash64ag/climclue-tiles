Kyrenia at a glance

Among the 16 towns ClimaClue covers in Cyprus, Kyrenia is 8th warmest (20.4 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 3,141 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (450 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nicosia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Kyrenia is 0.9 °C cooler in August and 1.4 °C warmer in January, with 65 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.0 °C), averages 16.6 °C more than its coolest, January (12.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Kyrenia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kyrenia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.336°N, 33.317°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-486-2133), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
