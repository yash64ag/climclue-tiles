Limassol weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Limassol, Cyprus, from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Limassol is warmest in August, with 34.3 °C (94 °F) days and 22.8 °C nights, and coolest in February, with 17.7 °C (64 °F) days and 8.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (98 mm); August is driest (1 mm).

Limassol at a glance

Among the 16 towns ClimaClue covers in Cyprus, Limassol is the 3rd warmest (20.7 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 3,089 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Káto Polemídia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Limassol is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 27 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.6 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, February (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.9 °C (48 °F), in February. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Limassol has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Limassol itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.684°N, 33.038°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-493-2130), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
