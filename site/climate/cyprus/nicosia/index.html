Nicosia at a glance

Among the 16 towns ClimaClue covers in Cyprus, Nicosia is 13th warmest (19.8 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 3,138 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (385 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Stróvolos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Nicosia is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.6 °C), averages 19.1 °C more than its coolest, January (10.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Nicosia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

What is the coldest month in Nicosia?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nicosia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.173°N, 33.354°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-488-2133), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 200,452, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
