Kuala Lumpur at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Kuala Lumpur is 50th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 49th sunniest (about 2,214 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (2,938 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bukit Bintang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Kuala Lumpur is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 90 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.1 °C), averages 1.2 °C more than its coolest, December (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.7 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kuala Lumpur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

No month in Kuala Lumpur is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are January (26.1 °C, 194 mm), November (26.1 °C, 353 mm) and December (25.9 °C, 269 mm).

Why Kuala Lumpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
