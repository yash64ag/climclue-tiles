Miri at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Miri is 49th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 2,293 hours of sunshine a year) and 9th wettest (3,027 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bintulu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (173 km away), Miri is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 854 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.3 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, January (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.5 °C (76 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Miri, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Miri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
