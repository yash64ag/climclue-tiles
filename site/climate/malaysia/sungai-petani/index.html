Sungai Petani at a glance

Among the 352 towns ClimaClue covers in Malaysia, Sungai Petani is the 2nd warmest (27.7 °C year average). Compared with Sungai Lalang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Sungai Petani is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.3 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, December (26.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.9 °C (73 °F), in December. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Sungai Petani has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sungai Petani itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.647°N, 100.488°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-783-2804), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
