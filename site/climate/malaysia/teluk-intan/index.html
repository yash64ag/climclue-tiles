Why Teluk Intan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Teluk Intan climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Teluk Intan?
April, with an average of 27.9 °C (82 °F), day highs around 31.0 °C (88 °F) and night lows around 24.0 °C (75 °F).
What is the coldest month in Teluk Intan?
December, with an average of 26.6 °C (80 °F), day highs around 29.1 °C (84 °F) and night lows around 22.8 °C (73 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Teluk Intan?
November, with 264 mm (10.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 2,144 mm (84.4 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Teluk Intan?
June, with 96 mm (3.8 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Teluk Intan?
March, with about 221 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Teluk Intan?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Teluk Intan?
No month meets the definition used here (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation).

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Teluk Intan’s GeoNames location (4.022°N, 101.021°E; ClimaClue area sq-799-2810), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 232,800 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
