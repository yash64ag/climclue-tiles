Bintulu at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Bintulu is the 2nd wettest (3,880 mm a year). Compared with Sibu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (166 km away), Bintulu is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 210 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.1 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, January (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.0 °C (73 °F), in January. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 98% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Bintulu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
