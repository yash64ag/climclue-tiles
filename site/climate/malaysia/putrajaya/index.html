Putrajaya at a glance

Among the 352 towns ClimaClue covers in Malaysia, Putrajaya is 175th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 171st sunniest (about 2,238 hours of sunshine a year) and 71st wettest (2,947 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cyberjaya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Putrajaya is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 161 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.4 °C), averages 1.2 °C more than its coolest, December (26.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Putrajaya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Putrajaya

No month in Putrajaya is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are October (26.5 °C, 305 mm), November (26.3 °C, 360 mm) and December (26.2 °C, 277 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (27.4 °C), April (27.3 °C) and June (27.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): June (160 mm), July (183 mm) and August (193 mm).

Why Putrajaya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Putrajaya itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.935°N, 101.691°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-810-2816), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 50,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
