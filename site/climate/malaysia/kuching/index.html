Kuching at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Kuching is the least sunny (about 1,865 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (4,645 mm a year). Compared with Sibu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (185 km away), Kuching is 0.2 °C warmer in May and about as warm in January, with 1,021 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.1 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, January (25.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.2 °C (74 °F), in January. The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kuching has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kuching itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.550°N, 110.333°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-824-2903), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
