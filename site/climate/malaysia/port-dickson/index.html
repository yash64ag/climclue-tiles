Port Dickson at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Port Dickson is 24th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 29th sunniest (about 2,258 hours of sunshine a year) and 67th wettest (2,428 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sepang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Port Dickson is 0.2 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 200 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.8 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, December (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.4 °C (76 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Port Dickson, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Port Dickson has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Port Dickson itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.525°N, 101.797°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-814-2817), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
