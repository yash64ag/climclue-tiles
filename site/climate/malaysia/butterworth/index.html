Butterworth at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Butterworth is the 3rd warmest (27.6 °C year average). Compared with George Town, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Butterworth is about as warm in June and about as warm in November, with 98 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.2 °C), averages 1.2 °C more than its coolest, November (26.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.0 °C (75 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Butterworth, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Butterworth has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Butterworth itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.399°N, 100.364°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-786-2803), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
