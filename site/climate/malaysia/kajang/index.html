Kajang at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Kajang is 48th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 38th sunniest (about 2,229 hours of sunshine a year) and 23rd wettest (2,907 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bandar Mahkota Cheras, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Kajang is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C warmer in December, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.3 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, December (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.8 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 46 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kajang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kajang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 46 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
