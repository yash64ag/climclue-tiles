Ipoh at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Ipoh is 55th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 2,345 hours of sunshine a year) and 58th wettest (2,557 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bercham, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Ipoh is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 198 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.3 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, December (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.4 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 55 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ipoh, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ipoh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 55 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
