Shah Alam at a glance

Among the 82 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Malaysia, Shah Alam is 12th warmest (27.2 °C year average), 40th sunniest (about 2,222 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (2,771 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kampung Baru Subang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Shah Alam is 0.3 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 82 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.9 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, December (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.3 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shah Alam, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shah Alam has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
