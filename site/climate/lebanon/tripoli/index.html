Tripoli at a glance

Among the 5 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Lebanon, Tripoli is the warmest (20.0 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,952 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (1,121 mm a year). Compared with Beirut, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Tripoli is 0.8 °C warmer in August and 1.6 °C warmer in January, with 41 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.1 °C), averages 14.3 °C more than its coolest, January (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.7 °C (49 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tripoli, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tripoli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
