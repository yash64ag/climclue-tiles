Beirut at a glance

Among the 5 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Lebanon, Beirut is the 3rd warmest (19.0 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,942 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,081 mm a year). Compared with Aley, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Beirut is 2.3 °C warmer in August and 4.0 °C warmer in January, with 49 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.3 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.1 °C (45 °F), in February. The area lies about 18 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Beirut, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Beirut has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
