Szczecinek at a glance

Among the 345 towns ClimaClue covers in Poland, Szczecinek is 303rd warmest (7.9 °C year average), 243rd sunniest (about 1,518 hours of sunshine a year) and 70th wettest (728 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Złotów, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Szczecinek is 0.5 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 101 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.4 °C), averages 18.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 74 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Szczecinek, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Szczecinek

For mild weather, the best months to visit Szczecinek are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (17.4 °C), August (17.0 °C) and June (15.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (38 mm), April (44 mm) and March (53 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (226 h), June (222 h) and July (213 h).

Why Szczecinek has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Szczecinek itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.708°N, 16.699°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-302-1966), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
