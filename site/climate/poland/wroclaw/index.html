Wrocław at a glance

Among the 47 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Poland, Wrocław is the warmest (9.4 °C year average) and the 3rd least sunny (about 1,477 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Legnica, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Wrocław is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 45 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 19.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 339 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Wrocław, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.

Why Wrocław has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 339 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
