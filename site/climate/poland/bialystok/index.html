Białystok at a glance

Among the 345 towns ClimaClue covers in Poland, Białystok is 326th warmest (7.6 °C year average), 94th sunniest (about 1,587 hours of sunshine a year) and 187th wettest (599 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Łapy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Białystok is about as warm in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.4 °C), averages 21.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 246 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Białystok, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Białystok

For mild weather, the best months to visit Białystok are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (18.4 °C), August (17.5 °C) and June (16.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (28 mm), January (35 mm) and April (35 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (231 h), June (227 h) and August (220 h).

Why Białystok has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 246 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Białystok itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.133°N, 23.164°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-308-2031), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 295,683, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
