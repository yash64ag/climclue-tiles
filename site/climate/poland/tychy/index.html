Tychy at a glance

Among the 47 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Poland, Tychy is the 2nd wettest (820 mm a year). Compared with Katowice, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Tychy is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 44 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.8 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 478 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tychy, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tychy has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 478 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tychy itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.137°N, 18.966°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-338-1989), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
