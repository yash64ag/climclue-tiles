Łódź weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Łódź, Poland (Łódź Voivodeship), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Łódź is warmest in July, with 23.7 °C (75 °F) days and 13.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 0.9 °C (34 °F) days and -4.6 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 33 mm in February to 89 mm in July.

Łódź at a glance

Among the 345 towns ClimaClue covers in Poland, Łódź is 272nd warmest (8.3 °C year average), 268th sunniest (about 1,506 hours of sunshine a year) and 151st wettest (618 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Konstantynów Łódzki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Łódź is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.4 °C), averages 20.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 300 km from the sea.

Why Łódź has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 300 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Łódź itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (51.771°N, 19.474°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-322-1994), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 639,890, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
