Wałbrzych at a glance

Among the 47 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Poland, Wałbrzych is the coolest (6.8 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,472 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (813 mm a year). Compared with Legnica, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Wałbrzych is 2.9 °C cooler in July and 2.4 °C cooler in January, with 197 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.4 °C), averages 19.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 355 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Wałbrzych, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Wałbrzych has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 355 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
