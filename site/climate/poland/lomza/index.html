Łomża at a glance

Among the 345 towns ClimaClue covers in Poland, Łomża is 309th warmest (7.8 °C year average), 85th sunniest (about 1,588 hours of sunshine a year) and 203rd wettest (590 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zambrów, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Łomża is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.5 °C), averages 21.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 213 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Łomża, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Łomża has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 213 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Łomża itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.178°N, 22.059°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-308-2020), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
