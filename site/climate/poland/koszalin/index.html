Koszalin at a glance

Among the 47 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Poland, Koszalin is 38th warmest (8.5 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 1,535 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (763 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Szczecin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (136 km away), Koszalin is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 172 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.6 °C), averages 17.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Koszalin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Koszalin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
