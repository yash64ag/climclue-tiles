Koszalin at a glance

Among the 47 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Poland, Koszalin is 36th warmest (8.5 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 1,535 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (755 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Szczecin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (136 km away), Koszalin is 0.9 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 190 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.7 °C), averages 17.7 °C more than its coolest, January (0.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Koszalin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Koszalin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Koszalin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.194°N, 16.172°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-298-1961), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
