Malbork at a glance

Among the 345 towns ClimaClue covers in Poland, Malbork is 262nd warmest (8.3 °C year average), 164th sunniest (about 1,547 hours of sunshine a year) and 156th wettest (615 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tczew, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Malbork is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 53 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.2 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Why Malbork has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Malbork itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.036°N, 19.027°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-299-1990), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
