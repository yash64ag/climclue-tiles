Gdynia at a glance

Among the 47 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Poland, Gdynia is 42nd warmest (8.2 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 1,558 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (605 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gdańsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Gdynia is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 37 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.6 °C), averages 17.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 6 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gdynia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gdynia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
