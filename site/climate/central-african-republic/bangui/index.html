Bangui at a glance

Among the 5 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Central African Republic, Bangui is the warmest (26.1 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,162 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,343 mm a year). Compared with Bégoua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Bangui is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in August, with 143 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.9 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, August (24.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in August. The area lies about 974 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bangui, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bangui has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 974 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
