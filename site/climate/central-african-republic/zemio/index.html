Zemio at a glance

Among the 33 towns ClimaClue covers in the Central African Republic, Zemio is 19th warmest (25.4 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 2,437 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (1,477 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bangassou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (259 km away), Zemio is 1.0 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.5 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, July (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.5 °C (64 °F), in December. The area lies about 1,710 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Zemio, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Zemio has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,710 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zemio itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.031°N, 25.136°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-789-2051), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
