Carnot at a glance

Among the 5 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Central African Republic, Carnot is the 2nd coolest (25.4 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,080 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (1,319 mm a year). Compared with Berbérati, the nearest covered town with different numbers (76 km away), Carnot is 1.8 °C warmer in February and 0.8 °C warmer in July, with 272 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.6 °C), averages 3.6 °C more than its coolest, July (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.5 °C (69 °F), in January. The area lies about 695 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Carnot, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Carnot has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 695 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
