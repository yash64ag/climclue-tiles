Bambari at a glance

Among the 33 towns ClimaClue covers in the Central African Republic, Bambari is 10th warmest (26.3 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,385 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (1,438 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ippy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (82 km away), Bambari is 0.7 °C warmer in February and 0.7 °C warmer in August, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.6 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, August (24.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.5 °C (65 °F), in December. The area lies about 1,232 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bambari, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bambari has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,232 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bambari itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.768°N, 20.676°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-782-2006), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
