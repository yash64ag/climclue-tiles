Aku at a glance

Among the 269 towns ClimaClue covers in Nigeria, Aku is 204th warmest (25.7 °C year average), 161st sunniest (about 1,994 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (2,385 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nsukka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Aku is 0.7 °C warmer in February and 0.6 °C warmer in August, with 94 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.1 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, August (23.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.9 °C (68 °F), in December. The area lies about 258 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Aku, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Aku has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 258 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Aku itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.709°N, 7.318°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-772-1873), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
