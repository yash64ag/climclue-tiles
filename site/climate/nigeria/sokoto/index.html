Sokoto at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Sokoto is the 2nd warmest (28.9 °C year average) and the 5th driest (576 mm a year). Compared with Birnin Kebbi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (132 km away), Sokoto is 0.4 °C cooler in April and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 119 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (33.9 °C), averages 9.7 °C more than its coolest, January (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 755 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sokoto, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sokoto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 755 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
