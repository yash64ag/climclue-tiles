Abuja at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Abuja is 50th warmest (26.3 °C year average), 26th sunniest (about 2,472 hours of sunshine a year) and 65th wettest (1,468 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Suleja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Abuja is 1.1 °C cooler in March and 0.8 °C cooler in August, with 62 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (29.6 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, August (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.9 °C (70 °F), in August. The area lies about 448 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Abuja, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Abuja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 448 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
