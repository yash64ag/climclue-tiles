Bama at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Bama is the 3rd sunniest (about 3,019 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Maiduguri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Bama is 0.7 °C warmer in April and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 68 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (33.0 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.2 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 950 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bama, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bama has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 950 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
