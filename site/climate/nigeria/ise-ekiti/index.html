Ise-Ekiti at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Ise-Ekiti is 97th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 59th sunniest (about 1,939 hours of sunshine a year) and 59th wettest (1,491 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ikere-Ekiti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Ise-Ekiti is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.1 °C warmer in August, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.1 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, August (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.1 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 173 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ise-Ekiti, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ise-Ekiti has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 173 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
