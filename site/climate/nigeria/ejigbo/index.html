Ejigbo at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Ejigbo is 78th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 42nd sunniest (about 2,028 hours of sunshine a year) and 75th wettest (1,315 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Inisa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Ejigbo is 0.1 °C warmer in February and about as warm in August, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.0 °C), averages 4.3 °C more than its coolest, August (23.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in August. The area lies about 179 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ejigbo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ejigbo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 179 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ejigbo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.903°N, 4.314°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-760-1843), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
