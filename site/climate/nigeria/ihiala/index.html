Ihiala at a glance

Among the 269 towns ClimaClue covers in Nigeria, Ihiala is 194th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 243rd sunniest (about 1,694 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (2,350 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ozubulu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Ihiala is 0.3 °C cooler in February and 0.3 °C cooler in August, with 245 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.8 °C), averages 3.6 °C more than its coolest, August (24.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.8 °C (69 °F), in September. The area lies about 176 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ihiala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ihiala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 176 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ihiala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.855°N, 6.859°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-781-1868), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
