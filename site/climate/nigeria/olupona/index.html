Olupona at a glance

Among the 269 towns ClimaClue covers in Nigeria, Olupona is 181st warmest (25.9 °C year average), 166th sunniest (about 1,974 hours of sunshine a year) and 153rd wettest (1,307 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Iwo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Olupona is 0.1 °C cooler in February and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 51 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.1 °C), averages 4.2 °C more than its coolest, August (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.7 °C (69 °F), in August. The area lies about 145 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Olupona, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Olupona has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 145 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Olupona itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.600°N, 4.193°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-763-1841), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
