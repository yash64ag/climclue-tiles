Lagos weather in August

Average August weather in Lagos, Nigeria: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In August, Lagos averages 27.8 °C (82 °F) days and 21.7 °C (71 °F) nights, with 99 mm (3.9 in) of rain over the month and about 111 hours of sunshine.

How August compares in Lagos

August is the coolest month, the 6th driest of the 12 and the 2nd least sunny of the 12 in Lagos. Compared with July: 0.4 °C cooler, 131 mm less precipitation and 7 hours more sunshine. September is 0.5 °C warmer than August on average.

Lagos in August: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Lagos in August?
In August, Lagos averages a day high of 27.8 °C (82 °F) and a night low of 21.7 °C (71 °F), with 99 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation and about 111 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is August a good time to visit Lagos?
For mild weather, yes: August is one of Lagos’s mild months (August), counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Lagos in August?
99 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making August the 6th driest of the 12. The wettest month is June (383 mm), the driest January (14 mm).
Does it snow in Lagos in August?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for August in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Lagos get in August?
About 111 hours of bright sunshine, the 2nd least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Lagos in August?
About 25.4 °C (78 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, warm for swimming; its warmest month is May (29.2 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Lagos in August?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes for humid air and a compact umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
