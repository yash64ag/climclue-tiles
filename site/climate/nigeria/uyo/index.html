Uyo at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Uyo is 74th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 104th sunniest (about 1,551 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (2,459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ikot Ekpene, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Uyo is about as warm in February and 0.2 °C warmer in August, with 135 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.6 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Uyo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Uyo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Uyo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.051°N, 7.934°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-789-1879), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
