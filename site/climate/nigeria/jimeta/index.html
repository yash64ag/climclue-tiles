Jimeta at a glance

Among the 269 towns ClimaClue covers in Nigeria, Jimeta is 14th warmest (28.8 °C year average), 56th sunniest (about 2,796 hours of sunshine a year) and 225th wettest (801 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yola, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Jimeta is 0.1 °C warmer in April and about as warm in January, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (33.1 °C), averages 6.9 °C more than its coolest, January (26.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.4 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 665 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jimeta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jimeta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 665 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jimeta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.279°N, 12.458°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-747-1924), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
