Gwarzo at a glance

Among the 269 towns ClimaClue covers in Nigeria, Gwarzo is 224th warmest (25.3 °C year average), 40th sunniest (about 2,851 hours of sunshine a year) and 205th wettest (1,050 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Malumfashi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Gwarzo is 0.3 °C warmer in April and about as warm in January, with 62 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.2 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, January (20.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 741 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Gwarzo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 741 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gwarzo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.916°N, 7.934°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-720-1879), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
