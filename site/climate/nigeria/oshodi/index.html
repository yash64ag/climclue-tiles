Oshodi at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Oshodi is 42nd warmest (26.5 °C year average), 78th sunniest (about 1,871 hours of sunshine a year) and 33rd wettest (1,792 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mushin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Oshodi is about as warm in March and about as warm in August, with 53 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.9 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, August (24.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.1 °C (74 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Oshodi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Oshodi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-774-1833), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
