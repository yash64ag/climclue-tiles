Abakaliki at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Abakaliki is 15th warmest (27.5 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 1,941 hours of sunshine a year) and 26th wettest (1,790 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ugep, the nearest covered town with different numbers (57 km away), Abakaliki is 0.8 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in August, with 153 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (30.1 °C), averages 4.6 °C more than its coolest, August (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.8 °C (73 °F), in July. The area lies about 192 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Abakaliki, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Abakaliki has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 192 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
