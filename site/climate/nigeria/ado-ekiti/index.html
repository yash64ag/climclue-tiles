Ado-Ekiti at a glance

Among the 111 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Nigeria, Ado-Ekiti is 98th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 51st sunniest (about 1,977 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (1,403 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ikere-Ekiti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Ado-Ekiti is 0.3 °C warmer in February and about as warm in August, with 80 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (27.3 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, August (23.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.9 °C (70 °F), in August. The area lies about 179 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ado-Ekiti, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ado-Ekiti has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 179 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
