Ohafia-Ifigh at a glance

Among the 269 towns ClimaClue covers in Nigeria, Ohafia-Ifigh is 167th warmest (26.0 °C year average), 242nd sunniest (about 1,698 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (2,159 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Amaigbo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Ohafia-Ifigh is 0.5 °C cooler in February and 0.3 °C cooler in August, with 278 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.3 °C), averages 3.9 °C more than its coolest, August (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.8 °C (69 °F), in September. The area lies about 130 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ohafia-Ifigh, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ohafia-Ifigh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 130 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ohafia-Ifigh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.615°N, 7.812°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-783-1878), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
