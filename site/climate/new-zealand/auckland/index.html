Auckland at a glance

Among the 47 towns ClimaClue covers in New Zealand, Auckland is the warmest (15.7 °C year average). Compared with Mount Eden, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Auckland is 0.3 °C warmer in February and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 64 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (20.1 °C), averages 8.2 °C more than its coolest, July (11.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.2 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Auckland, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Auckland

For mild weather, the best months to visit Auckland are January, February, March, April, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (20.1 °C), January (19.6 °C) and March (18.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (73 mm), February (73 mm) and November (81 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (228 h), December (222 h) and February (194 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Auckland has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Auckland climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Auckland?
February, with an average of 20.1 °C (68 °F), day highs around 24.0 °C (75 °F) and night lows around 16.2 °C (61 °F).
What is the coldest month in Auckland?
July, with an average of 11.9 °C (53 °F), day highs around 15.6 °C (60 °F) and night lows around 8.2 °C (47 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Auckland?
July, with 142 mm (5.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,197 mm (47.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Auckland?
January, with 73 mm (2.9 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Auckland?
January, with about 228 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Auckland?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time to visit Auckland?
For mild weather (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, November and December. Climate only, from 1981–2010 averages: crowds and prices are not considered.
How warm is the sea in Auckland?
The nearby open sea averages 20.3 °C (69 °F) in February, its warmest month, and 13.6 °C (56 °F) in August. It is 20 °C (68 °F) or warmer in February. 1981–2010 averages (ERA5); beaches and shallow bays are often a little warmer in summer.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Auckland itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.849°S, 174.763°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1208-3547), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,547,200, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
