Manukau City at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in New Zealand, Manukau City is the 2nd warmest (15.5 °C year average) and the 3rd sunniest (about 2,005 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Auckland, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Manukau City is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.5 °C cooler in July, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (20.1 °C), averages 8.7 °C more than its coolest, July (11.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.8 °C (48 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Manukau City, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Manukau City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1209-3548), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
