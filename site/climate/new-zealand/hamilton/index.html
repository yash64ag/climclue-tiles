Why Hamilton has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Hamilton climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Hamilton?
February, with an average of 19.4 °C (67 °F), day highs around 22.9 °C (73 °F) and night lows around 14.9 °C (59 °F).
What is the coldest month in Hamilton?
July, with an average of 9.6 °C (49 °F), day highs around 13.0 °C (55 °F) and night lows around 5.8 °C (42 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Hamilton?
July, with 148 mm (5.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,336 mm (52.6 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Hamilton?
February, with 81 mm (3.2 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Hamilton?
January, with about 224 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Hamilton?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Hamilton?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February and March.
What type of climate does Hamilton have?
Oceanic (Köppen Cfb). Mild all year, with rain in every season. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.8 × 11 km, that contains Hamilton’s GeoNames location (37.783°S, 175.283°E; ClimaClue area sq-1217-3552), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 192,100 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).
