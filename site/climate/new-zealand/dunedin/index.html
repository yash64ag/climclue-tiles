Dunedin at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in New Zealand, Dunedin is the coolest (9.6 °C year average), the least sunny (about 1,604 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (931 mm a year). Compared with Christchurch, the nearest covered town with different numbers (310 km away), Dunedin is 3.1 °C cooler in January and 1.3 °C cooler in July, with 230 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (13.7 °C), averages 8.5 °C more than its coolest, July (5.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 2.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 19 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dunedin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dunedin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
