Christchurch at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in New Zealand, Christchurch is the 2nd coolest (11.8 °C year average) and the driest (700 mm a year). Compared with Wellington, the nearest covered town with different numbers (305 km away), Christchurch is 1.2 °C warmer in January and 3.0 °C cooler in July, with 516 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (16.8 °C), averages 10.3 °C more than its coolest, July (6.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, July, averages 3.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Christchurch, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Christchurch has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
