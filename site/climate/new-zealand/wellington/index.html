Wellington at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in New Zealand, Wellington is the 3rd driest (1,216 mm a year). Compared with Lower Hutt, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Wellington is 0.4 °C cooler in February and 0.7 °C warmer in July, with 131 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (15.9 °C), averages 6.5 °C more than its coolest, July (9.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Wellington, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Wellington

For mild weather, the best months to visit Wellington are January and February.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and February.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (15.9 °C), January (15.6 °C) and March (14.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (67 mm), January (70 mm) and March (85 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (233 h), December (214 h) and November (202 h).

Why Wellington has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
