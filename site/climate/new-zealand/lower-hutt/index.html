Lower Hutt at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in New Zealand, Lower Hutt is the 3rd coolest (12.4 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,849 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (1,347 mm a year). Compared with Wellington, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Lower Hutt is 0.4 °C warmer in February and 0.7 °C cooler in July, with 131 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (16.4 °C), averages 7.6 °C more than its coolest, July (8.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.8 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lower Hutt, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lower Hutt has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
