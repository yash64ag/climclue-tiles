Tauranga at a glance

Among the 8 towns of 100,000+ people covered in New Zealand, Tauranga is the 3rd warmest (15.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,075 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,690 mm a year). Compared with Hamilton, the nearest covered town with different numbers (78 km away), Tauranga is about as warm in February and 1.5 °C warmer in July, with 354 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (19.4 °C), averages 8.3 °C more than its coolest, July (11.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tauranga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tauranga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
