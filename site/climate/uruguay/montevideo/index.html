Montevideo at a glance

Among the 31 towns ClimaClue covers in Uruguay, Montevideo is the 5th coolest (16.4 °C year average), the 5th sunniest (about 2,486 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,075 mm a year). Compared with Paso de Carrasco, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Montevideo is 0.3 °C cooler in January and about as warm in July, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (22.1 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, July (10.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in July. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Montevideo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Montevideo

For mild weather, the best months to visit Montevideo are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): January (22.1 °C), February (21.9 °C) and March (20.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (76 mm), August (78 mm) and July (79 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (290 h), December (283 h) and November (247 h).

Why Montevideo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

July, with an average of 10.5 °C (51 °F), day highs around 14.5 °C (58 °F) and night lows around 6.6 °C (44 °F).

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Montevideo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.903°S, 56.188°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1189-1238), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
