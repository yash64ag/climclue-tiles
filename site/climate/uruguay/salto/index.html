Salto at a glance

Among the 3 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uruguay, Salto is the warmest (19.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,574 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,301 mm a year). Compared with Montevideo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (424 km away), Salto is 4.0 °C warmer in January and 1.5 °C warmer in July, with 222 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.9 °C), averages 13.9 °C more than its coolest, July (12.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in July. The area lies about 323 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Salto, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Salto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 323 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
