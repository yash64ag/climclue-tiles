Balkanabat at a glance

Among the 37 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkmenistan, Balkanabat is 9th warmest (17.7 °C year average), 28th sunniest (about 2,738 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (192 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jebel, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Balkanabat is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (32.3 °C), averages 28.8 °C more than its coolest, January (3.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 82 km from the sea.

Why Balkanabat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 82 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Balkanabat itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.511°N, 54.367°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-444-2343), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
