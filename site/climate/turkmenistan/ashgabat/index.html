Ashgabat at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Turkmenistan, Ashgabat is the warmest (17.4 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,708 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (274 mm a year). Compared with Mary, the nearest covered town with different numbers (306 km away), Ashgabat is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 126 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.5 °C), averages 28.0 °C more than its coolest, January (3.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 400 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ashgabat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 400 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ashgabat itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.950°N, 58.383°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-2383), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
