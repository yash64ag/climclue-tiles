Ashgabat at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Turkmenistan, Ashgabat is the warmest (17.3 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,708 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (278 mm a year). Compared with Mary, the nearest covered town with different numbers (306 km away), Ashgabat is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 129 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.4 °C), averages 28.0 °C more than its coolest, January (3.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 400 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 128% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ashgabat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 400 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
