San Francisco at a glance

Among the 35 towns ClimaClue covers in El Salvador, San Francisco is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,891 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with San Miguel, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), San Francisco is 0.8 °C cooler in April and 0.8 °C cooler in October, with 79 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.4 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, October (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.6 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 64 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Francisco, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit San Francisco

No month in San Francisco is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are September (26.2 °C, 289 mm), October (25.9 °C, 201 mm) and November (25.9 °C, 42 mm).

Why San Francisco has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 64 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Francisco itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.694°N, 88.104°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-703-918), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,152, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
