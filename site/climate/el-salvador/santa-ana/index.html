Santa Ana at a glance

Among the 7 towns of 100,000+ people covered in El Salvador, Santa Ana is the 3rd coolest (23.5 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,676 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,646 mm a year). Compared with Apopa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Santa Ana is 2.1 °C cooler in April and 2.2 °C cooler in January, with 483 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (24.9 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (22.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.1 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santa Ana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Santa Ana

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, November and December.

Why Santa Ana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
