Izalco at a glance

Among the 35 towns ClimaClue covers in El Salvador, Izalco is the 2nd wettest (2,311 mm a year). Compared with Sonzacate, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Izalco is 0.6 °C cooler in April and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.0 °C), averages 1.9 °C more than its coolest, January (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Izalco, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Izalco

For mild weather, the best months to visit Izalco are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (26.0 °C), May (25.6 °C) and March (25.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (4 mm), February (6 mm) and December (10 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (273 h), January (264 h) and December (260 h).

Why Izalco has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Izalco itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.745°N, 89.673°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-702-903), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
