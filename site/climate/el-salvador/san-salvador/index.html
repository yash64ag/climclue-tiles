San Salvador at a glance

Among the 7 towns of 100,000+ people covered in El Salvador, San Salvador is the 2nd coolest (23.5 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,711 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (2,109 mm a year). Compared with Mejicanos, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), San Salvador is 1.6 °C cooler in April and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 610 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (24.6 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, January (22.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.2 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Salvador, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Salvador has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
