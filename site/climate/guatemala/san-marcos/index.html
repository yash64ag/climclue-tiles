San Marcos at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, San Marcos is 94th warmest (13.8 °C year average), 62nd sunniest (about 2,520 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (3,543 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with San Pedro Sacatepéquez, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), San Marcos is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 355 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (14.8 °C), averages 2.4 °C more than its coolest, January (12.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 77 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Marcos, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Marcos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 77 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Marcos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.964°N, 91.795°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-690-882), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
