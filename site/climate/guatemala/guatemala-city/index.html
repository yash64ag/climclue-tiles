Guatemala City at a glance

Among the 18 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Guatemala, Guatemala City is 12th warmest (19.1 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 2,532 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (1,221 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chinautla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Guatemala City is 1.9 °C cooler in April and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 308 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (20.6 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, January (17.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.9 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 89 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guatemala City, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Guatemala City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 89 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
