El Tejar at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, El Tejar is 69th warmest (17.4 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 2,660 hours of sunshine a year) and 77th wettest (1,603 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chimaltenango, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), El Tejar is about as warm in April and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 74 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (18.6 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, January (16.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.9 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 89 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of El Tejar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why El Tejar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 89 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El Tejar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.647°N, 90.791°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-693-892), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
