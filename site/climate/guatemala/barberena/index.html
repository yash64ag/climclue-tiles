Barberena at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, Barberena is 54th warmest (19.9 °C year average), 90th sunniest (about 2,389 hours of sunshine a year) and 56th wettest (2,127 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cuilapa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Barberena is 1.7 °C cooler in April and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 247 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (21.0 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, January (18.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.0 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 60 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Barberena, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Barberena has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 60 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Barberena itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.307°N, 90.362°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-696-896), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
