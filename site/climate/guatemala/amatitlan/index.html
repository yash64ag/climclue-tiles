Amatitlán at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, Amatitlán is 48th warmest (20.4 °C year average), 78th sunniest (about 2,462 hours of sunshine a year) and 48th wettest (2,398 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Villa Nueva, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Amatitlán is 0.3 °C warmer in April and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 702 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (21.4 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, January (19.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.3 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Amatitlán, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Amatitlán has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C. The 10 km area around Amatitlán, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.7 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Amatitlán itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.477°N, 90.635°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-695-893), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
