Coatepeque at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, Coatepeque is the wettest (5,004 mm a year). Compared with Colomba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Coatepeque is 3.0 °C warmer in April and 2.6 °C warmer in December, with 131 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (25.4 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, December (23.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 54 km from the sea.

Why Coatepeque has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 54 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Coatepeque itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.704°N, 91.864°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-692-881), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
