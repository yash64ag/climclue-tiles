Escuintla at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, Escuintla is 21st warmest (24.9 °C year average), 29th sunniest (about 2,620 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (2,869 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Masagua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Escuintla is 1.3 °C cooler in April and 1.3 °C cooler in December, with 123 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.1 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, December (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.1 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Escuintla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Escuintla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C. The 10 km area around Escuintla, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 23.5 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Escuintla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.301°N, 90.788°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-696-892), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
