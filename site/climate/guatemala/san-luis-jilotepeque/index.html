San Luis Jilotepeque at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, San Luis Jilotepeque is 38th warmest (23.1 °C year average), 68th sunniest (about 2,511 hours of sunshine a year) and 97th wettest (974 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chiquimula, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), San Luis Jilotepeque is 1.9 °C cooler in May and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 92 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (25.1 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, January (20.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.2 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 119 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Luis Jilotepeque, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why San Luis Jilotepeque has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 119 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C. The 10 km area around San Luis Jilotepeque, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 21.1 °C a year, 1.9 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Luis Jilotepeque itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.647°N, 89.725°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-693-902), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
