Santa Cruz del Quiché at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, Santa Cruz del Quiché is 77th warmest (15.9 °C year average), 52nd sunniest (about 2,554 hours of sunshine a year) and 84th wettest (1,458 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chichicastenango, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Santa Cruz del Quiché is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 133 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (17.4 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.3 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 125 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santa Cruz del Quiché, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Santa Cruz del Quiché are March and April.

Why Santa Cruz del Quiché has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 125 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santa Cruz del Quiché itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.030°N, 91.149°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-689-888), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
