Sololá at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, Sololá is 76th warmest (16.1 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 2,639 hours of sunshine a year) and 47th wettest (2,402 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santiago Atitlán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Sololá is 2.0 °C cooler in May and 2.5 °C cooler in January, with 1,529 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (17.0 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, January (14.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.9 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 95 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sololá, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sololá has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 95 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sololá itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.771°N, 91.183°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-692-888), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 45,373, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
