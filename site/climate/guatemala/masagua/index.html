Masagua at a glance

Among the 102 towns ClimaClue covers in Guatemala, Masagua is the 4th warmest (26.1 °C year average) and the 3rd sunniest (about 2,800 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Escuintla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Masagua is 1.3 °C warmer in April and 1.3 °C warmer in December, with 123 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.5 °C), averages 2.2 °C more than its coolest, December (25.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.3 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Masagua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Masagua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Masagua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.203°N, 90.848°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-697-891), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
