Chongjin at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Chongjin is the 5th coolest (8.6 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,227 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (696 mm a year). Compared with Hyesan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (140 km away), Chongjin is 2.8 °C warmer in August and 9.0 °C warmer in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.4 °C), averages 28.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-6.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chongjin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chongjin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
