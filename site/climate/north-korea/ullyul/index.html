Ŭllyul at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Ŭllyul is the 4th warmest (11.4 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,463 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (813 mm a year). Compared with Chaeryŏng-ni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Ŭllyul is about as warm in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 27.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Ŭllyul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
