Kaech’ŏn at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Kaech’ŏn is 21st warmest (10.2 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 2,425 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (1,152 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sunch’ŏn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Kaech’ŏn is about as warm in August and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 145 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 32.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 61 km from the sea.

Why Kaech’ŏn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 61 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
