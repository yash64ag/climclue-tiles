Musan-ŭp weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Musan-ŭp, North Korea (North Hamgyong), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Musan-ŭp is warmest in August, with 26.2 °C (79 °F) days and 16.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -6.3 °C (21 °F) days and -18.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (129 mm); January is driest (6 mm).

Why Musan-ŭp has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 73 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.7 °C. The 10 km area around Musan-ŭp, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 5.1 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Musan-ŭp itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.226°N, 129.208°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-417-3092), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
