Uiju at a glance

Among the 97 towns ClimaClue covers in North Korea, Uiju is the 2nd sunniest (about 2,498 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Sinŭiju, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Uiju is about as warm in August and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 32 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.4 °C), averages 32.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 45 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 24 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Uiju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 45 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Uiju itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.199°N, 124.532°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-438-3045), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
