Kangsŏn at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Kangsŏn is 11th warmest (11.1 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 2,437 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (934 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Man’gyŏngdae-ri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Kangsŏn is 0.2 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.9 °C), averages 29.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 36 km from the sea.

Why Kangsŏn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 36 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
