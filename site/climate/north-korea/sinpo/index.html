Sinp’o at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Sinp’o is the 3rd least sunny (about 2,285 hours of sunshine a year) and the 4th driest (729 mm a year). Compared with Hŭngnam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (53 km away), Sinp’o is 0.8 °C cooler in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 143 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.4 °C), averages 25.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sinp’o, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sinp’o has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
