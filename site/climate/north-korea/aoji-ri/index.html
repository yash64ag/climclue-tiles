Aoji-ri at a glance

Among the 97 towns ClimaClue covers in North Korea, Aoji-ri is 83rd warmest (7.4 °C year average), 84th sunniest (about 2,236 hours of sunshine a year) and 76th wettest (693 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ŭndŏk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Aoji-ri is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 16 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.3 °C), averages 32.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Why Aoji-ri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Aoji-ri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.522°N, 130.396°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-414-3103), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
