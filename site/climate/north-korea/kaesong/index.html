Kaesŏng at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Kaesŏng is the 3rd warmest (11.6 °C year average) and the 2nd wettest (1,377 mm a year). Compared with Paech’ŏn-ŭp, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Kaesŏng is about as warm in August and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 99 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.1 °C), averages 29.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Why Kaesŏng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
