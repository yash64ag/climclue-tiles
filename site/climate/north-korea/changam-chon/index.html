Changam-ch’on at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Changam-ch’on is the 2nd coolest (6.6 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 2,256 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (607 mm a year). Compared with Sinp’o, the nearest covered town with different numbers (80 km away), Changam-ch’on is 1.8 °C cooler in August and 6.1 °C cooler in January, with 122 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.6 °C), averages 30.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Changam-ch’on, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Changam-ch’on has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.1 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
