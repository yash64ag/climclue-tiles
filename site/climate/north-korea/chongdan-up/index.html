Ch’ŏngdan-ŭp at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Ch’ŏngdan-ŭp is the 2nd warmest (11.6 °C year average) and the 4th wettest (1,173 mm a year). Compared with Haeju, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Ch’ŏngdan-ŭp is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.9 °C), averages 27.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 21 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Ch’ŏngdan-ŭp has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
