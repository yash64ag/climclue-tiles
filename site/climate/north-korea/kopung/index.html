Kop’ung at a glance

Among the 97 towns ClimaClue covers in North Korea, Kop’ung is 85th warmest (7.1 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 2,439 hours of sunshine a year) and 10th wettest (1,273 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Songwŏn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Kop’ung is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 244 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.5 °C), averages 34.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 144 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kop’ung, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kop’ung has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 144 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 34.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-433-3059), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
