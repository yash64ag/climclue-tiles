Ayang-ni at a glance

Among the 97 towns ClimaClue covers in North Korea, Ayang-ni is 22nd warmest (11.3 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 2,429 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (1,064 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sinwŏn-ŭp, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Ayang-ni is 0.1 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.9 °C), averages 29.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Why Ayang-ni has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ayang-ni itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.247°N, 125.795°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-457-3057), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
