Hamhŭng at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Hamhŭng is 18th warmest (10.4 °C year average), 24th sunniest (about 2,318 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (874 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hŭngnam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Hamhŭng is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.6 °C), averages 28.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Hamhŭng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
