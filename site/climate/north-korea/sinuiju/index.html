Sinŭiju at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Sinŭiju is the sunniest (about 2,508 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Paek'ak, the nearest covered town with different numbers (125 km away), Sinŭiju is 0.5 °C cooler in August and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.1 °C), averages 31.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-7.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Sinŭiju has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
