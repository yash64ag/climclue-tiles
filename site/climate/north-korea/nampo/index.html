Namp’o at a glance

Among the 29 towns of 100,000+ people covered in North Korea, Namp’o is the 5th sunniest (about 2,447 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Chaeryŏng-ni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Namp’o is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.9 °C), averages 29.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Why Namp’o has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
