Luwero at a glance

Among the 143 towns ClimaClue covers in Uganda, Luwero is 83rd warmest (21.3 °C year average), 60th sunniest (about 2,455 hours of sunshine a year) and 100th wettest (1,204 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wobulenzi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Luwero is 0.6 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.5 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, July (20.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.9 °C (59 °F), in July. The area lies about 960 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Luwero, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Luwero

For mild weather, the best months to visit Luwero are January, February, June, July and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, June, July and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (22.5 °C), March (22.2 °C) and January (21.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (45 mm), February (47 mm) and July (64 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (227 h), January (222 h) and June (213 h).

Why Luwero has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 960 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Luwero itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.849°N, 32.473°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-831-2124), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 49,100, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
