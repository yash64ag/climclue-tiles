Kasese at a glance

Among the 143 towns ClimaClue covers in Uganda, Kasese is 9th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 113th sunniest (about 2,044 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (1,457 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kisinga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Kasese is 4.4 °C warmer in February and 4.1 °C warmer in November, with 1,134 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.6 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, November (23.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in November. The area lies about 1,168 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kasese, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kasese has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 1,168 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C. The 10 km area around Kasese, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 22.4 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kasese itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.183°N, 30.083°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-838-2100), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
