Ibanda at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uganda, Ibanda is the coolest (20.0 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,889 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,007 mm a year). Compared with Mbarara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Ibanda is 0.5 °C cooler in February and 0.6 °C cooler in November, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (21.1 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, November (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.7 °C (58 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,113 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ibanda, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ibanda has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,113 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
