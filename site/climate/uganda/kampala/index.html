Kampala at a glance

Among the 23 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Uganda, Kampala is 15th warmest (21.3 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 2,454 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (1,395 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bunamwaya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Kampala is 0.5 °C cooler in February and 0.5 °C cooler in July, with 58 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.1 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, July (20.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in July. The area lies about 927 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kampala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kampala

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kampala are January, February, June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (22.1 °C), March (22.1 °C) and January (21.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (62 mm), July (67 mm) and January (72 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (223 h), January (221 h) and June (215 h).

Why Kampala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 927 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
