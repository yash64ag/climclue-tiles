Butunduzi at a glance

Among the 143 towns ClimaClue covers in Uganda, Butunduzi is 91st warmest (21.1 °C year average), 106th sunniest (about 2,078 hours of sunshine a year) and 132nd wettest (973 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kyegegwa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Butunduzi is 0.5 °C warmer in February and 0.4 °C warmer in November, with 82 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.6 °C), averages 2.3 °C more than its coolest, November (20.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.6 °C (58 °F), in November. The area lies about 1,111 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Butunduzi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Butunduzi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,111 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Butunduzi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.555°N, 30.855°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-834-2108), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
