Borikhan weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Borikhan, Laos (Bolikhamsai), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Borikhan is warmest in April, with 32.7 °C (91 °F) days and 21.9 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 26.8 °C (80 °F) days and 15.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (815 mm); December is driest (3 mm).

Borikhan at a glance

Among the 19 towns ClimaClue covers in Laos, Borikhan is the wettest (3,362 mm a year). Compared with Pakxan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Borikhan is 0.6 °C cooler in April and 0.4 °C cooler in December, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.3 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, December (21.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.1 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 215 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Borikhan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Borikhan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 215 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Borikhan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.563°N, 103.727°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-654-2837), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
