Luang Prabang at a glance

Among the 19 towns ClimaClue covers in Laos, Luang Prabang is the 2nd driest (1,386 mm a year). Compared with Muang Xay, the nearest covered town with different numbers (91 km away), Luang Prabang is 2.0 °C warmer in June and 2.7 °C warmer in December, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.6 °C), averages 7.7 °C more than its coolest, December (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.8 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 388 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Luang Prabang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Luang Prabang

For mild weather, the best months to visit Luang Prabang are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (28.6 °C), May (28.3 °C) and April (28.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (9 mm), February (14 mm) and December (14 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (212 h), February (201 h) and March (200 h).

Why Luang Prabang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 388 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.7 °C. The 10 km area around Luang Prabang, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 24.5 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Luang Prabang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.893°N, 102.152°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-641-2821), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
