Sidi Rahal at a glance

Among the 176 towns ClimaClue covers in Morocco, Sidi Rahal is 93rd warmest (18.3 °C year average), 79th sunniest (about 2,920 hours of sunshine a year) and 84th wettest (382 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Had Soualem, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Sidi Rahal is 0.6 °C cooler in August and 1.1 °C warmer in January, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.7 °C), averages 9.0 °C more than its coolest, January (13.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.7 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sidi Rahal, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sidi Rahal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sidi Rahal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.477°N, 7.936°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-505-1720), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
