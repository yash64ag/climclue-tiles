Midelt at a glance

Among the 176 towns ClimaClue covers in Morocco, Midelt is 165th warmest (15.6 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 3,071 hours of sunshine a year) and 156th wettest (181 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Boumia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Midelt is 1.7 °C warmer in July and 1.4 °C warmer in January, with 168 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.4 °C), averages 20.0 °C more than its coolest, January (6.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 257 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Midelt, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Midelt has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 257 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Midelt itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.685°N, 4.745°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-513-1752), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 60,390, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
