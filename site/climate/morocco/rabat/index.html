Why Rabat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Rabat climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Rabat?
August, with an average of 23.6 °C (75 °F), day highs around 27.6 °C (82 °F) and night lows around 20.1 °C (68 °F).
What is the coldest month in Rabat?
January, with an average of 13.3 °C (56 °F), day highs around 16.3 °C (61 °F) and night lows around 10.1 °C (50 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Rabat?
December, with 91 mm (3.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 484 mm (19.1 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Rabat?
July, with 1 mm (0.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Rabat?
July, with about 314 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Rabat?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Rabat?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October and November.
What type of climate does Rabat have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.2 × 11 km, that contains Rabat’s GeoNames location (34.013°N, 6.833°W; ClimaClue area sq-499-1731), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 1,655,753 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
