Tiznit at a glance

Among the 176 towns ClimaClue covers in Morocco, Tiznit is 49th warmest (19.0 °C year average), 104th sunniest (about 2,884 hours of sunshine a year) and 158th wettest (175 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sidi Ifni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Tiznit is 2.5 °C warmer in August and 1.5 °C cooler in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.6 °C), averages 9.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.5 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 29 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tiznit, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tiznit has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 29 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tiznit itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.697°N, 9.732°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-543-1702), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
