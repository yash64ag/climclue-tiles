Chichaoua at a glance

Among the 176 towns ClimaClue covers in Morocco, Chichaoua is 35th warmest (19.5 °C year average), 37th sunniest (about 3,049 hours of sunshine a year) and 154th wettest (199 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Imi-n-Tanout, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Chichaoua is 1.6 °C warmer in July and 2.3 °C warmer in January, with 93 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.1 °C), averages 15.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 86 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chichaoua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chichaoua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 86 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chichaoua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.544°N, 8.763°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-524-1712), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
