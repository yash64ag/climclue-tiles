Jalgaon weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Jalgaon, India (Maharashtra), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Jalgaon is warmest in May, with 41.3 °C (106 °F) days and 25.9 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 29.7 °C (85 °F) days and 12.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (233 mm); April is driest (2 mm).

Jalgaon at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Jalgaon is 1107th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 454th sunniest (about 2,841 hours of sunshine a year) and 2780th wettest (830 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yāval, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Jalgaon is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 58 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.6 °C), averages 12.6 °C more than its coolest, December (21.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 282 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jalgaon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jalgaon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 282 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jalgaon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.003°N, 75.566°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-629-2555), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
