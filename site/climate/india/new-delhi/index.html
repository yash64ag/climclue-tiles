New Delhi at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, New Delhi is 411th warmest (24.8 °C year average), 230th sunniest (about 2,679 hours of sunshine a year) and 467th wettest (641 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Delhi Cantonment, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), New Delhi is about as warm in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.4 °C), averages 19.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.7 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 869 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of New Delhi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit New Delhi

For mild weather, the best months to visit New Delhi are February, March and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (33.4 °C), May (32.8 °C) and July (30.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (6 mm), December (7 mm) and October (11 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (268 h), April (264 h) and October (264 h).

Why New Delhi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 869 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
