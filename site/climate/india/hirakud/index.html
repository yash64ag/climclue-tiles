Hīrākud weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Hīrākud, India (Odisha), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Hīrākud is warmest in May, with 40.8 °C (105 °F) days and 26.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 28.6 °C (83 °F) days and 14.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (509 mm); December is driest (9 mm).

Hīrākud at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Hīrākud is 745th warmest (27.2 °C year average), 2665th sunniest (about 2,464 hours of sunshine a year) and 740th wettest (1,718 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Burla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Hīrākud is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.4 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, January (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.1 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 254 km from the sea.

Why Hīrākud has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 254 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hīrākud itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.525°N, 83.873°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-624-2638), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
