Indore at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Indore is 341st warmest (25.3 °C year average), 64th sunniest (about 2,843 hours of sunshine a year) and 331st wettest (926 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pithampur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Indore is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 123 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.8 °C), averages 14.0 °C more than its coolest, January (18.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.5 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 356 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Indore, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Indore has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 356 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Indore itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.718°N, 75.833°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-612-2558), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
