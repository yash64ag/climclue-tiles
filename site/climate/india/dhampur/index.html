Dhāmpur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Dhāmpur is 3454th warmest (23.7 °C year average), 1932nd sunniest (about 2,627 hours of sunshine a year) and 1457th wettest (1,228 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sherkot, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Dhāmpur is about as warm in June and about as warm in January, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (31.6 °C), averages 18.2 °C more than its coolest, January (13.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.5 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,007 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dhāmpur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Dhāmpur

For mild weather, the best months to visit Dhāmpur are February, March, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (31.6 °C), May (31.1 °C) and July (29.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (5 mm), December (11 mm) and April (14 mm).

Why Dhāmpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,007 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dhāmpur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.309°N, 78.511°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-546-2585), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
