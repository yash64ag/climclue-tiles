Kota at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kota is 1140th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 353rd sunniest (about 2,867 hours of sunshine a year) and 3036th wettest (751 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kaithoon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Kota is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 37 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (35.8 °C), averages 18.7 °C more than its coolest, January (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.7 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 481 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kota, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kota

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kota are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (35.8 °C), June (34.5 °C) and April (32.1 °C).

Why Kota has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 481 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kota itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.183°N, 75.839°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-588-2558), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
