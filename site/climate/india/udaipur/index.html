Udaipur at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Udaipur is 423rd warmest (24.8 °C year average), 24th sunniest (about 2,976 hours of sunshine a year) and 484th wettest (613 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chittorgarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (98 km away), Udaipur is 1.9 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 64 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.1 °C), averages 15.5 °C more than its coolest, January (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.2 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 298 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Udaipur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Udaipur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 298 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Udaipur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.586°N, 73.713°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-594-2537), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
