North Guwāhāti weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in North Guwāhāti, India (Assam), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

North Guwāhāti is warmest in August, with 32.0 °C (90 °F) days and 25.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 24.4 °C (76 °F) days and 10.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (389 mm); December is driest (10 mm).

North Guwāhāti at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, North Guwāhāti is 3088th warmest (24.6 °C year average), 3330th sunniest (about 2,270 hours of sunshine a year) and 574th wettest (2,129 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guwahati, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), North Guwāhāti is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 22 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.6 °C), averages 11.0 °C more than its coolest, January (17.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.8 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 390 km from the sea.

Why North Guwāhāti has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 390 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for North Guwāhāti itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.197°N, 91.720°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-578-2717), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
