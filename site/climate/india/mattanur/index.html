Mattanur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Mattanur is 1765th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 2240th sunniest (about 2,551 hours of sunshine a year) and 90th wettest (3,741 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Keevallur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Mattanur is 0.3 °C cooler in April and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.0 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, July (25.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.9 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mattanur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mattanur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mattanur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.930°N, 75.572°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-720-2555), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 47,078, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
