Gannavaram at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Gannavaram is 200th warmest (28.3 °C year average), 1718th sunniest (about 2,662 hours of sunshine a year) and 1928th wettest (1,060 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Poranki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Gannavaram is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.1 °C), averages 8.9 °C more than its coolest, December (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.1 °C (66 °F), in December. The area lies about 63 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gannavaram, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gannavaram has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 63 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gannavaram itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.541°N, 80.802°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-674-2608), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
