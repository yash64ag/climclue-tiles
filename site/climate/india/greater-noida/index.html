Greater Noida weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Greater Noida, India (Uttar Pradesh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Greater Noida is warmest in June, with 38.9 °C (102 °F) days and 27.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 20.6 °C (69 °F) days and 6.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (202 mm); November is driest (6 mm).

Greater Noida at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Greater Noida is 3052nd warmest (24.7 °C year average), 1586th sunniest (about 2,674 hours of sunshine a year) and 3124th wettest (716 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dādri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Greater Noida is 0.1 °C warmer in June and about as warm in January, with 21 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.1 °C), averages 19.6 °C more than its coolest, January (13.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.5 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 868 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Greater Noida has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 868 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Greater Noida itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.496°N, 77.536°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-555-2575), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
