Nāndgaon weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nāndgaon, India (Maharashtra), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nāndgaon is warmest in May, with 40.3 °C (105 °F) days and 23.9 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 30.0 °C (86 °F) days and 12.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (162 mm); February is driest (1 mm).

Nāndgaon at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Nāndgaon is 1823rd warmest (26.2 °C year average), 603rd sunniest (about 2,800 hours of sunshine a year) and 3119th wettest (719 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manmād, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Nāndgaon is 1.2 °C warmer in May and 0.8 °C warmer in December, with 83 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.1 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, December (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 193 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nāndgaon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nāndgaon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 193 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nāndgaon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.307°N, 74.655°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-636-2546), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
