Sarauli at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Sarauli is 3097th warmest (24.6 °C year average), 1936th sunniest (about 2,626 hours of sunshine a year) and 1939th wettest (1,055 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shāhābād, Uttar Pradesh (28.6°N 79.0°E), the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Sarauli is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 49 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.4 °C), averages 18.2 °C more than its coolest, January (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 964 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sarauli, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sarauli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 964 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sarauli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.494°N, 79.092°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-555-2590), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,923, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
