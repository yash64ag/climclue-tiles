Rājaldesar at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Rājaldesar is 1862nd warmest (26.1 °C year average), 246th sunniest (about 2,922 hours of sunshine a year) and 3707th wettest (366 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ratangarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Rājaldesar is about as warm in June and about as warm in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 686 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Rājaldesar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 686 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-559-2544), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
