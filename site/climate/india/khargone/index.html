Khargone at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Khargone is 1028th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 403rd sunniest (about 2,853 hours of sunshine a year) and 2651st wettest (858 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kasrāwad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Khargone is 0.7 °C cooler in May and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 43 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.0 °C), averages 13.1 °C more than its coolest, January (20.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.9 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 315 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Khargone, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Khargone has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 315 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Khargone itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.823°N, 75.614°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-621-2556), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
