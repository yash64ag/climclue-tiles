Barauli at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Barauli is 2327th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 3395th sunniest (about 2,240 hours of sunshine a year) and 1560th wettest (1,192 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gopālganj, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Barauli is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 15 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.8 °C), averages 15.9 °C more than its coolest, January (15.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 606 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Barauli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 606 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Barauli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.381°N, 84.586°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-576-2645), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
