Nagpur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Nagpur is 1000th warmest (27.0 °C year average), 854th sunniest (about 2,764 hours of sunshine a year) and 1355th wettest (1,279 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wāri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Nagpur is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (35.4 °C), averages 15.0 °C more than its coolest, December (20.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.5 °C (55 °F), in December. The area lies about 577 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nagpur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Nagpur

For mild weather, the best months to visit Nagpur are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (35.4 °C), April (32.9 °C) and June (31.7 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): April (8 mm), December (10 mm) and February (16 mm).

Why Nagpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 577 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nagpur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.146°N, 79.085°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-628-2590), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
