Rabkavi weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Rabkavi, India (Karnataka), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Rabkavi is warmest in April, with 38.3 °C (101 °F) days and 23.6 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 30.1 °C (86 °F) days and 16.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (126 mm); February is driest (1 mm).

Rabkavi at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Rabkavi is 1576th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 1173rd sunniest (about 2,721 hours of sunshine a year) and 3453rd wettest (610 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rabkavi-Banhatti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Rabkavi is about as warm in April and about as warm in December, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (31.0 °C), averages 7.5 °C more than its coolest, December (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.6 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 183 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rabkavi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Rabkavi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 183 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rabkavi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.476°N, 75.111°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-675-2551), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
