Amravati at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Amravati is 156th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 91st sunniest (about 2,791 hours of sunshine a year) and 319th wettest (957 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Achalpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Amravati is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.4 °C cooler in December, with 28 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.8 °C), averages 14.7 °C more than its coolest, December (20.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.6 °C (56 °F), in December. The area lies about 510 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Amravati, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Amravati has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 510 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
