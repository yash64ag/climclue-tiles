Vazhapadi at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Vazhapadi is 918th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 2735th sunniest (about 2,453 hours of sunshine a year) and 2657th wettest (857 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Peddanāyakkanpālaiyam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Vazhapadi is 0.2 °C cooler in April and about as warm in December, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.5 °C), averages 7.0 °C more than its coolest, December (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.0 °C (64 °F), in January. The area lies about 152 km from the sea.

Why Vazhapadi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 152 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vazhapadi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.655°N, 78.401°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-723-2584), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
