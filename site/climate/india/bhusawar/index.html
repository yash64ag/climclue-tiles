Bhusawar at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bhusawar is 2412th warmest (25.5 °C year average), 950th sunniest (about 2,750 hours of sunshine a year) and 3376th wettest (637 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mahwa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Bhusawar is about as warm in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.0 °C), averages 19.8 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 716 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Bhusawar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 716 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bhusawar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.039°N, 77.048°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-569-2570), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
