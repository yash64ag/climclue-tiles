Nashik at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Nashik is 478th warmest (23.8 °C year average), 123rd sunniest (about 2,758 hours of sunshine a year) and 147th wettest (1,409 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Malegaon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (99 km away), Nashik is 4.3 °C cooler in May and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 703 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.9 °C), averages 8.2 °C more than its coolest, January (19.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.9 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 115 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nashik, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nashik has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 115 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
