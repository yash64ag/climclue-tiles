Jaggayyapeta at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Jaggayyapeta is 72nd warmest (28.7 °C year average), 1457th sunniest (about 2,686 hours of sunshine a year) and 2357th wettest (934 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kodār, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Jaggayyapeta is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.4 °C warmer in December, with 63 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.0 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, December (24.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.7 °C (66 °F), in December. The area lies about 130 km from the sea.

Why Jaggayyapeta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 130 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jaggayyapeta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.894°N, 80.098°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-671-2600), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
