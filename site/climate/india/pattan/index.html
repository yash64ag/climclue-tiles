Pattan at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Pattan is 3719th warmest (12.6 °C year average), 881st sunniest (about 2,761 hours of sunshine a year) and 2927th wettest (785 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sumbal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Pattan is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 23.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,252 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Pattan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,252 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pattan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.161°N, 74.556°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-498-2545), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
