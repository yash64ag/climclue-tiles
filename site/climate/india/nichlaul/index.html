Nichlaul weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nichlaul, India (Uttar Pradesh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nichlaul at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Nichlaul is 3024th warmest (24.7 °C year average), 3296th sunniest (about 2,293 hours of sunshine a year) and 686th wettest (1,793 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Siswā Bāzār, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Nichlaul is 0.2 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 173 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (30.5 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, January (14.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.9 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 741 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Nichlaul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 741 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nichlaul itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.312°N, 83.725°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-566-2637), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
