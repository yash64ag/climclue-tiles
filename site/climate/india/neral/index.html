Neral at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Neral is 1393rd warmest (26.6 °C year average), 1149th sunniest (about 2,723 hours of sunshine a year) and 514th wettest (2,381 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Karjat, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Neral is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 419 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.6 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.3 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 57 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Neral, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Neral has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 57 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Neral itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.025°N, 73.317°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-649-2533), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
