Sirālkoppa at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Sirālkoppa is 3209th warmest (24.4 °C year average), 2004th sunniest (about 2,612 hours of sunshine a year) and 1140th wettest (1,379 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shikaripura, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Sirālkoppa is 0.2 °C cooler in April and 0.2 °C cooler in August, with 96 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.5 °C), averages 4.6 °C more than its coolest, August (22.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.8 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 97 km from the sea.

Why Sirālkoppa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 97 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sirālkoppa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.381°N, 75.250°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-696-2552), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
