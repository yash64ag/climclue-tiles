Agra at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Agra is 350th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 176th sunniest (about 2,707 hours of sunshine a year) and 433rd wettest (721 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fīrozābād, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Agra is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 52 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.9 °C), averages 19.8 °C more than its coolest, January (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.0 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 793 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Agra, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Agra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 793 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
