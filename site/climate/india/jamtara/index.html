Jāmtāra at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Jāmtāra is 2816th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 3623rd sunniest (about 2,157 hours of sunshine a year) and 939th wettest (1,510 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chittaranjan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Jāmtāra is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 21 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.5 °C), averages 14.1 °C more than its coolest, January (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.0 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 269 km from the sea.

Why Jāmtāra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 269 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jāmtāra itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.963°N, 86.803°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-600-2668), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
