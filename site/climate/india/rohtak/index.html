Rohtak at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Rohtak is 400th warmest (24.9 °C year average), 155th sunniest (about 2,727 hours of sunshine a year) and 481st wettest (621 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bahadurgarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (41 km away), Rohtak is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.6 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 851 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rohtak, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Rohtak has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 851 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-551-2565), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
