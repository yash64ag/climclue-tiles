Chengamanād at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Chengamanād is 1066th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 2611th sunniest (about 2,469 hours of sunshine a year) and 334th wettest (2,972 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Aluva, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Chengamanād is 0.1 °C warmer in March and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 21 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.7 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, July (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 31 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chengamanād, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chengamanād has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 31 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chengamanād itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.154°N, 76.342°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-738-2563), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
