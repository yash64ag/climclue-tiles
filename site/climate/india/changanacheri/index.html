Changanācheri at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Changanācheri is 875th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 2848th sunniest (about 2,435 hours of sunshine a year) and 256th wettest (3,098 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Paippad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Changanācheri is 0.1 °C warmer in March and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 45 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (29.4 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, July (26.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in December. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Why Changanācheri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Changanācheri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.442°N, 76.536°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-745-2565), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
