Kurseong at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kurseong is 3705th warmest (15.7 °C year average), 3489th sunniest (about 2,205 hours of sunshine a year) and 171st wettest (3,342 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dārjiling, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Kurseong is 1.7 °C warmer in August and 1.8 °C warmer in January, with 696 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.3 °C), averages 12.0 °C more than its coolest, January (8.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 545 km from the sea.

Why Kurseong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 545 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.0 °C. The 10 km area around Kurseong, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.2 °C a year, 4.6 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kurseong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.883°N, 88.277°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-571-2682), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
