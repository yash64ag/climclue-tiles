Mandoli at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Mandoli is 421st warmest (24.8 °C year average), 239th sunniest (about 2,675 hours of sunshine a year) and 470th wettest (643 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Loni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Mandoli is about as warm in June and about as warm in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.3 °C), averages 19.7 °C more than its coolest, January (13.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.6 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 883 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mandoli, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mandoli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 883 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mandoli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.703°N, 77.310°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-552-2573), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
