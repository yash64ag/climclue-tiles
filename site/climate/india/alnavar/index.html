Alnāvar at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Alnāvar is 3204th warmest (24.4 °C year average), 1376th sunniest (about 2,692 hours of sunshine a year) and 1720th wettest (1,135 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Haliyal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Alnāvar is 0.1 °C cooler in April and 0.2 °C cooler in December, with 91 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.3 °C), averages 4.6 °C more than its coolest, December (22.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 99 km from the sea.

Why Alnāvar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 99 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alnāvar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.427°N, 74.741°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-685-2547), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
