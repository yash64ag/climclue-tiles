Anantnag at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Anantnag is the coolest (12.1 °C year average). Compared with Srinagar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Anantnag is 0.9 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 101 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.2 °C), averages 21.7 °C more than its coolest, January (0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,256 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Anantnag, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Anantnag has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 1,256 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Anantnag itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.731°N, 75.154°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-502-2551), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
