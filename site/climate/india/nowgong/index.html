Nowgong at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Nowgong is 2085th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 1075th sunniest (about 2,734 hours of sunshine a year) and 1886th wettest (1,074 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chhatarpur, Madhya Pradesh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Nowgong is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 123 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.5 °C), averages 18.7 °C more than its coolest, January (15.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.5 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 783 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Nowgong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 783 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nowgong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.061°N, 79.441°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-589-2594), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
