Takhatgarh at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Takhatgarh is 1799th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 3,084 hours of sunshine a year) and 3669th wettest (435 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sumerpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Takhatgarh is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 82 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.5 °C), averages 16.4 °C more than its coolest, January (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 361 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Takhatgarh, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Takhatgarh

For mild weather, the best months to visit Takhatgarh are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (33.5 °C), June (33.3 °C) and April (30.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): March (1 mm), December (2 mm) and February (2 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (317 h), April (295 h) and October (292 h).

Why Takhatgarh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 361 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Takhatgarh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.322°N, 73.005°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-586-2530), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,285, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
