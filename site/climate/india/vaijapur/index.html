Vaijāpur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Vaijāpur is 2100th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 651st sunniest (about 2,789 hours of sunshine a year) and 2932nd wettest (784 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kopargaon, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Vaijāpur is 0.4 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C cooler in December, with 100 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.7 °C), averages 10.9 °C more than its coolest, December (20.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 216 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Vaijāpur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Vaijāpur

For mild weather, the best months to visit Vaijāpur are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (31.7 °C), April (30.9 °C) and June (28.5 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (1 mm), January (2 mm) and March (3 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (309 h), April (293 h) and March (285 h).

Why Vaijāpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 216 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vaijāpur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.927°N, 74.728°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-640-2547), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 41,296, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
