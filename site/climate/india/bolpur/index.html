Bolpur weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bolpur, India (West Bengal), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bolpur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bolpur is 2067th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 3667th sunniest (about 2,124 hours of sunshine a year) and 981st wettest (1,484 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guskhara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Bolpur is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 40 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.4 °C), averages 12.8 °C more than its coolest, January (17.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.4 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 213 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bolpur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bolpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 213 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bolpur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.663°N, 87.697°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-603-2676), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 70,998, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
