Bābra at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bābra is 999th warmest (27.0 °C year average), 141st sunniest (about 2,992 hours of sunshine a year) and 2933rd wettest (784 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lāthi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Bābra is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.5 °C), averages 12.0 °C more than its coolest, January (20.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.4 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 93 km from the sea.

Why Bābra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 93 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bābra itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.846°N, 71.305°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-621-2513), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
