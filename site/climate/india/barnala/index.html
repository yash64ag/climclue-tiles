Barnāla at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Barnāla is 466th warmest (24.1 °C year average), 96th sunniest (about 2,788 hours of sunshine a year) and 505th wettest (529 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Māler Kotla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Barnāla is 0.4 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 96 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.1 °C), averages 21.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 963 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Barnāla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Barnāla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 963 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
