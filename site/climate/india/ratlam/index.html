Ratlām at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Ratlām is 362nd warmest (25.2 °C year average), 47th sunniest (about 2,881 hours of sunshine a year) and 268th wettest (1,046 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nagda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (41 km away), Ratlām is 0.8 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 172 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.4 °C), averages 14.0 °C more than its coolest, January (18.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.1 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 298 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ratlām, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ratlām has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 298 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
