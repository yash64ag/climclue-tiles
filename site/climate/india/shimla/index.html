Shimla at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Shimla is the 5th coolest (14.5 °C year average). Compared with Chandigarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Shimla is 10.5 °C cooler in June and 7.8 °C cooler in January, with 787 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (21.6 °C), averages 16.8 °C more than its coolest, January (4.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 1,104 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shimla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shimla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,104 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
