Ambikāpur at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Ambikāpur is 480th warmest (23.8 °C year average), 383rd sunniest (about 2,462 hours of sunshine a year) and 93rd wettest (1,641 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chirmiri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (86 km away), Ambikāpur is about as warm in May and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 105 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.3 °C), averages 15.2 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.5 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 441 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ambikāpur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ambikāpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 441 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ambikāpur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.119°N, 83.195°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-608-2631), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
