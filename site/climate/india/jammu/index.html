Jammu weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Jammu, India (Jammu and Kashmir), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Jammu is warmest in June, with 38.7 °C (102 °F) days and 24.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 17.9 °C (64 °F) days and 5.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (448 mm); November is driest (16 mm).

Jammu at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Jammu is 3635th warmest (22.2 °C year average), 263rd sunniest (about 2,912 hours of sunshine a year) and 948th wettest (1,504 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jammu Cantonment, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Jammu is 0.3 °C cooler in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (31.4 °C), averages 20.0 °C more than its coolest, January (11.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,158 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jammu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Jammu

For mild weather, the best months to visit Jammu are March, April, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, April, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (31.4 °C), July (29.6 °C) and May (29.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (16 mm), October (25 mm) and May (41 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (301 h), June (283 h) and October (279 h).

Why Jammu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,158 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jammu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.735°N, 74.862°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-512-2548), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 576,198, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
