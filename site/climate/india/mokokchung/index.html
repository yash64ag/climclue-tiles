Mokokchūng weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Mokokchūng, India (Nagaland), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Mokokchūng at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Mokokchūng is 3681st warmest (18.0 °C year average), 3248th sunniest (about 2,327 hours of sunshine a year) and 201st wettest (3,211 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tuensang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Mokokchūng is 1.2 °C cooler in July and 1.5 °C cooler in January, with 636 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.7 °C), averages 11.8 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 517 km from the sea.

Why Mokokchūng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 517 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.8 °C. The 10 km area around Mokokchūng, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.0 °C a year, 1.0 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mokokchūng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.325°N, 94.518°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-576-2745), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
