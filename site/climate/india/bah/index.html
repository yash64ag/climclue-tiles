Bāh weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bāh, India (Uttar Pradesh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bāh is warmest in June, with 39.9 °C (104 °F) days and 27.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 22.0 °C (72 °F) days and 6.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (227 mm); April is driest (6 mm).

Bāh at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bāh is 2552nd warmest (25.3 °C year average), 1315th sunniest (about 2,700 hours of sunshine a year) and 2929th wettest (784 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pināhat, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Bāh is 0.1 °C cooler in June and about as warm in January, with 28 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.8 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.7 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 809 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bāh, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bāh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 809 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bāh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.869°N, 78.594°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-571-2585), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
