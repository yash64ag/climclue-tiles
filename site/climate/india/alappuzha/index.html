Alappuzha at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Alappuzha is 138th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 388th sunniest (about 2,442 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (2,917 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kochi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Alappuzha is 0.3 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 75 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.3 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, August (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.1 °C (75 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alappuzha, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Alappuzha has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alappuzha itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.490°N, 76.326°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-745-2563), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
