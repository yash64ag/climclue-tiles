Chhiri at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Chhiri is 1641st warmest (26.4 °C year average), 599th sunniest (about 2,800 hours of sunshine a year) and 594th wettest (2,081 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vapi INA, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Chhiri is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.3 °C), averages 7.0 °C more than its coolest, January (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.1 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 24 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Chhiri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chhiri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.378°N, 72.941°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-636-2529), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
