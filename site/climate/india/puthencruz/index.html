Puthencruz at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Puthencruz is 1428th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 2702nd sunniest (about 2,459 hours of sunshine a year) and 245th wettest (3,114 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thiruvankulam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Puthencruz is 0.2 °C warmer in March and about as warm in July, with 88 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.7 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, July (25.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.6 °C (71 °F), in December. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Puthencruz, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Puthencruz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Puthencruz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.977°N, 76.410°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-740-2564), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
