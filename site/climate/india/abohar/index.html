Abohar at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Abohar is 355th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 41st sunniest (about 2,908 hours of sunshine a year) and 520th wettest (386 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sri Ganganagar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Abohar is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 61 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.1 °C), averages 21.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 900 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Abohar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Abohar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 900 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
