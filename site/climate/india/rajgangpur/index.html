Rajgangpur weather by month and climate

Rajgangpur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Rajgangpur is 1842nd warmest (26.1 °C year average), 3089th sunniest (about 2,386 hours of sunshine a year) and 916th wettest (1,529 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jalda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Rajgangpur is 0.3 °C warmer in May and about as warm in January, with 84 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.4 °C), averages 13.0 °C more than its coolest, January (19.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.1 °C (54 °F), in January. The area lies about 272 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rajgangpur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Rajgangpur

For mild weather, the best months to visit Rajgangpur are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (32.4 °C), April (31.2 °C) and June (30.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (12 mm), November (17 mm) and March (24 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (253 h), May (246 h) and March (243 h).

Why Rajgangpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 272 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rajgangpur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.200°N, 84.583°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-617-2645), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
