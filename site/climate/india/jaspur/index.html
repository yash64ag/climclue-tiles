Jaspur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Jaspur is 3482nd warmest (23.6 °C year average), 1975th sunniest (about 2,617 hours of sunshine a year) and 1701st wettest (1,140 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thakurdwara, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Jaspur is 0.2 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 31 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (31.4 °C), averages 18.0 °C more than its coolest, January (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.5 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,017 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Jaspur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,017 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jaspur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.279°N, 78.828°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-547-2588), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
