Chinnachowk weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Chinnachowk, India (Andhra Pradesh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Chinnachowk is warmest in May, with 39.8 °C (104 °F) days and 27.6 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 29.0 °C (84 °F) days and 18.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around October (140 mm); February is driest (2 mm).

Chinnachowk at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Chinnachowk is 57th warmest (28.9 °C year average), 1996th sunniest (about 2,614 hours of sunshine a year) and 3280th wettest (660 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kadapa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Chinnachowk is about as warm in May and about as warm in December, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.7 °C), averages 9.8 °C more than its coolest, December (23.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.6 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 147 km from the sea.

Why Chinnachowk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 147 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chinnachowk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.475°N, 78.835°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-695-2588), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
