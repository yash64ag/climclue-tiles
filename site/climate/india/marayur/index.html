Marayur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Marayur is 3639th warmest (21.9 °C year average), 3477th sunniest (about 2,213 hours of sunshine a year) and 618th wettest (2,022 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Munnar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Marayur is 3.3 °C warmer in March and 3.2 °C warmer in December, with 1,007 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (23.8 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, December (20.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.2 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 114 km from the sea.

Why Marayur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 114 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C. The 10 km area around Marayur, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 19.6 °C a year, 2.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Marayur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.276°N, 77.162°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-737-2571), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
