Bhadgaon at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bhadgaon is 791st warmest (27.2 °C year average), 516th sunniest (about 2,823 hours of sunshine a year) and 2701st wettest (847 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pāchora, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Bhadgaon is about as warm in May and about as warm in December, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.8 °C), averages 12.0 °C more than its coolest, December (21.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.0 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 250 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bhadgaon, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bhadgaon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 250 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bhadgaon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.669°N, 75.229°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-633-2552), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
