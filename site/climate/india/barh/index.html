Bārh at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bārh is 2109th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 3506th sunniest (about 2,200 hours of sunshine a year) and 1826th wettest (1,091 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bakhtiyārpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Bārh is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 43 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.9 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, January (16.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.6 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 467 km from the sea.

Why Bārh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 467 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bārh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.483°N, 85.709°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-585-2657), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
