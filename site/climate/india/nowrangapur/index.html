Nowrangapur at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Nowrangapur is 451st warmest (24.5 °C year average), 352nd sunniest (about 2,533 hours of sunshine a year) and 114th wettest (1,528 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jagdalpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Nowrangapur is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.8 °C cooler in December, with 36 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.9 °C), averages 11.1 °C more than its coolest, December (18.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.5 °C (55 °F), in December. The area lies about 184 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nowrangapur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nowrangapur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 184 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nowrangapur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.231°N, 82.548°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-647-2625), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
