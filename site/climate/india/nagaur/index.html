Why Nāgaur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 582 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Nāgaur climate: questions and answers

June, with an average of 34.3 °C (94 °F), day highs around 39.4 °C (103 °F) and night lows around 28.5 °C (83 °F).
What is the coldest month in Nāgaur?
January, with an average of 15.1 °C (59 °F), day highs around 22.1 °C (72 °F) and night lows around 8.0 °C (46 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Nāgaur?

What is the driest month in Nāgaur?

What is the sunniest month in Nāgaur?
May, with about 298 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Nāgaur?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Nāgaur?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.9 × 11 km, that contains Nāgaur’s GeoNames location (27.202°N, 73.734°E; ClimaClue area sq-567-2537), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 105,218 (GeoNames).
