Nāgaur at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Nāgaur is the 5th driest (344 mm a year). Compared with Sūjāngarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (91 km away), Nāgaur is about as warm in June and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.3 °C), averages 19.2 °C more than its coolest, January (15.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.0 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 582 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nāgaur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nāgaur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 582 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
