Gotan weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Gotan, India (Rajasthan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Gotan at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Gotan is 2261st warmest (25.6 °C year average), 117th sunniest (about 3,015 hours of sunshine a year) and 3680th wettest (425 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Merta Road, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Gotan is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.5 °C), averages 18.1 °C more than its coolest, January (15.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.1 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 517 km from the sea.

Why Gotan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 517 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gotan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.656°N, 73.737°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-573-2537), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 17,700, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
