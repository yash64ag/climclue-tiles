Umarga at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Umarga is 1725th warmest (26.3 °C year average), 1081st sunniest (about 2,733 hours of sunshine a year) and 2545th wettest (880 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Moram, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Umarga is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.5 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, December (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.0 °C (59 °F), in December. The area lies about 377 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Umarga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Umarga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 377 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Umarga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.838°N, 76.623°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-661-2566), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
