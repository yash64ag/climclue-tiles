Ghātampur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Ghātampur is 2384th warmest (25.5 °C year average), 1473rd sunniest (about 2,685 hours of sunshine a year) and 2480th wettest (897 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hamīrpur, Uttar Pradesh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Ghātampur is 0.5 °C cooler in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 17 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.5 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (15.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.4 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 881 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ghātampur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 881 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ghātampur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.153°N, 80.168°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-578-2601), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
