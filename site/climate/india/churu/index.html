Why Chūru has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 734 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

January, with an average of 14.3 °C (58 °F), day highs around 20.5 °C (69 °F) and night lows around 7.6 °C (46 °F).

What is the sunniest month in Chūru?

Does it snow in Chūru?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Chūru?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: February, March, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.8 × 11 km, that contains Chūru’s GeoNames location (28.304°N, 74.967°E; ClimaClue area sq-556-2549), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 120,157 (GeoNames).
