Bālurghāt at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bālurghāt is 2747th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 3626th sunniest (about 2,156 hours of sunshine a year) and 668th wettest (1,836 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gangārāmpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Bālurghāt is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 16 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.8 °C), averages 11.8 °C more than its coolest, January (17.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.2 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 368 km from the sea.

Why Bālurghāt has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 368 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bālurghāt itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.221°N, 88.777°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-587-2687), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
