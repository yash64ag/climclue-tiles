Satara at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Satara is 488th warmest (23.7 °C year average), 146th sunniest (about 2,737 hours of sunshine a year) and 145th wettest (1,402 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shahuwadi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (86 km away), Satara is 0.4 °C warmer in April and 1.4 °C cooler in December, with 1,061 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.3 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, December (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.7 °C (58 °F), in January. The area lies about 95 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Satara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Satara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 95 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-663-2539), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
