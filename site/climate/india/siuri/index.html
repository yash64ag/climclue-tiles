Siuri at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Siuri is 2298th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 3661st sunniest (about 2,128 hours of sunshine a year) and 946th wettest (1,505 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sainthia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Siuri is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.4 °C), averages 13.2 °C more than its coolest, January (17.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.9 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 248 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Siuri, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Siuri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 248 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Siuri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.908°N, 87.528°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-600-2675), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
