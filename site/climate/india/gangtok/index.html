Gangtok weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Gangtok, India (Sikkim), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Gangtok is warmest in August, with 25.5 °C (78 °F) days and 18.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 15.9 °C (61 °F) days and 4.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (1,037 mm); December is driest (48 mm).

Gangtok at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Gangtok is 3688th warmest (17.2 °C year average), 3325th sunniest (about 2,275 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (4,323 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kālimpong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Gangtok is 1.7 °C warmer in August and 1.6 °C warmer in January, with 400 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.0 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, January (10.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 578 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gangtok, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gangtok has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 578 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C. The 10 km area around Gangtok, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 14.1 °C a year, 3.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gangtok itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.326°N, 88.612°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-566-2686), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
