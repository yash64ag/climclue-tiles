Mukandgarh at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Mukandgarh is 2425th warmest (25.4 °C year average), 411th sunniest (about 2,849 hours of sunshine a year) and 3657th wettest (444 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nawalgarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Mukandgarh is 0.4 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.8 °C), averages 19.6 °C more than its coolest, January (14.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.5 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 704 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Mukandgarh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 704 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mukandgarh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.952°N, 75.224°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-560-2552), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
