Bhāyāvadar weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bhāyāvadar, India (Gujarat), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bhāyāvadar at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bhāyāvadar is 1384th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 71st sunniest (about 3,067 hours of sunshine a year) and 2971st wettest (771 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Upleta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Bhāyāvadar is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.9 °C), averages 10.3 °C more than its coolest, January (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.1 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 76 km from the sea.

Why Bhāyāvadar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 76 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bhāyāvadar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.855°N, 70.248°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-621-2502), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
