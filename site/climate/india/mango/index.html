Mango weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Mango, India (Jharkhand), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Mango at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Mango is 1989th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 3470th sunniest (about 2,216 hours of sunshine a year) and 958th wettest (1,500 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kopali, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Mango is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.5 °C), averages 13.2 °C more than its coolest, January (18.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.8 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 181 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mango, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mango has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 181 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mango itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.828°N, 86.216°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-611-2662), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 223,805, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
