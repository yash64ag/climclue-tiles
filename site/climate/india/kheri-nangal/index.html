Kheri Nāngal at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kheri Nāngal is 3230th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 1307th sunniest (about 2,701 hours of sunshine a year) and 3032nd wettest (752 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Panipat Taraf Rajputan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Kheri Nāngal is about as warm in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.9 °C), averages 20.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 918 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kheri Nāngal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 918 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kheri Nāngal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.354°N, 76.977°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-546-2569), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
