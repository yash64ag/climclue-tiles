Kollam at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Kollam is 112th warmest (27.0 °C year average), 401st sunniest (about 2,415 hours of sunshine a year) and 34th wettest (2,425 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thiruvananthapuram, the nearest covered town with different numbers (59 km away), Kollam is 0.7 °C warmer in April and 0.2 °C warmer in August, with 553 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.4 °C), averages 2.6 °C more than its coolest, August (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.4 °C (76 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kollam, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kollam has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
