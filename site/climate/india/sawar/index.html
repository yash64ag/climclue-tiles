Sawar at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Sawar is 1884th warmest (26.1 °C year average), 292nd sunniest (about 2,897 hours of sunshine a year) and 3463rd wettest (607 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Deoli, Rajasthan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Sawar is 0.1 °C cooler in May and about as warm in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.8 °C), averages 18.8 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 491 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sawar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sawar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 491 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sawar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.756°N, 75.223°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-582-2552), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
