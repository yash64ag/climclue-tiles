Devadurga at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Devadurga is 438th warmest (27.8 °C year average), 1322nd sunniest (about 2,699 hours of sunshine a year) and 3130th wettest (714 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shorāpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Devadurga is 0.6 °C warmer in May and 0.6 °C warmer in December, with 66 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.2 °C), averages 9.6 °C more than its coolest, December (23.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.4 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 357 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Devadurga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Devadurga

For mild weather, the best months to visit Devadurga are January and December.

Why Devadurga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 357 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Devadurga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.423°N, 76.936°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-675-2569), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 28,929, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
