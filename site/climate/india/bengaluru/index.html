Bengaluru at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Bengaluru is 498th warmest (23.4 °C year average), 416th sunniest (about 2,374 hours of sunshine a year) and 347th wettest (895 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yelahanka, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Bengaluru is 0.1 °C cooler in April and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 48 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.9 °C), averages 6.4 °C more than its coolest, December (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.8 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 270 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bengaluru, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bengaluru has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 270 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
