Sivakasi at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Sivakasi is 22nd warmest (29.1 °C year average), 2945th sunniest (about 2,418 hours of sunshine a year) and 2700th wettest (847 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Viswanatham, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Sivakasi is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.9 °C), averages 6.1 °C more than its coolest, December (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 86 km from the sea.

Why Sivakasi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 86 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sivakasi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.450°N, 77.798°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-745-2577), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
