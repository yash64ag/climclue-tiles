Rājpīpla weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Rājpīpla, India (Gujarat), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Rājpīpla is warmest in May, with 39.2 °C (103 °F) days and 25.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 30.3 °C (87 °F) days and 13.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (380 mm); March is driest (1 mm).

Rājpīpla at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Rājpīpla is 661st warmest (27.3 °C year average), 306th sunniest (about 2,891 hours of sunshine a year) and 1804th wettest (1,100 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dabhoi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Rājpīpla is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.4 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, January (21.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.0 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 109 km from the sea.

Why Rājpīpla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 109 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rājpīpla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.867°N, 73.500°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-621-2535), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
