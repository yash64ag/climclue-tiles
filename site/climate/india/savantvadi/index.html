Sāvantvādi at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Sāvantvādi is 1813th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 742nd sunniest (about 2,780 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (4,309 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kudāl, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Sāvantvādi is 0.5 °C cooler in May and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 1,120 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (28.6 °C), averages 3.8 °C more than its coolest, January (24.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.7 °C (66 °F), in January. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Why Sāvantvādi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sāvantvādi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.904°N, 73.822°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-680-2538), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
