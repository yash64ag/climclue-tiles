Pāchchal at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Pāchchal is 1571st warmest (26.5 °C year average), 2529th sunniest (about 2,483 hours of sunshine a year) and 2331st wettest (942 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tirupattur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Pāchchal is about as warm in May and about as warm in December, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.8 °C), averages 7.1 °C more than its coolest, December (22.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.9 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 166 km from the sea.

Why Pāchchal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 166 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pāchchal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.511°N, 78.555°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-714-2585), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
