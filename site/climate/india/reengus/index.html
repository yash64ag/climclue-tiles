Reengus at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Reengus is 3119th warmest (24.6 °C year average), 495th sunniest (about 2,830 hours of sunshine a year) and 3566th wettest (538 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sri Mādhopur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Reengus is 0.2 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.6 °C), averages 18.6 °C more than its coolest, January (13.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 657 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Reengus, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Reengus has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 657 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Reengus itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.364°N, 75.568°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-566-2555), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
