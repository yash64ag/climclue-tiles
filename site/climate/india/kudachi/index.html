Kudachi weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kudachi, India (Karnataka), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kudachi is warmest in April, with 37.8 °C (100 °F) days and 23.2 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 30.2 °C (86 °F) days and 16.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (116 mm); February is driest (1 mm).

Kudachi at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kudachi is 1825th warmest (26.2 °C year average), 1069th sunniest (about 2,735 hours of sunshine a year) and 3458th wettest (609 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shiraguppi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Kudachi is 0.6 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in December, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (30.5 °C), averages 7.1 °C more than its coolest, December (23.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.4 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 165 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kudachi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kudachi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 165 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kudachi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.628°N, 74.854°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-673-2548), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
