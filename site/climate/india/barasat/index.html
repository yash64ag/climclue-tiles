Bārāsat at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bārāsat is the 3rd least sunny (about 2,077 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Madhyamgram, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Bārāsat is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.1 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, January (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.6 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 123 km from the sea.

Why Bārāsat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 123 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bārāsat itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.722°N, 88.482°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-612-2684), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
