Fatwa at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Fatwa is 2114th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 3460th sunniest (about 2,218 hours of sunshine a year) and 2092nd wettest (1,013 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sabalpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Fatwa is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.3 °C), averages 16.2 °C more than its coolest, January (16.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.6 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 491 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Fatwa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Fatwa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 491 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Fatwa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.510°N, 85.305°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-584-2653), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 50,961, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
