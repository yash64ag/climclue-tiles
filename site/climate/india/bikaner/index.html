Bikaner at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Bikaner is the driest (267 mm a year). Compared with Nāgaur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (100 km away), Bikaner is 1.0 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 78 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.3 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.9 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 657 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bikaner, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bikaner has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 657 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
