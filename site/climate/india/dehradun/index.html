Dehradun at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Dehradun is 517th warmest (20.9 °C year average), 309th sunniest (about 2,618 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (2,513 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Haridwar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Dehradun is 3.0 °C cooler in June and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 884 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.1 °C), averages 16.7 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,069 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dehradun, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dehradun has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,069 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
