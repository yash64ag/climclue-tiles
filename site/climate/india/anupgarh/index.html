Anūpgarh at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Anūpgarh is the 4th driest (248 mm a year). Compared with Rāisinghnagar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Anūpgarh is 1.1 °C warmer in June and 0.8 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.5 °C), averages 21.6 °C more than its coolest, January (13.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 762 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Anūpgarh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 762 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Anūpgarh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.191°N, 73.209°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-548-2532), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
