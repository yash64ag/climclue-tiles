Budhlāda at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Budhlāda is 3106th warmest (24.6 °C year average), 632nd sunniest (about 2,793 hours of sunshine a year) and 3601st wettest (500 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bareta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Budhlāda is about as warm in June and about as warm in January, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. The area lies about 921 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Budhlāda has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 921 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Budhlāda itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.928°N, 75.562°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-540-2555), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
