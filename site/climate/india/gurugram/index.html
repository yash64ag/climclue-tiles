Gurugram at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Gurugram is 391st warmest (24.9 °C year average), 195th sunniest (about 2,695 hours of sunshine a year) and 472nd wettest (632 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Najafgarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Gurugram is about as warm in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.5 °C), averages 20.0 °C more than its coolest, January (13.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.6 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 839 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gurugram, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Gurugram has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 839 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
