Udupi at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Udupi is the wettest (4,863 mm a year). Compared with Mangaluru, the nearest covered town with different numbers (48 km away), Udupi is about as warm in April and 0.1 °C cooler in August, with 1,038 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.9 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, August (25.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Udupi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Udupi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Udupi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.335°N, 74.746°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-706-2547), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
