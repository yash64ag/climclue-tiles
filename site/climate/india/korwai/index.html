Korwai at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Korwai is 2522nd warmest (25.3 °C year average), 760th sunniest (about 2,777 hours of sunshine a year) and 1858th wettest (1,083 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bīna, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Korwai is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 55 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.2 °C), averages 17.8 °C more than its coolest, January (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.2 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 614 km from the sea (deep inland).

For mild weather, the best months to visit Korwai are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.

Why Korwai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 614 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Korwai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.118°N, 78.040°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-598-2580), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,136, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
