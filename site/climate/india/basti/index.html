Bastī at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Bastī is 352nd warmest (25.2 °C year average), 386th sunniest (about 2,448 hours of sunshine a year) and 170th wettest (1,293 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fyzābād, the nearest covered town with different numbers (56 km away), Bastī is 0.2 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 140 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.0 °C), averages 16.9 °C more than its coolest, January (15.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.5 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 739 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bastī, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bastī has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 739 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-572-2627), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
