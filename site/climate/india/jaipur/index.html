Jaipur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Jaipur is 2864th warmest (24.9 °C year average), 490th sunniest (about 2,831 hours of sunshine a year) and 3386th wettest (634 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bagru, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Jaipur is 0.5 °C cooler in May and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 48 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.1 °C), averages 18.8 °C more than its coolest, January (14.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.9 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 629 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jaipur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Jaipur

For mild weather, the best months to visit Jaipur are February, March, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (33.1 °C), June (32.9 °C) and July (29.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (4 mm), March (5 mm) and November (7 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (288 h), April (279 h) and October (273 h).

Why Jaipur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 629 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jaipur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.920°N, 75.788°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-570-2557), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 3,046,163, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
