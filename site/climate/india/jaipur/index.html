Jaipur at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Jaipur is 407th warmest (24.8 °C year average), 73rd sunniest (about 2,831 hours of sunshine a year) and 475th wettest (628 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tonk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (84 km away), Jaipur is 1.8 °C cooler in May and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.0 °C), averages 18.7 °C more than its coolest, January (14.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.0 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 629 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jaipur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Jaipur

For mild weather, the best months to visit Jaipur are February, March, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (33.0 °C), June (32.9 °C) and July (29.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (4 mm), March (5 mm) and November (7 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (288 h), April (279 h) and October (273 h).

Why Jaipur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 629 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
