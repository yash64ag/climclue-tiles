Kātrās at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kātrās is 2680th warmest (25.2 °C year average), 3511th sunniest (about 2,197 hours of sunshine a year) and 1065th wettest (1,432 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sijua, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Kātrās is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.2 °C), averages 14.7 °C more than its coolest, January (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.4 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 272 km from the sea.

Why Kātrās has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 272 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kātrās itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.798°N, 86.298°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-602-2662), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
