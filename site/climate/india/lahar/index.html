Lahār weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Lahār, India (Madhya Pradesh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Lahār is warmest in June, with 40.2 °C (104 °F) days and 28.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 22.6 °C (73 °F) days and 7.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (239 mm); April is driest (5 mm).

Lahār at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Lahār is 2300th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 1196th sunniest (about 2,719 hours of sunshine a year) and 2936th wettest (783 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mihona, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Lahār is 0.1 °C warmer in June and about as warm in January, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.1 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, January (14.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 795 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lahār, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lahār has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 795 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lahār itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.194°N, 78.941°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-578-2589), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 35,674, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
