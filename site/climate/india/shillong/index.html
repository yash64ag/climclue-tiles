Shillong at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Shillong is the 2nd wettest (4,505 mm a year). Compared with Guwahati, the nearest covered town with different numbers (70 km away), Shillong is 8.0 °C cooler in July and 8.4 °C cooler in January, with 2,398 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.4 °C), averages 11.2 °C more than its coolest, January (9.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 325 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shillong, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shillong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 325 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shillong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.569°N, 91.883°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-584-2718), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
