Sumbal weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Sumbal, India (Jammu and Kashmir), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Sumbal is warmest in July, with 29.3 °C (85 °F) days and 17.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 4.1 °C (39 °F) days and -4.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (112 mm); October is driest (26 mm).

Sumbal at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Sumbal is 3716th warmest (12.9 °C year average), 970th sunniest (about 2,747 hours of sunshine a year) and 2968th wettest (771 mm a year), near the middle on each. Why Sumbal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,266 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sumbal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.231°N, 74.647°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-497-2546), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
