Kailāras weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kailāras, India (Madhya Pradesh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kailāras is warmest in June, with 40.3 °C (105 °F) days and 28.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 22.7 °C (73 °F) days and 7.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (207 mm); March is driest (5 mm).

Kailāras at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kailāras is 2044th warmest (25.8 °C year average), 924th sunniest (about 2,754 hours of sunshine a year) and 3364th wettest (641 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jora, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Kailāras is about as warm in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 67 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.2 °C), averages 19.0 °C more than its coolest, January (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 704 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kailāras has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 704 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kailāras itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.305°N, 77.616°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-576-2576), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
