Belsand at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Belsand is 2427th warmest (25.4 °C year average), 3553rd sunniest (about 2,179 hours of sunshine a year) and 1146th wettest (1,376 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sheohar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Belsand is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.3 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, January (15.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.0 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 581 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Belsand, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Belsand has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 581 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Belsand itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.444°N, 85.401°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-575-2654), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,566, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
