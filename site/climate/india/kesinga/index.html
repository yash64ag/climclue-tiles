Kesinga weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kesinga, India (Odisha), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kesinga at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kesinga is 588th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 2346th sunniest (about 2,528 hours of sunshine a year) and 1107th wettest (1,405 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Titlāgarh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Kesinga is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.4 °C warmer in December, with 58 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.3 °C), averages 13.0 °C more than its coolest, December (21.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.3 °C (58 °F), in December. The area lies about 207 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kesinga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kesinga

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kesinga are January, November and December.

Why Kesinga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 207 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kesinga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.188°N, 83.219°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-638-2632), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
