Malegaon weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Malegaon, India (Maharashtra), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Malegaon at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Malegaon is 1612th warmest (26.4 °C year average), 572nd sunniest (about 2,808 hours of sunshine a year) and 3170th wettest (700 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dyāne, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Malegaon is about as warm in May and about as warm in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.3 °C), averages 10.9 °C more than its coolest, January (21.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.9 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 178 km from the sea.

Why Malegaon has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 178 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Malegaon itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.550°N, 74.535°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-634-2545), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
