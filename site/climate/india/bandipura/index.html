Bandipura weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bandipura, India (Jammu and Kashmir), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bandipura is warmest in July, with 28.4 °C (83 °F) days and 17.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.5 °C (37 °F) days and -5.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around March (109 mm); November is driest (26 mm).

Bandipura at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Bandipura is 3731st warmest (11.8 °C year average), 1296th sunniest (about 2,703 hours of sunshine a year) and 3024th wettest (755 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sumbal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Bandipura is 0.8 °C cooler in July and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.7 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,283 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Bandipura has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 1,283 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C. The 10 km area around Bandipura, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 9.6 °C a year, 2.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bandipura itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.417°N, 74.643°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-495-2546), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
