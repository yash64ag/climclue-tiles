Thiruvarur weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Thiruvarur, India (Tamil Nadu), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Thiruvarur is warmest in May, with 35.3 °C (96 °F) days and 26.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 28.0 °C (82 °F) days and 22.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around November (393 mm); March is driest (19 mm).

Thiruvarur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Thiruvarur is 201st warmest (28.3 °C year average), 2322nd sunniest (about 2,533 hours of sunshine a year) and 1243rd wettest (1,329 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Koothanallur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Thiruvarur is 0.7 °C cooler in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 91 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (31.0 °C), averages 5.9 °C more than its coolest, January (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.1 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Thiruvarur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Thiruvarur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Thiruvarur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.773°N, 79.637°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-732-2596), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
