Why Rāmpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 996 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Rāmpur climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Rāmpur?
June, with an average of 31.9 °C (89 °F), day highs around 36.9 °C (98 °F) and night lows around 26.3 °C (79 °F).
What is the coldest month in Rāmpur?
January, with an average of 13.9 °C (57 °F), day highs around 20.5 °C (69 °F) and night lows around 8.1 °C (47 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Rāmpur?
July, with 367 mm (14.4 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,183 mm (46.6 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Rāmpur?
November, with 4 mm (0.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Rāmpur?
April, with about 264 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Rāmpur?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Rāmpur?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: February, March, October, November and December.
What type of climate does Rāmpur have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.8 × 11 km, that contains Rāmpur’s GeoNames location (28.810°N, 79.027°E; ClimaClue area sq-551-2590), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 296,418 (GeoNames).
