Rāmpur at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Rāmpur is 457th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 311th sunniest (about 2,615 hours of sunshine a year) and 197th wettest (1,200 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Morādābād, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Rāmpur is 0.2 °C cooler in June and about as warm in January, with 47 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (31.9 °C), averages 18.0 °C more than its coolest, January (13.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.2 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 996 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Rāmpur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 996 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers
