Mannachanallur weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Mannachanallur, India (Tamil Nadu), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Mannachanallur is warmest in May, with 37.4 °C (99 °F) days and 27.7 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 29.0 °C (84 °F) days and 21.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around October (172 mm); January is driest (12 mm).

Mannachanallur at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Mannachanallur is 38th warmest (29.1 °C year average), 2484th sunniest (about 2,495 hours of sunshine a year) and 2743rd wettest (838 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bhikshāndārkovil, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Mannachanallur is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C cooler in December, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.5 °C), averages 7.3 °C more than its coolest, December (25.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.1 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 117 km from the sea.

Why Mannachanallur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 117 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mannachanallur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.910°N, 78.699°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-730-2586), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
