Kālka at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kālka is 3640th warmest (21.8 °C year average), 1367th sunniest (about 2,693 hours of sunshine a year) and 836th wettest (1,611 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pinjaur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Kālka is 1.2 °C cooler in June and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 174 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (29.8 °C), averages 18.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,066 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kālka, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kālka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,066 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.1 °C. The 10 km area around Kālka, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.1 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kālka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.840°N, 76.941°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-531-2569), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 34,134, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
