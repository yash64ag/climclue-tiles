Nūzvīd weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nūzvīd, India (Andhra Pradesh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nūzvīd is warmest in May, with 37.9 °C (100 °F) days and 26.9 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 28.8 °C (84 °F) days and 18.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (249 mm); January is driest (5 mm).

Nūzvīd at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Nūzvīd is 443rd warmest (27.7 °C year average), 1746th sunniest (about 2,659 hours of sunshine a year) and 1676th wettest (1,147 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gannavaram, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Nūzvīd is 0.6 °C cooler in May and 0.5 °C cooler in December, with 87 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.4 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, December (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.6 °C (65 °F), in December. The area lies about 77 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nūzvīd, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nūzvīd has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 77 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nūzvīd itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (16.789°N, 80.846°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-672-2608), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
