Chāmpa at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Chāmpa is 1361st warmest (26.7 °C year average), 2255th sunniest (about 2,549 hours of sunshine a year) and 1043rd wettest (1,448 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jānjgīr, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Chāmpa is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 37 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.5 °C), averages 14.8 °C more than its coolest, December (19.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.7 °C (55 °F), in December. The area lies about 380 km from the sea.

Why Chāmpa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 380 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chāmpa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.035°N, 82.642°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-619-2626), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
