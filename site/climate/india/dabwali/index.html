Dabwāli at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Dabwāli is 2699th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 384th sunniest (about 2,859 hours of sunshine a year) and 3659th wettest (442 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mandi Dabwāli, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Dabwāli is about as warm in June and about as warm in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.0 °C), averages 21.0 °C more than its coolest, January (13.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.4 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 897 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Dabwāli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 897 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dabwāli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.949°N, 74.738°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-540-2547), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
