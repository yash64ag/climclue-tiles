Ranchi weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ranchi, India (Jharkhand), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ranchi is warmest in May, with 36.2 °C (97 °F) days and 22.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 22.1 °C (72 °F) days and 9.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (363 mm); November is driest (10 mm).

Ranchi at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Ranchi is 3582nd warmest (23.2 °C year average), 3385th sunniest (about 2,243 hours of sunshine a year) and 1095th wettest (1,413 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kānke, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Ranchi is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 102 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.4 °C), averages 13.9 °C more than its coolest, January (15.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.0 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 281 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ranchi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ranchi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 281 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ranchi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.343°N, 85.309°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-606-2653), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
