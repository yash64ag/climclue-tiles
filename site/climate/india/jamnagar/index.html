Jamnagar at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Jamnagar is the 3rd sunniest (about 3,141 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Gāndhīdhām, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Jamnagar is 0.5 °C cooler in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 141 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (30.6 °C), averages 10.1 °C more than its coolest, January (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.6 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jamnagar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jamnagar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
