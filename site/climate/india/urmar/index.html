Urmar at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Urmar is 3566th warmest (23.3 °C year average), 659th sunniest (about 2,789 hours of sunshine a year) and 2011th wettest (1,035 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bhogpur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Urmar is 0.1 °C cooler in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 111 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.3 °C), averages 20.2 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.2 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,103 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Urmar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,103 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Urmar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.681°N, 75.635°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-523-2556), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
