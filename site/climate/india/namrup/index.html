Nāmrup weather by month and climate

Nāmrup at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Nāmrup is 3399th warmest (23.9 °C year average), 3176th sunniest (about 2,371 hours of sunshine a year) and 466th wettest (2,569 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nahorkatiya, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Nāmrup is about as warm in August and about as warm in January, with 156 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.5 °C), averages 11.4 °C more than its coolest, January (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.3 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 636 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Nāmrup has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 636 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nāmrup itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.194°N, 95.319°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-568-2753), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
