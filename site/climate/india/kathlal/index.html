Kathlāl weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kathlāl, India (Gujarat), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kathlāl is warmest in May, with 40.9 °C (106 °F) days and 25.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 28.3 °C (83 °F) days and 11.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (392 mm); March is driest (1 mm).

Kathlāl at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kathlāl is 894th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 165th sunniest (about 2,972 hours of sunshine a year) and 2195th wettest (982 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mahudha, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Kathlāl is about as warm in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (33.4 °C), averages 13.3 °C more than its coolest, January (20.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 93 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kathlāl, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kathlāl has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 93 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kathlāl itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.898°N, 72.993°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-611-2529), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
