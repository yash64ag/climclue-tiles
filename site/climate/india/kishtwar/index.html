Kishtwār weather by month and climate

Jammu and Kashmir · India

Kishtwār at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kishtwār is 3737th warmest (10.7 °C year average), 1193rd sunniest (about 2,719 hours of sunshine a year) and 1811th wettest (1,098 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bunjwah, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Kishtwār is 2.4 °C warmer in July and 5.1 °C warmer in January, with 820 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.5 °C), averages 19.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,265 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kishtwār has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,265 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 11.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kishtwār itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.313°N, 75.767°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-506-2557), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,553, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
