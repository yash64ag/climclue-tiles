Surat at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Surat is 103rd warmest (27.0 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 2,865 hours of sunshine a year) and 138th wettest (1,443 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Navsari, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Surat is about as warm in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 184 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (30.4 °C), averages 8.2 °C more than its coolest, January (22.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.2 °C (63 °F), in January. The area lies about 25 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Surat, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Surat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 25 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
