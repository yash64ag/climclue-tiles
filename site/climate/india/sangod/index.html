Sangod at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Sangod is 1432nd warmest (26.6 °C year average), 405th sunniest (about 2,852 hours of sunshine a year) and 2362nd wettest (933 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Anta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Sangod is about as warm in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (35.6 °C), averages 18.6 °C more than its coolest, January (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.7 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 497 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sangod, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sangod has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 497 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sangod itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.927°N, 76.286°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-590-2562), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
