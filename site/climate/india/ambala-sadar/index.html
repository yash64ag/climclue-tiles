Ambala Sadar at a glance

Among the 526 towns of 100,000+ people covered in India, Ambala Sadar is 477th warmest (23.8 °C year average), 173rd sunniest (about 2,708 hours of sunshine a year) and 298th wettest (999 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ambāla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Ambala Sadar is 0.1 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 57 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.0 °C), averages 19.2 °C more than its coolest, January (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.4 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,012 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Ambala Sadar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,012 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
