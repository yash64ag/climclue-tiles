Lal Bahadur Nagar weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Lal Bahadur Nagar, India (Telangana), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Lal Bahadur Nagar is warmest in May, with 38.9 °C (102 °F) days and 26.0 °C nights, and coolest in December, with 28.3 °C (83 °F) days and 15.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (172 mm); December is driest (5 mm).

Lal Bahadur Nagar at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Lal Bahadur Nagar is 1481st warmest (26.6 °C year average), 1394th sunniest (about 2,691 hours of sunshine a year) and 2862nd wettest (805 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jilādiguda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Lal Bahadur Nagar is about as warm in May and about as warm in December, with 5 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (32.4 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, December (21.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.2 °C (59 °F), in December. The area lies about 269 km from the sea.

Why Lal Bahadur Nagar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 269 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lal Bahadur Nagar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.348°N, 78.558°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-666-2585), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
