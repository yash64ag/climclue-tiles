Kaithal at a glance

Among the 3743 towns ClimaClue covers in India, Kaithal is 3335th warmest (24.1 °C year average), 1068th sunniest (about 2,735 hours of sunshine a year) and 3288th wettest (657 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pūndri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Kaithal is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 62 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.8 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (12.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 945 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kaithal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 945 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kaithal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.802°N, 76.400°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-541-2564), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
