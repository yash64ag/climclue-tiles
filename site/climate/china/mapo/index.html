Mapo weather by month and climate

Mapo at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Mapo is 1218th warmest (14.8 °C year average), 691st sunniest (about 2,303 hours of sunshine a year) and 1234th wettest (838 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zhangzhuang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Mapo is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.3 °C), averages 26.8 °C more than its coolest, January (0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 204 km from the sea.

Why Mapo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 204 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mapo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.517°N, 117.067°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-494-2970), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 35,358, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
