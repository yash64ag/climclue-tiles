Alamaiti weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Alamaiti, China (Xinjiang), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Alamaiti is warmest in July, with 33.8 °C (93 °F) days and 19.5 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.6 °C (35 °F) days and -10.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (13 mm); December is driest (2 mm).

Alamaiti at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Alamaiti is 1489th warmest (13.2 °C year average), 257th sunniest (about 2,614 hours of sunshine a year) and 2053rd wettest (75 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Awati, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Alamaiti is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.7 °C), averages 31.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,794 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 25 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 122% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Alamaiti has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,794 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alamaiti itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.582°N, 77.476°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-454-2574), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
