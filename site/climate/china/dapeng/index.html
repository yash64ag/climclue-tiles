Dapeng weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Dapeng, China (Jiangsu), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Dapeng at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Dapeng is 1197th warmest (14.8 °C year average), 765th sunniest (about 2,262 hours of sunshine a year) and 1208th wettest (868 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Liuji, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Dapeng is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.3 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (0.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 212 km from the sea.

Why Dapeng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 212 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dapeng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.284°N, 117.075°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-497-2970), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
