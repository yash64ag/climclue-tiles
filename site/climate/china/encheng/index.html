Encheng at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Encheng is 116th warmest (22.7 °C year average), 1257th sunniest (about 1,912 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (2,390 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nalong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Encheng is about as warm in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 198 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 14.0 °C more than its coolest, January (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.8 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 46 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Encheng, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Encheng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 46 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Encheng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.187°N, 112.304°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-618-2923), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 110,921, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
