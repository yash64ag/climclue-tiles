Baijiawan at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Baijiawan is 1775th warmest (9.8 °C year average), 990th sunniest (about 2,100 hours of sunshine a year) and 1851st wettest (452 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gangu Chengguanzhen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Baijiawan is 2.0 °C cooler in July and 1.9 °C cooler in January, with 73 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.6 °C), averages 25.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,191 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Baijiawan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,191 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.1 °C. The 10 km area around Baijiawan, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 7.9 °C a year, 1.9 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Baijiawan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.696°N, 105.289°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-493-2852), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
