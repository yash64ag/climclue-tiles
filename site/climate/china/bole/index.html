Bole at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Bole is 1863rd warmest (8.3 °C year average), 140th sunniest (about 2,729 hours of sunshine a year) and 1995th wettest (183 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jelilyüzi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (111 km away), Bole is 2.6 °C warmer in July and 5.4 °C cooler in January, with 215 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.1 °C), averages 37.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 2,245 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bole, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bole has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 2,245 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 37.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bole itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.893°N, 82.070°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-391-2620), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 235,585, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
