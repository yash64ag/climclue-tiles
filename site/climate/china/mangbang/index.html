Mangbang weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Mangbang, China (Yunnan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Mangbang is warmest in August, with 26.2 °C (79 °F) days and 18.9 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 19.4 °C (67 °F) days and 3.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (239 mm); December is driest (14 mm).

Mangbang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Mangbang is 642nd warmest (18.1 °C year average), 985th sunniest (about 2,103 hours of sunshine a year) and 654th wettest (1,285 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wuhe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Mangbang is 0.7 °C cooler in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 38 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.5 °C), averages 11.0 °C more than its coolest, January (11.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 765 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mangbang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mangbang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 765 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.0 °C. The 10 km area around Mangbang, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.6 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mangbang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.932°N, 98.670°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-590-2786), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
