Lhasa weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Lhasa, China (Tibet), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Lhasa is warmest in July, with 21.1 °C (70 °F) days and 9.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 5.0 °C (41 °F) days and -12.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (143 mm); January is driest (2 mm).

Lhasa at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Lhasa is 1916th warmest (6.5 °C year average), 73rd sunniest (about 2,806 hours of sunshine a year) and 1762nd wettest (520 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chéngguān Qū, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Lhasa is 0.2 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.2 °C), averages 19.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 779 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 4 km from Lhasa measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 5.1 °C (9.2 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Lhasa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 779 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lhasa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.650°N, 91.100°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-543-2711), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 867,900, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
