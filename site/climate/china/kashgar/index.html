Kashgar at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Kashgar is 492nd warmest (12.7 °C year average), 101st sunniest (about 2,633 hours of sunshine a year) and 684th wettest (89 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Artux, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Kashgar is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.8 °C), averages 31.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,801 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 124% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kashgar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,801 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
