Lingyuan at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Lingyuan is 1802nd warmest (9.3 °C year average), 109th sunniest (about 2,743 hours of sunshine a year) and 1914th wettest (385 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yebaishou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Lingyuan is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.6 °C), averages 33.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 142 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lingyuan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Lingyuan

For mild weather, the best months to visit Lingyuan are May, June, August and September.

Why Lingyuan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 142 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 33.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lingyuan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.240°N, 119.401°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-427-2994), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 646,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
