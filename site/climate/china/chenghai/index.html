Chenghai at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Chenghai is 328th warmest (19.1 °C year average), 781st sunniest (about 2,250 hours of sunshine a year) and 1255th wettest (822 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Qina, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Chenghai is 1.3 °C cooler in June and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 81 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (23.9 °C), averages 11.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 903 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chenghai, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chenghai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 903 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.0 °C. The 10 km area around Chenghai, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.9 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chenghai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.462°N, 100.679°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-575-2806), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
