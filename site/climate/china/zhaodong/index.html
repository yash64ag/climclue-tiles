Zhaodong at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Zhaodong is 2000th warmest (4.6 °C year average), 277th sunniest (about 2,598 hours of sunshine a year) and 1791st wettest (503 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lanxi, Heilongjiang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Zhaodong is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 41.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-18.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 554 km from the sea.

Why Zhaodong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 554 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 41.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Zhaodong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.052°N, 125.955°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-379-3059), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 154,406, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
