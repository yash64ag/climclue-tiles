Xihe at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Xihe is 999th warmest (16.3 °C year average), 1178th sunniest (about 1,947 hours of sunshine a year) and 1025th wettest (1,060 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Suizhou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Xihe is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 24.7 °C more than its coolest, January (3.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 646 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Xihe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 646 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Xihe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.686°N, 113.466°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-523-2934), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 90,422, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
