Bikou at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Bikou is 1112th warmest (15.5 °C year average), 1492nd sunniest (about 1,657 hours of sunshine a year) and 1263rd wettest (812 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Luotang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Bikou is 3.0 °C warmer in July and 4.1 °C warmer in January, with 86 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.4 °C), averages 21.2 °C more than its coolest, January (4.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,279 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bikou, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

For mild weather, the best months to visit Bikou are April, May, June and October.

Why Bikou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,279 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.2 °C. The 10 km area around Bikou, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.5 °C a year, 2.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bikou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.746°N, 105.241°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-512-2852), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,247, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
