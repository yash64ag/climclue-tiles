Qina at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Qina is 217th warmest (20.1 °C year average), 770th sunniest (about 2,254 hours of sunshine a year) and 1162nd wettest (903 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Taoyuan, Yunnan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Qina is 2.0 °C cooler in June and 1.7 °C cooler in December, with 166 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (25.2 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, December (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.7 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 895 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qina, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Qina has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 895 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C. The 10 km area around Qina, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.6 °C a year, 2.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qina itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.309°N, 100.625°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-576-2806), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
