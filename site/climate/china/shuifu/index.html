Shuifu weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Shuifu, China (Yunnan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Shuifu at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Shuifu is 616th warmest (18.2 °C year average), 1572nd sunniest (about 1,525 hours of sunshine a year) and 965th wettest (1,103 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baixi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Shuifu is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 23 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.2 °C), averages 19.3 °C more than its coolest, January (7.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.2 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 883 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shuifu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shuifu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 883 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shuifu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.632°N, 104.408°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-553-2844), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 102,143, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
