Why Shuangyashan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 382 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 40.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

What is the warmest month in Shuangyashan?

What is the coldest month in Shuangyashan?
January, with an average of -18.1 °C (-1 °F), day highs around -14.0 °C (7 °F) and night lows around -23.2 °C (-10 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Shuangyashan?

What is the driest month in Shuangyashan?

What is the sunniest month in Shuangyashan?
May, with about 236 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Shuangyashan?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 98 cm (38.7 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in March; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 7 months.
When is the best time for mild weather in Shuangyashan?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: June.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 7.6 × 11 km, that contains Shuangyashan’s GeoNames location (46.677°N, 131.133°E; ClimaClue area sq-373-3111), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 600,000 (GeoNames).
