Guyuan at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Guyuan is 1879th warmest (7.8 °C year average), 630th sunniest (about 2,381 hours of sunshine a year) and 1874th wettest (432 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pingliang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Guyuan is 2.1 °C cooler in July and 3.0 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.2 °C), averages 26.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-6.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,048 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guyuan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Guyuan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,048 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Guyuan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.007°N, 106.281°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-479-2862), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
