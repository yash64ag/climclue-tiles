Dadamtu at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Dadamtu is 1680th warmest (11.1 °C year average), 126th sunniest (about 2,735 hours of sunshine a year) and 1954th wettest (313 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bayandai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Dadamtu is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 23 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.8 °C), averages 29.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 2,206 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Dadamtu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 2,206 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dadamtu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.980°N, 81.314°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-400-2613), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
