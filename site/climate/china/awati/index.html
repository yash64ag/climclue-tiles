Awati at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Awati is 1485th warmest (13.2 °C year average), 259th sunniest (about 2,614 hours of sunshine a year) and 2054th wettest (74 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Alamaiti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Awati is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.7 °C), averages 31.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,798 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Awati has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,798 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-453-2573), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
