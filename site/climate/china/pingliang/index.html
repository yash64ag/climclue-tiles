Pingliang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Pingliang is 1734th warmest (10.2 °C year average), 778th sunniest (about 2,251 hours of sunshine a year) and 1868th wettest (441 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guyuan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Pingliang is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 3.3 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.1 °C), averages 25.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,034 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Pingliang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,034 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pingliang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.539°N, 106.686°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-484-2866), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 504,848, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
