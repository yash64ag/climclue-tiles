Qingxichang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Qingxichang is 871st warmest (17.1 °C year average), 1742nd sunniest (about 1,350 hours of sunshine a year) and 849th wettest (1,172 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pingkai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Qingxichang is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 180 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.6 °C), averages 22.4 °C more than its coolest, January (5.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 757 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Qingxichang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Qingxichang

For mild weather, the best months to visit Qingxichang are September and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): September and October.

Why Qingxichang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 757 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qingxichang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.404°N, 108.904°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-555-2889), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
