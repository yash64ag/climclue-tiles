Dayang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Dayang is 1803rd warmest (9.3 °C year average), 962nd sunniest (about 2,114 hours of sunshine a year) and 1692nd wettest (554 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Xichuan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Dayang is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.1 °C), averages 25.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,111 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dayang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dayang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 1,111 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dayang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.990°N, 106.072°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-490-2860), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 21,846, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
