Hangzhou at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Hangzhou is 247th warmest (17.1 °C year average), 487th sunniest (about 1,882 hours of sunshine a year) and 144th wettest (1,569 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chóngfú, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Hangzhou is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 116 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.4 °C), averages 24.8 °C more than its coolest, January (4.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 87 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 109% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Hangzhou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 87 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
