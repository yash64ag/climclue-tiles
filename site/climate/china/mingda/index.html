Mingda at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Mingda is 729th warmest (17.7 °C year average), 1746th sunniest (about 1,348 hours of sunshine a year) and 424th wettest (1,443 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lirang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Mingda is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 41 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.6 °C), averages 20.5 °C more than its coolest, January (7.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,017 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mingda, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mingda has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,017 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mingda itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.726°N, 107.657°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-532-2876), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,686, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
