Harbin at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Harbin is 650th warmest (4.6 °C year average), 154th sunniest (about 2,550 hours of sunshine a year) and 560th wettest (549 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hulan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Harbin is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.1 °C), averages 41.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-18.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 494 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 102% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Harbin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 494 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 41.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
