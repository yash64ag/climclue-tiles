Guangzhou at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Guangzhou is 80th warmest (23.0 °C year average), 1406th sunniest (about 1,800 hours of sunshine a year) and 84th wettest (2,053 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Foshan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Guangzhou is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 72 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.5 °C), averages 15.0 °C more than its coolest, January (14.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.3 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 78 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guangzhou, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Guangzhou

For mild weather, the best months to visit Guangzhou are February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (29.5 °C), August (29.2 °C) and June (28.5 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (33 mm), November (45 mm) and January (51 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (224 h), August (201 h) and October (189 h).

Why Guangzhou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 78 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Guangzhou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.117°N, 113.250°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-608-2932), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 18,676,605, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
