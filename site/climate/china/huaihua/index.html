Huaihua at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Huaihua is 244th warmest (17.2 °C year average), 624th sunniest (about 1,466 hours of sunshine a year) and 204th wettest (1,435 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hongjiang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (50 km away), Huaihua is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 76 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 22.7 °C more than its coolest, January (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 664 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Huaihua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Huaihua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 664 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
