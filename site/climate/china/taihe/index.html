Taihe at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Taihe is 449th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 1972nd sunniest (about 1,235 hours of sunshine a year) and 920th wettest (1,131 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Longxing, Chongqing (30.2°N 106.1°E), the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Taihe is 0.7 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 171 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.4 °C), averages 20.2 °C more than its coolest, January (8.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 969 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Taihe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Taihe

For mild weather, the best months to visit Taihe are April and October.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April and October.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (28.4 °C), August (28.3 °C) and June (25.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (19 mm), February (23 mm) and December (24 mm).

Why Taihe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 969 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Taihe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.099°N, 106.049°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-539-2860), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 66,506, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
