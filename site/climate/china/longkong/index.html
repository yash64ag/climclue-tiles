Longkong at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Longkong is 560th warmest (18.3 °C year average), 1798th sunniest (about 1,315 hours of sunshine a year) and 995th wettest (1,084 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gaojia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Longkong is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.3 °C), averages 20.6 °C more than its coolest, January (7.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.0 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 937 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Longkong, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Longkong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 937 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.6 °C. The 10 km area around Longkong, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.2 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Longkong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.045°N, 107.915°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-539-2879), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
