Why Anning has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 657 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Anning climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Anning?
June, with an average of 21.1 °C (70 °F), day highs around 23.4 °C (74 °F) and night lows around 16.2 °C (61 °F).
What is the coldest month in Anning?
December, with an average of 9.8 °C (50 °F), day highs around 13.8 °C (57 °F) and night lows around 4.7 °C (40 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Anning?
July, with 190 mm (7.5 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 870 mm (34.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Anning?
December, with 12 mm (0.5 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Anning?
March, with about 249 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Anning?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Anning?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: March, April, May and October.
What type of climate does Anning have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Anning’s GeoNames location (24.923°N, 102.485°E; ClimaClue area sq-590-2824), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 106,795 (GeoNames).
