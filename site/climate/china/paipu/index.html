Paipu weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Paipu, China (Hainan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Paipu is warmest in July, with 33.2 °C (92 °F) days and 25.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 23.1 °C (74 °F) days and 15.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (268 mm); January is driest (21 mm).

Paipu at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Paipu is 6th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 1056th sunniest (about 2,033 hours of sunshine a year) and 484th wettest (1,401 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baimajing, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Paipu is 0.1 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 65 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.4 °C), averages 10.4 °C more than its coolest, January (19.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.1 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Paipu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Paipu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Paipu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.640°N, 109.159°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-643-2891), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
