Longquan at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Longquan is 376th warmest (18.9 °C year average), 875th sunniest (about 2,172 hours of sunshine a year) and 1285th wettest (790 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Anning, the nearest covered town with different numbers (43 km away), Longquan is 1.9 °C warmer in June and 1.6 °C warmer in December, with 34 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (23.5 °C), averages 11.5 °C more than its coolest, December (12.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.1 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 656 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Longquan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 656 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.5 °C. The 10 km area around Longquan, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.5 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Longquan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.672°N, 102.161°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-593-2821), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
