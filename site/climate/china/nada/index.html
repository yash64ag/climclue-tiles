Why Nada has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 46 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Nada climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Nada?
July, with an average of 28.6 °C (83 °F), day highs around 32.6 °C (91 °F) and night lows around 25.0 °C (77 °F).
What is the coldest month in Nada?
January, with an average of 18.4 °C (65 °F), day highs around 23.1 °C (74 °F) and night lows around 14.7 °C (58 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Nada?
August, with 323 mm (12.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,912 mm (75.3 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Nada?
January, with 16 mm (0.6 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Nada?
July, with about 231 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Nada?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Nada?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, November and December.
What type of climate does Nada have?

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Nada’s GeoNames location (19.521°N, 109.579°E; ClimaClue area sq-644-2895), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 256,652 (GeoNames).
