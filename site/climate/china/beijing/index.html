Beijing at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Beijing is 479th warmest (13.0 °C year average), 67th sunniest (about 2,704 hours of sunshine a year) and 544th wettest (568 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Daxing, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Beijing is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 38 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.4 °C), averages 31.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 163 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Best time to visit Beijing

For mild weather, the best months to visit Beijing are April, May and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April, May and September.

Why Beijing has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 163 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
