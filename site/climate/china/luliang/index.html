Lüliang weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Lüliang, China (Shanxi), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Lüliang is warmest in July, with 30.6 °C (87 °F) days and 18.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.1 °C (34 °F) days and -12.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (107 mm); January is driest (4 mm).

Lüliang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Lüliang is 1703rd warmest (10.7 °C year average), 275th sunniest (about 2,599 hours of sunshine a year) and 1820th wettest (480 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jiexiu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (87 km away), Lüliang is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 3.0 °C cooler in January, with 50 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.6 °C), averages 30.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 582 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lüliang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lüliang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 582 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lüliang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.519°N, 111.144°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-464-2911), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
