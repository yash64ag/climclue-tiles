Mawang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Mawang is 764th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 1749th sunniest (about 1,347 hours of sunshine a year) and 375th wettest (1,480 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Longtan, Chongqing (28.8°N 109.0°E), the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Mawang is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 25 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.7 °C), averages 21.5 °C more than its coolest, January (6.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 801 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 19 km from Mawang by up to 1.8 °C (3.2 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Mawang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 801 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.5 °C. The 10 km area around Mawang, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.4 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mawang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.893°N, 108.951°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-551-2889), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 33,456, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
