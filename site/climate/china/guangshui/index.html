Guangshui at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Guangshui is 347th warmest (16.1 °C year average), 384th sunniest (about 1,984 hours of sunshine a year) and 267th wettest (1,235 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Xinyang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (56 km away), Guangshui is about as warm in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 92 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.7 °C), averages 24.8 °C more than its coolest, January (2.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 608 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guangshui, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Guangshui has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 608 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Guangshui itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.620°N, 113.998°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-523-2939), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
