Gongheyong weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Gongheyong, China (Guangdong), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Gongheyong at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Gongheyong is 103rd warmest (22.9 °C year average), 1283rd sunniest (about 1,893 hours of sunshine a year) and 53rd wettest (2,149 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Maozhou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Gongheyong is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 13.3 °C more than its coolest, January (15.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.1 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Gongheyong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gongheyong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.759°N, 113.795°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-612-2937), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
