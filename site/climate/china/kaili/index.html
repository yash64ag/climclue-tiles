Kaili at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Kaili is 894th warmest (16.8 °C year average), 1766th sunniest (about 1,335 hours of sunshine a year) and 953rd wettest (1,109 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sankeshu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Kaili is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 240 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.2 °C), averages 20.6 °C more than its coolest, January (5.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 549 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kaili, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kaili

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kaili are September and October.

Why Kaili has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 549 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kaili itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.586°N, 107.980°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-574-2879), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
