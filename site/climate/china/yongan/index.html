Yong’an at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Yong’an is 139th warmest (19.2 °C year average), 618th sunniest (about 1,509 hours of sunshine a year) and 302nd wettest (1,149 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wushan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Yong’an is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 139 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.2 °C), averages 21.0 °C more than its coolest, January (8.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,007 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Yong’an, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Yong’an has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,007 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.0 °C. The 10 km area around Yong’an, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.3 °C a year, 1.9 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yong’an itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.019°N, 109.459°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-529-2894), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
