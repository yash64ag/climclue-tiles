Xiaodu at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Xiaodu is 447th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 1958th sunniest (about 1,238 hours of sunshine a year) and 977th wettest (1,096 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shaoyun, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Xiaodu is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 57 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.3 °C), averages 20.1 °C more than its coolest, January (8.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.8 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 964 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Xiaodu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Xiaodu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 964 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Xiaodu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.962°N, 105.887°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-540-2858), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
