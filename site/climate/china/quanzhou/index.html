Quanzhou at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Quanzhou is 185th warmest (21.5 °C year average), 1157th sunniest (about 1,958 hours of sunshine a year) and 873rd wettest (1,162 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jinjiang, Fujian, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Quanzhou is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 65 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.1 °C), averages 16.1 °C more than its coolest, January (13.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.5 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 38 km from the sea.

Why Quanzhou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 38 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Quanzhou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.914°N, 118.586°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-590-2985), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
