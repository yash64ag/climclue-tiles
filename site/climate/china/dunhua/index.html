Dunhua at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Dunhua is 1997th warmest (4.6 °C year average), 607th sunniest (about 2,404 hours of sunshine a year) and 1506th wettest (638 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dashitou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Dunhua is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 73 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.8 °C), averages 36.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-15.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, October, November and December. The area lies about 214 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dunhua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dunhua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 214 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 36.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dunhua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.370°N, 128.229°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-406-3082), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 148,844, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
