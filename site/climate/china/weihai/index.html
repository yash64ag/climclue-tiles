Weihai weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Weihai, China (Shandong), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Weihai is warmest in August, with 26.9 °C (80 °F) days and 21.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.9 °C (37 °F) days and -2.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (165 mm); February is driest (14 mm).

Weihai at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Weihai is 1624th warmest (12.1 °C year average), 358th sunniest (about 2,554 hours of sunshine a year) and 1372nd wettest (716 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tianfu, Shandong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Weihai is 0.7 °C cooler in August and 2.0 °C warmer in January, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.3 °C), averages 24.1 °C more than its coolest, January (0.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Weihai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Weihai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.509°N, 122.114°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-464-3021), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 844,310, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
