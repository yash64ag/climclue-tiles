Putian weather in March

Average March weather in Putian, China: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In March, Putian averages 18.1 °C (65 °F) days and 11.2 °C (52 °F) nights, with 132 mm (5.2 in) of rain over the month and about 106 hours of sunshine.

How March compares in Putian

March is the 4th coolest of the 12, the 6th driest of the 12 and the 2nd least sunny of the 12 in Putian. Compared with February: 2.5 °C warmer, 46 mm more precipitation and 16 hours more sunshine. April is 4.5 °C warmer than March on average.

Putian in March: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Putian in March?
In March, Putian averages a day high of 18.1 °C (65 °F) and a night low of 11.2 °C (52 °F), with 132 mm (5.2 in) of precipitation and about 106 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is March a good time to visit Putian?
March is not one of Putian’s mild months; those are October and November, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Putian in March?
132 mm (5.2 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making March the 6th driest of the 12. The wettest month is June (231 mm), the driest December (31 mm).
Does it snow in Putian in March?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for March in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Putian get in March?
About 106 hours of bright sunshine, the 2nd least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Putian in March?
About 14.6 °C (58 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, cold for most swimmers; its warmest month is August (27.7 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Putian in March?
Going by the averages: light layers and a rain jacket or umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
