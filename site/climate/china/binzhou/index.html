Binzhou at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Binzhou is 1401st warmest (13.6 °C year average), 327th sunniest (about 2,573 hours of sunshine a year) and 1631st wettest (579 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dongying, the nearest covered town with different numbers (43 km away), Binzhou is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.1 °C), averages 29.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 88 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Binzhou, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Binzhou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 88 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Binzhou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.367°N, 118.017°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-466-2980), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 682,717, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
