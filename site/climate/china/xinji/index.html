Xinji at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Xinji is 1338th warmest (14.2 °C year average), 354th sunniest (about 2,558 hours of sunshine a year) and 1776th wettest (514 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Beisu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (44 km away), Xinji is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.7 °C), averages 29.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 223 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Xinji, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Xinji has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 223 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Xinji itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.926°N, 115.219°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-2952), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 145,911, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
