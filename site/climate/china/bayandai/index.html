Bayandai at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Bayandai is 1662nd warmest (11.4 °C year average), 129th sunniest (about 2,735 hours of sunshine a year) and 1962nd wettest (290 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dadamtu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Bayandai is 0.4 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.2 °C), averages 29.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-4.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 2,198 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Bayandai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 2,198 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 29.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bayandai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.966°N, 81.249°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-400-2612), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
