Tangping at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Tangping is 106th warmest (22.8 °C year average), 1270th sunniest (about 1,904 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (2,568 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yangjiang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Tangping is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 121 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.5 °C), averages 13.4 °C more than its coolest, January (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.0 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tangping has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tangping itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.032°N, 111.935°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-619-2919), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 81,729, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
