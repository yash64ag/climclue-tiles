Layka weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Layka, China (Xinjiang), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Layka is warmest in July, with 32.1 °C (90 °F) days and 18.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.7 °C (35 °F) days and -9.3 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (12 mm); November is driest (1 mm).

Layka at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Layka is 1593rd warmest (12.5 °C year average), 314th sunniest (about 2,580 hours of sunshine a year) and 2075th wettest (61 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Buzhake, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Layka is 0.2 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.1 °C), averages 28.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,799 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Layka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 1,799 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Layka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.077°N, 79.732°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-469-2597), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 29,032, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
