Hanfeng at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Hanfeng is 119th warmest (19.6 °C year average), 630th sunniest (about 1,442 hours of sunshine a year) and 271st wettest (1,227 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Taibai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Hanfeng is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 67 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.7 °C), averages 20.9 °C more than its coolest, January (8.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,057 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hanfeng, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hanfeng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,057 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hanfeng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.169°N, 108.403°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-528-2884), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
