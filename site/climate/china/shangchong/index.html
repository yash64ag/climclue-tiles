Shangchong at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Shangchong is 774th warmest (17.4 °C year average), 1713th sunniest (about 1,384 hours of sunshine a year) and 791st wettest (1,205 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jiuchao, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Shangchong is 1.6 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 201 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.0 °C), averages 21.1 °C more than its coolest, January (5.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 511 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Shangchong, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Shangchong has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 511 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shangchong itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.253°N, 108.658°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-577-2886), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
