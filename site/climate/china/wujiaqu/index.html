Wujiaqu at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Wujiaqu is 1848th warmest (8.6 °C year average), 94th sunniest (about 2,771 hours of sunshine a year) and 1982nd wettest (193 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Changji, the nearest covered town with different numbers (24 km away), Wujiaqu is 1.2 °C warmer in July and 2.3 °C cooler in January, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.9 °C), averages 42.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-15.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 2,416 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Wujiaqu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 2,416 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 42.5 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Wujiaqu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.163°N, 87.522°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-398-2675), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
