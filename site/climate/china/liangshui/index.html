Liangshui at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Liangshui is 1100th warmest (15.5 °C year average), 1343rd sunniest (about 1,847 hours of sunshine a year) and 1898th wettest (400 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Longnan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Liangshui is 0.4 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 33 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.5 °C), averages 21.7 °C more than its coolest, January (3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 1,286 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Liangshui has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,286 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.7 °C. The 10 km area around Liangshui, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.5 °C a year, 3.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Liangshui itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.432°N, 104.809°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-505-2848), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
