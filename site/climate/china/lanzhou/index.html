Lanzhou at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Lanzhou is 531st warmest (10.8 °C year average), 208th sunniest (about 2,476 hours of sunshine a year) and 654th wettest (311 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baiyin, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Lanzhou is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 2.3 °C warmer in January, with 114 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.9 °C), averages 26.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,252 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Lanzhou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,252 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
