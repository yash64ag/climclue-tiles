Lincang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Lincang is 24th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 928th sunniest (about 2,133 hours of sunshine a year) and 1106th wettest (953 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fudong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (85 km away), Lincang is 8.4 °C warmer in June and 8.7 °C warmer in January, with 878 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.2 °C), averages 9.7 °C more than its coolest, January (18.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.9 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 762 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 15 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 83% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Lincang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 762 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.7 °C. The 10 km area around Lincang, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 21.3 °C a year, 3.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lincang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.880°N, 100.095°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-601-2800), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 323,708, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
