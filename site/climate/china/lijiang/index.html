Lijiang at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Lijiang is 482nd warmest (12.9 °C year average), 282nd sunniest (about 2,235 hours of sunshine a year) and 352nd wettest (1,025 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shangri-La, the nearest covered town with different numbers (118 km away), Lijiang is 5.5 °C warmer in June and 9.3 °C warmer in January, with 150 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (18.4 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, January (6.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 962 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 25 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.2 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Lijiang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 962 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
