Shangri-La at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Shangri-La is 633rd warmest (5.8 °C year average), 291st sunniest (about 2,206 hours of sunshine a year) and 392nd wettest (875 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lijiang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (118 km away), Shangri-La is 5.0 °C cooler in July and 9.3 °C cooler in January, with 150 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (13.2 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 1,012 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Shangri-La has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 1,012 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
