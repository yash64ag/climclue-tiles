Lixian at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Lixian is 1686th warmest (11.0 °C year average), 1109th sunniest (about 1,982 hours of sunshine a year) and 1824th wettest (475 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shiqiao, Gansu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Lixian is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 32 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.4 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,224 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Lixian has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,224 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lixian itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.190°N, 105.172°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-498-2851), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
