Xinhua at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Xinhua is 444th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 968th sunniest (about 2,112 hours of sunshine a year) and 220th wettest (1,671 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Puchuan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Xinhua is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 129 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.8 °C), averages 10.5 °C more than its coolest, January (12.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 738 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Xinhua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Xinhua

For mild weather, the best months to visit Xinhua are March, April and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, April and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (22.8 °C), June (22.7 °C) and July (22.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (14 mm), January (17 mm) and February (29 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (242 h), December (239 h) and March (225 h).

Why Xinhua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 738 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.5 °C. The 10 km area around Xinhua, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.1 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Xinhua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.753°N, 98.483°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-592-2784), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 16,336, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
