Lidu at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Lidu is 315th warmest (19.1 °C year average), 1917th sunniest (about 1,246 hours of sunshine a year) and 976th wettest (1,097 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Longqiao, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Lidu is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 1 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.1 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (8.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 912 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Lidu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 912 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lidu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.739°N, 107.295°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-542-2872), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
