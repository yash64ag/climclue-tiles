Shangyu weather in March

Average March weather in Shangyu, China: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In March, Shangyu averages 14.7 °C (58 °F) days and 6.7 °C (44 °F) nights, with 143 mm (5.6 in) of rain over the month and about 122 hours of sunshine.

How March compares in Shangyu

March is the 4th coolest of the 12, the 4th wettest of the 12 and the 2nd least sunny of the 12 in Shangyu. Compared with February: 3.8 °C warmer, 57 mm more precipitation and 18 hours more sunshine. April is 5.8 °C warmer than March on average.

Shangyu in March: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Shangyu in March?
In March, Shangyu averages a day high of 14.7 °C (58 °F) and a night low of 6.7 °C (44 °F), with 143 mm (5.6 in) of precipitation and about 122 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is March a good time to visit Shangyu?
March is not one of Shangyu’s mild months; those are October, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Shangyu in March?
143 mm (5.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making March the 4th wettest of the 12. The wettest month is June (224 mm), the driest December (48 mm).
Does it snow in Shangyu in March?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for March in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Shangyu get in March?
About 122 hours of bright sunshine, the 2nd least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Shangyu in March?
About 9.1 °C (48 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, cold for most swimmers; its warmest month is August (28.6 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Shangyu in March?
Going by the averages: a jacket or sweater and a rain jacket or umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
