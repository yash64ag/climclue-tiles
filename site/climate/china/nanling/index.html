Nanling at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Nanling is 234th warmest (19.7 °C year average), 907th sunniest (about 2,150 hours of sunshine a year) and 501st wettest (1,382 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Menglang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Nanling is 1.5 °C cooler in June and 1.9 °C cooler in December, with 107 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (23.3 °C), averages 9.5 °C more than its coolest, December (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 692 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 17 km from Nanling measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.2 °C (4.0 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Nanling has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 692 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.5 °C. The 10 km area around Nanling, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.6 °C a year, 1.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nanling itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.711°N, 99.996°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-612-2799), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 24,059, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
