Lhoka at a glance

Among the 692 towns of 100,000+ people covered in China, Lhoka is 625th warmest (5.9 °C year average), 39th sunniest (about 2,767 hours of sunshine a year) and 611th wettest (461 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lhasa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (79 km away), Lhoka is 1.5 °C cooler in July and 2.1 °C warmer in January, with 83 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (13.3 °C), averages 15.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 734 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Lhoka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 734 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
