Xingcheng weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Xingcheng, China (Liaoning), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Xingcheng is warmest in August, with 29.2 °C (85 °F) days and 20.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 0.3 °C (33 °F) days and -11.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (162 mm); January is driest (3 mm).

Xingcheng at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Xingcheng is 1714th warmest (10.5 °C year average), 176th sunniest (about 2,684 hours of sunshine a year) and 1596th wettest (591 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Huludao, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Xingcheng is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 30.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Xingcheng has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Xingcheng itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.617°N, 120.717°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-433-3007), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
