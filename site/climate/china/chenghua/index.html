Chenghua at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Chenghua is 148th warmest (22.2 °C year average), 1078th sunniest (about 2,009 hours of sunshine a year) and 279th wettest (1,588 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Anbu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Chenghua is 0.2 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 132 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.6 °C), averages 14.0 °C more than its coolest, January (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.1 °C (52 °F), in January. The area lies about 23 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 11 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 90% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Chenghua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chenghua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (23.461°N, 116.770°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-605-2967), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
