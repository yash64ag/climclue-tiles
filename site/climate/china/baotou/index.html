Baotou at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Baotou is 1886th warmest (7.6 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 2,964 hours of sunshine a year) and 1950th wettest (321 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shiguai, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Baotou is 1.9 °C warmer in July and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 37 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.9 °C), averages 35.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 700 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Baotou, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Baotou has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 700 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 35.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Baotou itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.652°N, 109.844°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-433-2898), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
