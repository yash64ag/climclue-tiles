Yingjiang at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Yingjiang is 180th warmest (21.7 °C year average), 888th sunniest (about 2,160 hours of sunshine a year) and 151st wettest (1,826 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Taiping, Yunnan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Yingjiang is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 51 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 10.5 °C more than its coolest, January (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.4 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 691 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Yingjiang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 691 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.5 °C. The 10 km area around Yingjiang, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 20.2 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Yingjiang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.710°N, 97.937°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-592-2779), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
