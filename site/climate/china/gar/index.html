Gar at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Gar is 2083rd warmest (0.2 °C year average), 470th sunniest (about 2,508 hours of sunshine a year) and 2012th wettest (152 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Langgar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (484 km away), Gar is 11.8 °C cooler in July and 7.8 °C cooler in January, with 68 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (13.2 °C), averages 26.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, May, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,378 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Gar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,378 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.2 °C. The 10 km area around Gar, which the globe shows when you click there, averages -1.2 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.501°N, 80.098°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-514-2600), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
