Lianghu at a glance

Among the 2105 towns ClimaClue covers in China, Lianghu is 786th warmest (17.4 °C year average), 1318th sunniest (about 1,866 hours of sunshine a year) and 348th wettest (1,505 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shangyu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Lianghu is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 45 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.5 °C), averages 24.5 °C more than its coolest, January (5.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 48 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lianghu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lianghu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 48 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lianghu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.992°N, 120.898°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-540-3008), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 155,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
