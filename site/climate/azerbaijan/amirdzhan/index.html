Amirdzhan weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Amirdzhan, Azerbaijan (Baki), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Amirdzhan is warmest in August, with 29.9 °C (86 °F) days and 21.3 °C nights, and coolest in February, with 8.3 °C (47 °F) days and 2.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around November (38 mm); June is driest (5 mm).

Amirdzhan at a glance

Among the 65 towns ClimaClue covers in Azerbaijan, Amirdzhan is 33rd warmest (14.8 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 2,124 hours of sunshine a year) and 50th wettest (235 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bakıxanov, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Amirdzhan is 0.1 °C cooler in August and about as warm in February, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.6 °C), averages 20.1 °C more than its coolest, February (5.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Amirdzhan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Amirdzhan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Amirdzhan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.426°N, 49.984°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-435-2299), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
