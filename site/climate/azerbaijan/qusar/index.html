Qusar at a glance

Among the 65 towns ClimaClue covers in Azerbaijan, Qusar is the 3rd coolest (9.4 °C year average) and the 2nd least sunny (about 1,902 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Quba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Qusar is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.9 °C), averages 22.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 47 km from the sea.

Why Qusar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 47 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Qusar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.428°N, 48.430°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-425-2284), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
