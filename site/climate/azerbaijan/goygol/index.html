Goygol at a glance

Among the 65 towns ClimaClue covers in Azerbaijan, Goygol is 59th warmest (12.0 °C year average), 7th sunniest (about 2,236 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (577 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ganja, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Goygol is 2.3 °C cooler in July and 1.6 °C cooler in January, with 124 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.4 °C), averages 23.1 °C more than its coolest, January (0.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 241 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 17 km from Goygol measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 3.2 °C (5.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Goygol has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 241 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.1 °C. The 10 km area around Goygol, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 10.7 °C a year, 1.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-434-2263), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
