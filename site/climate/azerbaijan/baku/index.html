Baku at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Azerbaijan, Baku is the 3rd warmest (14.9 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 2,108 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (236 mm a year). Compared with Khirdalan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Baku is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.8 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, February (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 4.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Baku, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Baku has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
