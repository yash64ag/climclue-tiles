Baku weather by month and climate

Baku at a glance

Among the 65 towns ClimaClue covers in Azerbaijan, Baku is the 2nd driest (213 mm a year). Compared with Qaraçuxur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Baku is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.9 °C), averages 20.3 °C more than its coolest, February (5.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Baku, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Baku has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Baku itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.378°N, 49.892°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-436-2298), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,351,300, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
