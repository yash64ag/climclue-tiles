Biny Selo weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Biny Selo, Azerbaijan (Baki), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Biny Selo at a glance

Among the 65 towns ClimaClue covers in Azerbaijan, Biny Selo is the 3rd driest (214 mm a year). Compared with Yeni Suraxanı, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Biny Selo is about as warm in August and about as warm in February, with 16 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.7 °C), averages 20.0 °C more than its coolest, February (5.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Biny Selo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Biny Selo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.451°N, 50.087°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-435-2300), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
