Arfa Moussaïa at a glance

Among the 47 towns ClimaClue covers in Guinea, Arfa Moussaïa is 29th warmest (25.5 °C year average), 16th sunniest (about 2,491 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (1,418 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dabola, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Arfa Moussaïa is 1.4 °C cooler in April and 1.3 °C cooler in August, with 117 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (29.3 °C), averages 6.0 °C more than its coolest, August (23.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.8 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 287 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Arfa Moussaïa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Arfa Moussaïa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 287 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Arfa Moussaïa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.767°N, 11.367°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-732-1686), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
