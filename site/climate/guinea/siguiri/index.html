Siguiri at a glance

Among the 12 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Guinea, Siguiri is the warmest (27.3 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,736 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (1,158 mm a year). Compared with Kankan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (116 km away), Siguiri is 2.0 °C warmer in April and 0.5 °C warmer in August, with 315 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (32.0 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, August (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.4 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 535 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Siguiri, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Siguiri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 535 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Siguiri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.423°N, 9.169°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-725-1708), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
