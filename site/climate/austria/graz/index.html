Graz at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Austria, Graz is the 3rd warmest (9.7 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 1,772 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (861 mm a year). Compared with Klagenfurt am Wörthersee, the nearest covered town with different numbers (99 km away), Graz is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 211 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.1 °C), averages 21.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 208 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Graz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 208 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
