Innsbruck at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Austria, Innsbruck is the 2nd coolest (9.4 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,798 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (897 mm a year). Compared with Salzburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (137 km away), Innsbruck is about as warm in July and 1.7 °C warmer in January, with 559 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.9 °C), averages 19.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 224 km from the sea.

Why Innsbruck has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 224 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.0 °C. The 10 km area around Innsbruck, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 6.6 °C a year, 2.8 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Innsbruck itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.263°N, 11.395°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-367-1913), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
