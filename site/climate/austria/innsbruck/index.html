Innsbruck at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Austria, Innsbruck is the coolest (6.6 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,798 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,055 mm a year). Compared with Salzburg, the nearest covered town with different numbers (137 km away), Innsbruck is 2.1 °C cooler in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 493 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.1 °C), averages 19.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 224 km from the sea.

Why Innsbruck has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 224 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 11.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
