Salzburg at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Austria, Salzburg is the 2nd coolest (8.3 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,693 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,549 mm a year). Compared with Linz, the nearest covered town with different numbers (108 km away), Salzburg is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 1.2 °C cooler in January, with 622 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.3 °C), averages 20.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 234 km from the sea.

Why Salzburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 234 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 8.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
