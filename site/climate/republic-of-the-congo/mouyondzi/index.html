Mouyondzi at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in the Republic of the Congo, Mouyondzi is the 5th coolest (24.0 °C year average), the 5th least sunny (about 1,581 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th wettest (1,694 mm a year). Compared with Bouansa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Mouyondzi is 1.4 °C cooler in March and 0.9 °C cooler in July, with 524 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (25.0 °C), averages 3.0 °C more than its coolest, July (22.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.1 °C (65 °F), in July. The area lies about 253 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mouyondzi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mouyondzi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 253 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mouyondzi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.994°S, 13.927°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-879-1939), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
