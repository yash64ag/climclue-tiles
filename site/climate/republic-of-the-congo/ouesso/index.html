Ouesso at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in the Republic of the Congo, Ouesso is 15th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 1,756 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (1,578 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pokola, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Ouesso is 0.1 °C warmer in March and 0.1 °C warmer in September, with 119 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.1 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, September (24.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.5 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 701 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ouesso, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ouesso has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 701 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ouesso itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.614°N, 16.052°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-823-1960), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
