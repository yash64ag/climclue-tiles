Makoua at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in the Republic of the Congo, Makoua is 14th warmest (25.1 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 1,772 hours of sunshine a year) and 10th wettest (1,644 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Owando, the nearest covered town with different numbers (62 km away), Makoua is about as warm in March and about as warm in July, with 35 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.0 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, July (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.9 °C (68 °F), in October. The area lies about 676 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Makoua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Makoua

For mild weather, the best months to visit Makoua are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): March (26.0 °C), February (25.9 °C) and April (25.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (58 mm), August (80 mm) and June (86 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (173 h), May (164 h) and February (162 h).

Why Makoua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 676 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Makoua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.007°N, 15.633°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-839-1956), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 20,550, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
