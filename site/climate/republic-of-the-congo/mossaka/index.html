Mossaka at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in the Republic of the Congo, Mossaka is the warmest (25.9 °C year average) and the 2nd sunniest (about 2,125 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Oyo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (92 km away), Mossaka is 0.4 °C warmer in February and 0.6 °C warmer in July, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.6 °C), averages 1.3 °C more than its coolest, July (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.1 °C (70 °F), in July. The area lies about 675 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mossaka, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mossaka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 675 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mossaka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.220°S, 16.799°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-852-1967), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
