Pointe-Noire at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Republic of the Congo, Pointe-Noire is the 3rd warmest (24.7 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,722 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (1,457 mm a year). Compared with Dolisie, the nearest covered town with different numbers (110 km away), Pointe-Noire is 1.4 °C warmer in March and 0.2 °C warmer in July, with 72 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.8 °C), averages 5.2 °C more than its coolest, July (21.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.6 °C (67 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pointe-Noire, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pointe-Noire has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
