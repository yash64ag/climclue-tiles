Oyo at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in the Republic of the Congo, Oyo is the 4th warmest (25.5 °C year average) and the 5th sunniest (about 2,022 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Owando, the nearest covered town with different numbers (76 km away), Oyo is 0.2 °C warmer in February and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.3 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, July (24.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.3 °C (69 °F), in July. The area lies about 609 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Oyo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Oyo

For mild weather, the best months to visit Oyo are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (26.3 °C), March (26.3 °C) and April (26.1 °C).

Why Oyo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 609 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Oyo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.165°S, 15.970°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-851-1959), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
