Brazzaville at a glance

Among the 23 towns ClimaClue covers in the Republic of the Congo, Brazzaville is the 3rd warmest (25.5 °C year average). Compared with Mouyondzi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (153 km away), Brazzaville is 1.4 °C warmer in March and 1.7 °C warmer in July, with 134 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (26.4 °C), averages 2.7 °C more than its coolest, July (23.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.1 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 368 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Brazzaville, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Brazzaville

For mild weather, the best months to visit Brazzaville are June, July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): March (26.4 °C), April (26.4 °C) and February (26.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (2 mm), June (7 mm) and August (7 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (195 h), February (186 h) and March (183 h).

Why Brazzaville has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 368 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Brazzaville itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.266°S, 15.283°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-882-1952), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
