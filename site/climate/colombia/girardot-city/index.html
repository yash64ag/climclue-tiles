Girardot City at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Girardot City is 19th warmest (25.3 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,049 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (1,714 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ibagué, the nearest covered town with different numbers (46 km away), Girardot City is 6.2 °C warmer in February and 4.7 °C warmer in July, with 507 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (26.3 °C), averages 1.9 °C more than its coolest, July (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.3 °C (69 °F), in July. The area lies about 274 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Girardot City, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Girardot City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 274 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.9 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
