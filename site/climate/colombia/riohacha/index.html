Riohacha at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Riohacha is the 2nd sunniest (about 2,503 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (815 mm a year). Compared with Albania, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Riohacha is 0.9 °C warmer in July and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 343 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.4 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, January (26.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.4 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Riohacha, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Riohacha has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Riohacha itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (11.544°N, 72.907°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-724-1070), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
