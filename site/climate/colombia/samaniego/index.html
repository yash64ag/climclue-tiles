Samaniego at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Samaniego is 247th warmest (18.7 °C year average), 235th sunniest (about 1,642 hours of sunshine a year) and 272nd wettest (1,277 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Túquerres, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Samaniego is 7.5 °C warmer in May and 7.3 °C warmer in November, with 265 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (19.0 °C), averages 0.6 °C more than its coolest, November (18.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.5 °C (56 °F), in July. The area lies about 149 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Samaniego, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Samaniego has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 149 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Samaniego itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.338°N, 77.596°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-826-1024), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
