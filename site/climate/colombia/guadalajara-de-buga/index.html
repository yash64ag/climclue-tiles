Guadalajara de Buga at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Guadalajara de Buga is 27th warmest (21.3 °C year average), 29th sunniest (about 1,769 hours of sunshine a year) and 20th wettest (2,138 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tuluá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Guadalajara de Buga is 0.6 °C warmer in March and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 321 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (21.6 °C), averages 0.6 °C more than its coolest, July (21.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.6 °C (62 °F), in July. The area lies about 113 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Guadalajara de Buga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Guadalajara de Buga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 113 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
