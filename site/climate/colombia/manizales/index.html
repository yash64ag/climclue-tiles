Manizales at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Manizales is 36th warmest (18.5 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 1,857 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (2,694 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dosquebradas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Manizales is 0.6 °C warmer in April and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 931 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (18.9 °C), averages 0.7 °C more than its coolest, July (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.8 °C (57 °F), in July. The area lies about 210 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Manizales, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Manizales has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 210 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
