Palmira at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Palmira is 26th warmest (21.6 °C year average), 24th sunniest (about 1,813 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (1,839 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cali, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Palmira is 0.9 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C warmer in July, with 474 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (22.1 °C), averages 0.7 °C more than its coolest, July (21.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.4 °C (62 °F), in August. The area lies about 113 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 10 km from Palmira measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 3.4 °C (6.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Palmira has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 113 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
