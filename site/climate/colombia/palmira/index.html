Palmira at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Palmira is 210th warmest (21.8 °C year average), 173rd sunniest (about 1,813 hours of sunshine a year) and 202nd wettest (1,662 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pradera, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Palmira is 0.2 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C cooler in July, with 122 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (22.3 °C), averages 0.7 °C more than its coolest, July (21.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.6 °C (60 °F), in August. The area lies about 113 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 10 km from Palmira measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 3.2 °C (5.8 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Palmira has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 113 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Palmira itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.535°N, 76.298°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-804-1037), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 312,519, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
