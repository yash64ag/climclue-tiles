Cartago at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Cartago is 24th warmest (22.3 °C year average), 20th sunniest (about 1,874 hours of sunshine a year) and 30th wettest (1,666 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pereira, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Cartago is 4.4 °C warmer in February and 4.2 °C warmer in October, with 1,959 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (22.4 °C), averages 0.5 °C more than its coolest, October (21.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.0 °C (66 °F), in September. The area lies about 166 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cartago, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cartago has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 166 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
