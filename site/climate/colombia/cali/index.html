Cali at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Cali is 181st warmest (23.5 °C year average), 212th sunniest (about 1,711 hours of sunshine a year) and 274th wettest (1,251 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yumbo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Cali is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in November, with 24 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.0 °C), averages 1.0 °C more than its coolest, November (23.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 85 km from the sea.

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 20 km from Cali by up to 2.5 °C (4.5 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Cali

For mild weather, the best months to visit Cali are January, February, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (24.0 °C), September (23.7 °C) and July (23.7 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (36 mm), August (40 mm) and June (59 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (167 h), January (166 h) and August (158 h).

Why Cali has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 85 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.0 °C. The 10 km area around Cali, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 22.3 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cali itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.431°N, 76.520°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-805-1034), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,392,877, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
