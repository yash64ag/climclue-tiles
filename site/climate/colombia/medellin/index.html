Medellín at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Medellín is 40th warmest (17.7 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 1,823 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (2,015 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Itagüí, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Medellín is 2.2 °C warmer in April and 2.2 °C warmer in January, with 253 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (17.9 °C), averages 0.5 °C more than its coolest, January (17.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.6 °C (55 °F), in July. The area lies about 204 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Medellín, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Medellín has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 204 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
