Campo de la Cruz at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Campo de la Cruz is 7th warmest (27.9 °C year average), 48th sunniest (about 2,334 hours of sunshine a year) and 285th wettest (1,151 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Candelaria, Atlántico, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Campo de la Cruz is 0.1 °C warmer in March and about as warm in October, with 94 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (28.9 °C), averages 2.0 °C more than its coolest, October (27.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.4 °C (71 °F), in December. The area lies about 74 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Campo de la Cruz, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Campo de la Cruz has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 74 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Campo de la Cruz itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.378°N, 74.884°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-736-1051), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
