Balboa at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Balboa is 239th warmest (19.4 °C year average), 226th sunniest (about 1,667 hours of sunshine a year) and 76th wettest (2,842 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Patía, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Balboa is 6.1 °C cooler in August and 5.8 °C cooler in November, with 1,141 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.2 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, November (18.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.2 °C (58 °F), in September. The area lies about 110 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Balboa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Balboa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 110 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 16.9 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Balboa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.042°N, 77.216°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-819-1027), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 18,910, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
