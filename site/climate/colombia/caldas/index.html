Caldas at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Caldas is 261st warmest (18.0 °C year average), 166th sunniest (about 1,827 hours of sunshine a year) and 67th wettest (2,975 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with La Estrella, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Caldas is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C cooler in October, with 126 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (18.3 °C), averages 0.7 °C more than its coolest, October (17.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.2 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 189 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Caldas, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Caldas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 189 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Caldas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.091°N, 75.636°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-779-1043), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
