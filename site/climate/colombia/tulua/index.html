Tuluá at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Tuluá is 30th warmest (20.8 °C year average), 40th sunniest (about 1,631 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (2,459 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Guadalajara de Buga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Tuluá is 0.5 °C cooler in April and 0.5 °C cooler in July, with 321 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (21.1 °C), averages 0.6 °C more than its coolest, July (20.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.2 °C (61 °F), in July. The area lies about 127 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tuluá, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tuluá has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 127 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
