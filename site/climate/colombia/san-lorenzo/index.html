San Lorenzo at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, San Lorenzo is 285th warmest (15.7 °C year average), 191st sunniest (about 1,772 hours of sunshine a year) and 162nd wettest (1,949 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with La Unión, Nariño, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), San Lorenzo is 3.1 °C cooler in August and 3.1 °C cooler in November, with 103 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.5 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, November (15.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.4 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 154 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 11 km from San Lorenzo measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 3.9 °C (6.9 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why San Lorenzo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 154 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C. The 10 km area around San Lorenzo, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.5 °C a year, 2.7 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Lorenzo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (1.503°N, 77.215°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-824-1027), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
