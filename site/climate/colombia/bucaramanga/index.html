Bucaramanga at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Bucaramanga is 195th warmest (22.3 °C year average), 197th sunniest (about 1,758 hours of sunshine a year) and 189th wettest (1,749 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Floridablanca, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Bucaramanga is 0.6 °C cooler in April and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 267 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (22.7 °C), averages 0.7 °C more than its coolest, January (21.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.5 °C (62 °F), in January. The area lies about 277 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bucaramanga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Bucaramanga

For mild weather, the best months to visit Bucaramanga are January and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January and December.

Why Bucaramanga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 277 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bucaramanga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.125°N, 73.119°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-768-1068), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
