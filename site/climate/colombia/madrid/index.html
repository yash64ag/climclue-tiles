Madrid at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Madrid is 45th warmest (14.4 °C year average), 45th sunniest (about 1,508 hours of sunshine a year) and 37th wettest (1,505 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Facatativá, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Madrid is 1.9 °C warmer in August and 1.5 °C warmer in January, with 794 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (15.6 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.3 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 351 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 13 km from Madrid measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.4 °C (4.3 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Best time to visit Madrid

For mild weather, the best months to visit Madrid are July and August.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): July and August.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (15.6 °C), July (15.2 °C) and June (14.8 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (66 mm), July (67 mm) and September (75 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (175 h), December (159 h) and February (143 h).

Why Madrid has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 351 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
