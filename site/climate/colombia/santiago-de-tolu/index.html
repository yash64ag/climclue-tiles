Santiago de Tolú at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Santiago de Tolú is 51st warmest (27.3 °C year average), 80th sunniest (about 2,083 hours of sunshine a year) and 267th wettest (1,330 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tolú Viejo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Santiago de Tolú is 0.1 °C warmer in March and 0.6 °C warmer in October, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.9 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, October (26.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.8 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santiago de Tolú, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santiago de Tolú has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santiago de Tolú itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.524°N, 75.581°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-744-1044), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
