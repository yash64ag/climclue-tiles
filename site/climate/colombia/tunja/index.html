Tunja at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Tunja is the coolest (12.5 °C year average). Compared with Sogamoso, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Tunja is 1.5 °C cooler in April and 1.8 °C cooler in July, with 376 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (13.1 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, July (11.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.4 °C (45 °F), in August. The area lies about 442 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tunja, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tunja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 442 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tunja itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (5.545°N, 73.358°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-784-1066), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
