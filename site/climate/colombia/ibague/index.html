Ibagué at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Ibagué is 32nd warmest (19.8 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 1,698 hours of sunshine a year) and 19th wettest (2,221 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Girardot City, the nearest covered town with different numbers (46 km away), Ibagué is 5.1 °C cooler in September and 5.6 °C cooler in November, with 507 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (20.3 °C), averages 0.8 °C more than its coolest, November (19.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.4 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 235 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ibagué, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ibagué has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 235 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 7.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
