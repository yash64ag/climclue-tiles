Puerto Asís at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Puerto Asís is the 5th least sunny (about 1,152 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Orito, the nearest covered town with different numbers (46 km away), Puerto Asís is 0.3 °C warmer in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 854 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (25.8 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, July (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.1 °C (66 °F), in July. The area lies about 295 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Puerto Asís, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Puerto Asís

No month in Puerto Asís is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are July (23.6 °C, 385 mm), August (24.4 °C, 338 mm) and September (24.9 °C, 346 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): January (25.8 °C), February (25.6 °C) and December (25.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (245 mm), December (262 mm) and February (310 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (134 h), November (119 h) and January (115 h).

Why Puerto Asís has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 295 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Puerto Asís itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.505°N, 76.496°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-834-1035), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 29,782, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
