Pasto at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Pasto is the 3rd coolest (12.6 °C year average) and the least sunny (about 1,174 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Pitalito, the nearest covered town with different numbers (154 km away), Pasto is 7.1 °C cooler in April and 6.1 °C cooler in July, with 16 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (12.9 °C), averages 0.8 °C more than its coolest, July (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in August. The area lies about 184 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 23 km from Pasto measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 7.6 °C (13.7 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Pasto has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 184 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.8 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
