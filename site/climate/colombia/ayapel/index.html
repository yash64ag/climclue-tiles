Ayapel at a glance

Among the 317 towns ClimaClue covers in Colombia, Ayapel is 62nd warmest (27.2 °C year average), 127th sunniest (about 1,934 hours of sunshine a year) and 105th wettest (2,495 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Caucasia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Ayapel is 0.1 °C warmer in February and 0.2 °C warmer in October, with 322 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (28.2 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, October (26.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in January. The area lies about 144 km from the sea.

Why Ayapel has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 144 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ayapel itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.314°N, 75.140°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-756-1048), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
