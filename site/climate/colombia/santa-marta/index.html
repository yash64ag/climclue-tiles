Santa Marta at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Santa Marta is 15th warmest (26.0 °C year average), 6th sunniest (about 2,455 hours of sunshine a year) and 43rd wettest (1,367 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Barranquilla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (71 km away), Santa Marta is 0.8 °C cooler in June and 1.5 °C cooler in January, with 518 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (26.9 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, January (25.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.9 °C (75 °F), in January. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Santa Marta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Santa Marta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
