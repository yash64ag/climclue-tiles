Malambo at a glance

Among the 55 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Colombia, Malambo is the 5th sunniest (about 2,467 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (949 mm a year). Compared with Soledad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Malambo is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in December, with 100 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (27.8 °C), averages 0.9 °C more than its coolest, December (26.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.8 °C (77 °F), in January. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Malambo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Malambo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
