Ankazobe weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ankazobe, Madagascar (Analamanga), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ankazobe is warmest in January, with 25.8 °C (78 °F) days and 16.7 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 20.1 °C (68 °F) days and 9.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around January (426 mm); June is driest (4 mm).

Ankazobe at a glance

Among the 108 towns ClimaClue covers in Madagascar, Ankazobe is 84th warmest (18.9 °C year average), 46th sunniest (about 2,654 hours of sunshine a year) and 44th wettest (1,828 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fenoarivo Be, the nearest covered town with different numbers (59 km away), Ankazobe is 1.2 °C cooler in January and 2.0 °C cooler in July, with 79 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (21.2 °C), averages 6.4 °C more than its coolest, July (14.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.6 °C (49 °F), in July. The area lies about 228 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ankazobe, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ankazobe has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 228 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ankazobe itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.317°S, 47.117°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1023-2271), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
