Befandriana at a glance

Among the 108 towns ClimaClue covers in Madagascar, Befandriana is 26th warmest (25.0 °C year average), 40th sunniest (about 2,735 hours of sunshine a year) and 42nd wettest (1,882 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Antsohihy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (73 km away), Befandriana is 1.1 °C cooler in November and 2.5 °C cooler in July, with 394 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (26.8 °C), averages 4.4 °C more than its coolest, July (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.6 °C (64 °F), in July. The area lies about 117 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Befandriana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Befandriana

For mild weather, the best months to visit Befandriana are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): November (26.8 °C), December (26.5 °C) and March (26.2 °C).

Why Befandriana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 117 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Befandriana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (15.257°S, 48.542°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-992-2285), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
