Bealanana weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bealanana, Madagascar (Sofia), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bealanana is warmest in December, with 25.7 °C (78 °F) days and 17.1 °C nights, and coolest in July, with 20.6 °C (69 °F) days and 11.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around February (355 mm); September is driest (12 mm).

Bealanana at a glance

Among the 108 towns ClimaClue covers in Madagascar, Bealanana is 80th warmest (19.4 °C year average), 45th sunniest (about 2,663 hours of sunshine a year) and 58th wettest (1,592 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Maromandia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (80 km away), Bealanana is 4.7 °C cooler in December and 7.1 °C cooler in July, with 390 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (21.4 °C), averages 5.3 °C more than its coolest, July (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.5 °C (53 °F), in July. The area lies about 107 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bealanana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bealanana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 107 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bealanana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.543°S, 48.750°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-985-2287), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 32,764, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
