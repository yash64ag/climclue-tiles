Cilacap at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Cilacap is 112th warmest (26.0 °C year average), 45th sunniest (about 2,734 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (3,346 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Purwokerto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Cilacap is 0.3 °C warmer in April and 0.5 °C warmer in August, with 180 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (26.8 °C), averages 1.9 °C more than its coolest, August (24.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.5 °C (74 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cilacap, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cilacap has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
