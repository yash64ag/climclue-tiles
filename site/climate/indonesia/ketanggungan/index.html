Ketanggungan at a glance

Among the 449 towns ClimaClue covers in Indonesia, Ketanggungan is 62nd warmest (27.1 °C year average), 116th sunniest (about 2,869 hours of sunshine a year) and 270th wettest (2,369 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bulakamba, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Ketanggungan is 0.2 °C cooler in October and 0.2 °C cooler in August, with 70 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.6 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, August (26.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.9 °C (71 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ketanggungan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ketanggungan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ketanggungan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.938°S, 108.891°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-909-2888), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
