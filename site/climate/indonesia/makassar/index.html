Makassar at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Makassar is 39th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 2,852 hours of sunshine a year) and 22nd wettest (3,336 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Watampone, the nearest covered town with different numbers (120 km away), Makassar is 0.3 °C warmer in October and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 1,057 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.6 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, January (26.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.9 °C (75 °F), in August. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Makassar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Makassar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
