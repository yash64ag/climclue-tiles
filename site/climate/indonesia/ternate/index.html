Ternate at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Ternate is 95th warmest (26.5 °C year average), 157th sunniest (about 2,082 hours of sunshine a year) and 38th wettest (3,051 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bitung, the nearest covered town with different numbers (261 km away), Ternate is 0.3 °C cooler in May and 0.6 °C cooler in September, with 521 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (26.8 °C), averages 0.6 °C more than its coolest, September (26.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 25.3 °C (78 °F), in July. The area lies about 16 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ternate, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ternate has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 0.6 °C. The 10 km area around Ternate, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 24.8 °C a year, 1.7 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ternate itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.791°N, 127.384°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-832-3073), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
