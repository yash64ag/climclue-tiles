Pekalongan at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Pekalongan is 13th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 28th sunniest (about 2,892 hours of sunshine a year) and 45th wettest (3,039 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kedungwuni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Pekalongan is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 87 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.6 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, January (26.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.2 °C (76 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pekalongan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pekalongan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
