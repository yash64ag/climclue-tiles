Blitar at a glance

Among the 449 towns ClimaClue covers in Indonesia, Blitar is 373rd warmest (25.1 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 3,108 hours of sunshine a year) and 264th wettest (2,375 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ngunut, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Blitar is 0.5 °C cooler in April and 0.5 °C cooler in August, with 233 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (25.9 °C), averages 2.1 °C more than its coolest, August (23.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.9 °C (64 °F), in August. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Blitar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Blitar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Blitar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.098°S, 112.168°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-920-2921), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
