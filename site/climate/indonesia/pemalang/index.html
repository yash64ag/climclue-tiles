Pemalang at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Pemalang is 6th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 2,900 hours of sunshine a year) and 69th wettest (2,805 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tegal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Pemalang is 0.1 °C warmer in May and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 323 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.9 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, January (26.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 24.1 °C (75 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pemalang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Pemalang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pemalang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.892°S, 109.383°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-908-2893), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
