Karawang at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Karawang is 44th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 48th sunniest (about 2,717 hours of sunshine a year) and 110th wettest (2,431 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rengasdengklok, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Karawang is about as warm in October and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 58 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.4 °C), averages 1.2 °C more than its coolest, January (26.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.4 °C (72 °F), in July. The area lies about 40 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Karawang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Karawang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 40 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Karawang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.305°S, 107.320°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-903-2873), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
