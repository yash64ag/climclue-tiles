Bungku at a glance

Among the 449 towns ClimaClue covers in Indonesia, Bungku is 263rd warmest (26.3 °C year average), 315th sunniest (about 2,354 hours of sunshine a year) and 271st wettest (2,363 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kendari, the nearest covered town with different numbers (170 km away), Bungku is 0.4 °C cooler in December and 0.2 °C cooler in August, with 599 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, December (26.9 °C), averages 1.4 °C more than its coolest, August (25.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 20.7 °C (69 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bungku, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bungku has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.4 °C. The 10 km area around Bungku, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 25.2 °C a year, 1.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bungku itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (2.547°S, 121.972°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-865-3019), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
