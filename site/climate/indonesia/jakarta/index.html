Jakarta at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Jakarta is 55th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 63rd sunniest (about 2,648 hours of sunshine a year) and 90th wettest (2,701 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bekasi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Jakarta is about as warm in October and about as warm in January, with 42 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.4 °C), averages 1.2 °C more than its coolest, January (26.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.5 °C (73 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jakarta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Jakarta

No month in Jakarta is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are January (26.1 °C, 411 mm), February (26.1 °C, 368 mm) and December (26.4 °C, 286 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): October (27.4 °C), September (27.3 °C) and May (27.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): August (105 mm), July (116 mm) and September (125 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): August (286 h), July (273 h) and September (255 h).

Why Jakarta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
