Grogol at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Grogol is 107th warmest (26.4 °C year average), 75th sunniest (about 2,619 hours of sunshine a year) and 142nd wettest (2,092 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Surakarta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Grogol is 0.1 °C cooler in October and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.0 °C), averages 1.1 °C more than its coolest, January (25.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in July. The area lies about 68 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Grogol, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Grogol has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 68 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Grogol itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.601°S, 110.819°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-916-2908), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
