Singaraja at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Singaraja is 129th warmest (25.6 °C year average), 21st sunniest (about 2,922 hours of sunshine a year) and 41st wettest (3,103 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sukawati, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Singaraja is 1.9 °C warmer in October and 1.3 °C warmer in July, with 229 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (26.4 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, July (24.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 22.2 °C (72 °F), in August. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Singaraja, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Singaraja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
