Singaraja at a glance

Among the 449 towns ClimaClue covers in Indonesia, Singaraja is 186th warmest (26.6 °C year average), 90th sunniest (about 2,922 hours of sunshine a year) and 321st wettest (2,184 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Banjar, Bali, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Singaraja is 0.2 °C cooler in October and 0.1 °C cooler in July, with 410 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.3 °C), averages 1.5 °C more than its coolest, July (25.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.9 °C (71 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Singaraja, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Singaraja has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.5 °C. The 10 km area around Singaraja, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 25.6 °C a year, 1.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-921-2950), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
