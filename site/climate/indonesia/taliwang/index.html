Taliwang at a glance

Among the 449 towns ClimaClue covers in Indonesia, Taliwang is 242nd warmest (26.4 °C year average), 85th sunniest (about 2,960 hours of sunshine a year) and 430th wettest (1,610 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Selong, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Taliwang is 0.5 °C warmer in April and 0.6 °C warmer in August, with 477 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (27.1 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, August (25.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.3 °C (67 °F), in August. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Taliwang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Taliwang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Taliwang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (8.745°S, 116.853°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-927-2968), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
