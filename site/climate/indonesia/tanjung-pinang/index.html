Tanjung Pinang at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Tanjung Pinang is 10th warmest (27.2 °C year average), 162nd sunniest (about 2,047 hours of sunshine a year) and 35th wettest (3,128 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Batam, the nearest covered town with different numbers (55 km away), Tanjung Pinang is 0.3 °C warmer in May and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 531 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (27.9 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, January (26.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 25.2 °C (77 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tanjung Pinang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tanjung Pinang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
