Surabaya at a glance

Among the 449 towns ClimaClue covers in Indonesia, Surabaya is the warmest (27.8 °C year average). Compared with Kamal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Surabaya is 0.3 °C warmer in October and about as warm in February, with 48 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (28.9 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, February (27.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.3 °C (74 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Surabaya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

No month in Surabaya is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are January (27.1 °C, 328 mm), February (27.1 °C, 297 mm) and July (27.4 °C, 35 mm).

Why Surabaya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Surabaya itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.249°S, 112.751°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-912-2927), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 3,018,022, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
