Surabaya at a glance

Among the 169 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Indonesia, Surabaya is the warmest (27.8 °C year average). Compared with Sidoarjo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Surabaya is 0.4 °C cooler in October and 0.3 °C warmer in February, with 76 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (28.9 °C), averages 1.8 °C more than its coolest, February (27.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 23.8 °C (75 °F), in July. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Surabaya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Surabaya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Surabaya itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (7.249°S, 112.751°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-912-2927), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
