Cluj-Napoca at a glance

Among the 127 towns ClimaClue covers in Romania, Cluj-Napoca is 96th warmest (9.3 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 1,946 hours of sunshine a year) and 77th wettest (592 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Turda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Cluj-Napoca is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.0 °C), averages 22.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 478 km from the sea.

Why Cluj-Napoca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 478 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cluj-Napoca itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.767°N, 23.600°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-372-2036), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 286,598, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
