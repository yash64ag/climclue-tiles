Mediaş at a glance

Among the 127 towns ClimaClue covers in Romania, Mediaş is 75th warmest (10.1 °C year average), 89th sunniest (about 1,850 hours of sunshine a year) and 73rd wettest (598 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Târnăveni, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Mediaş is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.8 °C), averages 22.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 401 km from the sea.

Why Mediaş has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 401 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mediaş itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (46.167°N, 24.350°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-378-2043), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 53,040, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
