Botoşani weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Botoşani, Romania (Botoșani County), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Botoşani is warmest in July, with 27.3 °C (81 °F) days and 15.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.4 °C (35 °F) days and -6.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (97 mm); January is driest (22 mm).

Botoşani at a glance

Among the 127 towns ClimaClue covers in Romania, Botoşani is 80th warmest (9.9 °C year average), 84th sunniest (about 1,861 hours of sunshine a year) and 83rd wettest (572 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dorohoi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Botoşani is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.4 °C), averages 23.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 327 km from the sea.

Why Botoşani has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 327 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Botoşani itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.750°N, 26.667°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-362-2066), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 90,010, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
