Minsk weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Minsk, Belarus (Minsk City), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Minsk is warmest in July, with 22.9 °C (73 °F) days and 13.1 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -2.2 °C (28 °F) days and -7.9 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 36 mm in February to 90 mm in June.

Minsk at a glance

Among the 57 towns ClimaClue covers in Belarus, Minsk is 48th warmest (6.3 °C year average), 26th sunniest (about 1,741 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (686 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Lyasny, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Minsk is 0.3 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 52 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.0 °C), averages 23.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.0 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 416 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Minsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 416 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Minsk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.900°N, 27.567°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-300-2075), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,742,124, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
