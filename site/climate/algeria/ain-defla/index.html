Aïn Defla at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, Aïn Defla is 147th warmest (18.1 °C year average), 136th sunniest (about 2,894 hours of sunshine a year) and 135th wettest (476 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Khemis Miliana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Aïn Defla is 0.6 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 58 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.9 °C), averages 18.2 °C more than its coolest, January (9.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.5 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Why Aïn Defla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Aïn Defla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.264°N, 1.968°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-477-1819), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
