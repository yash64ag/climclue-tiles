’Aïn el Turk weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in ’Aïn el Turk, Algeria (Oran), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

’Aïn el Turk at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, ’Aïn el Turk is 86th warmest (18.6 °C year average), 268th sunniest (about 2,823 hours of sunshine a year) and 225th wettest (315 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mers el Kebir, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), ’Aïn el Turk is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 19 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.2 °C), averages 11.9 °C more than its coolest, January (13.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of ’Aïn el Turk, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why ’Aïn el Turk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for ’Aïn el Turk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.744°N, 0.769°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-482-1792), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
