Aïn Kercha at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, Aïn Kercha is 220th warmest (16.4 °C year average), 177th sunniest (about 2,886 hours of sunshine a year) and 162nd wettest (392 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Aïn M’Lila, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Aïn Kercha is 0.2 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 6 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.2 °C), averages 20.0 °C more than its coolest, January (7.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 114 km from the sea.

Why Aïn Kercha has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 114 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Aïn Kercha itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.925°N, 6.695°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-480-1866), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
