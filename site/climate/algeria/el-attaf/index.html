El Attaf at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, El Attaf is 46th warmest (19.3 °C year average), 170th sunniest (about 2,887 hours of sunshine a year) and 179th wettest (367 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Abadia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), El Attaf is 0.5 °C warmer in August and about as warm in January, with 53 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (29.3 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.3 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 44 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of El Attaf, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why El Attaf has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 44 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El Attaf itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.224°N, 1.672°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-477-1816), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 33,349, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
