Dar Chioukh at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, Dar Chioukh is 252nd warmest (15.6 °C year average), 50th sunniest (about 3,052 hours of sunshine a year) and 244th wettest (265 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Djelfa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Dar Chioukh is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.0 °C), averages 22.7 °C more than its coolest, January (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 216 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dar Chioukh, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dar Chioukh has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 216 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dar Chioukh itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.896°N, 3.485°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-491-1834), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
