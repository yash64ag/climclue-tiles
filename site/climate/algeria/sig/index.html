Sig at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, Sig is 43rd warmest (19.3 °C year average), 196th sunniest (about 2,881 hours of sunshine a year) and 232nd wettest (299 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mohammadia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Sig is 0.4 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.8 °C), averages 16.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sig, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sig has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sig itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.528°N, 0.194°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-484-1798), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
