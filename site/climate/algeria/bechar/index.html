Béchar at a glance

Among the 42 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Algeria, Béchar is the 4th warmest (22.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 3,467 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (105 mm a year). Compared with Tlemcen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (372 km away), Béchar is 9.7 °C warmer in July and 2.6 °C warmer in January, with 300 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (34.6 °C), averages 24.4 °C more than its coolest, January (10.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 389 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Béchar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Béchar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 389 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Béchar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.617°N, 2.217°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-523-1777), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
