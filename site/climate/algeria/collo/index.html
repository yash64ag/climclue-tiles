Collo at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, Collo is 70th warmest (18.8 °C year average), 284th sunniest (about 2,779 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (803 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kerkera, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Collo is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 44 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.6 °C), averages 14.0 °C more than its coolest, January (12.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in February. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Collo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Collo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Collo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.006°N, 6.560°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-469-1865), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 29,354, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
