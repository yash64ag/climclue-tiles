Algiers at a glance

Among the 42 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Algeria, Algiers is 21st warmest (18.0 °C year average), 27th sunniest (about 2,886 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (749 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Baraki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Algiers is 0.8 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 93 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 14.7 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.0 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 15 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 122% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Algiers has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
