El Hadjar weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in El Hadjar, Algeria (Annaba), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

El Hadjar is warmest in August, with 33.8 °C (93 °F) days and 22.4 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 16.2 °C (61 °F) days and 7.0 °C nights. Most rain falls around December (111 mm); July is driest (3 mm).

El Hadjar at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, El Hadjar is the 4th least sunny (about 2,753 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Sidi Amar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), El Hadjar is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.1 °C), averages 16.6 °C more than its coolest, January (11.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.0 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why El Hadjar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for El Hadjar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.804°N, 7.737°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-471-1877), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
