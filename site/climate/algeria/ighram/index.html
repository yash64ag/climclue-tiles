Ighram at a glance

Among the 294 towns ClimaClue covers in Algeria, Ighram is 186th warmest (17.2 °C year average), 114th sunniest (about 2,902 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (716 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Akbou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Ighram is 0.8 °C cooler in August and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 134 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.4 °C), averages 19.3 °C more than its coolest, January (8.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.7 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ighram, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ighram has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-475-1845), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs.
