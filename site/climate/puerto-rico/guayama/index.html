Guayama at a glance

Among the 21 towns ClimaClue covers in Puerto Rico, Guayama is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,948 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (1,241 mm a year). Compared with Cayey, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Guayama is 2.1 °C warmer in August and 2.6 °C warmer in February, with 531 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.3 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, February (24.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.8 °C (68 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 1 km from Guayama by up to 1.9 °C (3.3 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Guayama has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Guayama itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.984°N, 66.114°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-660-1138), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
