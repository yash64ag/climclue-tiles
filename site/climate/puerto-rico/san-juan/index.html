San Juan at a glance

Among the 4 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Puerto Rico, San Juan is the warmest (26.2 °C year average), the sunniest (about 2,930 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (1,663 mm a year). Compared with Bayamón, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), San Juan is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 121 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.6 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, January (24.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.8 °C (71 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 10 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 96% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why San Juan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
