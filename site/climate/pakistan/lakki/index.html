Lakki at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Lakki is 233rd warmest (24.0 °C year average), 180th sunniest (about 2,983 hours of sunshine a year) and 143rd wettest (522 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sarai Naurang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Lakki is 0.1 °C cooler in June and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 21 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.4 °C), averages 22.2 °C more than its coolest, January (12.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 922 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lakki, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Lakki

For mild weather, the best months to visit Lakki are March, October and November.

Why Lakki has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 922 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lakki itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.607°N, 70.912°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-513-2509), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 36,391, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
