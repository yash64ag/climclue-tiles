Rawalakot at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Rawalakot is the 3rd wettest (2,065 mm a year). Compared with Khai Gala, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Rawalakot is 0.6 °C warmer in June and 0.8 °C warmer in January, with 317 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (22.4 °C), averages 19.7 °C more than its coolest, January (2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and December. The area lies about 1,179 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Rawalakot has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,179 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rawalakot itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.858°N, 73.760°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-501-2537), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
