Khanpur Mahar at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Khanpur Mahar is 51st warmest (27.3 °C year average), 83rd sunniest (about 3,308 hours of sunshine a year) and 338th wettest (123 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ghotki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Khanpur Mahar is 0.2 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (36.5 °C), averages 21.0 °C more than its coolest, January (15.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 408 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Khanpur Mahar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Khanpur Mahar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 408 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Khanpur Mahar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.841°N, 69.413°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-561-2494), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
