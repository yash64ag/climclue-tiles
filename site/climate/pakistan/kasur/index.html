Kasur at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Kasur is 230th warmest (24.1 °C year average), 301st sunniest (about 2,903 hours of sunshine a year) and 126th wettest (576 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Raja Jang, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Kasur is 0.2 °C cooler in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.4 °C), averages 21.2 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,012 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kasur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kasur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,012 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kasur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.119°N, 74.450°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-528-2544), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
