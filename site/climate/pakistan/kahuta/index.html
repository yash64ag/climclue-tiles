Kahuta weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kahuta, Pakistan (Punjab), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kahuta at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Kahuta is 331st warmest (20.8 °C year average), 309th sunniest (about 2,888 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (1,567 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sharifabad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Kahuta is 0.2 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 198 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (30.0 °C), averages 20.2 °C more than its coolest, January (9.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,130 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kahuta, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kahuta

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kahuta are April, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April, October and November.

Why Kahuta has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,130 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kahuta itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.592°N, 73.387°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-504-2533), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 22,551, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
