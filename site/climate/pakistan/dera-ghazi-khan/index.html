Dera Ghazi Khan at a glance

Among the 134 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Pakistan, Dera Ghazi Khan is 38th warmest (26.3 °C year average), 41st sunniest (about 3,195 hours of sunshine a year) and 85th wettest (252 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jampur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Dera Ghazi Khan is 0.4 °C cooler in June and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 48 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (36.0 °C), averages 22.0 °C more than its coolest, January (14.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.1 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 666 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dera Ghazi Khan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dera Ghazi Khan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 666 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
