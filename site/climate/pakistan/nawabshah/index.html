Nawabshah at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Nawabshah is 63rd warmest (27.1 °C year average), 73rd sunniest (about 3,326 hours of sunshine a year) and 316th wettest (146 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sakrand, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Nawabshah is about as warm in June and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 1 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.5 °C), averages 19.3 °C more than its coolest, January (16.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 218 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nawabshah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Nawabshah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 218 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nawabshah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.239°N, 68.404°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-577-2484), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 363,138, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
