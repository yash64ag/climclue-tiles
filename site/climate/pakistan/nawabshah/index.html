Nawabshah at a glance

Among the 134 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Pakistan, Nawabshah is 16th warmest (27.3 °C year average), 25th sunniest (about 3,326 hours of sunshine a year) and 119th wettest (146 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Daur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Nawabshah is 0.5 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.5 °C), averages 19.2 °C more than its coolest, January (16.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.4 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 218 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nawabshah, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nawabshah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 218 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
