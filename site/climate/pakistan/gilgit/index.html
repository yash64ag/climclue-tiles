Gilgit weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Gilgit, Pakistan (Gilgit-Baltistan), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Gilgit is warmest in August, with 26.7 °C (80 °F) days and 14.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.0 °C (34 °F) days and -7.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around April (31 mm); November is driest (6 mm).

Gilgit at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Gilgit is the 5th coolest (8.8 °C year average). Compared with Jaglot, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Gilgit is 0.3 °C cooler in August and 1.8 °C cooler in January, with 54 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.5 °C), averages 24.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,399 km from the sea (deep inland).

For mild weather, the best months to visit Gilgit are June, July, August and September.

Why Gilgit has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,399 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.0 °C. The 10 km area around Gilgit, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 6.4 °C a year, 2.4 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gilgit itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.919°N, 74.312°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-480-2543), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 216,760, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
