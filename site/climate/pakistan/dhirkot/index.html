Dhirkot at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Dhirkot is 357th warmest (13.8 °C year average), 347th sunniest (about 2,800 hours of sunshine a year) and 9th wettest (1,636 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bāgh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Dhirkot is 3.1 °C cooler in July and 2.3 °C cooler in January, with 268 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.3 °C), averages 20.2 °C more than its coolest, January (2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 1,185 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dhirkot, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dhirkot has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 1,185 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.2 °C. The 10 km area around Dhirkot, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 15.5 °C a year, 1.8 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dhirkot itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.039°N, 73.577°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-499-2535), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 195,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
