Shahpur Chakar at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Shahpur Chakar is 61st warmest (27.1 °C year average), 61st sunniest (about 3,348 hours of sunshine a year) and 309th wettest (154 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sinjhoro, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Shahpur Chakar is 0.4 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.9 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.5 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 229 km from the sea.

Why Shahpur Chakar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 229 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Shahpur Chakar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (26.154°N, 68.650°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-578-2486), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
