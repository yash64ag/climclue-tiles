Mardan at a glance

Among the 134 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Pakistan, Mardan is 117th warmest (22.1 °C year average), 126th sunniest (about 2,824 hours of sunshine a year) and 29th wettest (715 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Charsadda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Mardan is 1.4 °C cooler in June and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 94 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.7 °C), averages 22.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,117 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mardan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mardan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,117 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-498-2520), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
