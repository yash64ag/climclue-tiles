Gujranwala at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Gujranwala is 254th warmest (23.5 °C year average), 228th sunniest (about 2,969 hours of sunshine a year) and 71st wettest (937 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Eminabad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Gujranwala is 0.1 °C cooler in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.3 °C), averages 21.4 °C more than its coolest, January (11.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,065 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Gujranwala, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Gujranwala

For mild weather, the best months to visit Gujranwala are March, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (33.3 °C), May (31.3 °C) and July (31.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (9 mm), December (19 mm) and October (20 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (304 h), October (282 h) and June (277 h).

Why Gujranwala has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,065 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Gujranwala itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.156°N, 74.187°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-518-2541), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,511,118, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
