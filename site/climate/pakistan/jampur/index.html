Jampur at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Jampur is 91st warmest (26.7 °C year average), 109th sunniest (about 3,235 hours of sunshine a year) and 260th wettest (200 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dajal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Jampur is 0.4 °C cooler in June and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 13 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (36.4 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (14.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 626 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jampur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jampur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 626 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jampur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.642°N, 70.595°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-543-2505), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 155,243, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
