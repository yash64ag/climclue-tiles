Why Jampur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 626 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Jampur climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Jampur?
June, with an average of 36.5 °C (98 °F), day highs around 42.1 °C (108 °F) and night lows around 30.1 °C (86 °F).
What is the coldest month in Jampur?
January, with an average of 14.7 °C (58 °F), day highs around 20.8 °C (69 °F) and night lows around 8.8 °C (48 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Jampur?
July, with 54 mm (2.1 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 204 mm (8.0 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Jampur?
November, with 1 mm (0.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Jampur?
May, with about 307 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Jampur?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Jampur?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: February, March, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.7 × 11 km, that contains Jampur’s GeoNames location (29.642°N, 70.595°E; ClimaClue area sq-543-2505), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 155,243 (GeoNames).
