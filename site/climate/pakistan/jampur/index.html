Jampur at a glance

Among the 134 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Pakistan, Jampur is 30th warmest (26.8 °C year average), 38th sunniest (about 3,235 hours of sunshine a year) and 100th wettest (204 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dajal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Jampur is 0.3 °C cooler in June and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 14 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (36.5 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (14.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.8 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 626 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jampur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jampur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 626 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
