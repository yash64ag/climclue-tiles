Karachi at a glance

Among the 134 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Pakistan, Karachi is 39th warmest (26.1 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 3,044 hours of sunshine a year) and 107th wettest (180 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Hub, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Karachi is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (30.3 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, January (19.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.0 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Karachi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Karachi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Karachi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.861°N, 67.010°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-591-2470), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
