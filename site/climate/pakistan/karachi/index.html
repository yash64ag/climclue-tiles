Why Karachi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Karachi climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Karachi?
June, with an average of 30.4 °C (87 °F), day highs around 33.1 °C (92 °F) and night lows around 27.7 °C (82 °F).
What is the coldest month in Karachi?
January, with an average of 19.6 °C (67 °F), day highs around 24.2 °C (76 °F) and night lows around 14.9 °C (59 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Karachi?
July, with 69 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 183 mm (7.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Karachi?
November, with 1 mm (0.0 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Karachi?
May, with about 311 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Karachi?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Karachi?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 10 × 11 km, that contains Karachi’s GeoNames location (24.861°N, 67.010°E; ClimaClue area sq-591-2470), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 11,624,219 (GeoNames).
