Alahabad at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Alahabad is 192nd warmest (24.9 °C year average), 291st sunniest (about 2,925 hours of sunshine a year) and 152nd wettest (468 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chunian, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Alahabad is about as warm in June and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 17 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.0 °C), averages 21.3 °C more than its coolest, January (12.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 964 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alahabad, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Alahabad

For mild weather, the best months to visit Alahabad are February, March and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (34.0 °C), May (32.5 °C) and July (32.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (4 mm), December (6 mm) and October (8 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (292 h), October (276 h) and April (270 h).

Why Alahabad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 964 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alahabad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.877°N, 74.055°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-531-2540), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 80,084, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
