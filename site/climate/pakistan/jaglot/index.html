Jaglot at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Jaglot is 361st warmest (10.2 °C year average), 360th sunniest (about 2,601 hours of sunshine a year) and 240th wettest (231 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Gilgit, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Jaglot is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 1.8 °C warmer in January, with 54 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.9 °C), averages 22.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, November and December. The area lies about 1,388 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Jaglot has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,388 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.6 °C. The 10 km area around Jaglot, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 7.7 °C a year, 2.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jaglot itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.688°N, 74.624°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-483-2546), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
