Bat Khela at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Bat Khela is 333rd warmest (20.0 °C year average), 355th sunniest (about 2,701 hours of sunshine a year) and 76th wettest (889 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kabal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (35 km away), Bat Khela is 2.9 °C warmer in June and 1.4 °C warmer in January, with 210 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (30.3 °C), averages 22.3 °C more than its coolest, January (8.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,161 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bat Khela, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bat Khela has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,161 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.3 °C. The 10 km area around Bat Khela, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 18.5 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bat Khela itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (34.618°N, 71.972°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-493-2519), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
