Thal at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Thal is the 4th coolest (6.5 °C year average) and the 3rd least sunny (about 2,523 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Upper Dir, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Thal is 5.9 °C cooler in July and 4.1 °C cooler in January, with 3 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.1 °C), averages 22.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 1,253 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Thal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,253 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.2 °C. The 10 km area around Thal, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 4.5 °C a year, 1.9 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Thal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (35.478°N, 72.244°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-485-2522), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
