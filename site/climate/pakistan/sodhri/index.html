Sodhri at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Sodhri is 264th warmest (23.2 °C year average), 203rd sunniest (about 2,975 hours of sunshine a year) and 58th wettest (1,084 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Wazirabad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Sodhri is 0.1 °C cooler in June and about as warm in January, with 33 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (32.9 °C), averages 21.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,088 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sodhri, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Sodhri

For mild weather, the best months to visit Sodhri are March, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): March, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (32.9 °C), July (30.9 °C) and May (30.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (10 mm), October (22 mm) and December (25 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (306 h), October (283 h) and June (282 h).

Why Sodhri has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,088 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sodhri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.462°N, 74.182°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-515-2541), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
