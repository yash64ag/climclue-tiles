Hyderabad at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Hyderabad is 80th warmest (26.9 °C year average), 92nd sunniest (about 3,301 hours of sunshine a year) and 290th wettest (170 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kotri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Hyderabad is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.3 °C), averages 16.1 °C more than its coolest, January (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.0 °C (50 °F), in January. The area lies about 152 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hyderabad, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hyderabad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 152 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hyderabad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.397°N, 68.377°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-586-2483), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
