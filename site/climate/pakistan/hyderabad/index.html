Hyderabad at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Hyderabad is the 4th warmest (27.9 °C year average). Compared with Kotri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Hyderabad is 0.1 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 2 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (34.0 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, January (18.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 152 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hyderabad, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Hyderabad

For mild weather, the best months to visit Hyderabad are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (34.0 °C), May (33.9 °C) and July (32.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (1 mm), January (1 mm) and December (2 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (323 h), October (303 h) and April (292 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Hyderabad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 152 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hyderabad itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.397°N, 68.377°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-586-2483), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,921,275, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
