Sukkur at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Sukkur is 44th warmest (27.4 °C year average), 91st sunniest (about 3,301 hours of sunshine a year) and 357th wettest (112 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rohri, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Sukkur is about as warm in June and about as warm in January, with about the same precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (36.5 °C), averages 20.6 °C more than its coolest, January (15.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.6 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 358 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Sukkur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Sukkur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 358 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sukkur itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (27.703°N, 68.859°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-562-2488), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
