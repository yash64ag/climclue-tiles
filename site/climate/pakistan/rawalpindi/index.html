Rawalpindi weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Rawalpindi, Pakistan (Punjab), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Rawalpindi is warmest in June, with 38.5 °C (101 °F) days and 23.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 17.7 °C (64 °F) days and 2.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (323 mm); November is driest (20 mm).

Rawalpindi at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Rawalpindi is 322nd warmest (21.4 °C year average), 308th sunniest (about 2,889 hours of sunshine a year) and 24th wettest (1,338 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Alipur, Islamabad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Rawalpindi is 0.5 °C warmer in June and about as warm in January, with 170 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (31.0 °C), averages 20.8 °C more than its coolest, January (10.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,113 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Rawalpindi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Rawalpindi

For mild weather, the best months to visit Rawalpindi are April, October and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): April, October and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (31.0 °C), July (29.7 °C) and August (28.6 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (20 mm), October (34 mm) and December (44 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (302 h), June (292 h) and October (271 h).

Why Rawalpindi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 1,113 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Rawalpindi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.597°N, 73.048°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-504-2530), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
