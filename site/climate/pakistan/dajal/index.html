Dajal at a glance

Among the 134 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Pakistan, Dajal is 19th warmest (27.1 °C year average), 37th sunniest (about 3,263 hours of sunshine a year) and 96th wettest (213 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jampur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Dajal is 0.4 °C warmer in June and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 13 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (36.8 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (14.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.1 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 605 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dajal, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dajal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 605 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dajal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.558°N, 70.376°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-544-2503), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
