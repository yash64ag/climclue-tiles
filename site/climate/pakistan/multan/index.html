Multan at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Multan is 131st warmest (25.8 °C year average), 137th sunniest (about 3,083 hours of sunshine a year) and 241st wettest (230 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Qadirpur Ran, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Multan is 0.2 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.6 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (13.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 726 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Multan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Multan

For mild weather, the best months to visit Multan are February, March and November.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March and November.
  • Warmest (average temperature): June (35.6 °C), July (34.1 °C) and August (33.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): November (2 mm), October (6 mm) and December (7 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (296 h), October (277 h) and September (276 h).

Why Multan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 726 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Multan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (30.197°N, 71.478°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-538-2514), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,169,915, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
