Dera Ismail Khan at a glance

Among the 134 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Pakistan, Dera Ismail Khan is 68th warmest (25.3 °C year average), 58th sunniest (about 3,015 hours of sunshine a year) and 62nd wettest (366 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bhakkar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (27 km away), Dera Ismail Khan is 0.2 °C warmer in June and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 10 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.5 °C), averages 22.4 °C more than its coolest, January (13.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.6 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 846 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dera Ismail Khan, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dera Ismail Khan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 846 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
