Dera Ismail Khan weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Dera Ismail Khan, Pakistan (Khyber Pakhtunkhwa), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Dera Ismail Khan is warmest in June, with 42.9 °C (109 °F) days and 28.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 21.1 °C (70 °F) days and 5.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (90 mm); November is driest (4 mm).

Dera Ismail Khan at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Dera Ismail Khan is 165th warmest (25.3 °C year average), 157th sunniest (about 3,015 hours of sunshine a year) and 183rd wettest (363 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Shorkot, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Dera Ismail Khan is about as warm in June and about as warm in January, with 16 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (35.5 °C), averages 22.5 °C more than its coolest, January (13.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.1 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 846 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Dera Ismail Khan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 846 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dera Ismail Khan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.831°N, 70.902°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-521-2509), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
