Jand at a glance

Among the 367 towns ClimaClue covers in Pakistan, Jand is 288th warmest (22.7 °C year average), 304th sunniest (about 2,897 hours of sunshine a year) and 97th wettest (686 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pindi Gheb, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Jand is 0.3 °C warmer in June and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 66 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (33.1 °C), averages 22.3 °C more than its coolest, January (10.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 1,051 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Jand, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Jand has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 1,051 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Jand itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (33.433°N, 72.019°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-505-2520), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 18,170, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
