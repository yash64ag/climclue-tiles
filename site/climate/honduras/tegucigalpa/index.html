Tegucigalpa at a glance

Among the 12 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Honduras, Tegucigalpa is the 2nd coolest (21.3 °C year average), the least sunny (about 2,332 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd driest (1,372 mm a year). Compared with Danlí, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Tegucigalpa is 0.9 °C cooler in April and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 110 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (23.3 °C), averages 3.6 °C more than its coolest, January (19.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.9 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 105 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Tegucigalpa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Tegucigalpa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 105 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
