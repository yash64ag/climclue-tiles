Sivas at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Sivas is 386th warmest (9.8 °C year average), 322nd sunniest (about 2,744 hours of sunshine a year) and 377th wettest (425 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Şarkışla, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Sivas is 0.9 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 63 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.0 °C), averages 23.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 159 km from the sea.

Why Sivas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 159 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sivas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.748°N, 37.016°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-442-2170), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 264,022, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
