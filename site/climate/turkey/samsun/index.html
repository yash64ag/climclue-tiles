Samsun at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Samsun is 244th warmest (14.2 °C year average), 390th sunniest (about 2,566 hours of sunshine a year) and 113th wettest (834 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tekkeköy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Samsun is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 30 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.2 °C), averages 17.1 °C more than its coolest, February (6.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Samsun has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.1 °C. The 10 km area around Samsun, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.1 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Samsun itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.280°N, 36.336°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-427-2163), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
