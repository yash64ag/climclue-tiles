İdil at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, İdil is 107th warmest (16.3 °C year average), 81st sunniest (about 3,060 hours of sunshine a year) and 128th wettest (801 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cizre, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), İdil is 2.8 °C cooler in July and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 96 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (30.6 °C), averages 27.8 °C more than its coolest, January (2.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 434 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of İdil, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why İdil has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 434 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for İdil itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.335°N, 41.889°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-466-2218), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
