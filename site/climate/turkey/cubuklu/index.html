Çubuklu at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Çubuklu is 168th warmest (14.9 °C year average), 342nd sunniest (about 2,713 hours of sunshine a year) and 141st wettest (783 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Şişli, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Çubuklu is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in February, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.3 °C), averages 17.9 °C more than its coolest, February (6.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Çubuklu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Çubuklu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.9 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Çubuklu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.104°N, 29.083°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-428-2090), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
