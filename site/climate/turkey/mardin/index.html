Mardin at a glance

Among the 112 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Turkey, Mardin is 43rd warmest (15.1 °C year average), 35th sunniest (about 3,011 hours of sunshine a year) and 68th wettest (599 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kızıltepe, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Mardin is 3.1 °C cooler in July and 2.8 °C cooler in January, with 140 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.3 °C), averages 27.3 °C more than its coolest, January (2.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 414 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mardin, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mardin has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 414 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.1 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mardin itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.313°N, 40.744°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-466-2207), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
