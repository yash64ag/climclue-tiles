Kelkit weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kelkit, Turkey (Gümüşhane Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kelkit is warmest in August, with 28.6 °C (83 °F) days and 12.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -1.4 °C (29 °F) days and -9.5 °C nights. Most rain falls around May (42 mm); August is driest (5 mm).

Kelkit at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Kelkit is the driest (284 mm a year). Compared with Gümüşhane, the nearest covered town with different numbers (37 km away), Kelkit is 0.9 °C warmer in August and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 277 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.7 °C), averages 26.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 106 km from the sea.

Why Kelkit has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 106 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kelkit itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.127°N, 39.434°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-438-2194), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
