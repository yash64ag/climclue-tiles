Urla at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Urla is 78th warmest (17.7 °C year average), 23rd sunniest (about 3,171 hours of sunshine a year) and 253rd wettest (605 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Seferihisar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (15 km away), Urla is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 125 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.3 °C), averages 16.3 °C more than its coolest, February (10.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.2 °C (43 °F), in February. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Urla, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Urla has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Urla itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.323°N, 26.764°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-456-2067), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
