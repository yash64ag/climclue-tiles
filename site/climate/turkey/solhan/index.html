Solhan at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Solhan is 376th warmest (10.1 °C year average), 280th sunniest (about 2,793 hours of sunshine a year) and 108th wettest (842 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Varto, the nearest covered town with different numbers (42 km away), Solhan is 1.3 °C warmer in August and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 192 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.3 °C), averages 28.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-3.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 243 km from the sea.

Why Solhan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Far from the moderating sea, winters turn cold below freezing while summers are warm. Summer high pressure brings a dry season; most precipitation arrives in winter, often as snow. About 243 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Solhan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.965°N, 41.054°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-450-2210), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
