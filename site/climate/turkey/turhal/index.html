Turhal weather by month and climate

Turhal at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Turhal is 273rd warmest (13.4 °C year average), 253rd sunniest (about 2,828 hours of sunshine a year) and 393rd wettest (396 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zile, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Turhal is 1.0 °C warmer in August and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.0 °C), averages 21.6 °C more than its coolest, January (2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 116 km from the sea.

Why Turhal has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 116 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Turhal itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.388°N, 36.081°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-436-2160), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
