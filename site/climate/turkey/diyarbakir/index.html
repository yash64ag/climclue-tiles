Diyarbakır weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Diyarbakır, Turkey (Diyarbakır Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Diyarbakır is warmest in July, with 39.1 °C (102 °F) days and 23.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 6.4 °C (44 °F) days and -1.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around February (83 mm); July is driest (1 mm).

Diyarbakır at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Diyarbakır is 109th warmest (16.2 °C year average), 100th sunniest (about 3,017 hours of sunshine a year) and 292nd wettest (558 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bismil, the nearest covered town with different numbers (40 km away), Diyarbakır is 0.5 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 51 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (31.1 °C), averages 28.7 °C more than its coolest, January (2.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 345 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Diyarbakır, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Diyarbakır has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 345 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Diyarbakır itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.914°N, 40.217°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-2202), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
