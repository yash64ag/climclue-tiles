Eğirdir at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Eğirdir is 267th warmest (13.6 °C year average), 191st sunniest (about 2,899 hours of sunshine a year) and 260th wettest (601 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Isparta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Eğirdir is 1.3 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 64 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.5 °C), averages 21.4 °C more than its coolest, January (3.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 122 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Eğirdir, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Eğirdir has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 122 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.4 °C. The 10 km area around Eğirdir, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 12.3 °C a year, 1.3 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Eğirdir itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.875°N, 30.850°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-461-2108), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
