Senirkent at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Senirkent is 332nd warmest (12.0 °C year average), 204th sunniest (about 2,879 hours of sunshine a year) and 290th wettest (559 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dinar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Senirkent is 1.8 °C cooler in August and 1.9 °C cooler in January, with 56 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.9 °C), averages 21.4 °C more than its coolest, January (1.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 156 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Senirkent, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Senirkent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 156 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.4 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Senirkent itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.104°N, 30.549°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-458-2105), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
