Istanbul at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Istanbul is 175th warmest (14.8 °C year average), 310th sunniest (about 2,763 hours of sunshine a year) and 140th wettest (783 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Fatih, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Istanbul is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.1 °C), averages 17.7 °C more than its coolest, February (6.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Istanbul, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Istanbul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Istanbul climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Istanbul?
August, with an average of 24.1 °C (75 °F), day highs around 28.4 °C (83 °F) and night lows around 19.8 °C (68 °F).
What is the coldest month in Istanbul?
February, with an average of 6.4 °C (43 °F), day highs around 9.3 °C (49 °F) and night lows around 3.4 °C (38 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Istanbul?
December, with 122 mm (4.8 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 783 mm (30.8 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Istanbul?
July, with 28 mm (1.1 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Istanbul?
July, with about 364 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Istanbul?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 16 cm (6.3 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in February; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time to visit Istanbul?
For mild weather (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August, September and October. Climate only, from 1981–2010 averages: crowds and prices are not considered.
How warm is the sea in Istanbul?
The nearby open sea averages 23.0 °C (73 °F) in August, its warmest month, and 7.2 °C (45 °F) in February. It is 20 °C (68 °F) or warmer in July, August and September. 1981–2010 averages (ERA5); beaches and shallow bays are often a little warmer in summer.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Istanbul itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.014°N, 28.950°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-429-2089), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,701,602, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
