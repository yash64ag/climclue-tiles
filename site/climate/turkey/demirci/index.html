Demirci at a glance

Among the 417 towns ClimaClue covers in Turkey, Demirci is 311th warmest (12.4 °C year average), 215th sunniest (about 2,865 hours of sunshine a year) and 138th wettest (788 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Simav, the nearest covered town with different numbers (28 km away), Demirci is 0.3 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 109 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.9 °C), averages 20.3 °C more than its coolest, January (2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 156 km from the sea.

Why Demirci has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 156 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.3 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Demirci itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.046°N, 28.659°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-449-2086), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
