Hato Mayor del Rey at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in the Dominican Republic, Hato Mayor del Rey is 24th warmest (25.5 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,788 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (1,626 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Cruz de El Seibo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Hato Mayor del Rey is 0.1 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.0 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, January (23.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 17.7 °C (64 °F), in February. The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hato Mayor del Rey, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hato Mayor del Rey has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hato Mayor del Rey itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.766°N, 69.256°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-652-1107), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
