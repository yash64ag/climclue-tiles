Azua at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in the Dominican Republic, Azua is the 4th warmest (26.4 °C year average), the 4th sunniest (about 2,901 hours of sunshine a year) and the 5th driest (1,007 mm a year). Compared with San José de Ocoa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Azua is 2.8 °C warmer in July and 3.0 °C warmer in February, with 565 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.9 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, February (25.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 19.3 °C (67 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Azua, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Azua has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Azua itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.453°N, 70.735°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-655-1092), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
