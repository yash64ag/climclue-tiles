San Juan de la Maguana at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in the Dominican Republic, San Juan de la Maguana is the 2nd driest (880 mm a year). Compared with Las Matas de Farfán, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), San Juan de la Maguana is 0.6 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 351 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.7 °C), averages 2.8 °C more than its coolest, January (22.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.7 °C (60 °F), in January. The area lies about 79 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of San Juan de la Maguana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit San Juan de la Maguana

For mild weather, the best months to visit San Juan de la Maguana are January, February, March, April, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, April, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): August (25.7 °C), July (25.6 °C) and September (25.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (18 mm), January (21 mm) and December (24 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): August (251 h), July (250 h) and March (239 h).

Why San Juan de la Maguana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 79 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Juan de la Maguana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (18.806°N, 71.226°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-651-1087), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
