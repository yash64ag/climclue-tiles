Dajabón at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in the Dominican Republic, Dajabón is the 5th warmest (26.3 °C year average). Compared with San Fernando de Monte Cristi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (34 km away), Dajabón is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 283 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.8 °C), averages 4.7 °C more than its coolest, January (24.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 18.5 °C (65 °F), in January. The area lies about 35 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dajabón, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dajabón has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 35 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dajabón itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.548°N, 71.708°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-644-1082), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
