Alicante at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Alicante is 12th warmest (18.4 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 2,788 hours of sunshine a year) and 60th wettest (321 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Elche, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Alicante is 0.7 °C cooler in August and 0.7 °C warmer in January, with 43 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.1 °C), averages 14.4 °C more than its coolest, January (11.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.3 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Alicante, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Alicante

Why Alicante has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
