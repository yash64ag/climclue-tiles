Málaga at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Málaga is 20th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 9th sunniest (about 2,789 hours of sunshine a year) and 31st wettest (561 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Marbella, the nearest covered town with different numbers (47 km away), Málaga is 1.8 °C warmer in August and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 229 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 13.5 °C more than its coolest, January (11.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.6 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Málaga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
