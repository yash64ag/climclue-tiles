Alcobendas at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Alcobendas is 51st warmest (14.5 °C year average), 40th sunniest (about 2,551 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (483 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chamartín, the nearest covered town with different numbers (10 km away), Alcobendas is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.6 °C), averages 20.4 °C more than its coolest, January (5.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 314 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 12 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 125% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Alcobendas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 314 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Alcobendas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.547°N, 3.642°W). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-434-1763), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
