A Coruña at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, A Coruña is 52nd warmest (14.4 °C year average), 59th sunniest (about 1,916 hours of sunshine a year) and 6th wettest (1,145 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ourense, the nearest covered town with different numbers (123 km away), A Coruña is 1.1 °C cooler in August and 3.0 °C warmer in January, with 215 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (19.1 °C), averages 8.8 °C more than its coolest, January (10.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in February. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why A Coruña has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
