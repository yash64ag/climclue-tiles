Mijas at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Mijas is 278th warmest (16.2 °C year average), 112th sunniest (about 2,767 hours of sunshine a year) and 109th wettest (755 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Benalmádena, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Mijas is 0.7 °C cooler in August and 1.0 °C cooler in January, with 29 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.2 °C), averages 12.7 °C more than its coolest, January (10.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.4 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 15 km from Mijas measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.8 °C (5.1 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Mijas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.7 °C. The 10 km area around Mijas, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.7 °C a year, 1.5 °C warmer than the town itself, because it takes in lower ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mijas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.596°N, 4.637°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-474-1753), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
