Teruel at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Teruel is 532nd warmest (12.3 °C year average), 374th sunniest (about 2,491 hours of sunshine a year) and 465th wettest (368 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Onda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (83 km away), Teruel is 2.7 °C cooler in July and 5.5 °C cooler in January, with 179 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.3 °C), averages 18.5 °C more than its coolest, January (3.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 115 km from the sea.

Why Teruel has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 115 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Teruel itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.346°N, 1.106°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-436-1788), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 36,240, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
