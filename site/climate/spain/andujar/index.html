Andújar at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Andújar is 27th warmest (18.9 °C year average), 72nd sunniest (about 2,792 hours of sunshine a year) and 358th wettest (492 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bailén, the nearest covered town with different numbers (25 km away), Andújar is 1.1 °C warmer in July and 0.9 °C warmer in January, with 57 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.7 °C), averages 20.6 °C more than its coolest, January (9.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 156 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Andújar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Andújar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 156 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Andújar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.039°N, 4.051°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-459-1759), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
