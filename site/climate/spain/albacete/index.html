Albacete at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Albacete is 37th warmest (15.1 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 2,672 hours of sunshine a year) and 57th wettest (369 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Orihuela, the nearest covered town with different numbers (128 km away), Albacete is 1.0 °C cooler in July and 4.5 °C cooler in January, with 77 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.6 °C), averages 19.5 °C more than its coolest, January (6.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 144 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 102% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Albacete has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 144 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
