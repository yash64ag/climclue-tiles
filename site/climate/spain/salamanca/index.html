Salamanca at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Salamanca is the 5th coolest (12.6 °C year average). Compared with Valladolid, the nearest covered town with different numbers (109 km away), Salamanca is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.1 °C), averages 17.6 °C more than its coolest, January (4.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 260 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 126% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Salamanca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 260 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
