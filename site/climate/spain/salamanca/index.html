Salamanca at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Salamanca is 531st warmest (12.5 °C year average), 321st sunniest (about 2,583 hours of sunshine a year) and 389th wettest (461 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zamora, the nearest covered town with different numbers (60 km away), Salamanca is 0.7 °C cooler in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 47 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.0 °C), averages 17.6 °C more than its coolest, January (4.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 260 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 123% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Salamanca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 260 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Salamanca itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.969°N, 5.664°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-430-1743), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
