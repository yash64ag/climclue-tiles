Mollet del Vallès weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Mollet del Vallès, Spain (Catalonia), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Mollet del Vallès is warmest in August, with 28.1 °C (83 °F) days and 19.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 12.3 °C (54 °F) days and 4.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around October (76 mm); July is driest (22 mm).

Mollet del Vallès at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Mollet del Vallès is 331st warmest (15.7 °C year average), 431st sunniest (about 2,326 hours of sunshine a year) and 268th wettest (558 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Perpètua de Mogoda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Mollet del Vallès is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.9 °C), averages 15.6 °C more than its coolest, January (8.2 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Mollet del Vallès has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mollet del Vallès itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.540°N, 2.213°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-424-1822), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
