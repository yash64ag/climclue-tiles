Marbella at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Marbella is 30th warmest (16.1 °C year average), 13th sunniest (about 2,755 hours of sunshine a year) and 12th wettest (790 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Málaga, the nearest covered town with different numbers (47 km away), Marbella is 1.8 °C cooler in August and 0.6 °C cooler in January, with 229 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.9 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, January (10.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.8 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Marbella, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Marbella has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 6.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
