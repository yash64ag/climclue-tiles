Candelaria at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Candelaria is 98th warmest (18.3 °C year average), 203rd sunniest (about 2,692 hours of sunshine a year) and 459th wettest (377 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Güimar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Candelaria is 0.5 °C warmer in September and 1.2 °C warmer in February, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (21.1 °C), averages 5.0 °C more than its coolest, February (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.9 °C (55 °F), in February. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 13 km from Candelaria measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 12.0 °C (21.6 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Candelaria has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.0 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Candelaria itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.355°N, 16.373°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-556-1636), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
