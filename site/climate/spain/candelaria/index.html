Candelaria at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Candelaria is 7th warmest (19.8 °C year average), 203rd sunniest (about 2,692 hours of sunshine a year) and 459th wettest (377 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Güimar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Candelaria is 2.2 °C warmer in September and 2.2 °C warmer in February, with 20 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (22.8 °C), averages 5.7 °C more than its coolest, February (17.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.0 °C (57 °F), in February. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 13 km from Candelaria by up to 12.0 °C (21.6 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations at a similar height, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Candelaria has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.7 °C. The 10 km area around Candelaria, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 17.7 °C a year, 2.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Candelaria itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.355°N, 16.373°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-556-1636), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
