Almassora at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Almassora is 213th warmest (17.4 °C year average), 248th sunniest (about 2,626 hours of sunshine a year) and 350th wettest (500 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vila-real, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Almassora is about as warm in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.4 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, January (10.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.6 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Almassora has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Almassora itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.947°N, 0.063°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-440-1799), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
