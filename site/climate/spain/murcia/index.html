Murcia at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Murcia is 54th warmest (18.5 °C year average), 175th sunniest (about 2,717 hours of sunshine a year) and 527th wettest (271 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Palmar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Murcia is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 24 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.4 °C), averages 16.8 °C more than its coolest, January (10.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 45 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 86% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Why Murcia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 45 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Murcia itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.987°N, 1.130°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-460-1788), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
