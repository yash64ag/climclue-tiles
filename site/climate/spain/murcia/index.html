Murcia at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Murcia is the 5th driest (296 mm a year). Compared with Orihuela, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Murcia is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 4 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.8 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 45 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Murcia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 45 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
