Ubrique at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Ubrique is 255th warmest (16.6 °C year average), 110th sunniest (about 2,768 hours of sunshine a year) and 28th wettest (1,593 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ronda, the nearest covered town with different numbers (26 km away), Ubrique is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 1.3 °C warmer in January, with 728 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.2 °C), averages 15.9 °C more than its coolest, January (9.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.3 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 43 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Ubrique, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Ubrique has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 43 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-473-1745), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
