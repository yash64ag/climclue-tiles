Tacoronte at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Tacoronte is 225th warmest (17.3 °C year average), 213th sunniest (about 2,682 hours of sunshine a year) and 409th wettest (439 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with La Laguna, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Tacoronte is 0.8 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 22 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.0 °C), averages 6.6 °C more than its coolest, February (14.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.2 °C (52 °F), in February. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 8 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 79% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tacoronte has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.6 °C. The 10 km area around Tacoronte, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.2 °C a year, 1.1 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tacoronte itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.477°N, 16.410°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-555-1635), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
