Why Santa Cruz de Tenerife has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Santa Cruz de Tenerife climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Santa Cruz de Tenerife?
August, with an average of 21.4 °C (70 °F), day highs around 22.0 °C (72 °F) and night lows around 20.9 °C (70 °F).
What is the coldest month in Santa Cruz de Tenerife?
February, with an average of 15.8 °C (60 °F), day highs around 16.5 °C (62 °F) and night lows around 15.2 °C (59 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Santa Cruz de Tenerife?
November, with 69 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 427 mm (16.8 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Santa Cruz de Tenerife?
July, with 4 mm (0.2 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Santa Cruz de Tenerife?
July, with about 313 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Santa Cruz de Tenerife?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Santa Cruz de Tenerife?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, April, May, June, July, August, September, October, November and December.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 9.8 × 11 km, that contains Santa Cruz de Tenerife’s GeoNames location (28.468°N, 16.255°W; ClimaClue area sq-555-1637), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 211,359 (GeoNames).
