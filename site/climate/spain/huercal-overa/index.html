Huercal-Overa at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Huercal-Overa is 163rd warmest (18.0 °C year average), 87th sunniest (about 2,786 hours of sunshine a year) and 519th wettest (284 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Águilas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Huercal-Overa is 0.5 °C warmer in August and 2.2 °C cooler in January, with 82 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.2 °C), averages 15.7 °C more than its coolest, January (10.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.0 °C (43 °F), in January. The area lies about 28 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Huercal-Overa, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Huercal-Overa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 28 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Huercal-Overa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.389°N, 1.943°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-466-1780), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
