Cuenca at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Cuenca is 524th warmest (12.9 °C year average), 357th sunniest (about 2,538 hours of sunshine a year) and 135th wettest (682 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tarancón, the nearest covered town with different numbers (75 km away), Cuenca is 1.8 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 217 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 19.4 °C more than its coolest, January (4.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January. The area lies about 176 km from the sea.

Why Cuenca has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 176 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cuenca itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.067°N, 2.133°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-439-1778), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
