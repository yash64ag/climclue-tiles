Why Madrid has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 318 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Madrid climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Madrid?
July, with an average of 25.9 °C (79 °F), day highs around 31.9 °C (89 °F) and night lows around 17.8 °C (64 °F).
What is the coldest month in Madrid?
January, with an average of 5.3 °C (41 °F), day highs around 9.6 °C (49 °F) and night lows around 0.6 °C (33 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Madrid?
October, with 60 mm (2.3 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 439 mm (17.3 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Madrid?
July, with 11 mm (0.4 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Madrid?
July, with about 349 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Madrid?
Yes. ERA5-Land gives about 11 cm (4.2 in) of fresh snow in a typical year (estimated as 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), most in February; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time for mild weather in Madrid?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: May, June and September.
What type of climate does Madrid have?
Mediterranean, hot summer (Köppen Csa). Hot, dry summers and mild, wetter winters. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 8.5 × 11 km, that contains Madrid’s GeoNames location (40.416°N, 3.703°W; ClimaClue area sq-435-1762), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 3,255,944 (GeoNames).
