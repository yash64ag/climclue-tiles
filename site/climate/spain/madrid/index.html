Madrid at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Madrid is 44th warmest (14.8 °C year average), 35th sunniest (about 2,597 hours of sunshine a year) and 49th wettest (439 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Ciudad Lineal, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Madrid is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 16 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.9 °C), averages 20.7 °C more than its coolest, January (5.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 0.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 318 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Madrid has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 318 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
