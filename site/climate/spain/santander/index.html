Santander weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Santander, Spain (Cantabria), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Santander at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Santander is 403rd warmest (14.8 °C year average), 514th sunniest (about 1,733 hours of sunshine a year) and 35th wettest (1,474 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with El Astillero, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Santander is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.5 °C), averages 10.5 °C more than its coolest, January (10.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.8 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 122% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Santander has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Santander itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.466°N, 3.805°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-405-1761), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
