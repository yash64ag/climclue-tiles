Felanitx at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Felanitx is 192nd warmest (17.7 °C year average), 243rd sunniest (about 2,631 hours of sunshine a year) and 347th wettest (505 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Manacor, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Felanitx is 0.2 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.4 °C), averages 14.1 °C more than its coolest, January (11.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.2 °C (45 °F), in January. The area lies about 17 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Felanitx has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Felanitx itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.470°N, 3.148°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-445-1831), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
