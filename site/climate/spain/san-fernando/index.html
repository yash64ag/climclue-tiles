San Fernando at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, San Fernando is 106th warmest (18.3 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 2,919 hours of sunshine a year) and 202nd wettest (606 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Puerto Real, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), San Fernando is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.6 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, January (12.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.2 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 110% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why San Fernando has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Fernando itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.476°N, 6.198°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-475-1738), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
