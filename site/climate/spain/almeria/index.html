Almería at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Almería is the 4th warmest (18.7 °C year average) and the driest (274 mm a year). Compared with Granada, the nearest covered town with different numbers (109 km away), Almería is about as warm in August and 4.5 °C warmer in January, with 242 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.3 °C), averages 14.1 °C more than its coolest, January (12.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.0 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 131% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Almería has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
