Almería at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Almería is 11th warmest (19.2 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 2,828 hours of sunshine a year) and 530th wettest (264 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Roquetas de Mar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Almería is 0.8 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.4 °C), averages 13.5 °C more than its coolest, January (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.8 °C (48 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 127% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Almería has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.5 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Almería itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.838°N, 2.460°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-471-1775), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
