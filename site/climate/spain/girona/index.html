Girona at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Girona is 367th warmest (15.2 °C year average), 456th sunniest (about 2,235 hours of sunshine a year) and 122nd wettest (706 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Salt, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Girona is 0.2 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 36 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.0 °C), averages 16.6 °C more than its coolest, January (7.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Girona has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Girona itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.983°N, 2.825°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-420-1828), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
