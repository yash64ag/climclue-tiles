Girona at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Girona is 40th warmest (15.0 °C year average), 53rd sunniest (about 2,235 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (726 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mataró, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Girona is 1.0 °C warmer in July and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.6 °C), averages 16.4 °C more than its coolest, January (7.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Girona has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
