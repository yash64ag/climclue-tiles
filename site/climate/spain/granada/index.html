Granada at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Granada is 24th warmest (16.4 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 2,802 hours of sunshine a year) and 38th wettest (516 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Jaén, the nearest covered town with different numbers (67 km away), Granada is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.6 °C), averages 19.0 °C more than its coolest, January (7.7 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 2.2 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 56 km from the sea.

Why Granada has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 56 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
