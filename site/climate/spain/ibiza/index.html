Ibiza at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Ibiza is 12th warmest (19.1 °C year average), 177th sunniest (about 2,717 hours of sunshine a year) and 391st wettest (460 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Santa Eulària des Riu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Ibiza is 0.2 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.9 °C), averages 13.9 °C more than its coolest, January (12.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.3 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ibiza has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ibiza itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (38.909°N, 1.433°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-450-1814), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 49,727, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
