Tías at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Tías is the 4th warmest (20.3 °C year average). Compared with Puerto del Carmen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Tías is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 0.9 °C cooler in January, with 28 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.7 °C), averages 8.2 °C more than its coolest, January (16.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.2 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: before adjustment, the modelled temperatures differed from a NOAA weather station 5 km from Tías by up to 3.0 °C (5.5 °F) in one month. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Tías has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tías itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.961°N, 13.645°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-550-1663), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 19,849, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
