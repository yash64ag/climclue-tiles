Güimar at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Güimar is 207th warmest (17.5 °C year average), 199th sunniest (about 2,694 hours of sunshine a year) and 449th wettest (397 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Candelaria, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Güimar is 0.5 °C cooler in September and 1.4 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (20.6 °C), averages 5.8 °C more than its coolest, January (14.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.7 °C (53 °F), in February. The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 8 km from Güimar measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 10.5 °C (18.9 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Güimar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 5.8 °C. The 10 km area around Güimar, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 14.6 °C a year, 3.0 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Güimar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.311°N, 16.413°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-556-1635), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
