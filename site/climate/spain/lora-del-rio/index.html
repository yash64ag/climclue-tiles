Lora del Río at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Lora del Río is 16th warmest (19.4 °C year average), 74th sunniest (about 2,791 hours of sunshine a year) and 158th wettest (645 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Palma del Río, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Lora del Río is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 49 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.1 °C), averages 18.6 °C more than its coolest, January (10.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.2 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 125 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lora del Río, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Lora del Río has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 125 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lora del Río itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.659°N, 5.528°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-463-1744), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
