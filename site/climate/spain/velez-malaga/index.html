Vélez-Málaga at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Vélez-Málaga is 146th warmest (18.1 °C year average), 65th sunniest (about 2,795 hours of sunshine a year) and 294th wettest (546 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Torre del Mar, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Vélez-Málaga is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.8 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, January (12.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.2 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Vélez-Málaga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Vélez-Málaga itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.781°N, 4.103°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-472-1758), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
