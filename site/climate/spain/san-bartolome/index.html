San Bartolomé at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, San Bartolomé is 31st warmest (18.8 °C year average), 169th sunniest (about 2,724 hours of sunshine a year) and 536th wettest (182 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tías, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), San Bartolomé is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C cooler in February, with 8 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (22.2 °C), averages 6.3 °C more than its coolest, February (15.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.4 °C (54 °F), in February. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Why San Bartolomé has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for San Bartolomé itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.001°N, 13.613°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-549-1663), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
