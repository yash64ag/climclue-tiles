Tàrrega at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Tàrrega is 439th warmest (14.6 °C year average), 372nd sunniest (about 2,503 hours of sunshine a year) and 369th wettest (480 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Balaguer, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Tàrrega is 1.0 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 43 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (24.7 °C), averages 19.3 °C more than its coolest, January (5.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.3 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 67 km from the sea.

Why Tàrrega has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 67 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tàrrega itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.647°N, 1.140°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-423-1811), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
