Toledo weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Toledo, Spain (Castille-La Mancha), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Toledo is warmest in July, with 34.0 °C (93 °F) days and 19.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 10.3 °C (51 °F) days and 1.9 °C nights. Most rain falls around December (48 mm); July is driest (8 mm).

Toledo at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Toledo is 326th warmest (15.7 °C year average), 218th sunniest (about 2,667 hours of sunshine a year) and 467th wettest (363 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Illescas, the nearest covered town with different numbers (33 km away), Toledo is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 82 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (26.8 °C), averages 20.7 °C more than its coolest, January (6.0 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 1.9 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 328 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Toledo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 328 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 20.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Toledo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (39.858°N, 4.023°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-441-1759), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
