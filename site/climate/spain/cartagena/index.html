Cartagena at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Cartagena is 13th warmest (18.2 °C year average), 39th sunniest (about 2,558 hours of sunshine a year) and 61st wettest (308 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Murcia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Cartagena is 1.3 °C cooler in August and 2.1 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (25.4 °C), averages 13.1 °C more than its coolest, January (12.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 9.7 °C (49 °F), in January. The area lies about 22 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cartagena, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cartagena has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
