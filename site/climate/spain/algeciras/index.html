Algeciras at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Algeciras is 18th warmest (17.6 °C year average), 17th sunniest (about 2,725 hours of sunshine a year) and 13th wettest (787 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Marbella, the nearest covered town with different numbers (66 km away), Algeciras is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 2.3 °C warmer in January, with 4 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.3 °C), averages 10.4 °C more than its coolest, January (12.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.7 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 14 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 19 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 123% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Algeciras has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
