Totana at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Totana is 218th warmest (17.4 °C year average), 133rd sunniest (about 2,751 hours of sunshine a year) and 508th wettest (298 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Alhama de Murcia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Totana is 0.2 °C cooler in August and about as warm in January, with 18 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.0 °C), averages 16.5 °C more than its coolest, January (9.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, January, averages 4.8 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 38 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Totana, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Totana has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 38 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Totana itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.769°N, 1.502°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-462-1784), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 29,211, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
