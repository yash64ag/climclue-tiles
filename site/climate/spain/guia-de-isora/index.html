Guía de Isora at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Guía de Isora is 231st warmest (17.1 °C year average), 252nd sunniest (about 2,625 hours of sunshine a year) and 160th wettest (644 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Adeje, the nearest covered town with different numbers (11 km away), Guía de Isora is 1.0 °C cooler in August and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 176 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.1 °C), averages 7.1 °C more than its coolest, January (14.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.9 °C (52 °F), in February. The area lies about 20 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Guía de Isora has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 7.1 °C. The 10 km area around Guía de Isora, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 13.9 °C a year, 3.2 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown.
