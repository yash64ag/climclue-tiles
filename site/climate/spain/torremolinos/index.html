Torremolinos at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Torremolinos is 123rd warmest (18.2 °C year average), 80th sunniest (about 2,789 hours of sunshine a year) and 183rd wettest (626 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Arroyo de la Miel, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Torremolinos is 0.3 °C warmer in August and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 51 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.7 °C), averages 12.3 °C more than its coolest, January (12.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.5 °C (47 °F), in January. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Torremolinos has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Torremolinos itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (36.620°N, 4.500°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-473-1755), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
