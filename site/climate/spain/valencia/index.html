Valencia at a glance

Among the 66 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Spain, Valencia is 14th warmest (18.2 °C year average), 36th sunniest (about 2,585 hours of sunshine a year) and 36th wettest (524 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Castelló de la Plana, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Valencia is 1.0 °C warmer in August and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 30 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (26.3 °C), averages 15.2 °C more than its coolest, January (11.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 7.5 °C (46 °F), in January. The area lies about 9 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Valencia, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Valencia has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
