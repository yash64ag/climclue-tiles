Camas at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Camas is 22nd warmest (18.9 °C year average), 31st sunniest (about 2,838 hours of sunshine a year) and 200th wettest (608 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Castilleja de la Cuesta, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Camas is 0.6 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C warmer in January, with 42 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (27.7 °C), averages 17.0 °C more than its coolest, January (10.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.7 °C (42 °F), in January. The area lies about 77 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 108% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Camas has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 77 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Camas itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (37.402°N, 6.033°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-465-1739), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
