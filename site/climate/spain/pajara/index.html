Pájara at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Pájara is the 4th driest (154 mm a year). Compared with Puerto del Rosario, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Pájara is 0.4 °C warmer in August and 1.2 °C cooler in January, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.4 °C), averages 8.3 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.0 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pájara, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Pájara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 8.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pájara itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (28.350°N, 14.108°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-556-1658), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
