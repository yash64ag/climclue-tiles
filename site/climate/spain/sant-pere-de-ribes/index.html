Sant Pere de Ribes at a glance

Among the 544 towns ClimaClue covers in Spain, Sant Pere de Ribes is 309th warmest (16.0 °C year average), 385th sunniest (about 2,449 hours of sunshine a year) and 254th wettest (567 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sitges, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Sant Pere de Ribes is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 19 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.9 °C), averages 14.6 °C more than its coolest, January (9.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 5.1 °C (41 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 25 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 97% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Sant Pere de Ribes has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sant Pere de Ribes itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.260°N, 1.774°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-427-1817), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
