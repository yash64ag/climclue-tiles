Sevan weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Sevan, Armenia (Gegharkunik), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Sevan is warmest in August, with 23.1 °C (74 °F) days and 9.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -1.0 °C (30 °F) days and -10.4 °C nights. Most rain falls around May (98 mm); December is driest (31 mm).

Sevan at a glance

Among the 19 towns ClimaClue covers in Armenia, Sevan is the coolest (5.6 °C year average). Compared with Hrazdan, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Sevan is 1.4 °C cooler in August and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 115 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (16.1 °C), averages 21.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 307 km from the sea.

Why Sevan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 307 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 21.8 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sevan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (40.548°N, 44.953°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-434-2249), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
