Cayenne at a glance

Among the 5 towns ClimaClue covers in French Guiana, Cayenne is the 2nd warmest (26.2 °C year average), the 3rd sunniest (about 2,166 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (3,258 mm a year). Compared with Rémire-Montjoly, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Cayenne is about as warm in October and about as warm in January, with 168 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.1 °C), averages 1.7 °C more than its coolest, January (25.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.8 °C (71 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 12 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 94% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Cayenne has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cayenne itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (4.938°N, 52.335°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-790-1276), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
