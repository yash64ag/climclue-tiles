Nakuru at a glance

Among the 28 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Kenya, Nakuru is 19th warmest (18.7 °C year average), 21st sunniest (about 2,424 hours of sunshine a year) and 21st wettest (882 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Naivasha, the nearest covered town with different numbers (60 km away), Nakuru is 1.9 °C warmer in March and 2.1 °C warmer in July, with 123 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (20.4 °C), averages 3.1 °C more than its coolest, July (17.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in September. The area lies about 542 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nakuru, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nakuru has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 542 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
