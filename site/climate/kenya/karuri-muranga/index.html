Karuri, Murang'A at a glance

Among the 120 towns ClimaClue covers in Kenya, Karuri, Murang'A is 57th warmest (20.3 °C year average), 105th sunniest (about 2,374 hours of sunshine a year) and 40th wettest (1,317 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Murang’a, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Karuri, Murang'A is 0.4 °C warmer in March and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 3 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (21.9 °C), averages 3.5 °C more than its coolest, July (18.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.4 °C (56 °F), in July. The area lies about 419 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Karuri, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Karuri, Murang'A has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 419 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Karuri itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.700°S, 37.183°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-846-2171), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
