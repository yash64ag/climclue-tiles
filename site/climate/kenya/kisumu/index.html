Kisumu at a glance

Among the 120 towns ClimaClue covers in Kenya, Kisumu is 27th warmest (23.1 °C year average), 32nd sunniest (about 2,844 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (1,530 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Mbale, the nearest covered town with different numbers (21 km away), Kisumu is 3.2 °C warmer in February and 3.3 °C warmer in July, with 443 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (24.0 °C), averages 1.6 °C more than its coolest, July (22.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.0 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 681 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kisumu, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kisumu

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kisumu are January, February, July and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, July and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (24.0 °C), March (23.9 °C) and January (23.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): February (83 mm), January (90 mm) and July (94 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (269 h), February (258 h) and March (255 h).

Why Kisumu has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 681 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 1.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kisumu itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.102°S, 34.762°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-841-2147), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 397,957, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
