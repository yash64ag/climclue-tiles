Nairobi at a glance

Among the 28 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Kenya, Nairobi is 18th warmest (18.9 °C year average), 18th sunniest (about 2,464 hours of sunshine a year) and 22nd wettest (860 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kiambu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (13 km away), Nairobi is 0.5 °C warmer in March and 0.4 °C warmer in July, with 94 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (20.6 °C), averages 3.7 °C more than its coolest, July (16.9 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.2 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 418 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nairobi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nairobi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 418 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
