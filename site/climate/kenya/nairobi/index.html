Why Nairobi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 418 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

Nairobi climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Nairobi?
March, with an average of 20.6 °C (69 °F), day highs around 26.1 °C (79 °F) and night lows around 15.0 °C (59 °F).
What is the coldest month in Nairobi?
July, with an average of 16.9 °C (63 °F), day highs around 21.1 °C (70 °F) and night lows around 12.2 °C (54 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Nairobi?
April, with 193 mm (7.6 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 860 mm (33.9 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Nairobi?
August, with 18 mm (0.7 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Nairobi?
January, with about 282 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Nairobi?
Not in a typical year: ERA5-Land gives less than 1 cm of fresh snow a year for this area (1981–2010).
When is the best time for mild weather in Nairobi?
Counting months with an average temperature of 15–25 °C (59–77 °F) and under 100 mm (3.9 in) of precipitation as mild: January, February, March, June, July, August, September, October and December.
What type of climate does Nairobi have?
Mediterranean, warm summer (Köppen Csb). Warm, dry summers and mild, wet winters. Calculated from this area’s 1981–2010 monthly averages.

About these numbers

All values are modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for the 0.1° area, about 11 × 11 km, that contains Nairobi’s GeoNames location (1.283°S, 36.817°E; ClimaClue area sq-852-2168), averaged over the area’s land. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station. Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010, ~1 km cells. Dew point and snow: ERA5-Land, 1981–2010, ~9 km cells. Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990, ~18 km cells (a different period from the rest). The climate type is calculated from the area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). Population 4,397,073 (GeoNames).

Sources: CHELSA V2.1 (1981–2010; temperature, precipitation, wind, humidity), ERA5-Land (Copernicus, 1981–2010; dew point, snow; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information), CRU CL 2.0 (New et al. 2002, about 1961–1990; sunshine), GeoNames (town names and populations, CC BY 4.0), climate types by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
