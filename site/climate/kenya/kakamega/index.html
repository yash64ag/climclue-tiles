Kakamega at a glance

Among the 28 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Kenya, Kakamega is the 3rd wettest (1,729 mm a year). Compared with Khwisero, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Kakamega is 0.4 °C cooler in February and 0.8 °C cooler in July, with 111 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, February (21.6 °C), averages 2.3 °C more than its coolest, July (19.3 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.7 °C (58 °F), in July. The area lies about 701 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kakamega, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Kakamega

For mild weather, the best months to visit Kakamega are January, February and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): February (21.6 °C), March (21.4 °C) and January (20.9 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (86 mm), February (89 mm) and December (97 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (266 h), December (259 h) and March (248 h).

Why Kakamega has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months. Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry. About 701 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
