Kerugoya at a glance

Among the 120 towns ClimaClue covers in Kenya, Kerugoya is 87th warmest (18.3 °C year average), 112th sunniest (about 2,210 hours of sunshine a year) and 18th wettest (1,520 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Karatina, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Kerugoya is 1.6 °C warmer in March and 1.4 °C warmer in July, with 76 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (19.8 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, July (16.4 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.8 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 423 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kerugoya, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kerugoya has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 423 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C. The 10 km area around Kerugoya, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 16.8 °C a year, 1.5 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-844-2172), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
