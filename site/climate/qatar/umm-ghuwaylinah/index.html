Umm Ghuwaylīnah at a glance

Among the 10 towns ClimaClue covers in Qatar, Umm Ghuwaylīnah is the 2nd least sunny (about 3,369 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (105 mm a year). Compared with Umm Şalāl Muḩammad, the nearest covered town with different numbers (32 km away), Umm Ghuwaylīnah is 0.8 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (35.7 °C), averages 19.1 °C more than its coolest, January (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.8 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 37 km from the sea.

Why Umm Ghuwaylīnah has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 37 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Umm Ghuwaylīnah itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (25.587°N, 51.150°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-584-2311), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
