Adjarra at a glance

Among the 66 towns ClimaClue covers in Benin, Adjarra is the 2nd wettest (1,396 mm a year). Compared with Avrankou, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Adjarra is about as warm in March and about as warm in August, with 7 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.9 °C), averages 3.2 °C more than its coolest, August (24.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.9 °C (71 °F), in August. The area lies about 33 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Adjarra, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Adjarra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 33 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.2 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Adjarra itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (6.534°N, 2.668°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-774-1826), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
