Thị Trấn Đại Từ at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Thị Trấn Đại Từ is 279th warmest (23.6 °C year average), 303rd sunniest (about 1,613 hours of sunshine a year) and 122nd wettest (1,917 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thái Nguyên, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Thị Trấn Đại Từ is 0.1 °C cooler in June and about as warm in January, with 96 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (28.7 °C), averages 12.6 °C more than its coolest, January (16.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.0 °C (55 °F), in January. The area lies about 167 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Thị Trấn Đại Từ, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Thị Trấn Đại Từ has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 167 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Thị Trấn Đại Từ itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.634°N, 105.638°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-623-2856), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
