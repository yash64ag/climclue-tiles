Hạ Long at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Hạ Long is 273rd warmest (23.7 °C year average), 184th sunniest (about 1,743 hours of sunshine a year) and 108th wettest (1,966 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cẩm Phả, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Hạ Long is 0.6 °C warmer in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 530 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (28.9 °C), averages 12.1 °C more than its coolest, January (16.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.5 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hạ Long, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hạ Long has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hạ Long itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (20.950°N, 107.073°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-630-2870), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
