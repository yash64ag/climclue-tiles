Phong Điền, Can Tho City at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Phong Điền, Can Tho City is 79th warmest (27.0 °C year average), 95th sunniest (about 2,039 hours of sunshine a year) and 232nd wettest (1,768 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Cái Răng, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Phong Điền, Can Tho City is about as warm in April and about as warm in January, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.9 °C), averages 3.4 °C more than its coolest, January (25.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in January. The area lies about 77 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Phong Điền, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Phong Điền, Can Tho City has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 77 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Phong Điền itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (9.998°N, 105.667°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-740-2856), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
