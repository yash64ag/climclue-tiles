Chiềng Cơi weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Chiềng Cơi, Vietnam (Sơn La Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Chiềng Cơi is warmest in June, with 29.2 °C (85 °F) days and 22.2 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 19.9 °C (68 °F) days and 11.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (245 mm); December is driest (17 mm).

Chiềng Cơi at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Chiềng Cơi is 305th warmest (21.8 °C year average), 197th sunniest (about 1,731 hours of sunshine a year) and 303rd wettest (1,333 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sơn La, the nearest covered town with different numbers (3 km away), Chiềng Cơi is 0.3 °C cooler in June and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 50 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (25.7 °C), averages 9.9 °C more than its coolest, January (15.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.6 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 275 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Chiềng Cơi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Chiềng Cơi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 275 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 9.9 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.7 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chiềng Cơi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.300°N, 103.900°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-626-2839), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
