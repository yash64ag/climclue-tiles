Pleiku at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Pleiku is 286th warmest (23.1 °C year average), 67th sunniest (about 2,110 hours of sunshine a year) and 25th wettest (2,667 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kon Tum, the nearest covered town with different numbers (41 km away), Pleiku is 1.1 °C cooler in April and 0.7 °C cooler in December, with 1,007 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (25.1 °C), averages 4.0 °C more than its coolest, December (21.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.7 °C (62 °F), in December. The area lies about 134 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Pleiku, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Pleiku

For mild weather, the best months to visit Pleiku are January, February, March, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, March, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): April (25.1 °C), May (24.5 °C) and March (24.4 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): January (3 mm), February (6 mm) and December (17 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): March (218 h), January (207 h) and April (198 h).

Why Pleiku has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 134 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 4.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Pleiku itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (13.983°N, 108.000°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-700-2880), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 114,225, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
