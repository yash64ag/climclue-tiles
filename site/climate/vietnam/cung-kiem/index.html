Cung Kiệm weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Cung Kiệm, Vietnam (Bac Ninh), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Cung Kiệm is warmest in July, with 33.1 °C (92 °F) days and 25.9 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 19.6 °C (67 °F) days and 13.8 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (322 mm); December is driest (25 mm).

Cung Kiệm at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Cung Kiệm is 253rd warmest (24.0 °C year average), 249th sunniest (about 1,654 hours of sunshine a year) and 213th wettest (1,829 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bắc Ninh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Cung Kiệm is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 38 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.4 °C), averages 12.8 °C more than its coolest, January (16.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.8 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 91 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Cung Kiệm, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Cung Kiệm has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 91 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Cung Kiệm itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (21.187°N, 106.161°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-628-2861), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 80,000, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
