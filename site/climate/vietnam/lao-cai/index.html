Lào Cai at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Lào Cai is 171st warmest (24.5 °C year average), 293rd sunniest (about 1,634 hours of sunshine a year) and 288th wettest (1,524 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thị Trấn Phố Lu, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Lào Cai is 0.4 °C warmer in June and 0.6 °C warmer in January, with 43 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, June (29.9 °C), averages 13.2 °C more than its coolest, January (16.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.1 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 357 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lào Cai, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lào Cai has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 357 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.2 °C. The 10 km area around Lào Cai, which the globe shows when you click there, averages 22.9 °C a year, 1.6 °C cooler than the town itself, because it takes in higher ground.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lào Cai itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.486°N, 103.971°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-615-2839), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
