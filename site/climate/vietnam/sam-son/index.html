Sầm Sơn weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Sầm Sơn, Vietnam (Thanh Hóa Province), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Sầm Sơn is warmest in July, with 33.0 °C (91 °F) days and 26.3 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 21.5 °C (71 °F) days and 14.1 °C nights. Most rain falls around September (373 mm); February is driest (28 mm).

Sầm Sơn at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Sầm Sơn is 174th warmest (24.3 °C year average), 134th sunniest (about 1,890 hours of sunshine a year) and 223rd wettest (1,799 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Thanh Hóa, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Sầm Sơn is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.3 °C warmer in January, with 49 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (29.6 °C), averages 11.8 °C more than its coolest, January (17.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 14.1 °C (57 °F), in January. The area lies about 10 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Sầm Sơn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Sầm Sơn itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (19.733°N, 105.900°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-642-2859), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
