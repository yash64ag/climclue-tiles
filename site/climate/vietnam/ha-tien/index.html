Hà Tiên at a glance

Among the 310 towns ClimaClue covers in Vietnam, Hà Tiên is the 3rd sunniest (about 2,327 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Hàm Ninh, the nearest covered town with different numbers (53 km away), Hà Tiên is 0.6 °C warmer in April and 0.5 °C cooler in December, with 957 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (28.7 °C), averages 2.5 °C more than its coolest, December (26.2 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.8 °C (71 °F), in January. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hà Tiên, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hà Tiên has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hà Tiên itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (10.383°N, 104.488°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-736-2844), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 100,560, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
