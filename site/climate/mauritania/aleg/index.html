Aleg at a glance

Among the 26 towns ClimaClue covers in Mauritania, Aleg is 10th warmest (30.2 °C year average), 12th sunniest (about 3,100 hours of sunshine a year) and 16th wettest (187 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bogué, the nearest covered town with different numbers (63 km away), Aleg is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 36 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (34.4 °C), averages 10.8 °C more than its coolest, January (23.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.9 °C (61 °F), in January. The area lies about 240 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Aleg, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Aleg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large. Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare. About 240 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Aleg itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (17.053°N, 13.913°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-669-1660), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
