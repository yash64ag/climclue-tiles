Malabo at a glance

Among the 6 towns ClimaClue covers in Equatorial Guinea, Malabo is the warmest (26.0 °C year average), the sunniest (about 1,504 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (2,527 mm a year). Compared with Bata, the nearest covered town with different numbers (237 km away), Malabo is 0.2 °C warmer in March and 0.3 °C warmer in August, with 351 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, March (27.0 °C), averages 2.3 °C more than its coolest, August (24.7 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.8 °C (71 °F), in September. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Malabo, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Malabo has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Malabo itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.756°N, 8.782°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-802-1887), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
