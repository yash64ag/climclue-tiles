Khabarovsk at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Khabarovsk is 129th warmest (2.2 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 2,317 hours of sunshine a year) and 39th wettest (704 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Komsomolsk-on-Amur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (270 km away), Khabarovsk is 1.2 °C warmer in July and 1.4 °C warmer in January, with 149 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.6 °C), averages 43.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-21.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 317 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 104% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Khabarovsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 317 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 43.0 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
