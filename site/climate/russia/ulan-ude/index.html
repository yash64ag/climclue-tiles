Ulan-Ude at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Ulan-Ude is the 4th sunniest (about 2,356 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (245 mm a year). Compared with Irkutsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (232 km away), Ulan-Ude is 1.3 °C warmer in July and 3.5 °C cooler in January, with 203 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.7 °C), averages 43.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-23.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,602 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, under 1 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ulan-Ude has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,602 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 43.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.0 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
