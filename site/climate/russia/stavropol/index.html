Stavropol at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Stavropol is 23rd warmest (9.0 °C year average), 28th sunniest (about 2,139 hours of sunshine a year) and 22nd wettest (729 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Nevinnomyssk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Stavropol is 1.6 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 178 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.1 °C), averages 23.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 246 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 130% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Stavropol has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 246 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 23.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
