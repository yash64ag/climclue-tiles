Chita at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Chita is the sunniest (about 2,455 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Ulan-Ude, the nearest covered town with different numbers (405 km away), Chita is 0.5 °C warmer in July and 2.0 °C cooler in January, with 75 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.6 °C), averages 44.9 °C more than its coolest, January (-25.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,379 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Chita has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 1,379 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 44.9 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
