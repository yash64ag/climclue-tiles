Tyumen weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Tyumen, Russia (Tyumen Oblast), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Tyumen is warmest in July, with 24.4 °C (76 °F) days and 13.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -11.0 °C (12 °F) days and -19.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around July (91 mm); February is driest (16 mm).

Tyumen at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Tyumen is 767th warmest (2.1 °C year average), 279th sunniest (about 2,043 hours of sunshine a year) and 794th wettest (498 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Borovskiy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (17 km away), Tyumen is 0.2 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 26 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.0 °C), averages 34.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-15.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,060 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 103% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Tyumen has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,060 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 34.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tyumen itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (57.152°N, 65.527°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-268-2455), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 768,358, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
