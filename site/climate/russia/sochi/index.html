Sochi at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Sochi is the warmest (14.5 °C year average) and the wettest (2,157 mm a year). Compared with Maykop, the nearest covered town with different numbers (116 km away), Sochi is 1.3 °C warmer in August and 5.7 °C warmer in February, with 1,315 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (23.9 °C), averages 18.0 °C more than its coolest, February (5.8 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 3.4 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 8 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 4 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 127% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Sochi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.0 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
