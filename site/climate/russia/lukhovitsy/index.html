Lukhovitsy at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Lukhovitsy is 306th warmest (5.7 °C year average), 659th sunniest (about 1,774 hours of sunshine a year) and 492nd wettest (620 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kolomna, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Lukhovitsy is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C warmer in February, with 21 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.5 °C), averages 27.2 °C more than its coolest, February (-7.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 769 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lukhovitsy, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Why Lukhovitsy has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lukhovitsy itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.977°N, 39.044°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-290-2190), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 32,387, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
