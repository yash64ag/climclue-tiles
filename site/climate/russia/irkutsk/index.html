Irkutsk at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Irkutsk is 155th warmest (0.7 °C year average), 19th sunniest (about 2,178 hours of sunshine a year) and 140th wettest (480 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Angarsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (39 km away), Irkutsk is 1.1 °C cooler in July and 1.0 °C warmer in January, with 67 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.1 °C), averages 38.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-19.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,797 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Irkutsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 1,797 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 38.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
