Kaluga at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Kaluga is 52nd warmest (5.5 °C year average), 128th sunniest (about 1,735 hours of sunshine a year) and 64th wettest (658 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Obninsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (68 km away), Kaluga is about as warm in July and about as warm in February, with 27 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.8 °C), averages 26.2 °C more than its coolest, February (-7.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 703 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kaluga, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kaluga has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 703 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.2 °C.
