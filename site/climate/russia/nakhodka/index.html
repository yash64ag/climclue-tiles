Nakhodka at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Nakhodka is 43rd warmest (6.1 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 2,105 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (764 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Artëm, the nearest covered town with different numbers (82 km away), Nakhodka is 0.2 °C cooler in August and 2.0 °C warmer in January, with 42 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (21.1 °C), averages 32.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-11.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Nakhodka, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Nakhodka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.4 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nakhodka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.844°N, 132.918°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-411-3129), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
