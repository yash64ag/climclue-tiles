Ufa at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Ufa is 106th warmest (4.1 °C year average), 73rd sunniest (about 1,944 hours of sunshine a year) and 56th wettest (684 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Sterlitamak, the nearest covered town with different numbers (123 km away), Ufa is 0.4 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 118 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.0 °C), averages 32.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 903 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.7 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 116% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Ufa has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 903 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 32.0 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ufa itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (54.743°N, 55.968°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-292-2359), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
