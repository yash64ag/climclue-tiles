Kirov at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Kirov is 123rd warmest (2.8 °C year average), 137th sunniest (about 1,718 hours of sunshine a year) and 38th wettest (711 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Glazov, the nearest covered town with different numbers (182 km away), Kirov is 0.2 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C warmer in January, with 40 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.3 °C), averages 30.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 853 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.0 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kirov has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 853 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 30.4 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kirov itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (58.598°N, 49.658°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-254-2296), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
