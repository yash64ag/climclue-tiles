Malgobek weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Malgobek, Russia (Ingushetiya Republic), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Malgobek is warmest in July, with 28.6 °C (83 °F) days and 16.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.8 °C (35 °F) days and -5.7 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (92 mm); January is driest (28 mm).

Malgobek at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Malgobek is 104th warmest (10.3 °C year average), 536th sunniest (about 1,845 hours of sunshine a year) and 554th wettest (593 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Staryy Malgobek, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Malgobek is 0.9 °C warmer in July and about as warm in January, with 41 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.7 °C), averages 24.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 209 km from the sea.

Why Malgobek has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 209 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Malgobek itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.511°N, 44.591°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-404-2245), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
