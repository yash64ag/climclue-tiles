Abakan at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Abakan is the 5th driest (350 mm a year). Compared with Mezhdurechensk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (221 km away), Abakan is 2.7 °C warmer in July and 1.7 °C cooler in January, with 812 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.4 °C), averages 38.8 °C more than its coolest, January (-17.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,707 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 9 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 113% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Abakan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. About 1,707 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 38.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
