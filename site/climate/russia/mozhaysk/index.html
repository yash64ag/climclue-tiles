Mozhaysk at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Mozhaysk is 456th warmest (5.2 °C year average), 814th sunniest (about 1,704 hours of sunshine a year) and 231st wettest (698 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tuchkovo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Mozhaysk is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 18 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.4 °C), averages 25.9 °C more than its coolest, February (-7.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 601 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Mozhaysk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mozhaysk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (55.502°N, 36.027°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-284-2160), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 31,557, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
