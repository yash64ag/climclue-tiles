Armavir at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Armavir is 67th warmest (11.2 °C year average), 62nd sunniest (about 2,214 hours of sunshine a year) and 401st wettest (648 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Novokubansk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Armavir is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (23.5 °C), averages 24.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 194 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 0.9 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Armavir has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 194 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Armavir itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.998°N, 41.115°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-390-2211), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
