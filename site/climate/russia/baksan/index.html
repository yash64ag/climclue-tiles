Baksan at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Baksan is 145th warmest (9.4 °C year average), 471st sunniest (about 1,889 hours of sunshine a year) and 315th wettest (672 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dugulubgey, the nearest covered town with different numbers (2 km away), Baksan is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 35 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.8 °C), averages 24.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 210 km from the sea.

Why Baksan has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 210 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Baksan itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.684°N, 43.535°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-403-2235), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
