Nadym weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nadym, Russia (Yamalo-Nenets), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nadym is warmest in July, with 19.5 °C (67 °F) days and 11.8 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -18.8 °C (-2 °F) days and -27.2 °C nights. Most rain falls around August (95 mm); February is driest (27 mm).

Nadym at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Nadym is 964th warmest (-5.4 °C year average), 948th sunniest (about 1,553 hours of sunshine a year) and 391st wettest (651 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Novyy Urengoy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (197 km away), Nadym is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 1.5 °C warmer in January, with 35 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (15.7 °C), averages 38.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-23.1 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, May, October, November and December. The area lies about 90 km from the sea.

Why Nadym has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 90 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 38.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nadym itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (65.533°N, 72.517°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 4.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-184-2525), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
