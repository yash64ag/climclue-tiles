Omsk at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Omsk is 130th warmest (2.1 °C year average), 21st sunniest (about 2,171 hours of sunshine a year) and 159th wettest (386 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Tobolsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (474 km away), Omsk is 1.2 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 96 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.9 °C), averages 36.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-16.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,269 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 93% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Omsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,269 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 36.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
