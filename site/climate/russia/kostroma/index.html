Kostroma at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Kostroma is 102nd warmest (4.3 °C year average), 143rd sunniest (about 1,707 hours of sunshine a year) and 81st wettest (621 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yaroslavl, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Kostroma is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.7 °C), averages 28.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 667 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kostroma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 667 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.1 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kostroma itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (57.766°N, 40.928°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-262-2209), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
