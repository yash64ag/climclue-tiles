Kostroma at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Kostroma is 102nd warmest (4.3 °C year average), 143rd sunniest (about 1,707 hours of sunshine a year) and 79th wettest (626 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yaroslavl, the nearest covered town with different numbers (64 km away), Kostroma is about as warm in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.6 °C), averages 28.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-9.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 667 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 6 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.6 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 100% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kostroma has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 667 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 28.0 °C.
