Lesozavodsk at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Lesozavodsk is 599th warmest (4.0 °C year average), 34th sunniest (about 2,304 hours of sunshine a year) and 396th wettest (649 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Dalnerechensk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (57 km away), Lesozavodsk is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 10 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.8 °C), averages 40.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-18.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 238 km from the sea.

Why Lesozavodsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 238 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 40.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Lesozavodsk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (45.472°N, 133.398°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.8 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-385-3133), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
