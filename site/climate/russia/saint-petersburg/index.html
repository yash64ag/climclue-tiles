Saint Petersburg at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Saint Petersburg is 301st warmest (5.8 °C year average), 929th sunniest (about 1,621 hours of sunshine a year) and 257th wettest (690 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kudrovo, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Saint Petersburg is 0.6 °C cooler in July and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 2 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.6 °C), averages 24.6 °C more than its coolest, February (-5.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Saint Petersburg has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Saint Petersburg climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Saint Petersburg?
July, with an average of 18.6 °C (66 °F), day highs around 22.6 °C (73 °F) and night lows around 14.7 °C (58 °F).
What is the coldest month in Saint Petersburg?
February, with an average of -5.9 °C (21 °F), day highs around -3.2 °C (26 °F) and night lows around -8.7 °C (16 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Saint Petersburg?
August, with 84 mm (3.3 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 690 mm (27.2 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Saint Petersburg?
April, with 32 mm (1.3 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Saint Petersburg?
June, with about 275 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Saint Petersburg?
Yes. About 179 cm (70.5 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in December; snow lies on the ground (average depth 1 cm or more) in 6 months.

How warm is the sea in Saint Petersburg?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Saint Petersburg itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (59.939°N, 30.314°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 5.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-240-2103), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
