Kursk at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Kursk is 34th warmest (6.5 °C year average), 99th sunniest (about 1,839 hours of sunshine a year) and 56th wettest (680 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Staryy Oskol, the nearest covered town with different numbers (124 km away), Kursk is 1.0 °C cooler in July and about as warm in February, with 114 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.6 °C), averages 25.8 °C more than its coolest, February (-6.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 543 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 105% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kursk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 543 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
