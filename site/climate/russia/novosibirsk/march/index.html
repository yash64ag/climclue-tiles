Novosibirsk weather in March

Average March weather in Novosibirsk, Russia: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In March, Novosibirsk averages -1.5 °C (29 °F) days and -12.8 °C (9 °F) nights, with 18 mm (0.7 in) of rain and snow over the month and about 176 hours of sunshine.

How March compares in Novosibirsk

March is the 5th coolest of the 12, the 2nd driest of the 12 and the 6th least sunny of the 12 in Novosibirsk. Compared with February: 7.5 °C warmer, about the same precipitation and 64 hours more sunshine. April is 9.4 °C warmer than March on average.

Novosibirsk in March: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Novosibirsk in March?
In March, Novosibirsk averages a day high of -1.5 °C (29 °F) and a night low of -12.8 °C (9 °F), with 18 mm (0.7 in) of precipitation and about 176 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is March a good time to visit Novosibirsk?
March is not one of Novosibirsk’s mild months; those are June, July and August, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Novosibirsk in March?
18 mm (0.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making March the 2nd driest of the 12. The wettest month is July (66 mm), the driest February (18 mm).
Does it snow in Novosibirsk in March?
Yes, on average: ERA5-Land gives about 18 cm (7.0 in) of fresh snow in March (1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow), and snow lies on the ground with an average depth of 28 cm.
How many hours of sunshine does Novosibirsk get in March?
About 176 hours of bright sunshine, the 6th least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
What should I pack for Novosibirsk in March?
Going by the averages: a warm coat, a hat and gloves, an extra layer for cooler evenings and waterproof boots. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
