Novosibirsk at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Novosibirsk is 146th warmest (1.5 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 2,107 hours of sunshine a year) and 136th wettest (492 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Barnaul, the nearest covered town with different numbers (192 km away), Novosibirsk is 1.1 °C cooler in July and 1.1 °C cooler in January, with 20 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.1 °C), averages 35.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-16.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,380 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 107% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Novosibirsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,380 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 35.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
