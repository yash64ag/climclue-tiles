Samara at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Samara is 54th warmest (5.5 °C year average), 50th sunniest (about 2,055 hours of sunshine a year) and 132nd wettest (511 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Novokuybyshevsk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Samara is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 42 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.4 °C), averages 31.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-10.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 694 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 7 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 91% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Samara has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 694 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
