Kovrov weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Kovrov, Russia (Vladimir Oblast), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Kovrov is warmest in July, with 24.0 °C (75 °F) days and 14.0 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -5.2 °C (23 °F) days and -11.9 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 31 mm in March to 81 mm in June.

Kovrov at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Kovrov is 496th warmest (4.9 °C year average), 670th sunniest (about 1,766 hours of sunshine a year) and 477th wettest (624 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Vyazniki, the nearest covered town with different numbers (53 km away), Kovrov is 0.3 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 23 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.0 °C), averages 27.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-8.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 768 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Kovrov has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 768 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 27.6 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kovrov itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (56.358°N, 41.319°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-276-2213), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
