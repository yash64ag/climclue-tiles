Yakutsk at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Yakutsk is the 2nd coolest (-8.6 °C year average) and the 3rd driest (277 mm a year). Compared with Blagoveshchensk, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1,314 km away), Yakutsk is 2.3 °C cooler in July and 16.1 °C cooler in January, with 259 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 58.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-38.9 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 744 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 2 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 118% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Yakutsk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 744 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 58.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
