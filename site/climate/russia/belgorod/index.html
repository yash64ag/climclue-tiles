Belgorod weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Belgorod, Russia (Belgorod Oblast), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Belgorod at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Belgorod is 203rd warmest (7.5 °C year average), 629th sunniest (about 1,789 hours of sunshine a year) and 567th wettest (582 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Stroitel, the nearest covered town with different numbers (22 km away), Belgorod is 0.6 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 56 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.7 °C), averages 26.0 °C more than its coolest, January (-5.3 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 418 km from the sea.

Why Belgorod has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 418 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.0 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Belgorod itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (50.603°N, 36.581°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-333-2165), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
