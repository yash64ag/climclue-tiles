Nevinnomyssk weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Nevinnomyssk, Russia (Stavropol Kray), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Nevinnomyssk is warmest in July, with 28.6 °C (83 °F) days and 16.7 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 1.4 °C (35 °F) days and -6.6 °C nights. Most rain falls around June (91 mm); January is driest (24 mm).

Nevinnomyssk at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Nevinnomyssk is 118th warmest (10.0 °C year average), 203rd sunniest (about 2,103 hours of sunshine a year) and 676th wettest (546 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kochubeyevskoye, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Nevinnomyssk is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 58 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (22.7 °C), averages 25.3 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 209 km from the sea.

Why Nevinnomyssk has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 209 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 25.3 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nevinnomyssk itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (44.633°N, 41.944°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-393-2219), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
