Barnaul at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Barnaul is 704th warmest (2.7 °C year average), 92nd sunniest (about 2,180 hours of sunshine a year) and 813th wettest (485 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Novosilikatnyy, the nearest covered town with different numbers (9 km away), Barnaul is about as warm in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 14 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.1 °C), averages 35.6 °C more than its coolest, January (-15.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, October, November and December. The area lies about 1,576 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Barnaul has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 1,576 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 35.6 °C.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Barnaul itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (53.362°N, 83.728°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-306-2637), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 632,372, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
