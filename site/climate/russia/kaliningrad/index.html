Kaliningrad at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Kaliningrad is 27th warmest (8.0 °C year average), 156th sunniest (about 1,686 hours of sunshine a year) and 11th wettest (829 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Pskov, the nearest covered town with different numbers (594 km away), Kaliningrad is 0.3 °C cooler in July and 3.5 °C warmer in January, with 102 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.1 °C), averages 19.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.4 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 34 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 3 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.4 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Kaliningrad has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 34 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 19.5 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
