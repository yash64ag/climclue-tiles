Khimki at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Khimki is 79th warmest (5.1 °C year average), 154th sunniest (about 1,695 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (715 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zelenograd, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Khimki is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in February, with 11 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.8 °C), averages 26.5 °C more than its coolest, February (-7.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 618 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 14 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 1.1 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Khimki has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 618 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 26.5 °C.
