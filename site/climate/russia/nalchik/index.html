Nalchik at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Nalchik is 143rd warmest (9.4 °C year average), 435th sunniest (about 1,916 hours of sunshine a year) and 17th wettest (1,060 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Chegem, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Nalchik is about as warm in July and about as warm in January, with 178 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (21.5 °C), averages 24.2 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 205 km from the sea.

Why Nalchik has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 205 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.2 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.2 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Nalchik itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (43.498°N, 43.619°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-405-2236), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
