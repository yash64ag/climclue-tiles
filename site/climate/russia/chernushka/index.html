Chernushka weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Chernushka, Russia (Perm Krai), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Chernushka is warmest in July, with 25.1 °C (77 °F) days and 13.6 °C nights, and coolest in January, with -8.5 °C (17 °F) days and -16.9 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 26 mm in March to 71 mm in July.

Chernushka at a glance

Among the 981 towns ClimaClue covers in Russia, Chernushka is 663rd warmest (3.2 °C year average), 576th sunniest (about 1,821 hours of sunshine a year) and 563rd wettest (585 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Yanaul, the nearest covered town with different numbers (75 km away), Chernushka is 0.4 °C cooler in July and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 62 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 32.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-12.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, April, November and December. The area lies about 1,099 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why Chernushka has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Chernushka itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (56.507°N, 56.077°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 6.1 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-274-2360), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 35,942, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
