Vladivostok at a glance

Among the 171 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Russia, Vladivostok is 46th warmest (5.8 °C year average), 22nd sunniest (about 2,170 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (781 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Artëm, the nearest covered town with different numbers (38 km away), Vladivostok is 0.4 °C cooler in August and 2.5 °C warmer in January, with 66 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (20.9 °C), averages 31.5 °C more than its coolest, January (-10.7 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: a NOAA weather station 5 km from Vladivostok measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from this area’s (temperature by up to 2.2 °C (3.9 °F) in one month). One station can differ from a 10 km area’s average (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Why Vladivostok has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm. A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 31.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
