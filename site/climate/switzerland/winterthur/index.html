Winterthur at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Switzerland, Winterthur is the 2nd coolest (10.0 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,540 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (1,004 mm a year). Compared with Zürich, the nearest covered town with different numbers (20 km away), Winterthur is 0.2 °C warmer in July and 0.5 °C cooler in January, with 223 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.4 °C), averages 18.6 °C more than its coolest, January (0.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 356 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 18 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.5 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 89% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Winterthur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 356 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
