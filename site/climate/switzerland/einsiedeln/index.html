Einsiedeln at a glance

Among the 73 towns ClimaClue covers in Switzerland, Einsiedeln is the 2nd coolest (7.3 °C year average) and the wettest (2,036 mm a year). Compared with Rapperswil, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Einsiedeln is 3.3 °C cooler in July and 2.5 °C cooler in January, with 547 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (16.4 °C), averages 18.1 °C more than its coolest, January (-1.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 311 km from the sea.

Why Einsiedeln has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 311 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 18.1 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 5.5 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Einsiedeln itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (47.128°N, 8.747°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.6 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-368-1887), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
