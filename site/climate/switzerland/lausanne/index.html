Lausanne at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Switzerland, Lausanne is the 3rd warmest (10.4 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,704 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,508 mm a year). Compared with Geneva, the nearest covered town with different numbers (51 km away), Lausanne is 1.6 °C cooler in July and 0.5 °C warmer in January, with 361 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (19.1 °C), averages 16.6 °C more than its coolest, January (2.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January and February. The area lies about 294 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Lausanne, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Lausanne has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 294 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.6 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.6 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
