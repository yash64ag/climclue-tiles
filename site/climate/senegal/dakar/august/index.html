Dakar weather in August

Average August weather in Dakar, Senegal: modelled 1981–2010 averages for the city itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre) (climate normals, not a forecast).

In August, Dakar averages 29.8 °C (86 °F) days and 24.3 °C (76 °F) nights, with 170 mm (6.7 in) of rain over the month and about 221 hours of sunshine.

How August compares in Dakar

August is the 3rd warmest of the 12, the wettest month and the 2nd least sunny of the 12 in Dakar. Compared with July: 0.6 °C warmer, 113 mm more precipitation and 9 hours less sunshine. September is 0.3 °C warmer than August on average.

Dakar in August: questions and answers

What is the weather like in Dakar in August?
In August, Dakar averages a day high of 29.8 °C (86 °F) and a night low of 24.3 °C (76 °F), with 170 mm (6.7 in) of precipitation and about 221 hours of sunshine. These are 1981–2010 averages for the ~10 km area, not a forecast.
Is August a good time to visit Dakar?
August is not one of Dakar’s mild months; those are January, February, March, April, May, June and December, counting a month as mild with an average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation. Climate only: crowds and prices are not considered.
How much does it rain in Dakar in August?
170 mm (6.7 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water) on average, making August the wettest month of the year. The wettest month is August (170 mm), the driest April (0 mm).
Does it snow in Dakar in August?
Little or none on average: ERA5-Land gives under 0.5 cm of fresh snow for August in this area (1981–2010).
How many hours of sunshine does Dakar get in August?
About 221 hours of bright sunshine, the 2nd least sunny of the 12 (estimate for about 1961–1990).
How warm is the sea in Dakar in August?
About 28.1 °C (83 °F) on average in the nearby open sea, warm for swimming; its warmest month is September (28.4 °C). 1981–2010 averages (ERA5).
What should I pack for Dakar in August?
Going by the averages: light, breathable clothes for humid air and a rain jacket or umbrella. Check a forecast before you travel; these are long-term averages.
