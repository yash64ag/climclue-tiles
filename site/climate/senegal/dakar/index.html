Dakar at a glance

Among the 67 towns ClimaClue covers in Senegal, Dakar is the 3rd coolest (24.2 °C year average). Compared with Mermoz Boabab, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Dakar is 0.1 °C warmer in October and 0.2 °C warmer in February, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, October (27.6 °C), averages 6.7 °C more than its coolest, February (20.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 16.1 °C (61 °F), in February. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Dakar, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Dakar has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Dakar itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.694°N, 17.444°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-693-1625), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 2,646,503, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
