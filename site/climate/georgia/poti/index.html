Poti at a glance

Among the 17 towns ClimaClue covers in Georgia, Poti is 5th warmest (15.0 °C year average), 10th sunniest (about 2,071 hours of sunshine a year) and 4th wettest (2,094 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kobuleti, the nearest covered town with different numbers (36 km away), Poti is about as warm in August and 0.4 °C cooler in January, with 779 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.0 °C), averages 17.7 °C more than its coolest, January (6.3 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 2.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 8 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Poti, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Poti has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 17.7 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Poti itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.143°N, 41.674°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-418-2216), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
