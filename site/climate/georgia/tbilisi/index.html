Tbilisi weather by month and climate

Tbilisi at a glance

Among the 17 towns ClimaClue covers in Georgia, Tbilisi is 12th warmest (12.9 °C year average), 11th sunniest (about 2,047 hours of sunshine a year) and 14th wettest (567 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Rustavi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (19 km away), Tbilisi is 1.8 °C cooler in July and 0.8 °C cooler in January, with 102 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (25.1 °C), averages 24.6 °C more than its coolest, January (0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February and December. The area lies about 266 km from the sea.

Station check: a NOAA weather station 10 km from Tbilisi measured 1981–2010 averages that differ from these (temperature by up to 2.6 °C (4.7 °F) in one month). One station can differ from modelled values (hills, coasts, city heat), so treat these numbers with some care.

Best time to visit Tbilisi

For mild weather, the best months to visit Tbilisi are May, June, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, August and September.

Why Tbilisi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 266 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 24.6 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 4.3 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Tbilisi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (41.691°N, 44.834°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.3 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-423-2248), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
