Ts’khinvali weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Ts’khinvali, Georgia (Shida Kartli), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Ts’khinvali is warmest in July, with 26.6 °C (80 °F) days and 14.4 °C nights, and coolest in January, with 2.2 °C (36 °F) days and -6.5 °C nights. Rain is spread through the year, from 48 mm in September to 87 mm in May.

Ts’khinvali at a glance

Among the 17 towns ClimaClue covers in Georgia, Ts’khinvali is the coolest (9.3 °C year average). Compared with Gori, the nearest covered town with different numbers (30 km away), Ts’khinvali is 1.4 °C cooler in July and 1.3 °C cooler in January, with 232 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (20.5 °C), averages 22.7 °C more than its coolest, January (-2.2 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 193 km from the sea.

Why Ts’khinvali has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. About 193 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 22.7 °C. Within the 10 km area around it, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.8 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Ts’khinvali itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (42.228°N, 43.969°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 8.2 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-417-2239), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
