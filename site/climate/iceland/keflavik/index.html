Keflavík at a glance

Among the 6 towns ClimaClue covers in Iceland, Keflavík is the warmest (5.0 °C year average), the 3rd least sunny (about 1,064 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (1,315 mm a year). Compared with Reykjanesbær, the nearest covered town with different numbers (1 km away), Keflavík is about as warm in July and about as warm in February, with 26 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (10.9 °C), averages 10.4 °C more than its coolest, February (0.5 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March and December. The area lies about 5 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 5 km away, agrees with these values: monthly temperatures within 1.3 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 101% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern. Temperatures on this page are then adjusted toward nearby NOAA stations, so they follow the measurements more closely.

Best time to visit Keflavík

No month in Keflavík is mild by the rule below; the closest in temperature are June (9.1 °C, 69 mm), July (10.9 °C, 84 mm) and August (10.7 °C, 109 mm).

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): no month.
  • Warmest (average temperature): July (10.9 °C), August (10.7 °C) and June (9.1 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): June (69 mm), May (79 mm) and July (84 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): May (174 h), July (163 h) and June (152 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why Keflavík has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

Keflavík climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in Keflavík?
July, with an average of 10.9 °C (52 °F), day highs around 13.2 °C (56 °F) and night lows around 8.6 °C (47 °F).
What is the coldest month in Keflavík?
February, with an average of 0.5 °C (33 °F), day highs around 2.8 °C (37 °F) and night lows around -1.8 °C (29 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in Keflavík?
December, with 141 mm (5.5 in) of precipitation (rain and snow as water), out of 1,315 mm (51.8 in) in a typical year.
What is the driest month in Keflavík?
June, with 69 mm (2.7 in) of precipitation.
What is the sunniest month in Keflavík?
May, with about 174 hours of bright sunshine (estimate for about 1961–1990).
Does it snow in Keflavík?
Yes. About 77 cm (30.2 in) of fresh snow falls in a typical year (ERA5-Land snowfall at 1 mm of water ≈ 1 cm of snow, adjusted to nearby weather stations), most in January; it usually melts within days.
When is the best time to visit Keflavík?

How warm is the sea in Keflavík?
The nearby open sea averages 10.9 °C (52 °F) in August, its warmest month, and 4.6 °C (40 °F) in March. It stays below 20 °C (68 °F) all year. 1981–2010 averages (ERA5); beaches and shallow bays are often a little warmer in summer.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Keflavík itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (64.005°N, 22.562°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 4.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-199-1574), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 15,930, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
