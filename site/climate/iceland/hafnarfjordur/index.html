Hafnarfjörður at a glance

Among the 6 towns ClimaClue covers in Iceland, Hafnarfjörður is the 3rd warmest (4.9 °C year average), the 2nd sunniest (about 1,146 hours of sunshine a year) and the wettest (1,377 mm a year). Compared with Kópavogur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (5 km away), Hafnarfjörður is about as warm in July and 0.2 °C warmer in January, with 15 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (11.8 °C), averages 12.4 °C more than its coolest, January (-0.6 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in January, February, March, November and December. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Why Hafnarfjörður has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted. Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 12.4 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Hafnarfjörður itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (64.067°N, 21.938°W). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 4.9 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-199-1580), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
