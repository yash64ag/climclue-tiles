Mabote at a glance

Among the 11 towns ClimaClue covers in Lesotho, Mabote is the warmest (14.7 °C year average). Compared with Maseru, the nearest covered town with different numbers (6 km away), Mabote is 0.1 °C warmer in January and 0.1 °C warmer in July, with 11 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, January (20.9 °C), averages 14.1 °C more than its coolest, July (6.8 °C). Average night lows are below 0 °C (32 °F) in June and July. The area lies about 332 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mabote, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Mabote

For mild weather, the best months to visit Mabote are February, March, October, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): February, March, October, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): January (20.9 °C), February (20.1 °C) and December (20.0 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (7 mm), June (14 mm) and August (21 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): December (289 h), August (288 h) and January (271 h).

Why Mabote has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 332 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mabote itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (29.279°S, 27.524°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.7 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-1132-2075), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 38,047, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
