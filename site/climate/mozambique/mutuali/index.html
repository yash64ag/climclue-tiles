Mutuáli at a glance

Among the 65 towns ClimaClue covers in Mozambique, Mutuáli is 36th warmest (23.5 °C year average), 54th sunniest (about 2,709 hours of sunshine a year) and 30th wettest (1,084 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Malema, the nearest covered town with different numbers (45 km away), Mutuáli is 0.8 °C warmer in November and 0.5 °C warmer in July, with 53 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, November (26.6 °C), averages 6.8 °C more than its coolest, July (19.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.9 °C (55 °F), in June. The area lies about 302 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Mutuáli, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Mutuáli has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 302 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 6.8 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Mutuáli itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (14.871°S, 37.004°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-988-2170), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
