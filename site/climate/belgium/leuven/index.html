Leuven at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Belgium, Leuven is the sunniest (about 1,592 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd driest (838 mm a year). Compared with Schaerbeek, the nearest covered town with different numbers (23 km away), Leuven is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 25 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.3 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, January (3.1 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 113 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Leuven, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Leuven has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 113 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
