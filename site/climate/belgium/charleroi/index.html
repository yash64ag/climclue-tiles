Charleroi at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Belgium, Charleroi is the 2nd coolest (10.0 °C year average) and the 2nd wettest (903 mm a year). Compared with Namur, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Charleroi is 0.1 °C cooler in July and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 39 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.8 °C), averages 15.1 °C more than its coolest, January (2.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.1 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 131 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Charleroi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Charleroi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 131 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.1 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
