Namur at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Belgium, Namur is the coolest (9.9 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 1,553 hours of sunshine a year) and the 3rd wettest (863 mm a year). Compared with Charleroi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (31 km away), Namur is 0.1 °C warmer in July and 0.1 °C cooler in January, with 39 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (17.9 °C), averages 15.3 °C more than its coolest, January (2.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 148 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Namur, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Namur has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 148 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 15.3 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
