Kalmthout at a glance

Among the 224 towns ClimaClue covers in Belgium, Kalmthout is 139th warmest (10.5 °C year average), 156th sunniest (about 1,556 hours of sunshine a year) and 38th wettest (887 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Kapellen, the nearest covered town with different numbers (8 km away), Kalmthout is 0.1 °C cooler in July and about as warm in January, with 33 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.1 °C), averages 14.8 °C more than its coolest, January (3.4 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 0.5 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 70 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Kalmthout, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Kalmthout has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 70 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.8 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Kalmthout itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (51.384°N, 4.476°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 7.0 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-326-1844), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
