Gent at a glance

Among the 10 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Belgium, Gent is the 3rd warmest (10.6 °C year average), the 3rd least sunny (about 1,557 hours of sunshine a year) and the driest (834 mm a year). Compared with Brugge, the nearest covered town with different numbers (39 km away), Gent is 0.3 °C warmer in July and 0.7 °C cooler in January, with 6 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, July (18.1 °C), averages 14.6 °C more than its coolest, January (3.5 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 1.0 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 49 km from the sea.

Station check: the nearest NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages, 22 km away, agrees with this area’s values: monthly temperatures within 0.8 °C of the station’s, yearly precipitation 102% of the station’s, and a similar seasonal rain pattern.

Why Gent has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Mild for its latitude; moving air from the ocean keeps winters from getting very cold and summers from getting very hot. Passing storm systems and showers bring rain in every season, with no reliable dry month. About 49 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 14.6 °C.

Rain or snow is spread through much of the year.
