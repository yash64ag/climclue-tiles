N'Djamena at a glance

Among the 46 towns ClimaClue covers in Chad, N'Djamena is the 4th warmest (28.8 °C year average). Compared with Mandelia, the nearest covered town with different numbers (47 km away), N'Djamena is 0.1 °C warmer in April and 0.3 °C cooler in January, with 83 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, April (33.9 °C), averages 10.1 °C more than its coolest, January (23.8 °C). Even the coolest nights average 15.1 °C (59 °F), in January. The area lies about 1,094 km from the sea (deep inland).

Why N'Djamena has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Dry air allows wide temperature swings; the seasons are often sharp. It sits on the edge of a desert: some rain-bearing systems reach it in one season, but not enough to make it humid. About 1,094 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.1 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for N'Djamena itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (12.107°N, 15.044°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-718-1950), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
