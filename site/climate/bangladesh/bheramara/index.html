Bherāmāra at a glance

Among the 145 towns ClimaClue covers in Bangladesh, Bherāmāra is the 3rd driest (1,571 mm a year). Compared with Ishwardi, the nearest covered town with different numbers (14 km away), Bherāmāra is about as warm in May and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 12 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.2 °C), averages 11.6 °C more than its coolest, January (17.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.5 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 256 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bherāmāra, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Bherāmāra

For mild weather, the best months to visit Bherāmāra are January, February, November and December.

Why Bherāmāra has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Warm to hot summers; dry, clear winters can bring cool nights. Monsoon winds bring most of the rain in summer; winters are dry when winds blow from the land. About 256 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 11.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bherāmāra itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.025°N, 88.992°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-599-2689), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 38,159, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
