Bagerhat at a glance

Among the 54 towns of 100,000+ people covered in Bangladesh, Bagerhat is the 5th warmest (25.8 °C year average). Compared with Khulna, the nearest covered town with different numbers (29 km away), Bagerhat is 0.1 °C cooler in May and about as warm in January, with 164 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.3 °C), averages 10.6 °C more than its coolest, January (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 13.2 °C (56 °F), in January. The area lies about 91 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bagerhat, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bagerhat has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 91 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.6 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, day highs and night lows, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bagerhat itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.657°N, 89.791°E). Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-613-2697), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
