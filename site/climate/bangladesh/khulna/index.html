Khulna at a glance

Among the 145 towns ClimaClue covers in Bangladesh, Khulna is 12th warmest (25.9 °C year average), 136th sunniest (about 2,090 hours of sunshine a year) and 121st wettest (1,889 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Phultala, the nearest covered town with different numbers (12 km away), Khulna is 0.1 °C cooler in May and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, May (29.4 °C), averages 10.7 °C more than its coolest, January (18.6 °C). Even the coolest nights average 11.6 °C (53 °F), in January. The area lies about 119 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Khulna, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Khulna

For mild weather, the best months to visit Khulna are January, February, November and December.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): January, February, November and December.
  • Warmest (average temperature): May (29.4 °C), June (29.1 °C) and April (29.0 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): December (10 mm), January (15 mm) and February (31 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): January (239 h), December (235 h) and March (228 h).

Why Khulna has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 119 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Khulna itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (22.810°N, 89.564°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-611-2695), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 1,500,689, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al.
