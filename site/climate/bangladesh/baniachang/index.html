Baniachang weather by month and climate

Baniachang at a glance

Among the 145 towns ClimaClue covers in Bangladesh, Baniachang is 121st warmest (25.0 °C year average), 33rd sunniest (about 2,175 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (3,340 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Habiganj, the nearest covered town with different numbers (16 km away), Baniachang is 0.1 °C warmer in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 691 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (28.6 °C), averages 10.5 °C more than its coolest, January (18.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 10.8 °C (51 °F), in January. The area lies about 211 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Baniachang, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Baniachang has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

The tropical sun keeps it warm all year; the hottest weeks often come just before the rains. Seasonal monsoon winds bring a very wet season, and a short dry season follows when they reverse. About 211 km from the sea: inland, so ocean moderation is weaker and seasons are usually sharper. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 10.5 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Baniachang itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (24.519°N, 91.358°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 10 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-594-2713), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
