Karusi at a glance

Among the 14 towns ClimaClue covers in Burundi, Karusi is 10th warmest (19.7 °C year average), 8th sunniest (about 2,268 hours of sunshine a year) and 8th wettest (1,202 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Zanandore, the nearest covered town with different numbers (18 km away), Karusi is 0.5 °C cooler in September and 0.4 °C cooler in November, with 193 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, September (21.4 °C), averages 2.3 °C more than its coolest, November (19.1 °C). Even the coolest nights average 12.3 °C (54 °F), in July. The area lies about 1,017 km from the sea (deep inland).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Karusi, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit Karusi

For mild weather, the best months to visit Karusi are May, June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): May, June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): September (21.4 °C), August (20.9 °C) and October (20.3 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (4 mm), June (7 mm) and August (14 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): July (263 h), August (245 h) and June (242 h).

Why Karusi has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. About 1,017 km from the sea: deep inland, where land heats and cools quickly, which usually makes seasons extreme. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 2.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Karusi itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (3.101°S, 30.163°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-871-2101), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
