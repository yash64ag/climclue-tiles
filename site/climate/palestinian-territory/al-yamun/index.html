Al Yāmūn at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in the Palestinian Territory, Al Yāmūn is the least sunny (about 3,202 hours of sunshine a year). Compared with Janīn, the nearest covered town with different numbers (7 km away), Al Yāmūn is 0.1 °C cooler in August and 0.1 °C warmer in January, with 64 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.3 °C), averages 16.3 °C more than its coolest, January (11.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 6.8 °C (44 °F), in January. The area lies about 39 km from the sea.

Why Al Yāmūn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 39 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Al Yāmūn itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (32.486°N, 35.230°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.4 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-515-2152), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
