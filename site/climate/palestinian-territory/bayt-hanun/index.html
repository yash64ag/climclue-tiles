Bayt Ḩānūn weather by month and climate

Average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine and more for every month in Bayt Ḩānūn, Palestinian Territory (Gaza Strip), from modelled 1981–2010 averages for the town itself (about 3 × 3 km around its centre).

Bayt Ḩānūn at a glance

Among the 45 towns ClimaClue covers in the Palestinian Territory, Bayt Ḩānūn is 11th warmest (20.5 °C year average), 14th sunniest (about 3,362 hours of sunshine a year) and 32nd wettest (434 mm a year), near the middle on each. Compared with Bayt Lāhyā, the nearest covered town with different numbers (4 km away), Bayt Ḩānūn is about as warm in August and 0.2 °C cooler in January, with 9 mm less precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (27.2 °C), averages 13.7 °C more than its coolest, January (13.5 °C). Even the coolest nights average 8.4 °C (47 °F), in February. The area lies about 15 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Bayt Ḩānūn, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Bayt Ḩānūn has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 13.7 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for Bayt Ḩānūn itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (31.535°N, 34.536°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 9.5 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-524-2145), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts.
