Hebron at a glance

Among the 6 towns of 100,000+ people covered in the Palestinian Territory, Hebron is the coolest (17.2 °C year average), the 2nd least sunny (about 3,349 hours of sunshine a year) and the 2nd wettest (606 mm a year). Compared with Jabālyā, the nearest covered town with different numbers (58 km away), Hebron is 2.5 °C cooler in August and 5.1 °C cooler in January, with 172 mm more precipitation a year. Its warmest month, August (24.8 °C), averages 16.2 °C more than its coolest, January (8.6 °C). No month has an average night low below 0 °C (32 °F); the coldest, February, averages 4.6 °C (single nights can be colder). The area lies about 58 km from the sea.

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of Hebron, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Why Hebron has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this area; not a verified local cause.

Summers are warm under persistent high pressure; nearby sea often softens winter cold. In summer the subtropical high pressure moves over and blocks storms, so summers are dry; in winter it retreats and storms from the ocean bring rain. About 58 km from the sea: close enough for ocean air to moderate the seasons on many days. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 16.2 °C. Within this area, monthly temperatures differ by up to 3.4 °C — typical where height changes quickly, as in hills or mountains.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.
