São Tomé at a glance

Its warmest month, March (27.4 °C), averages 3.3 °C more than its coolest, July (24.0 °C). Even the coolest nights average 21.2 °C (70 °F), in July. The area lies about 11 km from the sea (coastal).

Station check: no NOAA GHCN-Daily station with 1981–2010 averages lies within 25 km of São Tomé, so these modelled values have not been compared with a nearby station.

Best time to visit São Tomé

For mild weather, the best months to visit São Tomé are June, July, August and September.

  • Mild (average temperature 15–25 °C / 59–77 °F and under 100 mm / 3.9 in of precipitation): June, July, August and September.
  • Warmest (average temperature): March (27.4 °C), April (27.2 °C) and February (27.0 °C).
  • Driest (least precipitation): July (12 mm), August (17 mm) and June (27 mm).
  • Sunniest (hours of sunshine, about 1961–1990): April (126 h), March (125 h) and February (122 h).

Climate only: these rules use 1981–2010 monthly averages and do not consider crowds, prices, events or holidays; any one year can differ.

Why São Tomé has this climate

Typical mechanisms for this climate type, plus facts measured for this place; not a verified local cause.

It stays warm all year; the dry season brings clearer skies and wider day–night swings. The tropical rain belt follows the sun: it brings a wet season when overhead and a long dry season when it moves away. On the coast: the sea warms and cools slowly, which usually softens seasonal swings. Here the coolest and warmest months differ by 3.3 °C.

Strongly seasonal: the wettest month gets more than five times the driest.

São Tomé climate: questions and answers

What is the warmest month in São Tomé?
March, with an average of 27.4 °C (81 °F), day highs around 31.1 °C (88 °F) and night lows around 23.7 °C (75 °F).
What is the coldest month in São Tomé?
July, with an average of 24.0 °C (75 °F), day highs around 26.8 °C (80 °F) and night lows around 21.2 °C (70 °F).
Which month gets the most rain in São Tomé?

What is the sunniest month in São Tomé?

About these numbers

Temperature, precipitation and wind: modelled monthly averages (climate normals) for São Tomé itself, the average of the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around its GeoNames location (0.338°N, 6.730°E). Day highs and night lows add the day–night range of the surrounding CRU cell (about 18 km), or on a few small islands the nearest weather station’s; where there is neither, they are not shown. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and describe the 0.1° area around it, about 11 × 11 km (ClimaClue area sq-836-1867), which is also what the globe shows when you click there; humidity is calculated from that dew point and the town’s own night lows and day highs. They are model estimates, not measurements at a weather station, and not forecasts. Sources: CHELSA V2.1, 1981–2010 (temperature, precipitation, wind); ERA5-Land, 1981–2010 (dew point and snow; snowfall matched to the area’s CHELSA climate; contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information); relative humidity calculated from the dew point and the night lows and day highs; CRU CL 2.0, about 1961–1990 (sunshine and the day–night range; a different period); GeoNames (name and population 53,300, CC BY 4.0); climate type by the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007). How ClimaClue makes these numbers.
