Climate map of the world

ClimaClue is a free interactive climate map on a 3D globe. Click any place on land, or search for a town, to see its climate type and its average temperature, rain and snow, sunshine, wind, humidity and dew point for every month of the year.

Open the climate map ›

How the climate map works

The globe is coloured by climate. On the first tab every area of land shows its Köppen–Geiger climate type, from tropical rainforest to ice cap, so the big patterns stand out at once: the deserts along the tropics, the Mediterranean belts on western coasts, the wide continental zones of the north. Switch to Temperature, Rainfall, Sunshine, Wind, Humidity or Snow and the colours change to that measure, with a key in the corner. For every measure except the climate type you can pick a single month, so you can watch the monsoon arrive over South Asia or the snow line move south across Canada.

Click any spot and the globe flies in to the area of about 10 km around it. A panel opens with that area’s climate profile: its climate type and what it means, monthly charts of day highs and night lows, rain and snow totals, hours of sunshine, wind and humidity, and the record high and low measured at the nearest long-running weather station. Where a value is missing from the source data it is left blank rather than filled in.

From there you can compare the place with another one side by side, save it, or open the chat and ask ClimaClue why the place has the climate it has. Every view can be shared as a link that reopens the same place.

What the colours and numbers are

Temperature and precipitation are CHELSA V2.1 monthly averages for 1981–2010, worked out for areas of about 10 km (0.1°). Sunshine comes from CRU CL 2.0 (about 1961–1990) and snow from ERA5-Land (1981–2010). They are modelled climate averages (normals), not weather forecasts or station records. Each area’s value is its average over its own land, so a mountain area and the valley next to it can look quite different, as they do in reality. Climate types are calculated from each area’s own monthly temperature and precipitation with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).

Averages over 30 years describe what a place is usually like in each month. They do not tell you about one particular day or year, about extremes, or about how the climate is changing now.

Questions and answers

Is the ClimaClue climate map free?
Yes. The globe, every measure and every town page are free to use in a web browser, with no sign-up.
What does the climate map show?
The Köppen–Geiger climate type of every area of land, plus monthly average temperature (with day highs and night lows), rain and snow, sunshine hours, wind, humidity, dew point and snowfall.
How detailed is the map?
Values are averaged over areas of about 10 km (0.1° of latitude and longitude), built from source data of about 1 km for temperature and precipitation.
What years does the data cover?
Temperature, precipitation, wind and humidity are 1981–2010 averages (CHELSA V2.1). Snow and dew point are 1981–2010 (ERA5-Land). Sunshine is an older estimate for about 1961–1990 (CRU CL 2.0).
Is this a weather forecast?
No. ClimaClue shows long-term monthly averages (climate normals), which describe what a place is usually like, not what the weather will be on a given day.
