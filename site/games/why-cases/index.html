Why? Cases: a climate science quiz

Explain the weather like a scientist. Each case shows a real town’s climate and asks why it has that pattern; pick the explanation that fits.

Play now ›

How to play

Each of the 5 rounds shows a real town’s monthly temperature and rain chart and its key numbers: warmest and coolest month, wettest month and yearly total. The question picks out one feature, for example why its summers are so dry when its winters bring rain, why it is hot all year but still has a long dry season, why it gets so little rain, or why its winters are so mild for how far it is from the equator. Choose from four explanations. The answer explains the science in plain words. There is one daily set, the same for everyone on the same date, and it changes at your local midnight. Your result can be shared as a short spoiler-free line.

What the answers are (and are not)

Each answer is the textbook mechanism typical of the town’s climate type, such as subtropical high pressure, the seasonal monsoon, a rain shadow or a nearby ocean. It is not a study of that particular town, and the game says so. Wrong options are checked so that none of them would also fit the town’s type, and tropical highland towns are left out because their mild climate comes from altitude, which the cases do not cover.

What you learn

Why? Cases connects the patterns on a chart to the processes behind them: how the sun’s seasonal shift moves rain belts, why the centres of continents swing between hot and cold, why west coasts and east coasts differ. It works well in a classroom: the daily set is the same for every student on the same date, a round takes a few minutes, and every answer comes with a short explanation to discuss.

The same explanations appear on ClimaClue’s climate type pages, for example Mediterranean, hot summer, Hot desert, Tropical savanna, Humid continental, warm summer.

Place-based games use towns of 100,000+ people. Each town’s values were compared with the nearest NOAA weather station that has 1981–2010 averages, and towns whose values disagree with that station are left out, so a round never rests on a doubtful number.

Questions and answers

What is a Why? Case?
A question about one feature of a real town’s climate (such as its dry summers) with four possible causes; you pick the one that typically explains it.
Are the explanations specific to each town?
No. Each is the textbook mechanism typical of the town’s climate type, not a study of that town, and the game says so.
Is Why? Cases suitable for school?
Yes. It covers topics found in earth science and geography courses, such as monsoons, rain shadows and continental climates.
