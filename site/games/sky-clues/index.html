Sky Clues: a meteorology and weather quiz

Free daily game

Sky Clues is a daily weather quiz on how the atmosphere works: clouds, thunderstorms, tornadoes, tropical storms, fronts, winds, fog, snow and more. Five questions a day, from easy to hard, and every answer explains the science.

Play now ›

How to play

Each day brings 5 multiple-choice questions, ordered from easiest to hardest, each labelled with its topic and level. Pick one of four answers. Right or wrong, a one- or two-sentence explanation follows, so every question teaches something. There is one daily set, the same for everyone on the same date, and it changes at your local midnight. Your result can be shared as a short spoiler-free line.

The question bank holds 456 questions, and each day takes the next five from a fixed shuffled order with the topics spread out, so the same question does not come back for about 91 days.

Two sample questions

Puffy, flat-bottomed cotton-ball clouds on a sunny afternoon are called…
Cumulus. Cumulus clouds grow from rising bubbles of sun-warmed air; their flat bases mark the height where that air cools enough to condense.
Why are clouds white?
Droplets scatter all colours about equally. Cloud droplets are much larger than air molecules, so they scatter all visible wavelengths together, which looks white.

Who it is for

Sky Clues suits anyone curious about the weather, students revising earth science or geography, and anyone learning to read the sky. It works well in a classroom: the daily set is the same for every student on the same date, a round takes a few minutes, and every answer comes with a short explanation to discuss. The questions cover standard, textbook atmospheric science, not claims about particular places. Each was checked once for accuracy; teachers should preview a set before relying on it in class.

What members get: members get unlimited practice rounds of every game and up to 50 saved places (free accounts: 5), from $1 a month (membership); the daily games stay free for everyone.
