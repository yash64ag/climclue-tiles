Where in the World? A geography climate game

A daily climate puzzle: from a few clues about a place’s climate, find its region on the globe. It tests how well you know where the world’s climates are.

Play now ›

How to play

Each puzzle is built around a real city. The answer is the region around it: the neighbouring land of the same climate family, up to about 300 km away. The clues are that place’s own yearly values, given as ranges: its climate type or family, the hemisphere, its yearly average temperature, how much rain and snow it gets in a year, and its hours of sunshine.

There are two levels. On Easy, up to five lettered regions appear on the globe, all with the same climate type; only one of them fits every clue, and a wrong pick shows which way to go. On Hard there are no letters. You start with two clues and each miss unlocks another, and a guess that fits every clue shown so far is free. Hard puzzles are checked against every 10 km of land on Earth so that only one region fits all the clues, and a click anywhere in it counts as right. You can also unlock a clue when you are stuck.

When the puzzle ends, the globe flies to the answer and shows the whole region, with the city’s climate type and why it has that climate. There is one daily set, the same for everyone on the same date, and it changes at your local midnight. Your result can be shared as a short spoiler-free line.

What you learn

To solve it you have to think like a geographer: a yearly average near 27 °C with a lot of rain points to the tropics; little rain and lots of sunshine to the subtropical deserts; a cold yearly average to high latitudes or high mountains. The same climate type turns up on several continents, so the clues teach you to tell them apart. It works well in a classroom: the daily set is the same for every student on the same date, a round takes a few minutes, and every answer comes with a short explanation to discuss.

Where the clues come from

Every clue is the target’s own value from ClimaClue’s yearly averages (CHELSA 1981–2010 temperature and precipitation, CRU sunshine for about 1961–1990), widened by a stated tolerance. Nothing is invented.

Questions and answers

How does Where in the World? work?
You get climate clues about a real place (climate type, hemisphere, yearly temperature, rain and sunshine) and click the region on the globe that fits them all.
What is the difference between Easy and Hard?
Easy shows up to five lettered regions of the same climate type and points you the right way after a miss. Hard starts with two clues, unlocks one per miss, and the answer is the only region on Earth that fits them all.
Is it a daily puzzle?
Yes. There is a new puzzle at local midnight, the same for everyone on the same date.
Is it good for geography lessons?
Yes. It practises where the world’s climate types occur and how temperature, rain and sunshine vary with latitude, altitude and the oceans.
