Mystery Chart: guess the city from its climate

A daily geography and climate game. You see one real town’s chart of monthly temperature and rain, with no name. Click the globe where you think it is, and use the clues from each guess to close in.

Play now ›

How to play

The chart shows twelve months of average temperature as a line and rain and snow as bars. Read it like a climate scientist: a hot, flat temperature line means the tropics; a big swing between summer and winter means a continental interior; a summer peak in July means the northern hemisphere, a peak in January the southern. Dry summers and wet winters suggest a Mediterranean climate; almost no rain at all, a desert.

Click anywhere on the globe to guess. You have 6 guesses. After each one you learn how far away you are and in which direction, how closely your guess’s own climate matches the mystery chart (as a percentage), and a short comparison such as “The mystery place is colder and much wetter”. A guess within 250 km finds the place and wins.

Each guess scores up to 5,000 points, by distance or by climate match, whichever is better, so a far-away place with the same climate still scores well. Only finding the place itself earns the full 5,000. At the end the answer is shown on the globe, with its climate type and a short explanation of why it has that climate. There is one daily set, the same for everyone on the same date, and it changes at your local midnight. Your result can be shared as a short spoiler-free line.

What you learn

Mystery Chart trains the skill of reading a climate graph (a climograph), which is part of most geography and earth science courses. After a few games the shapes become familiar: the double rain peak near the equator, the summer monsoon, the cold-winter steppe. It works well in a classroom: the daily set is the same for every student on the same date, a round takes a few minutes, and every answer comes with a short explanation to discuss.

Where the charts come from

Place-based games use towns of 100,000+ people. Each town’s values were compared with the nearest NOAA weather station that has 1981–2010 averages, and towns whose values disagree with that station are left out, so a round never rests on a doubtful number. Temperature and precipitation are CHELSA V2.1 monthly averages for 1981–2010, worked out for areas of about 10 km (0.1°). Sunshine comes from CRU CL 2.0 (about 1961–1990) and snow from ERA5-Land (1981–2010). They are modelled climate averages (normals), not weather forecasts or station records.

Questions and answers

How do you play Mystery Chart?
Look at a real town’s monthly temperature and rain chart, then click the globe where you think it is. You get 6 guesses, each with its distance, direction and climate match; within 250 km wins.
Is there a new Mystery Chart every day?
Yes. Everyone gets the same town on the same date, and a new one starts at local midnight.
Are the mystery towns real?
Yes. Every chart is a real town of 100,000+ people, using CHELSA 1981–2010 monthly averages for its ~10 km area, and towns that disagree with a nearby weather station are left out.
Is Mystery Chart free?
Yes, the daily game is free to play in a web browser.
