How the daily games work

Every game has a daily set that is the same for everyone on the same date and changes at your local midnight, so you can compare scores with friends or a class. Results can be shared as a short spoiler-free line, and the game picker ticks the quiz games you have already played today. When one game ends, ClimaClue suggests the next one you have not played yet.

Several games use the globe itself: in Mystery Chart and Where in the World? you guess by clicking a place, and the answers and the towns from each round are marked on the map afterwards.

Real data, checked against weather stations

Place-based games use towns of 100,000+ people. Each town’s values were compared with the nearest NOAA weather station that has 1981–2010 averages, and towns whose values disagree with that station are left out, so a round never rests on a doubtful number. Temperature and precipitation are CHELSA V2.1 monthly averages for 1981–2010, worked out for areas of about 10 km (0.1°). Sunshine comes from CRU CL 2.0 (about 1961–1990) and snow from ERA5-Land (1981–2010). They are modelled climate averages (normals), not weather forecasts or station records. Quiz rounds are only asked when the answer is clear-cut, so a near-tie never decides a round. Sky Clues is the exception: its questions cover general atmospheric science rather than particular places.

For classrooms

The games cover topics from geography and earth science courses: reading climate graphs, the Köppen climate types, seasons in both hemispheres, monsoons, rain shadows and ocean currents, clouds and storms. The daily set is the same for every student, a round fits into a few minutes, and every answer comes with an explanation to discuss.

Questions and answers

Are ClimaClue’s climate games free?
Yes. Every daily game is free to play in a web browser, with no sign-up.
What climate games are there?
Mystery Chart, Where in the World?, Higher or Lower, Climate Twins, Which Month?, Climate Type Sprint, Why? Cases, Sky Clues.
Is there a meteorology or weather quiz?
Yes: Sky Clues asks five questions a day on clouds, storms, fronts, winds and other sky science, each with an explanation.
Is there a game where you guess the city from its climate?
Yes: in Mystery Chart you see a real town’s monthly temperature and rain chart and guess where it is on the globe, with six guesses.
Can I use the games in class?
Yes. The daily set is the same for every student on the same date, and the games cover climate topics taught in geography and earth science.
