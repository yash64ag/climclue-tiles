About ClimaClue

ClimaClue is a globe for exploring the world's climates. Click any place on land, or search a town, to see its monthly temperature, rainfall, sunshine, wind, humidity, dew point and cloud cover. Compare up to four places on shared scales. ClimaClue describes long-term climate (typical conditions over decades), not weather forecasts.

How places work

Everywhere on land, a click opens a fixed 0.1° latitude–longitude area: about 11 km north–south, narrower east–west towards the poles. Each chart shows the area's average and how much the climate varies across its land, so you can see when an area mixes coast and inland, or valley and mountain. Only source cells lying wholly on land are used.

Town pages, the games and the Matchmaker describe each town itself: temperature, day highs and night lows, rain and wind are averaged over the ~1 km source cells in the 3 × 3 km around the town’s centre, so a town in a valley is not averaged with the mountainsides above it. Sunshine, dew point and snow come from coarser sources and keep the 10 km area’s values. Clicking the same town on the globe shows its 10 km area, which in steep terrain can be several degrees cooler.

You can save any place under your own name in Your places. Saved places are kept in your browser for now.

Why each place's climate is like it is

Every area's climate type is calculated from its own monthly temperature and rainfall using the standard Köppen–Geiger rules (Peel, Finlayson & McMahon, 2007, Hydrology and Earth System Sciences 11, 1633–1644). On a check of 25 well-known cities, 21 match their published types; the other four lie close to a class boundary, and the app flags areas like that. The "why" explanation then combines the mechanisms that typically produce that climate type, labelled as typical rather than verified for the exact spot, with facts measured for the area itself. Those facts include its distance from the sea (measured to the nearest open sea; lakes don't count), its latitude and day length, its seasonal swing, and how much it varies inside.

Honest data

  • Every value is modeled, estimated or observed, and labelled as such with its source and period.
  • Missing values stay missing. They are never filled with estimates or zero.
  • A smaller area does not make a coarse source more precise; each source's own resolution is stated.

Data sources and licences

Temperature, rainfall, wind: CHELSA V2.1 climatologies, 1981–2010, about 1 km (cloud cover about 3 km). Karger, D.N. et al. (2017) Climatologies at high resolution for the earth's land surface areas. Scientific Data 4, 170122; Karger, D.N. et al. (2021) CHELSA V2.1, EnviDat, doi:10.16904/envidat.228.v2.1. Licence: CC0. chelsa-climate.org

Dew point: ERA5-Land monthly averaged reanalysis, 2 m dew point temperature, 1981–2010, about 9 km. Contains modified Copernicus Climate Change Service information (2026). Neither the European Commission nor ECMWF is responsible for any use that may be made of the Copernicus information or data it contains. Licence: CC BY 4.0. Copernicus Climate Data Store

Relative humidity: calculated by ClimaClue, not taken from a source. For each month it is the average of the humidity at the area’s average night low and at its average day high (CHELSA), for the month’s average dew point (ERA5-Land), so it is no finer than the dew point’s 9 km. Checked against humidity measured at weather stations in 120 US cities (NOAA Comparative Climatic Data): within 5 points in four months out of five, about 3 points off on average. CHELSA’s own humidity field is not used: read as published, it came out about 9 points too low at those stations and far too low in humid places.

Sunshine: CRU CL 2.0, New, M., Lister, D., Hulme, M. and Makin, I. (2002) A high-resolution data set of surface climate over global land areas. Climate Research 21, 1–25. Period centred on 1961–1990, about 18 km. ClimaClue converts percent of possible sunshine into hours using astronomical day length. Climatic Research Unit

Snow: ERA5-Land monthly snowfall and snow depth, 1981–2010, about 9 km (Copernicus; licence CC BY 4.0). Snowfall is matched to each 10 km area’s own height and climate: it is recomputed from the area’s CHELSA precipitation and temperature, using ERA5’s share of precipitation that falls as snow at that temperature, and checked against about 2,800 weather stations (NOAA GHCN-Daily) it was not tuned on. In mild oceanic climates (where summer and winter differ by less than about 18 °C) months near or above freezing use the snow share measured at North American weather stations in similar climates instead, because there ERA5 put several times too much snow on coasts such as Vancouver’s. Snow on the ground is ERA5-Land as given.

Recorded highs and lows: NOAA GHCN-Daily, nearest station with a long record (NOAA NCEI).

Town names and search: GeoNames, towns of 5,000+ people. Licence: CC BY 4.0. geonames.org

Coastlines and borders: Natural Earth (public domain), via world-atlas. naturalearthdata.com
