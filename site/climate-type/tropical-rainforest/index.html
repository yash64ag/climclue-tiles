Tropical rainforest climate (Köppen Af)

Hot and wet all year, with no real dry season.

  • Every month is warm, with little change through the year
  • Rain in every month, often as afternoon downpours
  • Humid air and dense evergreen forest

Where the tropical rainforest climate occurs

351 of ClimaClue’s towns of 100,000+ people have this type, in 25 countries. Across them, the median warmest month averages 27.1 °C (81 °F), the median coolest month 25.8 °C (78 °F), and the median yearly precipitation is 2,562 mm (100.9 in).

Types are calculated from each town’s modelled 1981–2010 monthly averages for its ~10 km area, with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).

What typically causes this climate

Textbook mechanisms that typically produce this type; any one place can differ.

Temperature
Near the equator the midday sun is high all year, so temperatures barely change between months.
Rain and snow
Warm, moist air rises almost every day near the equatorial rain belt, building showers and thunderstorms, so no month is truly dry.
Sunshine and cloud
Daily towering clouds limit sunshine even though the sun is high.
Moisture
Hot air over wet forest and warm seas holds a lot of water vapour, so dew points stay high all year.
Wind
Winds are often light; rising air and daily storms matter more than steady breezes.
