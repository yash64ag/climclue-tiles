Subarctic climate (Köppen Dfc)

Short, cool summers and long, very cold winters.

  • No dry season, though totals are often modest
  • Only a few months are properly warm
  • Long, cold winters; the home of the northern conifer forest

Where the subarctic climate occurs

25 of ClimaClue’s towns of 100,000+ people have this type, in 6 countries. Across them, the median warmest month averages 16.6 °C (62 °F), the median coolest month -12.9 °C (9 °F), and the median yearly precipitation is 705 mm (27.7 in).

Types are calculated from each town’s modelled 1981–2010 monthly averages for its ~10 km area, with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).

What typically causes this climate

Textbook mechanisms that typically produce this type; any one place can differ.

Temperature
Inland from the sea, land loses heat in winter and warms quickly in summer, so seasons are strongly contrasted.
Rain and snow
Storm systems bring precipitation year-round: rain in summer, snow in winter.
Sunshine and cloud
Cloudiness follows passing storms; long summer days can still bring plenty of sunshine.
Moisture
Cold winter air holds little water vapour; summer dew points are much higher.
Wind
Storm tracks bring windier weather in the cold season.
