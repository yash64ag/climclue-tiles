Hot desert climate (Köppen BWh)

Very dry and hot: far more water could evaporate than ever falls.

  • Very little rain in any month
  • Scorching summers and mild to warm winters
  • Clear skies and big swings between day and night

Where the hot desert climate occurs

368 of ClimaClue’s towns of 100,000+ people have this type, in 39 countries. Across them, the median warmest month averages 33.6 °C (93 °F), the median coolest month 13.9 °C (57 °F), and the median yearly precipitation is 133 mm (5.2 in).

Types are calculated from each town’s modelled 1981–2010 monthly averages for its ~10 km area, with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).

What typically causes this climate

Textbook mechanisms that typically produce this type; any one place can differ.

Temperature
Clear, dry air lets the ground heat strongly by day and lose heat quickly at night, so swings are large.
Rain and snow
Sinking air in the subtropical high-pressure belt, distance from moist air, or mountains blocking it keeps rain rare.
Sunshine and cloud
Sinking dry air suppresses clouds, so sunshine is abundant.
Moisture
Little water is available to evaporate, so dew points are low and humidity drops sharply in the afternoon heat.
Wind
Strong daytime heating can stir gusty winds and dust.
