Subarctic, dry winter climate (Köppen Dwc)

Short, cool summers and long, very cold, dry winters.

  • Most of the little rain falls in summer
  • Only a few months are properly warm
  • Long, dry, bitterly cold winters

Where the subarctic, dry winter climate occurs

4 of ClimaClue’s towns of 100,000+ people have this type, in 3 countries. Across them, the median warmest month averages 14.5 °C (58 °F), the median coolest month -18.5 °C (-1 °F), and the median yearly precipitation is 595 mm (23.4 in).

Types are calculated from each town’s modelled 1981–2010 monthly averages for its ~10 km area, with the Köppen–Geiger rules of Peel et al. (2007).

What typically causes this climate

Textbook mechanisms that typically produce this type; any one place can differ.

Temperature
A huge seasonal swing: winter air flows out of the cold continental interior, while summer is warm.
Rain and snow
A cold, dry winter high pressure keeps winters almost snow-free; summer monsoon-like flow brings most of the rain.
Sunshine and cloud
Winters are clear and sunny under high pressure.
Moisture
Winter air is extremely dry; summer brings moisture with the rains.
Wind
Cold winter outflow winds can be strong and steady.
