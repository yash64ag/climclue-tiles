Where should you live, based on climate? ClimaClue’s free Climate Matchmaker lets you describe the climate you like, such as mild winters, summers that are warm but not too hot, plenty of sun and low humidity, and ranks towns around the world by how well they fit.

How the Climate Matchmaker works

Switch on only the things you care about, drag its two handles to the range you would be happy with (say, winters between 5 and 12 °C), and say how much it matters: a little, some or a lot. Under each slider a bar shows where the world’s towns sit, from the lowest 10% to the highest 10%, so you can see whether a wish is common or rare. You can also ask for a place near the coast.

The measures you can set are:

Typical temperatures

  • Winter averagecoolest month, day and night together
  • Summer averagewarmest month, day and night together
  • Summer daystypical afternoon high in the warmest month
  • Winter nightstypical overnight low in the coldest month

Extremes

  • Hot days99% of days have a high below this, at the nearest weather station
  • Cold nights99% of nights have a low above this, at the nearest weather station

Precipitation and snow

  • Precipitationrain and snow combined, total a year
  • Dry monthsmonths with under 30 mm
  • Snowfallfresh snow a year

Sun and air

  • Sunshinehours a year
  • Humidityaverage relative humidity
  • Mugginessmuggiest month’s dew point (above 20 °C feels sticky)
  • Windaverage wind speed

Each town gets a score out of 100% for every measure you switched on: 100% anywhere inside your range, falling in a straight line to 0% at a fixed distance beyond its nearer end: 10 °C for temperatures and dew point, three times too much or too little for precipitation and snow, 5 dry months, 1,000 hours of sunshine, 25 points of humidity and 3 m/s of wind. The match combines those scores as a weighted geometric mean, with “a lot” counting three times “some”, and “some” three times “a little”, so one big miss can’t hide behind good fits, and adding more wishes doesn’t drag every town to zero. Towns that fit every range score 100%; among them, the ones nearer the middle of your ranges come first. The list shows at most two towns per country, so you see the whole world rather than one country’s suburbs, and every result says which of your wishes it meets and which it misses. The matches light up on the globe, and one click opens a town’s full climate profile.

What the matches are based on

The Matchmaker uses every town of 15,000+ people (and every capital), one per 10 km area, about 25,000 in all, each described by its own monthly averages: CHELSA V2.1 temperature and precipitation (1981–2010), CRU sunshine (about 1961–1990) and ERA5-Land dew point and snow (1981–2010). Extremes are measured, not modelled: the 99th percentile of daily highs and the 1st percentile of nightly lows over 1981–2010 at the nearest qualifying NOAA GHCN-Daily weather station within 50 km; towns without one show no value. It matches climate only. Cost of living, jobs, safety and everything else that makes a place a good home are up to you, and averages say nothing about a single summer or winter, so treat the list as a starting point for research.

To check a result in detail, open its town page, for example London, New York City, Tokyo, Sydney, Dubai, or put two candidates side by side with the comparison tool.

What members get: members see the full list of 20 matches and can save up to 50 places (free accounts: 5), from $1 a month (membership).

Questions and answers

How do I find a city with my ideal climate?
Open the Climate Matchmaker, switch on the measures you care about (winter and summer temperature, rain, sunshine, humidity, snow, wind, the coast), set the ranges you like, and it ranks towns worldwide by how well they fit.
Where should I live based on climate?
That depends on what you like. The Matchmaker turns your preferences into a ranked list of towns and shows which wishes each one meets, so you can compare the candidates in detail.
Which towns does the Climate Matchmaker include?
Every town of 15,000+ people worldwide (and every capital), about 25,000, each described by its own modelled 1981–2010 monthly averages.
Does it consider cost of living or other factors?
No. It matches climate only: temperature, precipitation (rain and snow combined), sunshine, humidity, dew point, wind and closeness to the coast.
Is the Climate Matchmaker free?
Yes, it is free to use in a web browser.
